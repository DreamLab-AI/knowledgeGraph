# Quantitative Easing

Quantitative easing (QE) is an unconventional monetary policy in which a central bank purchases large quantities of longer-dated financial assets, typically government bonds, to inject liquidity into the financial system and lower long-term interest rates. It is deployed when short-term policy rates are already near zero and conventional rate cuts are exhausted. By expanding the central bank's balance sheet and the monetary base, QE aims to stimulate lending, asset prices and aggregate demand.
