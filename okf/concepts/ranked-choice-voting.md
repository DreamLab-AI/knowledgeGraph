# Ranked-Choice Voting

Ranked-choice voting is a voting method in which voters rank candidates in order of preference, and votes are redistributed in rounds by eliminating the lowest-ranked candidate until one candidate holds a majority. It is studied within social choice theory as an alternative to plurality voting intended to reduce spoiler effects and better reflect aggregate voter preference. It is used in some collective decision-making systems, including governance mechanisms for decentralised organisations.
