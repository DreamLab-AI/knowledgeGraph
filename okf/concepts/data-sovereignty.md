# Data Sovereignty

Data sovereignty is the legal and political principle that digital data is subject to the laws, governance structures, and enforcement jurisdiction of the nation, region, or community in which it originates or is processed. It encompasses requirements such as data localisation (mandating that data remain physically within specified geographic boundaries), restrictions on cross-border data flows, and the right of governments or communities to compel access for regulatory, security, or cultural-preservation purposes. Data sovereignty directly shapes cloud architecture decisions, AI training dataset curation, federated system design, and multinational data-sharing agreements. It intersects with — yet is conceptually distinct from — privacy rights, data protection law, and cybersecurity, being principally concerned with jurisdictional control rather than individual rights.
