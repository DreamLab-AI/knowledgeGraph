# Pervasive Computing

Pervasive computing — also termed ubiquitous computing — is a paradigm in which computational capability is embedded throughout the physical environment and integrated seamlessly into everyday objects and infrastructure, such that computation becomes a background utility rather than a discrete user activity. Coined by Mark Weiser at Xerox PARC in 1991, the vision encompasses context-aware, networked devices that sense, process, and communicate without explicit user interaction. It is the conceptual precursor to the Internet of Things and ambient intelligence.
