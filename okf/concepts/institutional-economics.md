# Institutional Economics

A school of economic thought that analyses how formal institutions (laws, regulations, contracts) and informal institutions (norms, customs, culture) shape economic behaviour, incentive structures, and aggregate outcomes. It departs from neoclassical assumptions of frictionless exchange by foregrounding transaction costs, bounded rationality, and the evolutionary path-dependence of rules. The field spans the original institutionalism of Veblen and Commons and the New Institutional Economics (NIE) of Coase, North, and Williamson, which provided micro-foundations for why institutions exist and how they change. Institutional economics informs applied work in development economics, regulatory design, organisational theory, and governance of digital platforms.
