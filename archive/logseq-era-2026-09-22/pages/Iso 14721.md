- ### Definition
- ISO 14721 specifies the Open Archival Information System reference model, the foundational framework for [[Digital Preservation]].
- Published by [[ISO]], it defines the functions and information packages an archive needs for [[Long Term Preservation]].
- It is widely applied in [[Cultural Heritage Preservation]] and depends on rich [[Metadata Standard]] practice.
- ### Overview
- OAIS provides a common vocabulary and conceptual model rather than an implementation. It describes an archive in terms of producers who deposit content, a designated community who consume it, and management who set policy.
- Information moves through the archive as packages: Submission Information Packages from producers, Archival Information Packages held internally, and Dissemination Information Packages delivered to users, each bundling content with preservation metadata.
- Functional entities — ingest, archival storage, data management, administration, preservation planning and access — divide the archive's responsibilities, giving repositories a shared blueprint and a basis for trustworthy-repository audit.
- ### Key aspects
- The producer, consumer and management roles framing archival responsibility.
- The SIP, AIP and DIP information-package model.
- Six core functional entities including preservation planning.
- Preservation description information ensuring future understandability.
- A basis for repository certification and audit frameworks.
- ### Applications
- National libraries, archives and research data repositories.
- Cultural heritage digitisation and long-term media preservation.
- Scientific and spatial data archives requiring decades-long accessibility.
