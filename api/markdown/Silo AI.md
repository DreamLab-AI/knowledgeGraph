- ### Definition
  - Silo AI is a Finnish artificial intelligence company founded in Helsinki in 2017, recognised as Europe's largest private AI lab prior to its acquisition by Advanced Micro Devices (AMD) in August 2024, specialising in end-to-end enterprise AI solutions, multilingual open-source large language model development, and European digital sovereignty through language technology research, having produced the Poro 34B model (2023) — the first major open Finnish/English LLM trained on the LUMI EuroHPC supercomputer — and the Viking model family (7B, 13B, 33B, 2024) covering all Nordic languages (Danish, Finnish, Icelandic, Norwegian, Swedish) and programming languages under the Apache 2.0 licence, training models on up to 4,096 AMD MI250X GPUs with datasets of 1–2 trillion tokens using architectures ranging from BLOOM with ALiBi embeddings (Poro) to Llama-2-style transformer stacks with flash attention, rotary embeddings and grouped query attention (Viking), both produced via the LumiOpen collaboration between Silo AI's SiloGen arm, the University of Turku's TurkuNLP group, and the EU-funded High Performance Language Technologies (HPLT) project, addressing the fundamental challenge of low-resource language modelling by cross-training under-resourced languages such as Finnish with high-resource English to exploit cross-lingual transfer signals rather than training purely monolingual models, achieving state-of-the-art performance on FIN-bench (Finnish evaluation benchmark) competitive with Llama, Mistral, and FinGPT whilst preserving English performance parity, operating as a 300-person organisation (approximately half with senior academic backgrounds) serving enterprise customers including Allianz, Philips, Rolls-Royce, and Unilever across 15+ European and North American markets, bootstrapped profitably from 2017–2021 with average 100% annual revenue growth (€12.3M revenue in 2021, €14.3M in 2022), receiving its first external capital from Swedish private equity firm Altor in 2022, and acquired by AMD on 10 July 2024 (announcement) and 12 August 2024 (completion) in an all-cash transaction valued at approximately $665 million — the largest AI acquisition in European history — with CEO Peter Sarlin continuing to lead the team as part of AMD's Artificial Intelligence Group, co-leading the OpenEuroLLM initiative (launched February 2025, €37.4M EU-funded consortium of 20 institutions building open LLMs for all 24 EU official languages under the Digital Europe Programme), exemplifying the convergence of sovereign AI policy, multilingual NLP research, European compute infrastructure, and corporate AI consolidation.

  - ## About Silo AI
  - **Silo AI** is a Helsinki-based artificial intelligence company founded in 2017, formally self-described as "Europe's largest private AI lab" prior to its acquisition by Advanced Micro Devices (AMD) in August 2024. The company occupies a distinctive position at the intersection of enterprise AI services, open-source multilingual language model research, and European digital sovereignty—demonstrating that a bootstrapped, revenue-led AI lab can reach global scale without the conventional Silicon Valley venture capital pathway.

  - Silo AI was founded by Peter Sarlin (CEO, PhD in machine learning, Aalto University background), Tero Ojanperä (former Nokia CTO), Johan Kronberg (former PwC CEO and Chairman), Ville Hulkko and Juha Hulkko (entrepreneur siblings), and Adj. Prof. Kaj-Mikael Björk. This founding team combined deep academic AI research credentials with senior industry leadership experience across telecommunications, finance, and technology—a configuration that enabled Silo AI to win major enterprise contracts from day one without the prolonged runway dependency typical of research-first startups. The lab operates a dual mandate: building bespoke, productised AI solutions for large enterprise customers, and advancing the state of open-source multilingual language modelling for European linguistic communities.

  - ### Business Model and Growth Trajectory

  - Silo AI was entirely bootstrapped from 2017 through 2021, funding its operations through revenue from enterprise AI contracts rather than external venture capital. This produced remarkable financial discipline: the company averaged 100% year-on-year revenue growth for four consecutive years between 2019 and 2022. By 2021, revenues reached €12.3M; by 2022, €14.3M—with losses of €1.5M in that year attributable to deliberate geographic expansion into North American markets. The company maintained approximately half its ~300 employees with senior academic backgrounds, creating an unusual density of research competence within a commercially oriented lab.

  - In 2022, Silo AI raised its first external capital from Altor, a Swedish private equity firm founded by Harald Mix. The investment was undisclosed but framed explicitly as a partnership "to build a leading European AI flagship"—reflecting the strategic imperative for European AI capability independent of US and Chinese hyperscalers. Altor later divested its stake as part of the AMD acquisition in 2024.

  - ### Poro 34B — Europe's Open Finnish LLM

  - The most significant early milestone in Silo AI's research agenda was **Poro 34B**, announced via the first training checkpoints on 13 November 2023. Poro was a collaboration between Silo AI's SiloGen generative AI arm, the TurkuNLP Group at the University of Turku (Finland), and the EU-funded High Performance Language Technologies (HPLT) project. Named after the Finnish word for reindeer, Poro 34B represents a 34.2-billion-parameter open language model trained primarily on English, Finnish, and programming language code.

  - The architectural choices for Poro 34B reflect the state of the field in late 2023: a BLOOM-based architecture with ALiBi (Attention with Linear Biases) positional embeddings, enabling context window extrapolation beyond training length. The model was trained on a dataset of approximately 1 trillion tokens using 512 AMD MI250X GPUs on the LUMI EuroHPC supercomputer in Kajaani, Finland. Training was conducted using a customised fork of the Megatron-DeepSpeed framework optimised for LUMI's AMD-based architecture.

  - Poro's approach to Finnish language modelling is technically instructive. Finnish is a morphologically complex Uralic language with agglutinative structure, free word order, and approximately 5.5 million speakers—a severely low-resource environment for LLM training given the global internet's English dominance. Rather than attempting a purely monolingual Finnish model, Poro cross-trains Finnish with high-resource English text, exploiting cross-lingual transfer signals to achieve higher Finnish performance than a monolingual model trained on the same Finnish data volume alone. After just 30% of its training run, Poro already achieved state-of-the-art Finnish base-model performance on FIN-bench, outperforming FinGPT, Llama, and Mistral variants—whilst maintaining English performance on par with comparable open English models. Basic Finnish-English translation capability emerged as a by-product of this cross-lingual training strategy.

  - Poro introduced the **Poro Research Checkpoints** programme: an unprecedented policy of releasing a series of model checkpoints throughout the training process, giving external researchers visibility into the dynamics of large-scale language model training at stages that would otherwise be inaccessible to groups without the compute resources to replicate such training runs. The final model was released under Apache 2.0 licence at `LumiOpen/Poro-34B` on Hugging Face, permitting both commercial and research use without restriction.

  - ### Viking Model Family — Nordic Languages at Scale

  - Building on the Poro foundation, Silo AI released the **Viking** model family in April–May 2024, representing the next generation of its multilingual open-source LLM programme. Viking came in three sizes—7B, 13B, and 33B parameters—extending language coverage from Poro's English/Finnish scope to all Nordic languages: Danish, Finnish, Icelandic, Norwegian, and Swedish, alongside English and multiple programming languages.

  - Viking's architecture shifted from Poro's BLOOM base to a **Llama 2-inspired transformer** design incorporating flash attention for memory-efficient long-sequence processing, rotary positional embeddings (RoPE) for improved length generalisation, and grouped query attention (GQA) for reduced inference memory footprint. This architectural modernisation reflected the rapid evolution of efficient transformer building blocks during 2023–2024. The training dataset expanded to 2 trillion tokens, and Viking 33B was trained using up to 4,096 AMD MI250X GPUs on LUMI—one of the largest open-access training runs on AMD hardware at that point.

  - Viking was produced using a **fully open-source training framework** specifically adapted for LUMI, itself a contribution to the broader European AI infrastructure commons. Benchmarks on translated MMLU, ARC-C, and HellaSwag showed Viking achieving best-in-class performance among open models for Nordic low-resource languages without regression on English or programming benchmarks—demonstrating the viability of scaling the cross-lingual transfer strategy beyond Finnish to the broader Nordic language family. All Viking variants are available at `LumiOpen/Viking-33B` (and sibling repositories) under Apache 2.0.

  - ### SiloGen — Enterprise Model-as-a-Service

  - Alongside the research-facing Poro and Viking releases, Silo AI developed **SiloGen**: a production-grade model-as-a-service platform providing enterprises with end-to-end capabilities to deploy and customise LLMs at scale. SiloGen offers guardrails, safety controls, and fine-tuning pipelines for domain-specific adaptation. In 2024, SiloGen was deployed with several enterprise customers including Tietoevry, Transcom, MySpeaker, Etals, and Doctrin. This dual-track strategy—open research models as a demonstration of capability and trust-building, proprietary deployment platform as the revenue vehicle—mirrors patterns seen in other European AI companies such as Aleph Alpha and Mistral AI.

  - ### AMD Acquisition — July–August 2024

  - On **10 July 2024**, AMD announced a definitive agreement to acquire Silo AI in an all-cash transaction valued at approximately **$665 million**—the largest AI company acquisition in European history at that point. AMD completed the transaction on **12 August 2024**, integrating Silo AI into the AMD Artificial Intelligence Group under Senior Vice President Vamsi Boppana, with Peter Sarlin continuing to lead the Silo AI team.

  - The strategic rationale from AMD's perspective was explicit: Silo AI brought a team of world-class AI scientists and engineers experienced in LLM development and enterprise deployment, specialised competencies in building and optimising models on AMD hardware (critical given AMD's competitive position against NVIDIA's CUDA ecosystem dominance), and an established portfolio of major European enterprise relationships. AMD had already invested over $125 million across approximately a dozen AI companies in the twelve months prior, and had acquired Mipsology and Nod.ai; Silo AI represented by far the largest and most strategically significant of these moves.

  - For Silo AI, the acquisition represented a supply-side transformation: access to AMD's global sales force, enterprise relationships, and hardware ecosystem amplified the company's ability to deploy at scale. Silo AI's open-source model work on LUMI had been conducted on AMD hardware throughout, creating natural alignment; AMD's interest in demonstrating the viability of its MI-series GPUs for frontier AI training at scale made Silo AI's track record directly commercially relevant.

  - ### OpenEuroLLM — EU Consortium for All 24 Official Languages

  - The most ambitious post-acquisition initiative co-led by Peter Sarlin is the **OpenEuroLLM** project, which commenced 1 February 2025. A consortium of 20 leading European research institutions, companies, and EuroHPC centres, OpenEuroLLM is co-ordinated by Prof. Jan Hajič (Charles University, Prague) and co-led by Peter Sarlin (AMD Silo AI). Its goal is to build a family of performant, multilingual, open large language foundation models covering all 24 official EU languages, trained on European supercomputing infrastructure, with full transparency on data, architecture, and training process.

  - Funding totals €37.4 million, with €20.6 million from the EU's Digital Europe Programme. OpenEuroLLM received the **STEP Seal** (Strategic Technologies for Europe Platform), making it the first initiative under the Digital Europe Programme to receive this designation—signalling its alignment with Europe's strategic autonomy objectives in digital technology. The project addresses the critical gap between US/China-led proprietary frontier models and European public-interest needs: sovereignty over training data, compliance with GDPR and EU AI Act requirements, and preservation of cultural and linguistic diversity across a continent where English remains one of 24 official languages.

  - ### LUMI and European HPC Infrastructure

  - The LUMI (Large Unified Modern Infrastructure) supercomputer in Kajaani, Finland, underpins Silo AI's entire research programme. LUMI is Europe's most powerful supercomputer and ranks fifth globally (Top500, June 2024), built on HPE Cray EX architecture with 11,912 AMD MI250X GPUs across 2,978 nodes. Operated by CSC – IT Center for Science, LUMI also ranks third among the top-500 supercomputers by energy efficiency, powered largely by hydroelectric energy. LUMI is the centrepiece of Finland's role in the EU AI Factories initiative—a European Commission programme providing integrated access to compute, data, and skills for AI researchers and industrial innovators. This Finnish compute infrastructure directly enables the Silo AI/AMD research roadmap.

  - ### Related European Open LLM Initiatives

  - Silo AI's work exists within a broader European ecosystem of sovereign open LLM development. The **EuroLLM** project, a separate consortium involving Instituto Superior Técnico (Lisbon), the University of Edinburgh, Unbabel, Sorbonne University, and others, launched **EuroLLM-22B** in late 2025—a fully open LLM trained on approximately 4 trillion tokens across 400 NVIDIA H100 GPUs on the MareNostrum5 supercomputer, covering 35 languages including all 24 official EU languages plus Arabic, Japanese, Korean, and others. EuroLLM was supported by Horizon Europe and EuroHPC. Silo AI's work through Poro and Viking pre-dated EuroLLM's release and helped establish the architectural and data curation foundations that later projects could iterate upon.

  - ### Use Cases / Major Application Families

  - Silo AI's enterprise AI solutions span five primary application domains. **Industry 4.0 and manufacturing** (Rolls-Royce, Philips clients): predictive maintenance, quality control computer vision, anomaly detection in industrial sensor streams. **Financial services** (Allianz client): risk assessment, fraud detection, document processing, regulatory compliance automation. **Healthcare and life sciences**: clinical NLP, patient documentation extraction, diagnostic support systems. **Customer-facing AI** (Transcom, Doctrin clients): conversational AI for customer service automation, multilingual chatbots for Northern European markets. **Nordic-language enterprise software**: domain-adapted LLMs for Swedish, Finnish, Danish, Norwegian, and Icelandic enterprise customers requiring GDPR-compliant, on-premises or private-cloud deployable language intelligence.

  - The Viking and Poro model families directly serve Nordic enterprise customers who cannot use US-hosted proprietary models for data sovereignty or regulatory reasons—a structural market driver unique to Europe that Silo AI exploited by being the first organisation to produce production-grade open Norse LLMs.

  - ### Academic Context

  - Silo AI occupies a liminal position between academic and industrial AI research. Approximately half of its 300 staff hold senior academic positions or hold PhDs, and the company maintains active co-authorship relationships with the University of Turku (TurkuNLP group led by Filip Ginter), Charles University Prague, and institutions within the HPLT consortium. The TurkuNLP group had previously produced Finnish GPT-3 scale models released in February 2023, providing the foundational infrastructure and expertise that enabled the Poro collaboration. The HPLT project itself is a €17M Horizon Europe initiative creating training-ready multilingual text corpora from web archives for all European languages—Silo AI's primary data supply chain for Poro and Viking.

  - Poro's release strategy—distributing research checkpoints throughout training rather than only at completion—was novel for a non-academic organisation and directly addressed a gap in LLM research access. It allowed academic groups without access to multi-hundred-GPU clusters to study training dynamics, emergent capabilities, and loss curve behaviour at scales previously reserved for hyperscalers. This research ethos, combined with full Apache 2.0 licencing, positioned Silo AI's models as genuine public goods rather than corporate-controlled artefacts with research-only access restrictions.

  - ### Current Landscape (2026)

  - As of May 2026, Silo AI operates as a division of AMD, with Peter Sarlin heading the Silo AI team within the AMD Artificial Intelligence Group. The team continues active model development and enterprise AI deployment under the AMD brand, with OpenEuroLLM in its second year of operation. AMD has expanded the Viking and Poro model families, adding Poro Chat (instruction-tuned) and further Viking variants across different scales and language coverage profiles. The integration with AMD's global sales network has expanded SiloGen's reach into North American and Asian enterprise markets beyond its original European footprint.

  - The OpenEuroLLM project is producing its first public model releases in 2025–2026, representing the most comprehensive EU-language open LLM family yet constructed. AMD Silo AI's co-leadership role positions the company as a critical actor in European AI governance discussions, particularly around the EU AI Act's requirements for transparency, data provenance, and systemic risk classification of general-purpose AI models.

  - The European open-source LLM landscape as a whole has matured significantly: where Silo AI was a pioneering outlier in 2023, by 2026 it co-exists with Mistral AI (France), Aleph Alpha (Germany), EuroLLM consortium (multinational), and national initiatives across Scandinavia, the Netherlands, and Poland. The AMD acquisition gives Silo AI durable structural advantages in compute access and enterprise distribution that smaller European AI labs lack.

  - ### UK Context

  - The UK equivalent of Silo AI's European sovereign AI mission has developed along parallel but distinct tracks. The **UK AI Safety Institute** (AISI), established November 2023 under Prime Minister Sunak's Bletchley Park summit, focused initially on safety evaluation of frontier models rather than sovereign LLM development. However, by 2024–2025 the UK sovereign AI discourse shifted towards capability development, with the **BritLLM** project at University College London (led by Prof. Pontus Stenetorp, UCL AI Centre) emerging as the most direct UK parallel to Silo AI's Poro work—training an open LLM on English, Welsh, and other UK languages on the **Isambard-AI** supercomputer in Bristol, with UCL confirmed as the sole UK partner of NVIDIA's sovereign AI platforms initiative.

  - The **University of Edinburgh**, which participated in the EuroLLM-22B consortium, provides the most direct UK-European open LLM linkage, connecting Scottish academic AI research directly into the broader European sovereignty architecture that Silo AI helped establish. Edinburgh's School of Informatics and its Centre for Language Technology have long-standing research partnerships with Nordic NLP groups, making this collaboration a natural extension of established academic relationships.

  - The broader **UK Sovereign AI Fund**, launched with £500M in backing from the UK government, funds British AI startups broadly but lacks the focused open multilingual LLM mandate of Silo AI or OpenEuroLLM—reflecting a different policy philosophy favouring market-led development over the EU's consortium-public-infrastructure model. **Arm Holdings** (Cambridge-headquartered, SoftBank-owned), plays the UK compute sovereignty role: Arm is a technology partner for Stargate UK and the UK-US AI Partnership, delivering the power-efficient compute platform for UK AI infrastructure expansion.

  - Manchester and Leeds industrial AI clusters, particularly in healthcare NLP (NHS data partnerships) and manufacturing AI (precision engineering sector), represent the closest UK functional equivalents to Silo AI's enterprise AI services model—though without the open foundational model research component that distinguishes Silo AI's dual mandate. The Northern England industrial AI ecosystem lacks a Silo AI analogue with equivalent research depth; this gap represents an opportunity for UK-based organisations capable of replicating Silo AI's combination of enterprise revenue self-sufficiency with academically credible open research output.

  - ### Future Directions (2026–2030)

  - Several trajectories shape Silo AI's post-acquisition roadmap. Within AMD, the team is expected to extend its multilingual LLM work to cover all 24 EU official languages at production quality—the explicit mandate of OpenEuroLLM—while simultaneously developing AMD-hardware-optimised inference and fine-tuning tooling competitive with NVIDIA's CUDA/cuDNN ecosystem. The Viking and Poro model families are likely to evolve towards multimodal architectures incorporating vision and speech modalities, given the natural extension from Finnish/Nordic text models towards Finnish/Nordic speech synthesis and recognition.

  - The EU AI Act's general-purpose AI (GPAI) requirements, phasing in through 2025–2026, create structural demand for open models with verifiable data provenance and training transparency—precisely what Silo AI's Poro Research Checkpoints and HPLT data pipeline provide. This regulatory tailwind strengthens the competitive position of Silo AI's openly documented models relative to closed proprietary alternatives for EU enterprise deployment.

  - Cross-lingual transfer learning methodology pioneered for Finnish and Nordic languages will likely be extended to other low-resource European languages (Basque, Catalan, Welsh, Irish Gaelic, Maltese) as OpenEuroLLM scales. The general principle—that cross-training low-resource languages with related high-resource languages achieves better performance than monolingual training on equivalent data volumes—has broad applicability beyond the original Finnish/English pairing and will inform multilingual NLP research globally.

  - AMD's strategic motivation for the acquisition—demonstrating that MI-series GPUs can train frontier AI models competitively with NVIDIA hardware—will intensify as the ROCm software ecosystem matures. Silo AI's track record of training Poro and Viking on LUMI (an entirely AMD MI250X cluster) provides AMD with credible proof points for enterprise compute procurement decisions.
