Radiometric calibration is the calibration of an Earth observation sensor's radiometric response, so that its recorded values can be traced to a reference standard and reported with a stated uncertainty.[^1][^2]

USGS requires the radiometric calibration of Landsat 8's Operational Land Imager to be metrologically traceable to the standards set by the National Institute of Standards and Technology.[^1] Digital number values are affected by sensor artefacts, viewing and illumination angles and atmospheric conditions, and USGS states that monitoring and regular characterization of sensor data are required to maintain radiometric calibration and stability.[^1] NASA lists radiometric and geometric calibration coefficients among the ancillary information that can annotate Level 1A data.[^2]

Calibration is separable from the atmospheric and geometric corrections applied later. USGS notes that no measurement can provide an exact representation of a target radiance or reflectance, and that an uncertainty estimate gives a range that should contain the true value.[^1]

Landsat practice and NASA's level scheme are all the retained sources describe. They do not give a conversion equation or coefficient values, and no figure is stated here because any figure would belong to a particular instrument and date.
