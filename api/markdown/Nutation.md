Nutation is a small nodding motion of Earth's axis that is superimposed on the long-period precession.[^1]

NASA gives the principal term a period of 18.6 years and an amplitude of 9.2 arc seconds, and attributes it to the difference between the plane of the Moon's orbit and the plane of Earth's orbit together with their mutual gravitational pull. This is an explanatory account of one term rather than a full model.[^1]

In the IAU 2000 framework as presented in the IERS Conventions, precession-nutation is the motion of the Celestial Intermediate Pole in the celestial reference system, and nutation is the part of that motion not classed as precession. The division is conventional.[^2]

The IERS states that observations show deficiencies in the adopted precession-nutation model, mainly because the free core nutation is not part of it, and it publishes observed celestial pole offsets as corrections. The sources opened here do not set out the nutation series itself.[^2]
