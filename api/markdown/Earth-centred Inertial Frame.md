An Earth-centred inertial frame is a geocentric coordinate frame, used mainly to describe satellite motion, whose axes are fixed relative to the stars and not to the rotating Earth.[^1]

In the J2000 form described by ESA's Navipedia, the origin is the Earth's centre of mass, the fundamental plane is the mean equator of epoch J2000.0, and the x axis points to the mean vernal equinox of that epoch. The z axis follows the Earth's mean rotation pole at J2000.0 and y completes a right-handed set. NASA defines J2000.0 as the mean equator and equinox of the year 2000, with only the long-term precession considered.[^1][^2]

It is quasi-inertial in the strict sense. Navipedia notes that the Earth's annual motion around the Sun subjects the origin to a certain acceleration, although the frame can be treated as inertial over short periods. The sources therefore support calling it a convenient inertial approximation, not an exactly inertial frame.[^1]

Other epochs of the equator and equinox, such as mean or true of date, give different frames with the same name pattern, so the epoch should be stated. A transformation to the Earth-fixed frame applies precession, nutation, Earth rotation and polar motion, and the last two rely on observed orientation parameters.[^3][^2]
