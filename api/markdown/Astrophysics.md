Astrophysics is the study of how the universe works and evolved, including the physical nature of stars, galaxies, black holes and other cosmic objects.[^1][^2]

NASA frames its astrophysics programme around how the universe began and evolved, the fundamental forces shaping it, and the extreme environments of black holes and neutron stars. Its missions observe from infrared through visible light to X-rays and beyond.[^1]

Black holes are a typical subject. ESA describes how X-ray and gamma-ray observatories probe the emission from matter near them, and how planned gravitational-wave and X-ray missions are intended to address questions about merging and growing black holes. Planned missions are not operating observatories.[^2][^3]

Sources here are programme and mission descriptions. No formal difference between astrophysics and astronomy is set out, and this page does not claim one.[^1]
