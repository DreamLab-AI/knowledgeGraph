- ### Definition
  - The Financial Conduct Authority (FCA) is the statutory regulator of financial services and markets in the United Kingdom, operating independently of government under the Financial Services and Markets Act 2000. It supervises conduct in retail and wholesale markets, authorises financial firms, and enforces anti-money-laundering obligations. In the blockchain and digital asset domain, the FCA registers crypto-asset businesses under the Money Laundering Regulations, oversees financial promotions for crypto assets, and implements policy derived from international standards bodies such as the Financial Action Task Force.

- ### Content
  - The Financial Conduct Authority is the principal conduct regulator for financial markets in the United Kingdom, established under the Financial Services and Markets Act 2000 and restructured by the Financial Services Act 2012. It is operationally independent of government and funded by fees charged to regulated firms. Its statutory objectives are to protect consumers, promote market integrity, and foster effective competition.
  - Within the crypto-asset sector, the FCA's remit has expanded substantially. Under the Money Laundering, Terrorist Financing and Transfer of Funds (Information on the Payer) Regulations 2017 (MLRs), all UK-based crypto-asset businesses must register with the FCA and comply with anti-money-laundering and counter-terrorist financing requirements. In 2023, the FCA gained powers to regulate crypto-asset financial promotions, requiring communications to be approved or issued by an FCA-authorised person. Further powers over crypto-asset markets, stablecoin issuance, and custody services are expected under forthcoming UK crypto-asset legislation.
  - The FCA operates in close coordination with the Bank of England's Prudential Regulation Authority (PRA) on matters of systemic importance, with HM Treasury on policy development, and with international bodies including the Financial Action Task Force, IOSCO, and the Financial Stability Board. Its supervisory approach emphasises outcomes-based regulation, industry sandboxes for fintech innovation, and engagement with the broader Compliance Framework for digital assets.
