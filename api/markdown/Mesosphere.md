Directly above the stratosphere and below the thermosphere lies the mesosphere, within which temperature decreases as altitude increases.[^1][^2] The Australian Antarctic Division and the draft ISO text both tie its limits to this temperature behaviour, with the base at the stratopause and the top at the mesopause.[^2][^3]

Published heights differ, since UCAR says it extends from about 50 to 85 km, the Australian Antarctic Division gives about 85 km with 95 km during the winter, and the draft ISO text places the upper boundary around 85 km to 100 km.[^1][^2][^3] UCAR notes that weather balloons and aircraft cannot reach this layer and that sounding rockets sample it directly in brief and infrequent flights.[^1]

SWEET bounds the class by the stratopause below and the mesopause above and gives it a negative temperature slope.[^4] The retained sources do not establish a single agreed pair of boundary altitudes.[^1][^2]
