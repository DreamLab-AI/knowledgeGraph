A dwarf planet is a celestial body in orbit around the Sun that has enough mass to assume a nearly round shape but has not cleared the neighbourhood around its orbit.[^1]

The International Astronomical Union adopted the category at its 2006 General Assembly and resolved that planets and dwarf planets are two distinct classes of objects. The same resolution lists the eight planets, calls Pluto a dwarf planet and treats it as the prototype of a new category of trans-Neptunian objects. A footnote to the resolution says an IAU process would be set up to assign borderline objects to the dwarf planet or other categories.[^1]

NASA says five dwarf planets are officially recognised: Ceres, Pluto, Haumea, Makemake and Eris. Ceres is the only one in the inner solar system, lying in the main asteroid belt, and it was the first to be visited by a spacecraft. NASA reports the IAU view that many more may await discovery, perhaps more than a hundred. The 2006 announcement named only three first members, so the recognised count has changed since the resolution.[^1][^2][^3]

NASA links the reclassification of Pluto in 2006 to the discovery of similar worlds in the Kuiper Belt. A dwarf planet is defined by its orbit and shape criteria rather than by a stated size, and the retained sources set no size limit. They also do not establish how the criteria apply to bodies around other stars.[^1][^2]
