At Earth, the boundary between the solar wind and the magnetic field is called the magnetopause.[^1] NOAA describes it as constantly in motion because the solar wind that buffets Earth keeps changing.[^1] ESA describes it as the boundary layer separating the magnetosphere from the solar wind.[^2]

Shielding from the solar wind is only partial. NOAA states that the magnetopause is far from impenetrable and that energy, mass and momentum are transferred from the solar wind to regions inside the magnetosphere.[^1] ESA names magnetic reconnection, in which Earth’s field lines interconnect with interplanetary field lines, as one efficient route for that transfer.[^2]

These sources give the boundary’s role and behaviour in general terms. They do not establish a single fixed position or shape, and neither ranks the transfer mechanisms against each other.[^1][^2]
