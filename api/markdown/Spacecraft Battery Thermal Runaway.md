Spacecraft battery thermal runaway is a failure in which a lithium-based cell overheats and can lead to fire or explosion.[^1]

NASA's survey says that flammable liquid electrolytes in lithium-based batteries can trigger it, and lists damage, overheating, overcharging, water exposure and manufacturing defects as causes. It adds that protecting against it can require extra mass for thermal management and protective housings.[^1]

In NASA tests of a non-rechargeable lithium battery for the Simplified Aid for EVA Rescue, a worst-case single-cell event led to cell-to-cell propagation, and the packaging design influenced how readily it spread. The authors framed this as an assessment of severity under a conservative assumption, and the result applies to that battery and configuration.[^2]

The International Space Station lithium-ion battery presentation lists two independent controls against thermal runaway and thermal radiant barriers between cell pairs as design features. These are examples from one design in slides rather than a standard, and the sources do not give a rate of occurrence for spacecraft batteries.[^3]
