
Satellite modulation is the process of mapping information-bearing symbols onto a radio-frequency carrier by varying one or more signal properties.

At the receiver, the selected modulation scheme is used to recover symbols from the received signal. Waveform and symbol-rate choices affect occupied bandwidth and spectral efficiency, but performance also depends on coding, amplifier behaviour, receiver design and the channel. No modulation format is best independently of the link and hardware in which it operates.[^1]

Modulation is generally distinct from channel coding: modulation shapes the transmitted waveform, while channel coding structures the information sequence to support reliable reception. Some schemes combine aspects of coding and modulation, so the implementation should be described accurately rather than forcing every design into a rigid boundary.[^1][^2]

In the CCSDS link architecture, RF and modulation occupy the physical layer below synchronisation and channel coding. CCSDS 413.0-G-3 is an informational Green Book that summarises selected bandwidth-efficient methods; it is not a Recommended Standard requiring a particular satellite waveform.[^1][^2][^3]

Modulation is also distinct from multiple access and spectrum allocation. A waveform does not by itself determine which users share a resource, which service has an international allocation, or whether a particular station has permission to transmit.[^2][^3]
