Signal-to-noise ratio in Earth observation is the ratio of the energy reflected or emitted from the surface to the noise contributed by the instrument itself or by external interference.[^1]

NASA describes a higher ratio as letting a sensor better distinguish true measurements from noise, which gives clearer and more precise data. The Landsat Operational Land Imager page links its improved ratio to design choices that include narrower bands chosen to limit atmospheric absorption, and to a push-broom detector arrangement.[^1]

Sensitivity is not obtained for free. A paper on ocean-colour sensors observes that the dynamic range of a sensor is often fixed, so a high-sensitivity band saturates at a lower radiance, and design has to balance the ratio against the range needed for bright scenes. The same paper notes that past sensors specified signal levels, formats and conventions differently, which makes cross-sensor comparison difficult.[^2]

No sources give a single reference radiance or a threshold for an adequate ratio, so a quoted figure is meaningful only with its stated conditions.[^1][^2]

[^2]: Hu et al., “Dynamic range and sensitivity requirements of satellite ocean color sensors: learning from the past”, *Applied Optics*, 2012. https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/20140006591/downloads/20140006591.pdf
