Large-scale structures across the universe, the cosmic web is a network of clusters, filaments, walls and voids in which matter and galaxies are arranged.[^1][^2]

NASA lists superclusters, empty voids and walls of galaxies among the large-scale structures that make up the web. An ESA description of a simulation image calls it a wispy network of clusters, filaments and voids, with galaxy clusters sitting at the densest knots.[^1][^2]

Simulations indicate that the web consists mainly of dark matter with some ordinary matter and acts as scaffolding for galaxies. ESA reports that XMM-Newton X-ray observations found filaments of hot gas flowing toward the cluster Abell 2744, and an ESA/Hubble study mapped filaments from galaxy positions and detected gas along them, noting that filaments form the boundaries between large voids.[^3][^4]

Many published images are simulations or inferred maps, so the sources do not establish a direct image of the whole web. The structure is also not a fixed catalogue of objects, and its boundaries are not sharply defined.
