A celestial reference frame is a realisation of a celestial reference system through catalogued source positions that fix its axes, and the International Celestial Reference Frame is the realisation adopted by the IAU.[^1]

Its third realisation, ICRF3, is based on nearly 40 years of very long baseline interferometry data. A subset of its sources is identified as defining sources that serve to define the axes, and the IAU adopted the frame at its 30th General Assembly in August 2018, replacing ICRF2 from 1 January 2019.[^1]

ICRF3 source positions are reported for epoch 2015.0 and must be propagated to other epochs for the most accurate needs, allowing for the acceleration toward the Galactic centre. The authors also report positional offsets between radio frequencies for some sources, and between radio and optical positions, and suggest extended source structure as a possible cause.[^1]

Navipedia describes a different construction, a conventional celestial reference system for satellite motion that is Earth centred and aligned to the mean equator and equinox of J2000.0. It calls this system quasi-inertial because of the annual motion of the Earth, usable as inertial over short periods.[^2] The IAU resolution on TDB names Barycentric Coordinate Time as the time scale of the Barycentric Celestial Reference System.[^3] The retained sources do not describe the transformation between these systems or between ICRF realisations.
