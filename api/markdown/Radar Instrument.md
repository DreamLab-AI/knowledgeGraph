A radar instrument is a sensor that transmits a microwave signal towards a target and detects the backscattered portion of the returned signal.[^1]

Natural Resources Canada gives RADAR as an acronym for RAdio Detection And Ranging, and describes radar as an active microwave sensor that supplies its own illumination. It divides active microwave sensors into imaging and non-imaging categories, with altimeters and scatterometers given as non-imaging examples.[^1]

Radar can control the polarisation of the transmitted and received signal, which NASA describes for SAR sensors that typically transmit linearly polarised signals.[^2] Natural Resources Canada states that penetration of cloud cover and most weather conditions, and imaging at any time, day or night, are the two primary advantages of radar.[^1]

These sources describe radar in the context of Earth observation.
