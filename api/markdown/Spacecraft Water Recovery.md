Spacecraft water recovery is the reclamation of water from wastewater, humidity condensate and similar sources aboard a crewed vehicle so that it can be reused.[^1]

On the International Space Station, NASA describes a Water Recovery System that reclaims wastewater, including water from crew urine, cabin humidity condensate and water from the hydration system inside spacesuits. NASA states that the recovered water must meet stringent purity standards before use.[^1]

NASA reports that the system recovers and recycles about 90 percent of the water on the station and gives 98 percent as a goal before the first human missions aboard a proposed Mars transport vehicle.[^1][^2] These are agency statements about one programme and a future target, not measured guarantees for any other vehicle.[^1][^2]

[^1]: NASA Marshall Space Flight Center, “Environmental Control and Life Support Systems (ECLSS)”, updated 4 April 2025. https://www.nasa.gov/reference/environmental-control-and-life-support-systems-eclss/
