- ### Definition
  - The [[International Financial Architecture]] is the framework of institutions, rules, standards, and cooperative arrangements that govern cross-border finance, monetary relations, and global [[Financial Stability]].
  - It comprises multilateral institutions such as the [[International Monetary Fund]], standard-setting bodies including the [[Financial Stability Board]] and the [[Bank for International Settlements]], and conventions on exchange rates, capital flows, and crisis resolution.
  - Its purpose is to coordinate [[Macroprudential Policy]], contain [[Systemic Risk]], and provide surveillance, liquidity provision, and orderly adjustment across the [[Financial System]].

- ### Overview
  - The architecture evolved from the post-war Bretton Woods settlement through the era of floating exchange rates and successive crises that reshaped institutions and rules.
  - It blends hard law (treaties, IMF articles), soft law (standards and codes), and informal coordination among central banks and finance ministries.
  - Surveillance, conditional lending, and standard-setting are its principal instruments, complemented by crisis facilities and swap lines for liquidity in stress.
  - Recurrent debate centres on representation and legitimacy, the adequacy of the global financial safety net, and adaptation to new risks from shadow banking, capital-flow volatility, and digital money.

- ### Key aspects
  - #### Multilateral institutions
    - The [[International Monetary Fund]] provides surveillance, conditional lending, and a financial safety net; development banks address longer-term financing.
  - #### Standard-setting and coordination
    - The [[Financial Stability Board]] coordinates national regulators; the [[Bank for International Settlements]] and Basel committees set prudential standards.
  - #### Monetary and exchange-rate arrangements
    - Frameworks governing exchange-rate regimes, reserve currencies, and the interaction of national [[Monetary Policy]] across borders.
  - #### Crisis prevention and resolution
    - [[Macroprudential Policy]], stress testing, sovereign-debt restructuring mechanisms, and liquidity backstops to limit contagion and [[Systemic Risk]].

- ### Applications
  - #### Crisis response
    - Coordinated liquidity provision, programme lending, and policy adjustment during financial and balance-of-payments crises.
  - #### Cross-border payments and infrastructure
    - Standards and oversight supporting safe, efficient [[Cross-Border Payments]] and emerging [[Central Bank Digital Currency]] interoperability.
  - #### Regulatory harmonisation
    - Convergence of capital, liquidity, and resolution standards to reduce regulatory arbitrage across jurisdictions.
