- ### Definition
  - [[Cryptocurrency]] is a digital currency secured by cryptography and maintained on a [[Blockchain]] network, where ownership is verified through [[Digital Signature]] mechanisms, transactions are recorded on a [[Distributed Ledger]], and [[Consensus Mechanism]] ensures transaction authenticity without requiring a centralised monetary authority.

- ### Content

  Cryptocurrency represents the first technology enabling reliable value transfer without requiring trusted intermediaries or government monetary authorities. Bitcoin, launched in 2009, demonstrated that economic incentives (mining rewards) and cryptographic mechanisms could coordinate millions of participants in maintaining a shared monetary ledger. Subsequent cryptocurrencies expanded this foundation with faster transaction processing, smart contract capability, and enhanced privacy features.

  Cryptocurrency's value proposition rests on three pillars: censorship resistance (nobody can freeze accounts or reverse transactions), financial inclusion (participation requires only internet access rather than approval from banks), and monetary transparency (all transactions publicly verifiable, preventing counterfeiting or hidden money supply increases). These properties proved especially valuable in jurisdictions with capital controls or unreliable monetary authorities.

  Cryptocurrencies introduce price volatility, technical complexity for average users, and environmental concerns regarding energy-intensive consensus mechanisms. Regulatory uncertainty continues limiting merchant acceptance and institutional adoption. Private key management remains a persistent security weak point where user errors enable theft that, unlike traditional financial systems, cannot be reversed through chargeback or refund mechanisms.
