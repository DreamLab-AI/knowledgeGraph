Measurement bias is an estimate of a systematic measurement error.[^1]

The VIM defines a systematic measurement error as the component of measurement error that stays constant or varies in a predictable manner across replicate measurements. Bias is the estimate of that component, so it is a quantity derived from a reference and not an observed true error.[^1]

Uncertainty components from systematic effects may be applied as corrections or, where an estimated effect is not corrected, included in the uncertainty. Averaging replicate readings does not remove a constant systematic error, which is why bias is treated separately from random scatter and from precision.[^1]

In Earth observation, calibration is described as defining a system's response to known and controlled inputs, and validation as independent assessment of derived products. These are the activities in which a bias would be found, though the CEOS page cited does not define bias itself.[^2]

[^2]: CEOS, “Working Group on Calibration & Validation (WGCV)”. https://ceos.org/ourwork/workinggroups/wgcv/
