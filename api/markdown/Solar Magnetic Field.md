Concentrated in sunspots and active regions of the Sun, the magnetic field is carried outward into interplanetary space by the solar wind.[^1][^2]

The Sun is made of plasma, and in its interior the magnetic field and the ionised plasma move together, so plasma motions can twist, tangle and transport field lines. NASA describes the Sun's changing surface and atmosphere as driven by magnetic forces generated by this moving plasma.[^3][^1][^4]

Hale first measured magnetic fields in sunspots, and the magnetic nature of the solar cycle became apparent once those observations covered more than one cycle. Systematic daily observations of the field itself are described as available only from the 1970s, so the direct record is much shorter than the sunspot record.[^3]

Sunspot pairs show a regular polarity pattern known as Hale's polarity law, and the polarities reverse from one cycle to the next. The Sun's polar fields were also found to change polarity. The High Altitude Observatory page links these regularities to the solar dynamo, but the sources used here do not set out a dynamo model, and none gives field strengths.[^5][^3]

Beyond the Sun, the solar wind carries it outward embedded in the plasma, and SWPC notes that a switch in the polarity of the wind field indicates that Earth has crossed the heliospheric current sheet.[^2]
