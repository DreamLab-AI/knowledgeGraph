A water column is an imaginary column extending through a water body from its floor to its surface.[^1][^2] NOAA applies the term to the ocean and describes it as the water between the ocean surface and the sea floor.[^3]

NOAA Ocean Exploration says the water column includes all of the water in the ocean between the surface and the seafloor, and calls it one of the most poorly explored environments on Earth.[^4] The EPA glossary describes it as the open-water environment, as distinct from the bed or shore, which may be inhabited by swimming marine, estuarine or freshwater organisms.[^2]

Properties vary within it: the EPA glossary describes the portion of the column where density changes rapidly because of salinity and temperature, and NOAA notes that nutrients and energy are carried through it as phytoplankton are eaten or die and sink.[^2][^3] SWEET classes the term as a hydrosphere feature.[^5]

The USGS definition covers any water body whereas the NOAA pages address the ocean, and the retained sources give no depth limit and no measurement method.
