- ### Definition
  - ChatGPT is a conversational AI assistant developed by [[OpenAI]] and publicly launched on 30 November 2022, representing the first large-scale deployment of instruction-tuned [[Large Language Models]] accessible to general consumers via a web interface, achieving 100 million monthly active users within 60 days — the fastest product adoption in technology history at that time — and reaching 200 million weekly active users by August 2024 and over 900 million weekly active users by early 2026, built on a lineage of successively more capable [[Generative Pre-trained Transformers]] (GPT-3.5-turbo at launch, [[GPT 4]] in March 2023, GPT-4 Turbo in November 2023, GPT-4o in May 2024, o1 reasoning model in September 2024, GPT-5 in August 2025), trained using [[Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback]] ([[RLHF]]) via a three-phase pipeline: (1) supervised fine-tuning (SFT) on human-written demonstration data, (2) reward model (RM) training from comparative human preference rankings, and (3) proximal policy optimisation (PPO) maximising the reward signal subject to a KL divergence penalty preventing excessive drift from the SFT policy — transforming a base completion model into an agent capable of [[Instruction Following]], multi-turn dialogue, task decomposition, code generation, mathematical reasoning, and open-domain question answering across 50+ languages, deployed through a tiered product architecture (Free, Plus $20/month, Pro $200/month, Team $25/seat, Enterprise, Edu) with differentiated model access and rate limits, operated via both a consumer chat interface (chat.openai.com) and an API (Chat Completions endpoint, Responses API, Batch API, Realtime API), extended through a custom-agent ecosystem — ChatGPT Plugins (March 2023), Custom GPTs and the GPT Store (November 2023) — and augmented by persistent [[Memory]] (February 2024), Canvas collaborative editing (October 2024), Projects team workspaces (December 2024), and Agent Mode autonomous task execution (July 2025), achieving state-of-the-art benchmark performance with o3 scoring 91.6% on AIME 2024, 83.3% on GPQA Diamond, and 25.2% on FrontierMath (where no prior model had exceeded 2%), and GPT-5 (August 2025) scoring 94.6% on AIME 2025, 74.9% on SWE-bench Verified, and 84.2% on MMMU, deployed at enterprise scale with 92% of Fortune 500 companies as customers, generating $25 billion in annualised revenue for [[OpenAI]] by February 2026, and positioning ChatGPT as the dominant consumer and enterprise [[AI Assistants]] platform globally and the primary interface through which hundreds of millions of people interact with [[Generative AI]].

  - ## About
  - **ChatGPT** (Chat Generative Pre-trained Transformer) is the flagship consumer-facing product of [[OpenAI]], launched 30 November 2022 as a research preview and immediately recognised as a watershed moment in public deployment of [[Large Language Models]].
  - The product is available at chat.openai.com and through iOS/Android mobile applications, desktop applications (Windows, macOS), API (api.openai.com), Azure OpenAI Service, and enterprise deployment configurations.
  - ChatGPT is classified under the OpenAI product taxonomy as a "frontier AI system" within its own Preparedness Framework, indicating the highest level of capability assessment and safety scrutiny applied to any [[OpenAI]] product.
  - The name "ChatGPT" has become a genericised brand reference — many users refer to any AI chatbot as "ChatGPT" regardless of the underlying model — reflecting the same linguistic pattern as "Googling" or "Hoovering" for the dominant product in a category.
  - [[OpenAI]] was founded in December 2015 by Sam Altman, Greg Brockman, Ilya Sutskever, Wojciech Zaremba, John Schulman, and Elon Musk (who departed the board in 2018) with a stated mission of ensuring that artificial general intelligence benefits humanity; ChatGPT represents the first commercially scaled realisation of this mission in consumer AI.
  - The organisational structure of [[OpenAI]] is a capped-profit company subsidiary of a non-profit, with investor returns capped at 100× investment — a structure intended to balance commercial viability with safety-first mission alignment, though under ongoing scrutiny as the company's valuation approaches $1 trillion.
  - Microsoft's multi-year partnership and multi-billion-dollar investment (announced January 2023) integrates [[OpenAI]] models across Azure cloud, Bing, Office 365 Copilot, Windows Copilot, and GitHub Copilot — distributing ChatGPT-class capabilities to every Microsoft product user globally.
  - The product has spawned an extensive ecosystem of third-party tooling: prompt libraries, browser extensions, API wrappers, evaluation frameworks, fine-tuning services, and educational resources — reflecting the same ecosystem dynamics that surrounded transformative developer platforms like iOS, Android, and AWS.
  - ChatGPT's position as the first conversational LLM to achieve mass consumer adoption makes it a historical reference point in the development of artificial intelligence, comparable in cultural and economic significance to the launch of the iPhone (2007) in mobile computing.
  - The product name is a portmanteau: "Chat" denotes the conversational, multi-turn interface maintaining dialogue history within a context window; "GPT" denotes the underlying [[Generative Pre-trained Transformers]] family descended from the transformer architecture (Vaswani et al., 2017) and the GPT pre-training paradigm (Radford et al., 2018, 2019).
  - Unlike research systems such as GPT-3 requiring careful prompt crafting, ChatGPT was trained for helpfulness, harmlessness, and honest behaviour through [[Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback]] ([[RLHF]]), formalised in the InstructGPT paper (Ouyang et al., NeurIPS 2022).
  - The initial model was GPT-3.5-turbo, subsequently upgraded through a rapid model progression: [[GPT 4]] (March 2023), GPT-4 Turbo with 128K context (November 2023), GPT-4o (May 2024), GPT-4o mini (July 2024), o1 (September–December 2024), o3 and o4-mini (April 2025), and GPT-5 (August 2025), with GPT-5.2 following before end of 2025.
  - The product disrupted human-computer interaction by demonstrating that a general-purpose [[Large Language Models]] system could engage in coherent multi-turn conversation across an enormous range of domains simultaneously — creative writing, translation, software debugging, legal research, mathematical problem solving, and medical explanation — without domain-specific configuration.
  - Prior AI assistants (Siri, Alexa, Google Assistant) required narrow task framing; ChatGPT's breadth of competence created what technologists described as a step-change inflection point in accessible AI capability.
  - Within two months of launch, ChatGPT attracted 100 million users — far outpacing adoption records previously held by TikTok (9 months to 100 million) — reflecting both pent-up demand for capable AI assistance and the friction-free web interface requiring no installation, purchase, or technical knowledge.
  - By August 2024 weekly active users reached 200 million; by early 2026 this figure surpassed 900 million weekly active users — more than 11% of the global population interacting with a single AI product each week.
  - Eloundou et al. (2023) estimated that 80% of US workers could have at least 10% of their tasks affected by GPT-class models, with 19% potentially affected at rates above 50% — labour market exposure far exceeding previous waves of automation.
  - Brynjolfsson and Mitchell (2023) contextualised ChatGPT as a "general-purpose technology" transition analogous to electricity or computing, with transformative effects anticipated across legal, financial, medical, educational, and software-development professions.

  - ## Components / Architecture
  - ### Base Model Pre-training
  - ChatGPT's conversational behaviour emerges from a pre-trained [[Generative Pre-trained Transformers]] foundation trained on hundreds of billions of tokens of text from the internet (Common Crawl, WebText2), books (Books1, Books2), and code (GitHub).
  - The decoder-only [[Transformer Architecture]] uses multi-head self-[[Attention Mechanism]] to model conditional probability distributions over token sequences: given a sequence of past tokens, the model predicts the probability distribution over the next token.
  - Byte-pair encoding (BPE) tokenisation maps subword units to vocabulary integers (50,257 for GPT-3.5; approximately 100,000 for GPT-4 and later), enabling efficient handling of rare words and multilingual text.
  - Context windows expanded from 4K tokens (GPT-3.5 at launch) through 8K/32K (GPT-4) to 128K (GPT-4 Turbo, GPT-4o).
  - GPT-3.5-turbo has approximately 175 billion parameters; GPT-4 and subsequent models are not officially disclosed but are widely estimated to use mixture-of-experts (MoE) architectures with 1–2 trillion total parameters and 100–300 billion active parameters per forward pass.
  - ### RLHF Training Pipeline
  - The three-phase [[Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback]] ([[RLHF]]) pipeline transforms a base completion model into a helpful assistant aligned to human preferences.
  - **Phase 1 — Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT):** [[OpenAI]]-contracted human labellers write demonstrations of ideal assistant responses to a diverse set of prompts; the base model is fine-tuned on this dataset using standard cross-entropy maximum likelihood training, producing an initial instruction-following policy.
  - **Phase 2 — Reward Model Training:** Labellers compare multiple model outputs for a given prompt and rank them by quality; a separate neural network (the Reward Model) is trained to assign scalar reward scores matching human preference rankings, learning a surrogate objective for "good" assistant behaviour across diverse task types and safety dimensions.
  - **Phase 3 — PPO Reinforcement Learning:** The SFT model serves as the starting policy and is iteratively updated using Proximal Policy Optimisation to maximise the reward model's scores, subject to a KL divergence penalty that prevents the policy from drifting too far from the SFT baseline — protecting against reward model overoptimisation (reward hacking) whilst maintaining diversity of responses.
  - This pipeline was first described for InstructGPT (Ouyang et al., 2022) and has since been refined through RLHF variants, Direct Preference Optimisation (DPO), and constitutional AI approaches combining AI-generated feedback with human preference data.
  - For o-series reasoning models (o1, o3, o4-mini), a further chain-of-thought reinforcement learning stage trains the model to generate extended internal reasoning tokens in a "scratchpad" before producing the final visible response, with correct final answers providing training signal — implementing a form of test-time compute scaling that dramatically improves performance on tasks requiring multi-step deduction, mathematical proof, and complex programming.
  - ### Model Family and Progression
  - ChatGPT's model roster has evolved rapidly through distinct generations, each representing meaningful capability jumps:
  - **GPT-3.5-turbo** (4K/16K context, November 2022–2023): Launch model, 175B parameters, MMLU ~70%, first model to demonstrate broad instruction following at scale.
  - **[[GPT 4]]** (8K/32K context, March 2023): 90th percentile on Uniform Bar Exam (vs GPT-3.5's 10th percentile), MMLU 86.4%, HumanEval 67.0%, first model with practical professional-grade reasoning.
  - **GPT-4 Turbo** (128K context, November 2023): 4× context extension, 3× API cost reduction, knowledge cutoff updated to April 2023.
  - **GPT-4o** (128K context, May 2024): Native multimodal unification of text/audio/image in a single model, 2× faster than GPT-4, 50% cheaper API, MMLU 88.7%, supporting 50+ languages covering 97% of global speakers.
  - **GPT-4o mini** (July 2024): Replaced GPT-3.5 Turbo as the free default; near-GPT-4 performance at approximately 8× lower cost, enabling broader free-tier access globally.
  - **o1-preview / o1** (September–December 2024): Introduced chain-of-thought reinforcement learning; o1 scored 74.3% on AIME 2024, demonstrating qualitatively stronger mathematical reasoning than GPT-4o.
  - **o3** (April 2025): 91.6% AIME 2024, 83.3% GPQA Diamond, 25.2% FrontierMath (prior SOTA 2%), 20% fewer major errors than o1 on real-world professional tasks.
  - **o4-mini** (April 2025): 92.7% AIME 2025 at lower cost than o3, optimised for cost-efficient reasoning in math, coding, and visual tasks.
  - **GPT-5** (August 2025): New state of the art across math (94.6% AIME 2025), coding (74.9% SWE-bench Verified, 88% Aider Polyglot), multimodal vision (84.2% MMMU), and clinical health (46.2% HealthBench Hard).
  - **GPT-5.2** (late 2025): Further reasoning and efficiency improvements, unified text/audio/video/image in a single model endpoint.
  - ### Multimodal Pipeline
  - GPT-4V (vision) introduced image understanding in March 2023 as a separate API feature before full integration into ChatGPT Plus.
  - GPT-4o (May 2024) achieved native multimodal unification: text, image, audio, and video processing in a single model with a single API call — unlike prior architectures chaining separate Whisper speech-to-text, language model, and text-to-speech modules.
  - Native audio processing enables voice-to-voice conversation with sub-second latency, emotional tone modulation, and natural interruption handling — qualitative advances over the stitched pipeline approach.
  - Advanced Voice Mode launched to Plus subscribers in September 2024 with multiple voice personas, emotional expressiveness (laughter, singing, whispering), and support for interruption mid-sentence.
  - [[DALL-E]] 3 is integrated directly into ChatGPT for text-to-image generation: the language model automatically rewrites user prompts into optimised image generation instructions before invoking the image model.
  - GPT-5 extended multimodal capabilities to video frame analysis, enhanced spatial reasoning over images (explaining 3D relationships in photographs), and stronger cross-modal inference (reasoning about relationships between text, images, and code simultaneously).
  - Sora video generation, integrated 2025, extends ChatGPT to short-form text-to-video production within the conversational interface, described as a scene in natural language and converted to a generated video clip.
  - These multimodal capabilities position ChatGPT to process and generate content across virtually all media types through a single conversational session, representing convergence toward a general-purpose [[Multimodal AI]] interface.
  - ### Memory and Context Management
  - Persistent [[Memory]] launched February 2024, allowing ChatGPT to store user-specific facts across sessions: professional context, preferences, ongoing project details, stylistic preferences, and recurring task patterns.
  - Memory entries are explicitly stored (model proposes storage, user confirms), implicitly inferred from conversation content, or manually added and deleted through a settings panel, giving users control over their accumulated context.
  - By May 2025, memory applied across all conversations (not only new ones), with project-level scoping allowing restriction of certain memories to specific project contexts.
  - ChatGPT Projects, launched December 2024, allow bundling conversations, uploaded files, custom instructions, and memory into persistently accessible named workspaces.
  - Team and Enterprise users can share Projects, creating collaborative knowledge hubs where multiple contributors add documents and conversation history that the model draws on in all project interactions — positioning ChatGPT as a persistent evolving knowledge management platform.
  - The accumulation of months or years of personal context creates significant switching costs relative to stateless competitors, representing a structural moat in the consumer AI assistant market.
  - ### Tool Use and Agentic Capabilities
  - Function calling (API) and tool use (ChatGPT interface) allow the model to invoke structured tools during generation: web search (Bing integration), Python code execution in a sandboxed environment (Code Interpreter), [[DALL-E]] image generation, file reading, and third-party API calls.
  - The tool-use architecture follows the model's autonomous determination of when external information is needed, executes the selected tool, incorporates results into the context window, and continues generation — enabling multi-step workflows combining retrieval, computation, and generation.
  - Code Interpreter (Advanced Data Analysis) allows execution of Python in a sandboxed environment within the ChatGPT interface, supporting data loading, statistical analysis, visualisation with matplotlib/seaborn/plotly, machine learning model fitting, file transformation, and result export.
  - ChatGPT Operator launched January 2025 as a Pro-tier feature enabling the model to control a web browser autonomously: navigating sites, filling forms, clicking interactive elements, completing purchase flows, and executing multi-stage digital tasks with the user observing but not directing each action step.
  - Operator was renamed Agent Mode in July 2025 and made available to Plus and Pro subscribers globally, with the o3 reasoning model as its backbone, providing substantially more reliable multi-step planning than earlier tool-use implementations.
  - Agent Mode's agentic capability positions ChatGPT as a platform for [[AI Agents]] rather than merely a conversational assistant — a strategic inflection from answering questions to executing tasks.
  - ### Custom GPTs and GPT Store
  - The GPT Store, launched November 2023, allows users and developers to create, publish, and monetise Custom GPTs — domain-specialised ChatGPT variants configured with system prompts, knowledge bases, tool permissions, and interface customisations, without coding.
  - Millions of Custom GPTs were created within months of launch, covering legal research, educational tutoring, recipe generation, customer service, fitness coaching, travel planning, and niche domain assistance.
  - Enterprise clients deploy private Custom GPTs restricted to employees, embedding proprietary documentation, internal policies, and product knowledge via [[Retrieval-Augmented Generation]], creating organisationally scoped AI assistants with company-specific knowledge.
  - [[Prompt Engineering]] skill is central to Custom GPT quality: system prompt design determines behaviour, tone, topic restrictions, output format, and knowledge grounding quality.
  - Custom GPT revenue sharing incentivises developers to invest in high-quality specialised agents, creating a long-tail ecosystem of purpose-built AI applications distributed through a familiar app-store model.
  - ### Canvas Interface
  - Canvas, introduced October 2024, provides a split-screen interface where the language model and user collaboratively edit a document or code file — enabling surgical edits to specific sections rather than full response regeneration.
  - Capabilities include inline annotation, tracked changes, iterative refinement without discarding broader document context, and structured document management across writing and coding modalities.
  - By May 2025, Canvas expanded to support interactive web application building: the model writes HTML/CSS/JavaScript, renders a live preview, accepts feedback on the visual output, and iterates — combining [[Code Generation]] with a rudimentary integrated development environment.
  - Canvas also supports external API calls from generated code, enabling prototyping of data-connected applications within the ChatGPT interface itself, competing with Replit, CodeSandbox, and lightweight IDE environments.
  - ### API Architecture
  - The Chat Completions API (POST /v1/chat/completions) accepts a structured JSON array of role-tagged messages — system (behaviour instructions), user (human turns), and assistant (prior model turns) — and returns completions with token usage statistics, finish reason, and optional tool-call payloads.
  - System prompts allow API users and Custom GPT builders to configure the model's persona, restrictions, output format, response style, and task context — the primary mechanism for [[Prompt Engineering]] in API-based applications.
  - The Responses API (2025) adds stateful session management, built-in tool definitions, structured output for o-series extended reasoning, and simplified state tracking across multi-turn agent interactions.
  - The Realtime API supports bidirectional streaming audio for voice application development, enabling integration of ChatGPT's voice capabilities into third-party products at production latency.
  - The Batch API provides 50% cost-reduced asynchronous processing for high-volume classification, annotation, extraction, or generation workloads where real-time response is not required.
  - Pricing spans from GPT-4o mini at approximately $0.15/million input tokens to o3 at $10/million input tokens as of mid-2025, with 50% caching discounts for repeated context prefixes enabling cost reduction in production deployments.

  - ## Academic Context
  - ### Technical Foundations
  - ChatGPT's technical foundation rests on InstructGPT, described in "Training language models to follow instructions with human feedback" (Ouyang et al., NeurIPS 2022), which demonstrated that [[RLHF]] with PPO fine-tuning substantially improves instruction adherence over raw pre-trained models — even when the RLHF-tuned 1.3B model is preferred by labellers over the raw 175B GPT-3 baseline, illustrating that alignment training quality can outweigh raw parameter count.
  - The underlying [[Transformer Architecture]] descends from "Attention Is All You Need" (Vaswani et al., NeurIPS 2017); the GPT pre-training paradigm from "Improving Language Understanding by Generative Pre-Training" (Radford et al., OpenAI 2018), "Language Models are Unsupervised Multitask Learners" (Radford et al., 2019), and "Language Models are Few-Shot Learners" (Brown et al., NeurIPS 2020 — GPT-3).
  - Constitutional AI (Bai et al., Anthropic 2022) and the foundational RLHF framework from Christiano et al. (NeurIPS 2017, "Deep Reinforcement Learning from Human Preferences") are key antecedents to ChatGPT's alignment training methodology.
  - ### GPT-4 and Reasoning Models
  - [[GPT 4]]'s capabilities were characterised in the GPT-4 Technical Report (OpenAI, March 2023), disclosing benchmark performance across MMLU (86.4%), HumanEval (67.0%), bar exam (90th percentile), SAT, GRE, and AP examinations — without specifying model architecture, training compute, or dataset composition, sparking debate about scientific transparency norms in AI research.
  - The o1 reasoning model's chain-of-thought reinforcement learning was described in the "OpenAI o1 System Card" (2024), documenting both safety evaluations and breakthrough benchmark results demonstrating qualitatively stronger multi-step reasoning than GPT-4-class models.
  - o3's ARC-AGI breakthrough — 83.3% on ARC-AGI-Pub in December 2024, compared to o1's approximately 32% — was reported by the ARC Prize organisation (Chollet et al.) as a qualitative shift in abstract reasoning and novel pattern generalisation, though debate continues about what this implies for progress toward [[Artificial General Intelligence]].
  - ### Key Benchmarks
  - **MMLU** (Massive Multitask Language Understanding, 57 tasks spanning academic subjects, Hendrycks et al., 2021): measures broad knowledge and reasoning; GPT-4o scores 88.7%, GPT-5 exceeds prior SOTA.
  - **GPQA Diamond** (Graduate-Level Google-Proof Q&A, PhD-level science, Rein et al., 2023): measures expert reasoning; o3 scores 83.3%, o4-mini scores 81.4%.
  - **HumanEval** (code generation pass@k, Chen et al., 2021) and **SWE-bench Verified** (GitHub issue resolution, Jimenez et al., 2024): measure software engineering capability; GPT-5 achieves 74.9% on SWE-bench Verified.
  - **AIME 2024/2025** (American Invitational Mathematics Examination): measures mathematical olympiad reasoning; o1 scores 74.3%, o3 scores 91.6%, GPT-5 scores 94.6% on AIME 2025.
  - **ARC-AGI** (Abstraction and Reasoning Corpus, Chollet, 2019): measures novel pattern generalisation; o3 scores 83.3%, far above prior SOTA.
  - **FrontierMath** (Glazer et al., 2024): measures frontier mathematical problem solving requiring novel mathematical insight; o3 scores 25.2% (prior SOTA: 2%).
  - **HealthBench** (OpenAI, 2025): measures clinical task performance across 26 medical specialties; GPT-5 scores 46.2% on Hard tasks.
  - ### Societal Impact Research
  - In labour economics, Eloundou et al. (2023) estimated GPT-class model exposure across occupational task structures, finding substantial white-collar automation potential with 80% of US workers having 10%+ task exposure.
  - In education research, studies document evolving concerns around academic integrity, assessment validity, and the shifting role of human expertise in AI-augmented learning environments.
  - In healthcare AI, papers in NEJM AI, Lancet Digital Health, and JAMA document both promise (documentation burden reduction, diagnostic support, patient communication) and risk (hallucination in clinical contexts, liability, algorithmic bias, deskilling).
  - In computer science, mechanistic interpretability researchers at [[OpenAI]], Anthropic, and DeepMind have characterised the internal representations of [[Large Language Models]], finding human-interpretable features (sentiments, syntax, entity types) in specific model circuit motifs.
  - ### Measurement and Evaluation Challenges
  - Benchmark contamination — where model training data includes benchmark test sets, inflating reported scores — is a persistent concern for published ChatGPT benchmark results; [[OpenAI]] addresses this through held-out test sets and post-training evaluation protocols, but the closed nature of training data makes independent verification difficult.
  - The gap between benchmark performance and real-world utility is significant: models that score highly on academic benchmarks may underperform on practical tasks with ambiguous instructions, unconventional formats, or edge cases not represented in the benchmark distribution.
  - Human evaluation studies (often cited by [[OpenAI]] and competitors to supplement benchmark results) measure user preference, perceived helpfulness, and task completion quality on naturalistic tasks — but suffer from rater variability, demand effects (users preferring newer/branded models), and difficulty controlling for prompt quality across compared systems.
  - Longitudinal capability tracking is complicated by model version changes: ChatGPT's behaviour changes with each model update (including unannounced "safety updates" or fine-tuning adjustments), making it difficult to establish stable baselines for comparative research or reproducibility.
  - The Chatbot Arena (LMSYS Org) crowdsourced Elo-rating system, which collects blind pairwise user preferences across AI systems in open-ended conversation, provides one of the most ecologically valid comparative evaluations of ChatGPT versus competitors, though it suffers from selection bias in its user population and task distribution.
  - Emerging benchmarks designed to resist contamination and assess harder capabilities — FrontierMath, LiveCodeBench (drawing from recently released competitive programming problems), and Humanity's Last Exam (collaboration of domain experts to create maximally difficult questions) — represent the frontier of ChatGPT capability measurement as of 2026.
  - ### Anthropological and Cognitive Science Perspectives
  - Research on human–AI interaction patterns with ChatGPT documents para-social relationship formation: users develop perceived rapport with the model, attribute personality traits, express preference for specific AI personas, and show reluctance to switch to alternatives — behaviours more associated with human relationships than tool use.
  - Cognitive offloading studies examine whether ChatGPT use improves or degrades human cognitive performance: short-term gains in task completion speed are often observed, but longitudinal studies on skill development, memory consolidation, and critical thinking quality under sustained AI assistance remain limited.
  - The "automation bias" literature (prior work on over-reliance on algorithmic recommendations in healthcare and aviation) applies to ChatGPT: users who receive confident AI-generated outputs may insufficiently scrutinise incorrect responses, particularly in domains where they lack independent expertise to detect errors.
  - Phenomenological research documents users' experience of ChatGPT as an interlocutor exhibiting apparent understanding, empathy, and creativity — raising philosophical questions about the nature of machine understanding, consciousness, and the appropriate ethical status of increasingly sophisticated AI systems that are beyond the current scientific consensus to resolve.

  - ## Prompt Engineering and System Prompt Design
  - [[Prompt Engineering]] is the practice of crafting input text that elicits desired outputs from ChatGPT, representing a new form of human-AI collaboration requiring understanding of how the model was trained and what patterns trigger specific behaviours.
  - **Zero-shot prompting:** Providing a task description and expecting the model to perform the task without examples — effective for well-defined tasks within the model's training distribution, such as translation, summarisation, classification, and simple code generation.
  - **Few-shot prompting:** Including two to ten worked examples within the prompt before the task instance, substantially improving performance on structured tasks, uncommon formats, and tasks requiring specific output schemas by demonstrating the desired input-output pattern.
  - **Chain-of-thought prompting (CoT):** Appending "Let's think step by step" or including worked examples that show explicit reasoning steps before final answers, eliciting the model's internal reasoning capacity and significantly improving accuracy on mathematical, logical, and multi-step tasks — formalised by Wei et al. (2022).
  - **System prompt design:** In the API and Custom GPTs, the system message establishes the model's persona, task scope, output format, restrictions, and grounding context. High-quality system prompts specify role ("You are a senior tax accountant"), output constraints ("Respond in UK English, using bullet points"), scope limits ("Only answer questions about UK VAT law"), and escalation behaviours ("If uncertain, direct the user to consult a qualified professional").
  - **Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) integration:** For Custom GPTs and enterprise deployments, [[Retrieval-Augmented Generation]] augments the system prompt with dynamically retrieved document chunks from vector stores (Pinecone, Weaviate, OpenAI Vector Store), enabling accurate responses grounded in proprietary or up-to-date knowledge beyond the model's training cutoff.
  - **Temperature and sampling parameters:** Temperature (0–2) controls output randomness; lower values (0.0–0.3) produce deterministic, factual outputs suited to classification and data extraction; higher values (0.7–1.2) produce diverse, creative outputs suited to brainstorming and storytelling. Top-p (nucleus) sampling and frequency/presence penalties further shape output diversity.
  - **Prompt injection risks:** System prompts can be overridden by adversarial user inputs that instruct the model to ignore prior instructions — a security concern for Custom GPT deployments with sensitive system prompt configurations or privileged tool access, addressed through prompt hardening, output filtering, and model-level safety training.
  - **Jailbreaking and safety circumvention:** Adversarial [[Prompt Engineering]] techniques (role-play framings, hypothetical scenarios, instruction injection) attempt to bypass [[OpenAI]]'s safety training to elicit harmful outputs; [[OpenAI]] maintains continuous red-teaming teams and safety fine-tuning updates to address newly discovered circumvention patterns.
  - **Meta-prompting and prompt generation:** Rather than manually engineering prompts, GPT-4 and GPT-5 can generate, evaluate, and iteratively refine prompts for downstream tasks — a form of automated [[Prompt Engineering]] where the model optimises its own input format for a specific objective.
  - **Structured output and JSON mode:** The JSON mode (API parameter `response_format: {type: "json_object"}`) and the Structured Outputs API (specifying exact JSON schemas) guarantee that model outputs conform to a defined data structure — critical for reliable downstream parsing in production applications where unstructured prose would cause integration failures.
  - **Grounding with time and context:** ChatGPT's training cutoff means it lacks knowledge of events after its cutoff date; web search tool integration (via Bing) enables retrieval of current information within conversations, while system prompt injections of the current date and relevant context mitigate temporal staleness in API-only deployments without search tool access.
  - **Evaluation and automated testing:** LLM-as-judge techniques use GPT-4 or GPT-5 to evaluate the quality of ChatGPT outputs at scale — assessing correctness, helpfulness, safety, and tone — enabling automated quality assurance pipelines for Custom GPT deployments and API integrations that would be impractical to evaluate manually at production volume.
  - **Cost optimisation strategies:** Production deployments reduce ChatGPT API costs through: (1) caching repeated system prompts (50% cost reduction via prompt caching API); (2) model selection routing (using GPT-4o mini for simple classification/extraction tasks, o3 only for complex reasoning); (3) batch processing with the Batch API (50% cost reduction for non-real-time workloads); and (4) context window management (truncating or summarising conversation history to avoid exceeding context limits unnecessarily).

  - ## Safety, Risks, and Alignment
  - ### Hallucination and Factual Accuracy
  - ChatGPT is subject to hallucination — the generation of fluent, confident, but factually incorrect statements — arising from the model's tendency to produce plausible text completions rather than recall verified facts, representing the most significant limitation for high-stakes deployments.
  - Hallucination rates vary substantially by domain and model version: GPT-4 and o-series models hallucinate significantly less than GPT-3.5 on factual Q&A tasks, but no version is hallucination-free, and confident incorrect assertions remain a serious risk in medical, legal, and financial contexts.
  - Mitigation strategies include [[Retrieval-Augmented Generation]] (grounding responses in retrieved verified documents), citation elicitation (prompting the model to provide source references that can be independently verified), and human review workflows for high-stakes outputs.
  - The TruthfulQA benchmark (Lin et al., 2022) measures model tendency to endorse common misconceptions; [[OpenAI]] consistently improves TruthfulQA performance across model generations through targeted fine-tuning, though the benchmark captures only a fraction of real-world hallucination scenarios.
  - ### Bias and Fairness
  - ChatGPT inherits biases present in its training data — web text reflecting historical inequalities, cultural stereotypes, and demographic imbalances — which can manifest as differential performance across demographic groups, stereotypical associations in generated content, and uneven quality of assistance across languages.
  - [[OpenAI]] addresses bias through training data curation, RLHF fine-tuning with explicit fairness objectives, and red-teaming for demographic and cultural bias, but perfect debiasing remains an unsolved research challenge given the scale and diversity of training corpora.
  - Cross-language performance disparities are significant: ChatGPT performs substantially better in English than in lower-resource languages, reflecting the English-heavy composition of web training data, creating accessibility inequities for non-English speaking users.
  - ### Data Privacy and Security
  - The Free and Plus tiers historically used conversation data to improve [[OpenAI]]'s models; Enterprise and Edu tiers guarantee that user inputs are not used for model training, a critical distinction for organisations processing sensitive personal or commercial data.
  - UK GDPR requirements for lawful basis of processing, data subject rights (access, erasure, portability), and restrictions on transfers to third countries apply to organisational ChatGPT deployments, driving adoption of Enterprise tier with Data Processing Agreements rather than consumer tier configurations.
  - Security researchers have demonstrated prompt injection attacks on ChatGPT-integrated applications where malicious content in retrieved documents or user inputs hijacks the model's instructions — a significant concern for agentic deployments where ChatGPT can take consequential actions (send emails, make purchases, modify files).
  - ### Safety Training and Red-Teaming
  - [[OpenAI]] employs dedicated safety teams conducting structured red-teaming — attempting to elicit harmful outputs — before each major model release, with results informing both additional safety fine-tuning and documented residual risks in model system cards.
  - The model is trained to refuse requests for content that facilitates violence, self-harm, CBRN (chemical, biological, radiological, nuclear) weapons synthesis, child sexual abuse material, and serious cybersecurity attacks, with refusal boundaries calibrated to minimise both harmful outputs and excessive over-refusal that limits legitimate use.
  - Scalable oversight research at [[OpenAI]] aims to maintain safety as model capabilities increase: as models become more capable than human evaluators in specific domains, ensuring that human oversight remains effective requires AI-assisted evaluation — a challenge that becomes more acute as capability scales.
  - The [[OpenAI]] Preparedness Framework (2023) establishes a red-line policy for models crossing specified capability thresholds in CBRN, cyberoffensive, persuasion, and autonomy domains, committing to pausing deployment if models demonstrate dangerous capability levels before adequate safety measures are in place.
  - ### Societal and Systemic Risks
  - AI-generated disinformation at scale represents a systemic risk associated with ChatGPT-class language quality: the ability to generate large volumes of plausible, varied, human-indistinguishable text in any persona or style lowers the production cost of targeted misinformation campaigns, synthetic review fraud, and influence operations.
  - Deskilling risks arise where professionals who delegate tasks to ChatGPT atrophy domain skills over time — particularly relevant in education (students not developing writing and research skills), medicine (clinicians not exercising diagnostic reasoning), and software development (engineers not developing algorithmic problem-solving proficiency).
  - Environmental impact: training GPT-4 class models consumes estimates of 1,000–10,000 MWh of electricity; inference at 900 million weekly users represents ongoing significant energy consumption; [[OpenAI]]'s partnership with nuclear energy providers and renewable energy procurement programmes address sustainability concerns, though independent lifecycle analysis of ChatGPT's environmental footprint remains limited.
  - Digital equity concerns: the richest capabilities (o3, GPT-5, Agent Mode) are concentrated in the most expensive subscription tiers, creating a two-tier AI access landscape where users with $200/month budgets access qualitatively superior AI than those constrained to free tier, potentially amplifying existing socioeconomic advantages in education, employment, and entrepreneurship.
  - Epistemic effects at scale: as hundreds of millions of people consult ChatGPT as a primary information source, the model's factual errors, framing choices, and knowledge gaps — shaped by its training data and [[RLHF]] labeller demographics — propagate at scale into public knowledge and belief, raising concerns about AI influence on collective epistemic processes that differ qualitatively from prior information technologies.
  - Dependency and resilience risks: organisations integrating ChatGPT deeply into critical workflows face risks from [[OpenAI]] service outages (historical incidents in 2023-2024 caused by demand surges), model changes (API model deprecations requiring prompt re-engineering), and pricing changes (API price increases affecting applications built on thin margins). Resilient architectures maintain fallback options and avoid single-point AI dependency.
  - ### Intellectual Property and Training Data
  - [[OpenAI]] has faced multiple lawsuits from authors, publishers, news organisations, and code repository owners alleging that training data inclusion of copyrighted material constitutes copyright infringement — with outcomes that will shape AI training data practices globally.
  - The New York Times lawsuit (filed December 2023) alleges verbatim memorisation of NYT articles in ChatGPT training data; [[OpenAI]] counters that training on publicly available data constitutes fair use under US law, an unsettled legal question with major implications for the AI industry.
  - UK copyright law's "computational analysis" exception (CDPA section 29A) may permit text and data mining for research but not commercial AI training without rights-holder permission — creating a more restrictive environment for training data use than the US fair use doctrine, with guidance from the UK IPO under development.
  - [[OpenAI]]'s data licensing deals with publishers (AP, Atlantic, Le Monde, Financial Times, Axel Springer), stock image providers (Shutterstock, Getty Images partnership with Apple), and other content owners represent an evolving model for compensating rights holders for training data contributions rather than relying solely on fair use defences.
