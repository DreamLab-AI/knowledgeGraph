A planetary magnetosphere is the region of space influenced by a planet’s magnetic field, as NASA describes it for Jupiter.[^1]

NASA says Jupiter’s magnetosphere rotates with the planet and sweeps up electrically charged particles, and that the field also produces aurorae at the poles.[^1] For Earth, NASA links the magnetic field to rapid rotation and a molten nickel-iron core, and describes the solar wind as a stream of charged particles ejected continuously from the Sun.[^2]

Opened pages describe two planets and do not state a general definition, a boundary location or the structure of a magnetosphere. Statements about other planets would need further sources.

[^1]: NASA Science, “Jupiter Facts”. https://science.nasa.gov/jupiter/jupiter-facts/
[^2]: NASA Science, “Facts About Earth”. https://science.nasa.gov/earth/facts/
