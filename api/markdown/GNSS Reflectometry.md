GNSS reflectometry is a remote sensing technique in which a receiver records a GNSS satellite signal after it has reflected from the Earth’s surface, in addition to the direct signal, so that the satellite acts as the transmitter of a bistatic radar.[^1]

ESA Navipedia notes that GNSS signals were optimised for navigation, yet the reflected signal still carries information about the state of the reflecting surface. The term GNSS-R is usually reserved for non-grazing reflections in which the reflected signal is essentially incoherent. Grazing reflections keep their coherence and interfere with the direct signal, as seen in radio occultation, and are treated separately.[^1]

Described applications are conceived uses rather than established services. For ocean altimetry, a receiver can observe the specular reflection points of all visible satellites at once, instead of only the sub-satellite point of a nadir radar. Normalised reflected power is said to carry information about soil moisture, and reflections from the cryosphere have been modelled in terms of snow and firn properties.[^1]

This source is a single explanatory article. It does not give accuracy figures, receiver designs or mission performance, so those are not asserted here. The technique is distinct from GNSS positioning, which uses the direct signal.[^1]

[^1]: ESA Navipedia, “Earth Sciences”. https://gssc.esa.int/navipedia/index.php/Earth_Sciences
