Earth's exosphere, the uppermost region of its atmosphere, has no clear upper boundary and gradually fades into outer space.[^1]

It lies directly above the thermosphere. The source states that the altitude of its lower boundary varies with the amount of incoming X-ray and ultraviolet radiation from the Sun, and that the air there is extremely thin.[^1]

Where the exosphere ends depends on the definition used. The source mentions one definition that places the outer limit about halfway to the Moon, and a faint ultraviolet glow of scattered hydrogen, called the geocorona, that satellites have detected at large heights.[^1]

Classification is not settled. Some scientists regard the exosphere as part of the atmosphere and others regard it as part of space, so a statement about what lies inside the atmosphere may depend on the convention in use. The cited page is an educational summary; it does not give a measured profile or a model of the region.[^1]
