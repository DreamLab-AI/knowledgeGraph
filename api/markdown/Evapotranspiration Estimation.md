Evapotranspiration estimation is the quantification of water lost to the atmosphere by evaporation from soil and transpiration from vegetation, here from satellite and related observations.[^1][^2]

The FAO crop guideline defines evapotranspiration as the combination of the two processes and notes that they occur simultaneously, with no easy way of distinguishing between them.[^1] That definition is agronomic, and a satellite product may report the total or a derived quantity differently.

The MODIS MOD16A2 product provides composited layers for evapotranspiration, latent heat flux, potential evapotranspiration and potential latent heat flux, with a quality control layer.[^2] OpenET describes daily, monthly and annual satellite-based evapotranspiration data at field scale for water management.[^3] Both are provider descriptions, and the pages do not assess accuracy.

Surface temperature offers an indirect signal. NASA describes vegetation as able to hold its temperature when it has sufficient water and as warming when water is insufficient, a rise that ECOSTRESS can measure, and names an Evaporative Stress Index among its planned core products.[^4] That temperature signal is not itself an evapotranspiration measurement, and the page does not establish that the index is operational.
