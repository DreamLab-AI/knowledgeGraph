A solar sail is a spacecraft propulsion device that uses solar radiation pressure to generate thrust by reflecting photons from a lightweight, highly reflective membrane.[^1][^2]

According to the Planetary Society, photons have no mass but carry momentum, and when light strikes a bright, mirror-like sail the momentum transferred gives it a small push, with a further small push as the photons bounce off.[^2] A sail facing the Sun directly is pushed away from it.[^2]

According to the NASA survey, solar sails are listed among propellant-less propulsion technologies, with tethers, electric sails and aerodynamic drag devices. Such technologies have long been investigated but have yet to move beyond small-scale demonstrations, with thrust and performance listed as to be determined.[^1]

In the survey, the Near-Earth Asteroid Scout is described as a mission whose main propulsion was a solar sail, assisted by a cold gas system.[^1] No thrust value for a sail is given in the sources, so none is stated here.

[^2]: The Planetary Society, “What is solar sailing?”. https://www.planetary.org/articles/what-is-solar-sailing
