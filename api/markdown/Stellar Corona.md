Stellar corona is the outermost layer of a star's atmosphere, a region of hot plasma lying above the lower atmospheric layers.[^1][^2]

In the ontology model used here, the corona is a kind of stellar atmosphere whose state is plasma.[^2] For the Sun, the corona is described as its outer atmosphere, with gases heated to more than a million degrees Celsius.[^1] Its appearance is reported to change with the sunspot cycle, and it can be observed in white light, in emission lines and in X-rays.[^1]

NASA lists the corona as the outermost part of the Sun's atmosphere, beyond the chromosphere and transition zone, and notes that material leaving it at supersonic speed becomes the solar wind. Notably, the same source presents the finding that the corona becomes hotter with distance from the surface as one of the Sun's open questions.[^3] These sources do not establish a heating mechanism, so none is given here.

Retained sources describe only the Sun's corona. No evidence shows that other stars share the same structure or temperature, and the ontology file records a class relation without measurement. It is also distinct from coronagraph images or eclipse views, which are ways of observing it rather than the layer itself.[^1]
