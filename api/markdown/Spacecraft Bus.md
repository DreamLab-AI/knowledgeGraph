A spacecraft bus is the part of a spacecraft that provides essential services to the payload.[^1]

NASA's small-spacecraft report lists the services as typically including power generation and storage, thermal control, attitude determination and control, navigation and timing, communications, propulsion, and command and data handling.[^1]

NASA's Spacecraft Basics frames the bus differently, as a major part of the structure subsystem, providing attachment points for components internally and externally, housing modules that need thermal and mechanical stability, influencing the basic geometry of the spacecraft and providing attachment points for booms, antennas and scan platforms. An example is the Voyager magnetometers, mounted on a boom 6 and 13 metres from the bus because they are sensitive to electric currents near it.[^2]

A bus can be acquired as a complete platform or used through hosted orbital services. Procurement does not remove the owner's responsibilities for payload-to-bus interface definition and verification, environmental qualification, licensing and operations.[^1] Market descriptions are a snapshot as of January 2026 for small spacecraft.[^1]
