An ice surface is the two-dimensional surface of a body of ice, composed primarily of water ice.[^1]

SWEET places the class beneath planetary boundary rather than beneath a body of ice, so the term names the interface and not the ice mass.[^1] In satellite altimetry the interface is observed as a height: the ICESat-2 ATL06 product provides estimates of the ice-sheet surface height together with the ancillary parameters needed to interpret and assess their quality.[^2] That document states those heights relative to the WGS-84 ellipsoid.[^2]

Neither retained source establishes how an ice surface is separated from overlying snow or from firn, and neither offers a second independent definition of the term. This page therefore bounds it as a surface of ice and leaves the ice bodies themselves to their own pages.
