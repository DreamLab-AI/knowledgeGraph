A pulsar is a rotating neutron star observed to emit pulses of radiation at very regular intervals, typically ranging from milliseconds to seconds.[^1]

NASA explains the pulses with a lighthouse picture. The neutron star has a strong magnetic field that funnels particles along its magnetic poles, and a beam sweeps through space as the star spins. When the beam crosses the line of sight, the star appears to turn on and off.[^1]

Most neutron stars are observed as pulsars, but the same source notes that many neutron stars are probably undetectable because they do not emit enough radiation. Pulsar describes how a star is observed, so it is a subset of neutron stars defined by detectable pulsation.[^1]

NASA reports that the NICER mission demonstrated the use of galactic pulsars as navigational beacons for future deep-space exploration. The source describes this as a demonstration, and it does not establish an operational navigation service.[^2]
