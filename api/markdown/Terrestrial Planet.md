A terrestrial planet is a planet with a solid surface, the name NASA gives to the four inner planets Mercury, Venus, Earth and Mars.[^1]

For exoplanets, NASA's planet-type scheme describes terrestrial planets as Earth sized and smaller and composed of rock, silicate, water or carbon, and as rocky worlds like Earth, Mars, Venus and Mercury but outside the solar system.[^2] The solar-system usage and the exoplanet usage are therefore related but worded differently, and a writer should name which one is meant.[^1][^2]

Planet status within the solar system rests on the IAU definition, which concerns orbit, shape and cleared neighbourhood. It does not define terrestrial planet as a class, so the term is a descriptive grouping used by NASA rather than an IAU category.[^3]

The NASA exoplanet page notes that further study is needed to learn which of these planets have atmospheres, oceans or other features suggesting they could support life. Being terrestrial does not imply habitability.[^2]
