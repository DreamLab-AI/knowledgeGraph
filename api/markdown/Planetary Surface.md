A planetary surface is the solid outer boundary of a rocky world, and the sources opened here describe what shapes it on particular bodies.[^1][^2][^3]

NASA describes the Moon as having a solid, rocky surface cratered and pitted by impacts, with too sparse an atmosphere to impede them.[^1] On Mars, NASA reports brown, gold and tan surface colours, with dust that appears to make the planet look reddish from afar.[^2]

On Io, NASA says volcanic deposits resurface the moon faster than comets and asteroids can mark it with large craters.[^3] Together these pages show that surface appearance reflects different processes on different bodies. None of them gives a general definition of a surface, so the opening sentence is an editorial framing.

[^1]: NASA Science, “Moon Facts”. https://science.nasa.gov/moon/facts/
[^2]: NASA Science, “Mars: Facts”. https://science.nasa.gov/mars/facts/
[^3]: NASA Science, “Io”. https://science.nasa.gov/jupiter/moons/io/
