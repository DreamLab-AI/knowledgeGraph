An accretion disc is a disc of gas that accumulates around a centre of gravitational attraction, such as a white dwarf, neutron star or black hole.[^1]

Gas around a black hole settles into a hot, bright, rapidly spinning disc, and the disc heats through gravitational and frictional forces.[^2][^3] In an active galactic nucleus the luminosity is powered by accretion onto a supermassive black hole, and material near the disc can be ejected into jets and winds.[^3][^4]

A disc is not a property of every black hole. NASA states that an isolated black hole that has consumed the matter surrounding it has no accretion disc and can be very difficult to find and study.[^2]

Sources used here describe the structure qualitatively. They do not give temperatures, sizes or luminosities for a particular object, and they do not fully explain how jets are launched, so those details are outside what this page states.
