Cis-Mars space radiation environment is the natural radiation environment of a cis-Mars space environment, as defined in NASA's Space Life Sciences Ontology.[^1]

Sitting under space radiation environment, the class is marked deprecated in the cited release and carries no quantities. Its definition is relational: it attaches whatever natural radiation is present in a region to the cis-Mars environment, which is itself defined only as the region between the Earth and Mars.[^1]

NASA sources name galactic cosmic radiation and solar particle events as the radiation that crews accumulate on a Mars mission, and describe space radiation beyond Earth's magnetic field as a serious hazard.[^2][^3] A 2017 NASA briefing lists deep space as offering no protection from solar particle events or galactic cosmic rays, gives a dose rate there of approximately 3 times that on the ISS, and reports that dose rates measured on a transit to Mars spiked during solar particle events.[^4]

Those figures are mission-level estimates and a measurement from one vehicle, made under particular shielding and solar conditions. They do not give a dose for the cis-Mars region as such, and the sources do not state how the values would change with a different craft or point in the solar cycle.[^4]
