Spacecraft power and thermal control is the pairing of the electrical power system, which generates, stores and distributes electrical energy, with the thermal control that manages the spacecraft's heat balance.[^1][^2]

NASA states spacecraft thermal control as a balance of energy flows: heat from the Sun, albedo and planetary radiation plus heat generated on board equals heat stored plus heat radiated away. The generated term depends on the power dissipation of spacecraft components.[^2] A lecture on spacecraft thermal control states the steady-state form as energy absorbed plus energy dissipated minus energy emitted equal to zero.[^3]

These subsystems constrain each other. NASA notes that electrical power systems for small spacecraft are often overdesigned to accommodate dynamic thermal environments, eclipse durations in low Earth orbit and varying sun angles.[^1] NASA also separates passive thermal control, which uses no electrically powered equipment, from active methods, which rely on input power.[^2]

These statements come from small-spacecraft guidance and a teaching lecture. They do not establish a general sizing relationship between power and thermal design, and they do not cover every mission type.
