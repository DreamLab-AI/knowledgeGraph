SAR coherence is the interferometric coherence between two complex SAR images, a measure of their complex correlation that serves as an index of the interference quality of the image pair.[^1][^2]

One study describes it as the complex correlation between two complex SAR images, made of a phase and a magnitude component, and another notes that it is synonymously called interferometric correlation.[^1][^2] Values run from complete decorrelation, where the interferometric phase is only noise, to full correlation, where phase noise is absent.[^1] For repeat-pass observations the coherence is determined by the signal-to-noise ratio, the loss of coherence associated with non-zero baselines, and temporal decorrelation.[^2]

Coherence governs how far the phase can be trusted. The Alaska Satellite Facility states that the quality and reliability of unwrapped results strongly depend on the input coherence and that reliable results can only be expected in areas with high coherence.[^3] The SAR Handbook adds that significant surface change reduces interferometric coherence, which allows change detection by coherence thresholding, although coherent image pairs are needed and that limits temporal sampling.[^4][^3]

No retained source gives a numerical threshold for what counts as high coherence or places coherence at a processing level.
