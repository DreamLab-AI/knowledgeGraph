Spaceflight omics is the generation and analysis of genomic, transcriptomic, proteomic and metabolomic data from spaceflight and related experiments. NASA's GeneLab is an open-access repository for storing, sharing and analysing the data.[^1]

GeneLab enables scientists to explore how microgravity, radiation and the space environment affect DNA, RNA, proteins and metabolites.[^1] It sits within NASA's Open Science Data Repository, which includes physiological, phenotypic and behavioural data through the Ames Life Sciences Data Archive.[^1][^2]

Such repositories accept data from spaceflight and analogue studies, so a dataset held there is not necessarily from flight.[^2] Most data is accessible without authentication. Studies with astronaut-derived data have publicly accessible processed data, while the sequence data is access-restricted and must be requested through the controlled access system.[^2]

These pages describe a repository and its purpose, not findings about biological change in space. What the repository holds depends on what investigators submit, and the sources retained here do not set out its coverage by organism or mission.[^1][^2]
