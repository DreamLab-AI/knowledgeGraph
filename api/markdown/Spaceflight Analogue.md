A spaceflight analogue is a ground-based facility or mission that reproduces selected conditions of spaceflight so that they can be studied without leaving Earth.[^1]

NASA’s Human Exploration Research Analog is a 650-square-foot habitat designed to serve as an analog for isolation, confinement and remote conditions in exploration scenarios.[^1] NASA also lists other analogs, including altitude chambers that simulate pressure levels met before and during spacewalks and a series of missions that simulate year-long stays on the surface of Mars.[^2]

Analog research addresses what NASA calls the isolation and confinement hazard, the behavioural responses of groups isolated in a small space over a long period.[^3] An analogue reproduces only the conditions it is built for. The sources do not say that any one facility reproduces microgravity or radiation.[^1][^2][^3]
