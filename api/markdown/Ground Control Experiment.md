A ground control experiment is a control run on the ground alongside a spaceflight experiment, designed to duplicate the flight environment except for effects such as microgravity and radiation.[^1]

NASA's compendium of animal and cell spaceflight research lists several designations. A synchronous control runs on the ground at the same time as the flight experiment, with subjects in flight-type housing and other conditions held equal apart from unique flight factors such as microgravity, radiation and launch and reentry stresses. An asynchronous or delayed control is similar but uses data from the flight period to reproduce conditions such as temperature. Vivarium controls keep subjects in standard laboratory conditions, and basal controls are equivalent subjects sacrificed at launch to give baseline data.[^1]

NASA's rodent research planning material calls proper ground-control experiments essential for scientifically sound spaceflight experiments, and says NASA will support synchronous ground controls housed in flight hardware as well as delayed ones.[^2]

Both sources are NASA documents about animal and cell research, and the compendium presents these designations as the ones typically used, so other disciplines or agencies may use different designs. The compendium lists gravity simulation on an in-flight centrifuge as a separate category, so a ground control by itself does not show which flight factor produced a difference.[^1]
