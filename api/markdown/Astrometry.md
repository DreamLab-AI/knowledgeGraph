Astrometry is the branch of astronomy that measures the positions of celestial objects, especially stars.[^1]

Astrometrists measure parallaxes and proper motions, which allow the distances and velocities of stars to be determined. Parallax is the apparent change in the position of an object seen against a more distant background when the viewpoint changes.[^1][^2]

ESA's Hipparcos mission surveyed the positions of stars from space, fixing more than one hundred thousand stars to high precision and more than one million to lesser precision. Gaia lists astrometry as one of three principal areas of measurement, defined as stellar position, parallax and proper motion.[^2][^3]

Astrometry is separate from photometry, which measures brightness, and from spectroscopy, which gives radial velocities and astrophysical parameters. The sources used here do not describe astrometry of minor planets, so this page does not cover it.
