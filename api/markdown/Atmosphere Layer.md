An atmosphere layer is a vertical division of the atmosphere, set by how temperature changes with altitude.[^1][^2] The Met Office states that the layers are defined according to changes in temperature with altitude and that their boundaries are not precisely fixed.[^1]

The Royal Meteorological Society describes the usual division as four isothermal layers called spheres, namely the troposphere, stratosphere, mesosphere and thermosphere, with the boundary of each marked by a pause where the temperature profile abruptly changes.[^2] The Met Office adds an exosphere, which has no well-defined upper limit because it merges with interplanetary space.[^1]

SWEET types the class as a planetary layer within the atmosphere realm.[^3] The retained sources do not agree on whether the exosphere is counted as a fifth layer, and they give differing heights for the tops of the layers, so the page states no altitude figures.

[^2]: Royal Meteorological Society MetLink, “Atmosphere”. https://www.metlink.org/resource/atmosphere/
