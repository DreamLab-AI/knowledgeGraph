
A GNSS antenna receives navigation signals from satellites and couples them into the receiver's radio-frequency path.

Its gain pattern, polarisation, bandwidth, filtering, amplifier and installation affect the signals presented to the receiver. Many user antennas are designed for right-hand circular polarisation and broad sky coverage, but the design is application-specific. Ground planes, radomes, cables and nearby objects can alter the pattern, signal level, multipath response or phase behaviour.[^1][^2]

GNSS measurements refer to an electrical antenna phase centre, which need not coincide with the geometric centre. Its offset and variation can depend on signal frequency and arrival direction. The antenna reference point (ARP) provides a practical mechanical reference, while calibration models describe frequency- and angle-dependent corrections. Precise survey and geodetic processing must keep the ARP, phase centre and monument or platform reference distinct.[^2][^3]

Spacecraft transmit-antenna patterns are a separate property from user antenna characteristics. The Galileo reference antenna-pattern document provides a model for the navigation antenna, including side lobes; it is not a calibration of every satellite or a specification for a receiver antenna.[^4] ESA also characterises receiver antennas by gain pattern, phase centre and related RF properties.[^5]
