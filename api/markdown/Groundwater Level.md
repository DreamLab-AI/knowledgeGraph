Groundwater Level is the elevation of the water table, expressed either as a height above a datum such as sea level or as a depth below the surface.[^1][^2]

USGS defines the level in exactly those two ways.[^1] The water table is the upper surface of the saturated zone, and water in the saturated zone is called ground water.[^2] The USGS water glossary words the water table as the top water surface of an unconfined aquifer at atmospheric pressure.[^3]

Depth to the water table can be determined by installing wells that penetrate the top of the saturated zone just far enough to hold standing water.[^2] Its configuration varies seasonally and from year to year because recharge follows the variation in precipitation, so a level is valid for the time of measurement only.[^2]

SWEET classes groundwater level as a hydrosphere feature and as a planetary surface level, without a prose definition.[^4] The retained sources describe in situ well measurement and do not establish how satellite data are converted to a groundwater level, nor any accuracy.[^1][^2][^4]
