Earth's geomagnetic field, whose main part is generated in the conducting, fluid outer core, includes contributions from several sources.[^1][^2]

Field observations show a sum of contributions: the main field varies in both time and space, the crustal field varies spatially but is treated as constant over the time-scales of the World Magnetic Model, and disturbance fields vary in space and rapidly in time. The model's authors state that the main field dominates at the surface and that secular variation is the slow change in time of the main field.[^1]

This field shapes Earth's magnetosphere, the region around a planet dominated by its magnetic field. NASA describes the Earth case as a comet-shaped bubble, and NOAA names the boundary with the solar wind the magnetopause and says it is constantly in motion.[^2][^3]

Observatories such as the British Geological Survey measure, record and model variations in the field and relate them to geomagnetic hazards. The World Magnetic Model represents only the main field, so it does not describe storm-time disturbance. The BGS page does not set out the physics of the dynamo, so the origin statement here rests on the NCEI and NASA pages.[^4][^1]
