Confined bed is not a defined term in the retained hydrogeological sources, which use confining bed for the low-permeability layer that holds an aquifer under pressure and confined for the condition of the aquifer below it.[^1][^2]

Western Oregon University defines a confining bed as a body of impermeable or distinctly less permeable material stratigraphically adjacent to one or more aquifers.[^1] The Groundwater Project describes a confined aquifer as one occurring beneath an aquitard, often called a confining bed.[^2]

USGS nomenclature guidance prefers confining unit to confining bed where more than a single bed makes up the layer, which limits where the word bed is appropriate.[^3][^1] This page therefore treats a confined bed as a bed whose water or pressure is confined by an adjoining confining bed, and that reading is an inference from the sources, not a quotation.[^1][^2]

Retained sources do not establish a standalone definition of confined bed or any figure for it, and they do not describe it from remote sensing.[^2][^3]
