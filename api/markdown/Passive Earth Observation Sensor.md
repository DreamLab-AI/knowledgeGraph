A passive Earth observation sensor measures energy that is naturally available, such as reflected sunlight or emitted thermal radiation, and supplies no illumination of its own.[^1]

The Canadian tutorial states that reflected energy can be detected only while the sun illuminates the Earth, while naturally emitted energy such as thermal infrared can be detected day or night if enough is emitted. It contrasts passive sensors with active sensors, which provide their own energy source, and gives a passive microwave radiometer as an example, so passive does not imply optical.[^1]

In the W3C sensor vocabulary, a sensor is a device, agent or software involved in implementing a procedure, responding to a stimulus and generating a result, and it can be hosted by a platform.[^2] The vocabulary does not classify sensors as passive or active.

The Operational Land Imager and the Sentinel-2 Multi-Spectral Instrument are spaceborne optical sensors described by NASA and ESA.[^3][^4] Neither page uses the word passive, and their classification follows from the tutorial's description of sensors that rely on natural illumination.
