Leftover radiation from the hot early universe, the cosmic microwave background fills the whole Universe and is observed as a nearly uniform glow at microwave wavelengths.[^1]

ESA states that the radiation was released about 380,000 years after the Big Bang. Before then photons kept interacting with free electrons and the early Universe was opaque. NASA describes the same epoch of recombination, when nuclei captured electrons and the cosmic fog cleared.[^1][^2]

NASA's COBE mission found that the spectrum is that of a nearly perfect blackbody, and ESA reports small temperature fluctuations across the sky. Mission pages interpret these fluctuations as differences in density that were seeds for galaxies, an interpretation drawn from the standard cosmological model.[^1][^2][^3]

Quoted temperatures differ slightly between pages, so a value should be given as approximate. The sources do not establish details of what happened before the radiation was released.
