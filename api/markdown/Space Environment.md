Space beyond Earth's protective atmosphere includes conditions such as radiation and microgravity that spacecraft and living systems experience in spaceflight.[^1][^2]

NASA describes the radiation component outside Earth's protective atmosphere as energetic protons and heavy nuclei that are hazardous to living organisms.[^1] It also names radiation and microgravity as space stressors acting on biological and physical systems.[^2]

Physical-science research under the same programme covers weightlessness and the partial gravity of planetary bodies.[^2] The retained sources name partial gravity without giving values for any body, so no gravity level is asserted here.

Ionising radiation on missions outside Earth's protective atmosphere is a health concern for astronauts.[^3] These sources do not list every component of the environment, and they describe NASA's programmes rather than a complete physical definition.

[^1]: NASA, “Space Radiation” https://www.nasa.gov/humans-in-space/space-radiation/
[^2]: NASA, “Biological and Physical Sciences” https://science.nasa.gov/biological-physical/
[^3]: NASA Human Research Program, “Space Radiation Element” https://www.nasa.gov/hrp/elements/radiation
