
A satellite uplink is a transmission from an Earth-based station or terminal to a satellite.

Uplink names the direction of transmission, not the information’s purpose. In spacecraft operations an uplink commonly carries commands; commercial satellite networks also use uplinks for user, gateway or feeder-link traffic. It may carry modulated data or a carrier used for functions such as coherent tracking. Not every mission uses coherent two-way operation.[^1][^2][^3]

Uplink design accounts for transmitter power, antenna gain and pointing, path loss, interference, atmospheric attenuation, receiver sensitivity, waveform and coding. Weather-driven power control can compensate some fading in applicable high-frequency feeder links, but it does not remove interference, licensing constraints or all propagation impairments.[^1][^7]

Spectrum use and earth-station permission depend on service and jurisdiction. The ITU Radio Regulations describe allocations with service, direction, Region and footnote qualifications. In the UK, Ofcom earth-station guidance specifies licence classes and technical deployments, including separate guidance for non-geostationary fixed-satellite-service stations. Those permissions are limited to their stated licence types and parameters; an uplink’s direction or band label does not grant authority to transmit.[^4][^5][^6]
