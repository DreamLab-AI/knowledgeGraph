Incidence angle is the angle between the incident radar beam on a surface and the normal to a reference surface.[^1]

Local incidence angle uses the normal to the surface at the target and so reflects local topography, whereas the angle to the ellipsoid ignores local slope. For a flat surface, ignoring Earth curvature, the incidence angle equals the look angle. For spaceborne systems curvature makes the incidence angle larger than the look angle on a flat surface, and treating the two as interchangeable is correct only for low-flying aircraft over terrain without topography.[^1][^2]

Within a SAR scene the incidence angle increases from near to far range. For diffuse reflection, larger angles give lower backscatter, and the effect is less pronounced for rough than for smooth surfaces. Training material from NASA describes the local incidence angle as accounting for local inclination of the surface and as influencing image brightness.[^1][^3]

Conventions differ. The NASA training slides measure the angle against the Earth’s surface plane, while the other sources use the surface normal, so a stated value needs its reference. The sources do not give values for any particular sensor.[^1][^2][^3]
