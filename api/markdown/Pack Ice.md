Pack ice is a drift ice mass with an ice concentration at or above 7/10, which covers 70% or more of a given area of a water body.[^1]

SWEET makes pack ice a subclass of drift ice, defined as a floating mass of ice that is unattached to land or land-fast ice and is moved by the action of winds or currents.[^1] NSIDC says that most sea ice is constantly moving and changing location.[^2]

Usage of the term has shifted. SWEET records that before approximately 2014 pack ice was used for all ranges of drift ice concentrations, so older usage is close to a synonym of drift ice.[^1] The Canadian Ice Service glossary pairs the words in its grade names, for example very close pack or drift at 9/10 to less than 10/10 and compact ice at 10/10 with no water visible, and it grades open drift at 4/10 to 6/10 with many leads and polynyas.[^3]

A reader therefore needs to check which convention a dataset follows. The retained sources do not establish how concentration is measured from satellite imagery, nor how widely pack ice occurs in either hemisphere.[^1][^3]
