Processing lineage is the record of the entities, activities and processing steps that produced a data product, held as provenance information that can be used to assess its quality and trustworthiness.[^1]

The W3C PROV overview describes provenance as information about entities, activities and people involved in producing data, usable to form assessments of quality, reliability or trustworthiness. PROV is a general web model and is not specific to Earth observation. Processing lineage is its application to the processing history of a product.[^1]

The CARD4L surface reflectance specification asks for processing chain provenance in the metadata, with the target requirement being a description of the processing steps and the software versions used. It also notes that users often judge fitness for purpose from the lineage of the data and not from a specific estimate of measurement uncertainty.[^2]

NASA organises its data products into processing levels from Level 0 to Level 4, so a product's level is one part of its lineage. A level label does not by itself record the steps, auxiliary data or software versions, and the sources opened here do not say that every product records them.[^2][^3]
