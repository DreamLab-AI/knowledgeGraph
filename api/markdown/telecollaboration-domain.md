  ## Definition

  The **Telecollaboration Domain** represents a top-level classification of knowledge focused on technology-mediated distributed collaboration. This domain encompasses theoretical frameworks, technical infrastructure, application platforms, coordination mechanisms, and socio-technical practices enabling effective remote teamwork.

  Unlike simple communication tools, telecollaboration integrates:
  - **Presence Systems**: Technologies creating psychological sense of remote presence
  - **Coordination Mechanisms**: Protocols for synchronous and asynchronous work
  - **Shared Artifacts**: Digital workspaces and collaborative documents
  - **Communication Infrastructure**: Real-time networking and data exchange
  - **Social Dynamics**: Trust-building and cultural adaptation in distributed teams

  ## Theoretical Foundations

  ### Social Presence Theory
  Short, Williams, & Christie (1976) established that telecommunication media vary in their capacity to convey psychological sense of being together, directly impacting collaboration effectiveness.

  ### Media Richness Theory
  Daft & Lengel (1986) demonstrated that communication channels differ in information-carrying capacity, with implications for task-media fit in distributed work.

  ### Common Ground Theory
  Clark & Brennan (1991) identified how collaborators establish shared understanding through grounding processes, critical for telecollaboration design.

  ### Coordination Theory
  Malone & Crowston (1994) formalized coordination mechanisms (mutual adjustment, standardization, plans) essential for distributed teamwork.
