High Earth Orbit is a broad altitude-based label for geocentric orbits beyond the medium-altitude region, with its boundary set by the convention in use.

A high-Earth label does not specify eccentricity, inclination, period or mission purpose. NASA's Earth-orbit catalogue uses “high Earth orbit” qualitatively for satellites farthest from the surface, while its SCaN glossary gives MEO as extending to 36,000 km. The ITU's 2024 Radio Regulations define a geosynchronous satellite by period rather than altitude; its separate public overview gives 35,786 km as the geostationary altitude. These descriptions should not be collapsed into a single universal cutoff.[^1390][^1391][^1398]

Operational catalogues may draw explicit altitude bands. UK cross-government Space Domain Awareness requirements place their GEO surveillance region between 35,586 and 37,000 km and treat HEO as a separate eccentricity-based surveillance category. These values specify a UK surveillance framework; they are not a redefinition of orbital mechanics or a global classification standard.[^1397]

High Earth Orbit is independent of orbit shape. A nearly circular geosynchronous orbit can be high by altitude while having very low eccentricity. A highly eccentric orbit may cross several altitude regions on each revolution, so apogee alone does not determine the environment or the object's full regime history. When using this label, name the boundary, altitude reference and whether the value refers to instantaneous altitude, perigee, apogee or another orbit statistic.[^1390][^1397][^1400]
