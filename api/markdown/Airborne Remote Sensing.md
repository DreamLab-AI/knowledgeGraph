Airborne remote sensing is remote sensing carried out with sensors mounted on aircraft or other platforms within the Earth's atmosphere.[^1][^2]

Natural Resources Canada defines remote sensing as acquiring information about the Earth's surface without contact, by sensing and recording reflected or emitted energy and then processing that information. It lists ground, in-atmosphere and spacecraft platforms, and names aircraft and balloons among the in-atmosphere options.[^1][^2]

According to the same tutorial, aerial platforms are mainly fixed-wing aircraft, with helicopters used occasionally, and aircraft are often used to collect very detailed images over virtually any part of the Earth's surface at any time. The tutorial does not address uncrewed aircraft, and it does not describe weather or airspace limits.[^2]

NASA's Airborne Science Program states that its aircraft systems further science and advance the use of satellite data, including calibration and validation of satellite measurements and the testing of new sensors before launch. That page describes one programme's objectives and should not be read as a definition of the whole field.[^3]
