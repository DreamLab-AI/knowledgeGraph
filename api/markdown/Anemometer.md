An anemometer is an instrument that measures wind, recording wind speed and in some arrangements direction and the strength of gusts.[^1]

Met Office guidance describes a cup anemometer, in which three or four cups on a vertical spindle rotate in the wind and the rate of rotation is close to proportional to wind speed in standard instruments. It also describes a heated sonic anemometer for extreme conditions, which has no moving parts and estimates speed and direction from acoustic signals between transducer pairs.[^1]

Measured wind speed depends on sensor height. The W3C sensor ontology notes that procedures define a standard anemometer height above ground, typically ten metres for meteorological measurements and a lower height in agrometeorology, so a reading should be reported with its height.[^1][^2]

Averaging period is part of the measurement definition too. NOAA National Data Buoy Center reports wind speed averaged over an eight-minute period for buoys and a two-minute period for land stations, so values from different networks are not directly comparable without checking their conventions.[^3]
