Cone of impression is the roughly conical rise of the water table or potentiometric surface that develops around a well into which water is injected, the mirror image of a cone of depression.[^1][^2]

Kansas Geological Survey describes it as a rise produced in the water table when injection exceeds the ability of the aquifer to transmit water away from the well, and says its size depends on duration, rate, hydraulic conductivity, specific yield and aquifer thickness.[^1] Western Oregon University states it as a rise of the potentiometric surface around an injection well, which ties the term to confined and artesian conditions as well as to the water table.[^2]

Both glossaries define the term only for an injection well. The retained sources do not establish whether it also covers mounds from natural or surface recharge, which the Western Oregon glossary treats under a separate ground-water mound entry that this page does not rely on.[^1][^2]

No retained source gives a figure for a cone of impression or describes its observation from satellites, so remote detection is not claimed here.[^1][^2]
