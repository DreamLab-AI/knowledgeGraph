Precession is the slow motion of Earth's rotation axis, which traces a circle over about 26,000 years.[^1]

NASA explains that the Moon's gravity, and to a lesser degree the Sun's, acts on the equatorial bulge, and that because of gyroscopic action the poles do not right themselves but precess at right angles to the applied force. Nutation is a smaller nodding motion superimposed on it.[^1]

The IERS Conventions define precession, in the IAU 2000 framework, as the secular part of the motion of the Celestial Intermediate Pole plus the term of 26000-year period, with nutation as the remainder. The split is a convention, and the IERS publishes observed pole offsets as corrections to the adopted model.[^2]

Because the axis moves, celestial coordinates need an epoch. NASA notes that only the precession part is considered in defining the mean equator and equinox for the standard epoch J2000.0. The sources opened here do not give the precession rate or the model equations.[^1][^2]
