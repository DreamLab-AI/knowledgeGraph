Land ice is any part of the seasonal or perennial ice cover that has formed over land, principally by the freezing of precipitation.[^1]

SWEET contrasts it with sea ice formed by the freezing of seawater, and NSIDC likewise defines sea ice as any form of ice found at sea which has originated from the freezing of sea water.[^1][^2] SWEET counts an iceberg or tabular iceberg as land ice, together with its parent glacier, ice sheet or ice shelf.[^1] The CF metadata discussion agrees that land ice includes ice formed on land which has moved out to sea and has very different characteristics to sea ice.[^3]

SWEET names the ice sheets of Greenland and Antarctica as the two major concentrations of land ice, and NSIDC defines an ice sheet as a dome-shaped mass of glacier ice greater than 50,000 square kilometres.[^1][^4] The retained sources give no mass or volume totals, so the page states none.
