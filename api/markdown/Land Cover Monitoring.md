Land cover monitoring is the repeated mapping of the physical surface of the land, such as vegetation, bare ground and built areas, from satellite imagery.[^1][^2]

Operational products exist at national and global scale. The Multi-Resolution Land Characteristics Consortium distributes the Annual National Land Cover Database as a suite of raster land use and land cover products for the conterminous United States.[^1] ESA WorldCover is described as a global baseline land cover product at 10 m resolution built from Sentinel-1 and Sentinel-2 data.[^2] The Copernicus Land Monitoring Service publishes annual global land cover maps and cover fraction layers at several classification levels.[^3]

A long, consistent image record underpins change detection. NASA describes Landsat as the longest continuous space-based record of Earth's surface.[^4] The Copernicus service states that tracking changes in land cover and land use helps users understand the forces behind them.[^3]

Products differ in classes, resolution, year range and method, and the sources opened here give an accuracy statement only for the Copernicus product, for its own latest version. A map from one product should not be compared with another without checking their class definitions.[^3][^1][^2]
