An ablation zone is the part of a glacier or ice sheet in which ice loss exceeds ice gain.[^1][^2]

The National Park Service places it in the lower elevation portion of a glacier, where ablation exceeds accumulation rates.[^2] Ablation there covers every process that decreases glacier mass, including melting and runoff, evaporation, sublimation, calving and wind erosion.[^2] In late summer, once the seasonal snow has melted, the bare ice of the zone is exposed.[^3]

Ice moves downslope from the accumulation zone into the ablation zone, and a thin band where gains and losses balance separates the two, usually called the equilibrium line.[^4] Essentially all glaciers have both zones.[^3]

Retained sources describe the zone qualitatively. They do not establish an elevation range, an area threshold or a satellite method for mapping it, and they treat the ice sheet case only through the SWEET definition.[^1][^2]
