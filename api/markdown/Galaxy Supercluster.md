A galaxy supercluster is an extended region with a high concentration of galaxies, a term that the Laniakea paper says is not precise.[^1]

NASA describes groups and clusters as arranged in superclusters, which are not gravitationally bound. It places superclusters, voids and walls of galaxies together as large-scale structures making up the cosmic web.[^2]

The Laniakea paper offers a definition from galaxy motions. It maps peculiar velocities, the line-of-sight departures from cosmic expansion, finds a surface where flows diverge, and defines a supercluster as the volume within that surface. Within that volume, galaxy motions are inward after the mean expansion and long-range flows are removed.[^1] NASA places the Local Group in the Laniakea supercluster.[^2]

Superclusters are not the same as clusters. The Chandra field guide calls clusters the largest gravitationally bound objects in the universe, whereas NASA describes superclusters as not bound.[^3] The sources do not establish a single boundary, so density-based and flow-based definitions can give different extents.
