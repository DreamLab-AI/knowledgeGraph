An Earth observation Level 3 data product holds variables mapped on a uniform space-time grid, usually with some completeness and consistency.[^1][^2]

NOAA describes Level 3 data as Level 2 data mapped on a uniform space-time grid and so averaged over space and/or time, and the Metrologia review says Level 3 products are made by aggregating Level 2 values in space and/or time on a regular space-time grid.[^3][^4] CEOS adds that such re-sampling may include averaging and compositing.[^2]

NASA lists Level 3A as generally periodic summaries of Level 2 products, with weekly, 10-day and monthly examples.[^1] The usual reason given for producing them is that gridded products are easier to use and have smaller volumes than Level 2 data, at the cost of lower sampling.[^4]

A Level 3 product is not necessarily complete. The Metrologia review states that Level 2 and Level 3 products are not gap free, which is why Level 4 production needs interpolation.[^4] The retained sources do not fix a single grid, averaging rule or completeness threshold for Level 3, so these must be read from each product's documentation.[^1][^2]
