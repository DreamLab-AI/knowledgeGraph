
A GNSS signal is a radio-frequency waveform transmitted by a navigation satellite for user equipment to acquire and track.

A specified signal can combine a carrier with satellite-specific ranging codes and, on a data component, navigation symbols. Ranging codes support signal identification and code-delay measurement; some designs include pilot components without navigation data to assist acquisition or tracking. Signal components, modulation and data content differ by constellation, frequency band and interface revision.[^1][^2]

Signal-in-space waveforms are distinct from the GNSS antenna and from a receiver's processing. The received signal is affected by antenna response, propagation, obstruction and interference, as well as receiver bandwidth and tracking design. Accordingly, a constellation's signal-in-space performance specification does not guarantee a particular receiver's availability or accuracy in its operating environment.[^3][^4]

Galileo Open Service signal requirements are defined in OS SIS ICD v2.2, listed as in force by the European GNSS Service Centre and dated November 2025. GPS signal-to-user interfaces are defined in interface specifications including IS-GPS-200N, dated August 2022; GPS.gov also lists a June 2026 revision notice. Implementers should check the applicable current documents and notices rather than rely on a generic or older waveform description.[^1][^5][^6]
