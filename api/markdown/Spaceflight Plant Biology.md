Spaceflight plant biology is the study of how plants grow and respond in the space environment, carried out on the International Space Station in growth systems such as Veggie and the Advanced Plant Habitat.[^1][^2]

NASA states that gravity affects how plants grow and the yield of crops, and that in its absence plants use other environmental factors, such as light, to orient and guide growth.[^2][^1] The sources give no yield magnitudes, and effects will depend on species and experiment.[^2]

Water delivery is described as a significant difficulty, because roots must be kept healthy without drowning them. The Veggie fact sheet attributes the difficulty to fluid physics and the lack of convective flow, and describes a fan that draws in cabin air.[^2][^3]

NASA reports that light type can affect plant size, nutritional content, microbial growth and taste, and that plants exposed to spaceflight undergo changes in how genes turn on or off without any change to the DNA sequence.[^2] Veggie experiments are paired with crops grown on the ground in step with the orbital runs as a control group.[^3] Veggie is also presented as a source of fresh food and well-being for crews, so not every Veggie run is a physiology experiment.[^1][^3]
