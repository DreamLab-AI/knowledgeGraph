A highly elliptical orbit is a bound elliptical orbit with high eccentricity and distinct perigee and apogee altitudes.

“Highly” has no universal numerical threshold. NASA's conjunction-assessment glossary defines a highly elliptic orbit as eccentricity greater than 0.25 and less than 1; UK cross-government SDA requirements use eccentricity of at least 0.25 for their HEO surveillance category. These are operational thresholds, so state the eccentricity and the convention when the classification matters. HEO can also be confused with “high Earth orbit”; spell out the term and do not infer altitude from the acronym.[^1402][^1397][^1391]

In an ideal two-body ellipse, the spacecraft moves fastest at perigee and slowest at apogee. Kepler's area law explains the slow motion near apogee and the longer time spent in that part of the orbit. Inclined Molniya-type trajectories use this geometry to provide extended visibility of high latitudes, where a geostationary satellite appears low above the horizon. The dwell interval depends on the orbit and the observer's elevation mask; one spacecraft does not provide continuous coverage by itself.[^1399][^1390][^1400]

An HEO is a geometric description, not a mission status. It may describe a transfer orbit, a maintained service orbit or an uncatalogued object. A geostationary transfer trajectory, for example, can pass through LEO, MEO and GEO altitude regions during one revolution. Record perigee, apogee, eccentricity, inclination, period, current orbital phase and whether the orbit is temporary or maintained.[^1397]

Environmental analysis must follow the whole trajectory. Apogee height does not capture particle exposure at other altitudes, and radiation varies over time: ESA describes the Galileo MEO shell crossing the outer Van Allen belt, while a British Antarctic Survey-led model validated against Galileo data reports changing electron populations. Apply mission-specific radiation and dose analysis rather than assigning a single environment from the HEO label.[^1395][^1403]
