Spectral resolution is the ability of an instrument to distinguish features in the electromagnetic spectrum, with finer resolution meaning more and narrower bands.[^1][^2]

NASA treats the narrowness of each band as the measure: the narrower the range of wavelengths in a band, the finer the spectral resolution. Instruments with a few bands are called multispectral, and instruments with hundreds to thousands of bands are called hyperspectral; the Royal Meteorological Society gives the same distinction, with a few discrete bands for multispectral systems.[^1][^3]

Both sources link finer resolution to discrimination between features such as rock and mineral types or vegetation species. NASA gives an airborne example in which one instrument records 224 spectral channels. The sources do not state that finer spectral resolution always improves classification accuracy.[^1][^3]

Band width also affects signal, since the Landsat instrument page says that narrower bands were chosen there to limit atmospheric absorption and so improve signal clarity. Spectral resolution is distinct from spatial resolution, which concerns pixel size, and from the count of bands alone.[^2][^4]
