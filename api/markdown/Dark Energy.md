Dark energy is the name given to the unknown cause of the accelerating expansion of the universe.[^1]

NASA states that in 1998 two teams of astronomers observing far-off supernovae found them dimmer than expected, a result interpreted as expansion that speeds up over time. NASA's short answer to what dark energy is runs to the effect that this is not known.[^1]

NASA's page sets out candidate explanations, including a vacuum energy that could correspond to the cosmological constant and a varying field called quintessence. It notes that predictions of the vacuum energy do not match what is needed, which it calls the cosmological constant problem, and that it is not known whether dark energy acts as a force at all.[^1]

NASA's dark matter page gives the universe's make-up as normal matter, dark matter and dark energy, with dark energy the largest share.[^2] ESA says Euclid is designed to determine the properties of dark energy and dark matter, but that scientists remain unsure what either actually is.[^3] Dark energy should not be conflated with dark matter, which NASA treats as a separate mystery that shapes the structure of galaxies and clusters.
