An active galactic nucleus is an unusually bright galactic nucleus whose light is not due to starlight, now understood as an active supermassive black hole at the centre of a galaxy.[^1][^2]

Material surrounding the black hole forms an accretion disc, and part of it is ejected as jets and winds. The emission is detected across the electromagnetic spectrum, from X-rays and visible light to infrared light and radio waves.[^2][^3]

Several classes fall under the term, including Seyfert galaxies, radio galaxies, quasars and blazars. NASA relates some of the differences to distance from Earth and to the direction in which a jet points, so a quasar is one kind of active galactic nucleus and not a synonym for the whole group.[^2]

Those two framings are complementary. One is an observational definition by brightness and the other an interpretation in terms of an accreting black hole. NASA describes how long quasar activity lasts as unsettled, and the sources do not give a complete unification scheme.
