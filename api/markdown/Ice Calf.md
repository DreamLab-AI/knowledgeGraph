Ice Calf is a piece of ice that has broken away from a larger ice mass such as a glacier front or a berg.[^1][^2]

SWEET defines it as an ice mass that has calved off a larger mass of ice, and notes that the definition is intentionally broad to cover the several definitions in use.[^1] The Encyclopedia Arctica glossary is narrower in its first sense, a piece of ice broken off from the front of a glacier or barrier, and adds that the word is sometimes used of a piece breaking from a large berg.[^2]

The USGS glossary describes calving as pieces of ice breaking away from a glacier terminus that ends in water or from the edge of a floating ice shelf, and calls the pieces icebergs once they enter the water.[^3] A calf is therefore the product of calving, whereas the terminus it leaves is the Glacier Terminus.[^3]

Retained sources give no size class that separates a calf from an iceberg in current usage, and they do not describe how calved pieces are detected by remote sensing.[^1][^2][^3]
