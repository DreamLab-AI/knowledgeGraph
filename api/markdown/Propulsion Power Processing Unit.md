A propulsion power processing unit is a component of an electric propulsion system that the NASA small-spacecraft survey counts alongside thrusters, feed systems and propellant management, separate from the spacecraft electric power supply.[^1]

According to the survey, some simple resistojets may run directly from spacecraft bus power, while other electrothermal devices may need a relatively complex unit. In some cases the cost and integration challenges of the unit can greatly exceed those of the thruster.[^1] The same chapter says power processing units were not reviewed in their own right, so it does not set out their internal design or a general performance figure.[^1]

At spacecraft level, NASA describes power management and distribution systems, also called power conditioning and distribution units, as regulating and distributing electricity to spacecraft subsystems and instruments.[^2] That chapter does not discuss the propulsion unit, so the relationship between the two is taken from the vault classification rather than from the source.

In the survey, the unit appears within specific systems, for example one that controls an RF thruster and its cathode in an integrated propulsion system.[^1] A power processing unit should not be confused with the electric power supply, which the survey excludes from its propulsion systems, or with the thruster it serves.[^1]
