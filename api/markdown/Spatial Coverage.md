Spatial coverage is the portion of the Earth's surface that a sensor or mission observes, determined by its swath, orbit and acquisition plan.[^1][^2]

Sentinel-2 is described as a wide-swath mission with an orbital swath width of 290 km. With two satellites in the same orbit, phased at 180 degrees, its design revisit is 5 days at the Equator. Landsat 9 uses the WRS-2 global reference grid to organise its scenes.[^1][^2]

Coverage is nominal. The Sentinel-2 revisit figure is a design value for two satellites, and the mission also runs an extension campaign that raises revisit over some regions. Actual coverage in a given period depends on acquisition plans and on surface conditions such as cloud.[^1][^3]

Coverage differs from sampling bias, although uneven coverage can cause it. Polar-orbiting records sample specific local times and drift over a mission, and clear-sky retrievals leave cloudy places unobserved, so complete geographic coverage does not guarantee a representative sample.[^3][^4]
