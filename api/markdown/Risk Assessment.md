# Risk Assessment

**Definition:** [[Risk Assessment]] is the systematic process of identifying, analysing, and evaluating potential hazards, vulnerabilities, and adverse outcomes within complex systems, networks, or operational environments. Risk assessment provides a structured methodology for understanding [[Distributed System|distributed systems]], their failure modes, and the impact chains that propagate through [[Metadata Management]] and [[Access Control System]] architectures.

## Content

Effective risk assessment in networked systems requires understanding both technical vulnerabilities and the cascading effects of failures across [[Distributed System|distributed architectures]]. Within [[Identity Management System]] designs, risk assessment must account for authentication failures, privilege escalation paths, and the security perimeter defined by [[API Gateway|API gateways]]. Each identified risk requires quantification in terms of likelihood, impact magnitude, and detectability.

Modern risk assessment frameworks integrate quantitative probability analysis with qualitative stakeholder judgement, enabling decision-makers to prioritise mitigation investments. For [[Compute Infrastructure]] platforms, risk assessment encompasses operational risks (hardware failure, availability loss), security risks (breach, data exfiltration), and compliance risks (regulatory penalties, liability). The interdependencies between these risk categories mean that risk assessment must model system dynamics holistically rather than in isolation.

Continuous risk assessment processes monitor systems for emerging vulnerabilities, changing threat landscapes, and degradation patterns. Integration with [[Predictive Maintenance]] systems enables proactive risk mitigation before failures occur. In mission-critical environments, risk assessment feeds directly into resilience design, redundancy specifications, and [[Control Theory|control mechanisms]] that maintain safety boundaries even under adverse conditions.
