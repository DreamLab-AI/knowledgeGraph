### Technical Details
Key characteristics include:
- **Voting Power**: Proportional to token holdings, enabling stakeholder influence
- **Smart Contract Integration**: Automated governance through on-chain voting contracts
- **Delegation**: Token holders can assign voting power to trusted representatives
- **Treasury Funding**: DAOs funded by token issuance with holders controlling fund allocation

### Token Distribution Patterns
As of 2024-2025, governance token holdings represent:
- **Native Tokens**: 67.3% of DAO treasuries
- **Stablecoins**: 18.2% for liquidity and risk management
- **Other Crypto**: 11.7% for diversification
- **Traditional Assets**: Only 2.8% in non-crypto assets

### Governance Models
- **Token-Based**: Pure voting power from token ownership (most common)
- **Reputation-Based**: Voting weight from contributions and activity
- **Hybrid**: Combination of token holdings and reputation metrics
- **Delegated**: Representatives vote on behalf of token holders

### Major Examples
- **UNI (Uniswap)**: One of largest DAO treasuries, though less than 6% of holders have voted
- **DYDX**: Community treasury management through governance token
- **OP (Optimism)**: Token House for protocol upgrades, treasury, and oversight

### Challenges
Average voter participation across DAOs is approximately 17%, highlighting engagement challenges. Token concentration can lead to plutocratic governance where large holders dominate decisions.
