Propellant is material carried or acquired by a vehicle and expelled to produce thrust. In a chemical rocket it also stores or participates in energy release. Cold-gas systems discharge pressurised fluid, while electric thrusters use electrical or magnetic fields to accelerate reaction mass.[^1][^3] “Fuel” names a combustible or energy-bearing constituent and is therefore not a synonym for every propellant.

## Propulsion roles

A monopropellant releases energy through catalytic or thermal decomposition. A bipropellant keeps fuel and oxidiser separate until injection; its mixture ratio is oxidiser mass flow divided by fuel mass flow.[^2] Solid motors combine fuel, oxidiser and binder in a cast grain. Cold-gas thrusters expand stored gas without combustion. Electric propulsion may use rare gas, vapourised metal or another feedstock and derives acceleration energy from the spacecraft power system.

These categories determine ignition, throttle and restart behaviour. Many liquid systems can be shut down and some can restart; a conventional solid grain generally cannot be stopped after ignition.[^1] A propellant name alone does not establish the complete behaviour because catalyst, injector, chamber, feed pressure and control architecture matter.

## Selection properties

Useful selection data extend beyond specific impulse:

- density and required tank volume;
- freezing, melting and boiling behaviour across storage and feed temperatures;
- vapour pressure, ullage state and pump or regulator inlet margin;
- surface tension, wetting and viscosity for acquisition and flow;
- chemical stability, decomposition and compatibility with tanks, seals, valves, welds and catalysts;
- ignition, flammability, detonation and adiabatic-compression hazards;
- toxicity, contamination, loading infrastructure and disposal;
- delivered impulse, thrust, minimum impulse bit, life and residual mass.[^2]

A dense storable fluid may reduce tank and thermal-control mass even when its exhaust performance is lower. A high-performance cryogen may need larger insulation, refrigeration or vent capacity. System comparison therefore includes tankage, pressurant, feed and thermal hardware rather than ranking propellant chemistry by `Isp` alone.

## Storage and delivery

Tank load is divided between liquid or solid propellant, gas or ullage and internal hardware. Temperature changes density and vapour pressure. ECSS requires chemical stability assessment and control of feed-system temperature; its examples include hydrazine freezing, xenon liquefaction and flow or ignition hazards during priming.[^2]

Low gravity removes ordinary bottom-of-tank settling. Liquid can occupy walls and leave gas near an outlet, while burns and attitude manoeuvres cause slosh and reorientation. Propellant-management devices use vanes, sponges or screen channels to separate phases and deliver vapour-free liquid by capillary forces.[^4] Settling thrust, positive-expulsion devices or diaphragms provide other approaches. Ground tests must justify how gravity, wetting and scale translate to flight.

Loaded mass is not all usable. Propellant can remain trapped, thermally stratified, vaporised, below required inlet pressure or paired with no remaining quantity of the other bipropellant. ECSS distinguishes loaded, ejected and residual mass, and requires their variation to be justified.[^2] Mission budgets should show deterministic unusable quantity, statistical gauging and performance uncertainty, operational reserve and disposal allocation separately.

## Cryogenic propellant

NASA groups liquid hydrogen, oxygen and methane within cryogenic fluid management, which covers storage, transfer and measurement.[^5] Heat entering a tank vaporises fluid and raises pressure. Venting controls pressure but loses propellant; mixing or active cooling can reduce stratification and boil-off.[^6] Long-duration storage requires insulation, thermal control, pressure management, liquid acquisition, transfer, chill-down and gauging to work together.

Evidence must follow fluid, scale and environment. NASA's ZBOT work uses small-scale microgravity experiments and simulant fluid to validate models of self-pressurisation and mixing.[^6] Such tests establish physical evidence within their configuration; they do not alone qualify mission-scale hydrogen, oxygen or methane tanks.

## Hazard, compatibility and “green” claims

Hydrazine is storable and widely used in spacecraft thrusters, but UKHSA documents inhalation, ingestion and skin hazards, organ effects and an IARC classification as possibly carcinogenic to humans.[^7] Handling burden therefore affects facility design, protective equipment, transport, loading schedule and cost, not just environmental messaging.

“Green propellant” is a comparative programme label. It can mean lower acute toxicity, simpler handling or less costly ground infrastructure than hydrazine. It does not mean harmless, non-corrosive or environmentally neutral. ESA's UK activity with European Astrotech tested candidate propellants against materials and welds to identify compatibility and modification needs.[^8]

ESA's peroxide-kerosene thruster activity tested catalysts, injectors, pulse and steady modes, and material compatibility. The recorded configuration closed at TRL 3 to 4 after sea-level experimental work.[^10] That supports a development result under stated conditions, not vacuum flight performance or generic safety for concentrated peroxide systems.

The UK Space Agency records government backing for a Westcott spacecraft propulsion test facility developed with ESA, RAL Space and Nammo UK.[^9] Facility availability supports UK development and qualification work. It does not supply performance or maturity evidence for every propellant or engine tested there.

## Reporting and uncertainty

Propellant data should identify composition and grade, temperature, pressure, density reference, batch and ageing state. Performance reports need mixture ratio, flow boundary, residual definition and reference environment. Compatibility, stability, long-duration storage and microgravity acquisition require separate evidence. Uncertainty in mass loading, gauging, flow, mixture ratio and residuals propagates into delivered impulse and delta-v; an unexplained percentage margin cannot reveal which mechanism controls the budget.
