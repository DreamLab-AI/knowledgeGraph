Seasonal ice is sea ice that has formed over a single freezing season.[^1][^2] NSIDC uses the term for sea ice that melts away and reforms annually, and notes that almost all Antarctic sea ice is of this kind.[^2][^1]

First-year ice is the related age class: NSIDC states that first-year ice has existed only since the end of the previous summer melt season, whereas multiyear ice has survived at least one summer melt season.[^3] NASA Earth Observatory adds that first-year ice is thinner, saltier and more prone to melt in the subsequent summer than multiyear ice.[^4][^3]

SWEET gives a thickness range of 0.3 to 2 metres in a comment on the class.[^1] NSIDC gives a lower bound for first-year ice of 30 centimetres.[^3] Whether seasonal ice and first-year ice are strictly the same class is not stated in the retained sources, and no upper thickness limit appears other than the SWEET comment.
