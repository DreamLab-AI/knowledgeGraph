A spacecraft is a device, crewed or uncrewed, designed to be placed into orbit about the Earth or onto a trajectory to another celestial body.[^1]

In flight-project usage a spacecraft is also called a flight system, to distinguish it from the ground system. A spacecraft may be organised into subsystems such as attitude control, and a subsystem into assemblies such as reaction wheels.[^2] The structure subsystem provides overall mechanical integrity.[^2]

Individual spacecraft can differ greatly in design and in how they solve similar problems, and not all classes carry the same subsystems. The JPL chapter concentrates on flyby and orbiter class spacecraft and treats simpler classes as largely covered by that discussion.[^2]

Ontology definitions are broad and do not separate launch vehicles, probes or crewed vehicles. The JPL source also notes that system and subsystem are often used arbitrarily, so a given project's terms may not match this hierarchy.[^1][^2]
