A prime contractor is the organisation that, in the sources used here, receives work from other suppliers and integrates it into a system delivered to a customer.[^1][^2]

In ESA spacecraft development, industry across Europe contributes elements that are received and integrated by the prime contractor, which then takes part in the system-level electrical and software testing of the flight model.[^1] A UK Government note on space supply chain risk describes a long reliance on Tier 1 suppliers and primes to manage the supply chain for the buyer, while the buyer keeps the delivery risk.[^2]

ESA also separates prime contractors from sub-contractors when it presents supplier awards, and UK Space Agency reporting treats growth of smaller firms into prime contractors as an unresolved difficulty for the UK sector.[^3][^4] These sources use the term in context and do not give a legal or contractual definition.

This term is sometimes confused with a system integrator or with any large company. In the sources used here, not every prime performs integration, nor is every integrator a prime, so the roles are treated as related rather than identical.[^1][^3]
