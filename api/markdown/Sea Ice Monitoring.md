Sea ice monitoring is the satellite observation of the extent, concentration and age of frozen sea water in polar regions, mainly by microwave sensors.[^1][^2]

NSIDC describes passive microwave instruments as a useful method because they can retrieve data through most clouds and darkness, and the data allow scientists to follow year-to-year variation and trends in sea ice cover.[^1] First-year and multiyear ice have different electromagnetic properties that satellite sensors can detect, which allows the two to be distinguished.[^1]

NASA describes a continuous, nearly complete satellite record of sea ice cover from 1979, built from the Scanning Multichannel Microwave Radiometer, then the Special Sensor Microwave/Imager and its successor on defence meteorological satellites.[^2] Each September the satellite record is used to identify the minimum extent, the smallest area reached before refreezing begins.[^3]

Extent and concentration are different from thickness. ESA presents CryoSat's radar altimeter as an instrument for floating ice in polar oceans, but the page does not describe how altimeter measurements are converted to thickness, so this page does not state a thickness accuracy.[^4] The minimum extent is an annual summary of the record, not a separate monitoring technique.[^3]
