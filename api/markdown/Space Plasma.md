Space plasma is ionised gas of electrically charged particles found in the space environment of the Sun and planets, including the Sun itself, the solar wind and planetary magnetospheres and ionospheres.[^1][^2]

NASA describes the Sun as made of super-hot ionised gas called plasma. The solar wind that leaves it consists mainly of protons and electrons in a plasma, with the solar magnetic field embedded in the flow and carried outward with it.[^1][^3]

Charged gas interacts with magnetic fields. NASA explains that the motion of the Sun's plasma can twist, turn and tangle magnetic field lines, and Hathaway describes the interior field and plasma as moving together.[^4][^5]

Near Earth, SWPC describes geomagnetic storms as arising when energy transfers efficiently from the solar wind into the magnetosphere, changing currents, plasmas and fields there. NASA also describes the ionosphere, thermosphere and mesosphere region as where neutral atmosphere transitions into the ionised plasma of space. These sources are explanatory pages and do not give formal plasma parameters or a technical definition.[^6][^1]
