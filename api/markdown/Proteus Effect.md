  #### Key Characteristics
  - **Behavioral Conformity**: Actions match avatar stereotypes
  - **Unconscious Process**: Users unaware of influence
  - **Context-Dependent**: Occurs in virtual environments
  - **Appearance-Driven**: Triggered by visual avatar traits
  - **Bidirectional**: Can persist into physical world

  ## Theoretical Foundation

  ### Self-Perception Theory
  - Individuals infer attitudes from observed behaviors
  - Avatar appearance influences self-observation
  - Changed behavior leads to changed self-concept

  ### Stereotype Activation
  - Cultural stereotypes associated with physical traits
  - Avatar appearance primes associated behaviors
  - Automatic and implicit cognitive process

  ### Embodied Cognition
  - Physical form influences cognitive processes
  - Virtual embodiment extends to digital representations
  - Sensorimotor feedback loop in virtual space

  ## Empirical Findings

  ### Height Effects
  - **Taller Avatars**: More confident negotiation, aggressive bargaining
  - **Shorter Avatars**: More accommodating, less assertive

  ### Attractiveness Effects
  - **Attractive Avatars**: More self-disclosure, intimate conversations
  - **Unattractive Avatars**: More reserved social interactions

  ### Age Effects
  - **Older Avatars**: More conservative decisions, long-term thinking
  - **Younger Avatars**: Risk-taking behavior, impulsivity

  ### Race/Ethnicity Effects
  - **Different-race Avatars**: Reduced implicit bias, increased empathy
  - **Stereotype Threat**: Conformity to negative stereotypes

  ## Definition

  Behavioral and attitudinal conformity phenomenon where individuals unconsciously adopt behaviors, attitudes, and self-perceptions that align with the stereotypical expectations of their digital avatar's visual characteristics within virtual environments.
