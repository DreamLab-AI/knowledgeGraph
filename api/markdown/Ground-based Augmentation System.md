A Ground-based Augmentation System (GBAS) is an airport-local aviation system that broadcasts GNSS corrections, integrity information and approach data to suitably equipped aircraft.[^1][^4]

At a GBAS facility, surveyed reference receivers compare satellite measurements with expected values. Ground processing monitors the signals and broadcasts correction and integrity data over a VHF Data Broadcast link, together with approach-path information. The aircraft uses the broadcast data and checks the resulting solution against the service’s alert limits. This safety role depends on monitoring, approved ground and airborne equipment, and operational procedures; a local differential receiver pair is not by itself GBAS.[^1][^4]

FAA descriptions of US installations give a typical architecture, including multiple reference antennas and a VDB transmitter. Their equipment details and service classifications describe the US implementation and should not be read as universal configuration requirements or evidence of service in another country.[^1][^2]

GBAS Approach Service Types describe defined capabilities, but a procedure and the aircraft operation also need the relevant approval. In the UK, the CAA’s 2024 Airspace Modernisation Strategy discusses enhanced and dual-frequency, multi-constellation GBAS capability and development of CAT II/III procedures as future deployment work. That strategy is planning evidence; it does not establish that a specific UK station, GLS approach or minima are currently in service.[^1][^3]
