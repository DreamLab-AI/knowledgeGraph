Space science is the study of the universe beyond Earth's surface, including the Sun, the planets and the wider cosmos, carried out largely through instruments on spacecraft.[^1][^2]

NASA describes its own science programme as seeking to discover the secrets of the universe, to search for life elsewhere and to protect and improve life on Earth and in space.[^1] Its science missions operate around the Earth, the Sun, the Moon, Mars and other Solar System destinations, and include spacecraft that observe further into the universe.[^2]

The NASA Science Mission Directorate is organised into divisions, and the heliophysics division is described as studying the Sun and its influence across the solar system.[^2] These divisions are administrative units. The sources opened here do not set out a complete list of subfields, so the divisions should not be read as a full partition of the discipline.

Both sources describe one agency's programme. They do not supply a formal definition of space science that other agencies or academic departments would necessarily share, and the boundary with Earth observation, space medicine or engineering is not drawn by them.[^1][^2]

[^2]: NASA, “About Us - NASA Science”, Science Mission Directorate, updated December 2025. https://science.nasa.gov/about-us/
