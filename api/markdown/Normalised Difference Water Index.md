The Normalised Difference Water Index names two different normalised-difference indices, and this page bounds the open-water form built from green and near-infrared reflectance.[^1][^2]

Two distinct indices of this name were independently introduced in 1996 by Gao and McFeeters, and the open-water form of McFeeters is the difference between green and near-infrared.[^1] Water surfaces reflect more strongly in the green band than in others, whereas vegetation and soil reflect more in the near-infrared band, so this form gives positive values for water and negative values for soil or vegetation.[^1] Sentinel Hub states that the green and near-infrared pair highlights water bodies, and warns that the index is sensitive to built-up land and can over-estimate water bodies.[^2]

Naming is unstable: Sentinel Hub notes that the name is often used synonymously with the moisture index NDMI, and separates the two on its repository by pairing near-infrared with shortwave infrared for NDMI and green with near-infrared for NDWI.[^2] A modified index, MNDWI, substitutes the shortwave-infrared band at approximately 1600 nm for the near-infrared band to improve detection in urban settings.[^1]

Thresholds are not settled in the retained sources: Sentinel Hub says values greater than 0.5 usually correspond to water bodies, while the Scientific Data description treats positive values as water.[^2][^1] A reader should therefore name the variant and the threshold used in any derived map.
