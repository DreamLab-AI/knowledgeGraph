Space systems engineering is the application of systems engineering to space missions, treating a spacecraft, its ground support and the associated processes as one system designed to meet requirements within constraints.[^1][^2]

According to the NASA handbook, systems engineering is the art and science of developing an operable system capable of meeting requirements within often opposed constraints.[^2] This work involves trade-offs and compromises, taking a broad crosscutting view of the system rather than a single-discipline view.[^2] A system is defined as a combination of elements that function together to produce a needed capability, including hardware, software, equipment, facilities, personnel, processes and procedures.[^2]

Technical work is organised around a programme and project life cycle that runs from concept studies through design, assembly, integration and test, and operations to closeout.[^2] Revision 2 reflects the use of model-based systems engineering and updates to the NASA procedural requirements document NPR 7123.1.[^1]

This is NASA guidance for NASA programmes, not a universal standard. These sources do not describe how other space agencies or commercial operators structure the same work.[^1][^2]
