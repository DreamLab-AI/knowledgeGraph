Tidal heating is the warming of a body's interior by the gravitational pull of a nearby larger body, as NASA describes for Jupiter's moons Europa and Io.[^1][^2]

NASA states that tidal heating from Jupiter maintains the liquid state of Europa's ocean and could also create partially melted pockets, or lakes, in the outer shell. The hedged wording means the source does not establish how much melt exists.[^1]

For Io, NASA describes the moon as the most volcanically active world in the solar system and as caught in a tug-of-war between Jupiter's gravity and the timed pulls of two neighbouring moons. The retained page links the squeezing to the heat behind the volcanism but gives no heat budget that is used here.[^2]

Heating is one input to whether an ocean world could be habitable, alongside other indicators such as organic molecules, and NASA frames habitability as a question under study.[^1][^3] The sources do not show that heating alone makes a body habitable.

[^2]: NASA, “Io”, last updated 25 June 2025. https://science.nasa.gov/jupiter/jupiter-moons/io/
