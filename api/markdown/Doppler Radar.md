Doppler radar is a radar instrument that measures the motion of targets, used in meteorology to detect precipitation and wind.[^1]

NEXRAD is a network of S-band Doppler weather radars run by the United States National Weather Service, the Federal Aviation Administration and the Air Force, and its data are processed to map precipitation patterns and movement.[^1] Its archived products include reflectivity-based and velocity-based measures, among them a maximum-velocity attribute in storm tables.[^1]

Velocity measurement involves a trade-off. Scan strategies called volume coverage patterns choose between sensitivity to weak echoes and a higher unambiguous velocity, and some patterns use multiple pulse repetition frequencies to reduce range and velocity aliasing, according to the NCEI page.[^1]

One ground-based weather network is described by the source. It does not derive the Doppler relation, and it does not establish how spaceborne radars use Doppler processing, so this page makes no claim about satellite radar beyond that scope.
