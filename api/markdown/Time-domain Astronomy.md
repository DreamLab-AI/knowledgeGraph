Time-domain astronomy is the study of how astronomical sources change with time, carried out through repeated observations of large areas of sky to find variable and transient phenomena.[^1][^2]

A Zwicky Transient Facility science paper describes the past decade as one in which time-domain astronomy expanded, driven by new instruments and facilities dedicated to repeated observation of large areas of sky.[^1] That facility is an optical time-domain survey using the Palomar 48-inch Schmidt telescope, and the programme sends alerts generated by its surveys to brokers in real time.[^1][^2]

LSST, the Large Synoptic Survey Telescope, was designed as a wide-field ground-based system to obtain repeated images of the sky visible from Cerro Pachón in northern Chile. Its design paper lists exploring the transient optical sky among its main science themes.[^3] That paper describes a design at an early stage, and the sources opened here do not establish current operating parameters.[^3]

Time-domain astronomy is a method defined by repeated observation of the sky, not a single wavelength band or object class, and the sources describe optical surveys only. Related multi-wavelength and multi-messenger work is outside what these sources support.[^1][^2][^3]
