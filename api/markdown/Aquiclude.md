An aquiclude is a hydrogeologic unit that can hold water in its pores but does not pass it on at rates useful to a well or spring.[^1][^2] The British Geological Survey glossary gives the same two-part test of storage without significant transmission.[^2]

The USGS glossary treats confining unit as the preferred general term that replaces aquitard, aquifuge and aquiclude, so the three names grade the same idea rather than naming separately measurable classes.[^1] Within SWEET the class is a direct subclass of hydrosphere feature and carries no further axiom, so the ontology itself adds no definition.[^3]

None of the retained sources establishes a numerical permeability or conductivity threshold that separates an aquiclude from an aquitard, and they do not link the term to any satellite observable.
