GNSS positioning accuracy is the degree of conformance of an estimated position with the true position, expressed as a statistical measure of solution error.[^1]

Navipedia lists accuracy as one of four performance parameters, alongside availability, continuity and integrity, drawn from the civil aviation concept of required navigation performance. Because accuracy is statistical, the source says a statement of it is meaningless without a statement of the uncertainty that applies. A figure therefore needs its percentile or confidence level and the conditions under which it holds.[^1]

Accuracy is separate from integrity. The source notes that a large and unwarned solution error can occur without violating an accuracy specification, which is why aviation defines integrity as the trust that can be placed in the information supplied. Availability, the percentage of time the service is usable, is also a distinct parameter, so a system can be accurate when available yet unavailable too often.[^1]

Accuracy requirements are set at specification stage and kept as operational requirements against which measured performance is verified. The cited article points to Galileo and GPS requirement documents but states no current values, and it dates from 2011. It does not define how accuracy differs from precision or how to compute a given error statistic.[^1]

[^1]: ESA Navipedia, “GNSS Performances”, 2011. https://gssc.esa.int/navipedia/index.php/GNSS_Performances
