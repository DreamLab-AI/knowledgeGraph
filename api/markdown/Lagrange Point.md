A Lagrange point is an equilibrium location in the rotating frame of a specified two-primary system, where the combined gravitational and frame-rotation terms permit a massless test body to remain fixed in the ideal model.[^1345][^1347]

## Geometry and frame

There are five points, labelled L1 to L5, for each ideal circular restricted three-body system. L1, L2 and L3 lie on the line through the primaries. L4 and L5 complete equilateral triangles with them. Their coordinates depend on the selected bodies, mass ratio, origin, axes and normalisation; “L2” alone is not enough to identify a position.[^1346][^1347]

This fixed-point description applies only in a frame rotating with the primaries. In an inertial frame, a spacecraft near Sun-Earth L2 travels around the Sun while remaining near Earth’s orbital distance and geometry. The equilibrium is not a physical object that acts as a central body. Describing a spacecraft as orbiting a Lagrange point means that its path loops around that location in the selected rotating frame.[^1347][^1349][^1350]

## Stability is conditional

L1, L2 and L3 are unstable equilibria in the circular restricted model. Small errors can grow along unstable modes, although nearby periodic or quasi-periodic trajectories and stable manifolds also exist. This structured dynamics is why mission design can use families of paths near the points even though exact station at the equilibrium is not inherently stable.[^1346][^1349]

L4 and L5 have conditional linear stability. In the classical model, the secondary’s mass fraction must be below about 0.03852, equivalent to a larger-to-smaller mass ratio above about 24.96. The Sun-Earth and Earth-Moon pairs satisfy that condition. It is a result for the ideal circular restricted model, not a claim that all real perturbations vanish or that the points attract arbitrary objects.[^1348]

## Spacecraft use and status

Missions generally target an orbit around L1 or L2 rather than the equilibrium itself. The orbit is selected to meet viewing, thermal, eclipse, communication, launch and maintenance constraints. JWST follows a halo orbit about Sun-Earth L2. ESA describes Gaia’s former orbit there as Lissajous; Gaia left L2 and was passivated in March 2025, so it should not be listed as a current L2 spacecraft.[^1350][^1352][^1353][^1370]

Treat the ideal points as design references. Higher-fidelity models include eccentric motion and additional bodies, so the exact equilibria disappear while useful nearby trajectory structures remain approximately. State the model, epoch and frame when comparing computed point positions or orbit families; flight products require ephemeris-based propagation and uncertainty assessment.[^1346]
