Spaceflight fluid shift is the redistribution of body fluid toward the head that occurs in microgravity, described in one review as a head-ward body fluid shift under chronic exposure.[^1]

That review connects fluid redistribution with rapid adaptive changes that are appropriate in space but contribute to deconditioning and orthostatic hypotension on return to Earth.[^1] Such shifts act as drivers of other adaptations rather than self-contained conditions.

A review of spaceflight associated neuro-ocular syndrome states that ocular and imaging changes may result from raised intracranial pressure caused by cephalad fluid shifts during long-duration flight. It also says that indirect radiographic evidence of raised pressure does not settle the competing explanation of a cephalad shift against an orbital one, so the mechanism is not established.[^2]

NASA describes compression cuffs worn on the thighs that keep blood in the lower extremities to counteract these fluid shifts.[^3] That source is a summary and does not give measured effectiveness.

[^2]: npj Microgravity, “Spaceflight associated neuro-ocular syndrome (SANS) and the neuro-ophthalmologic effects of microgravity: a review and an update”. https://www.nature.com/articles/s41526-020-0097-9
