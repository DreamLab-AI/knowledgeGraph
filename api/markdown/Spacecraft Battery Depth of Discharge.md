Spacecraft battery depth of discharge is the amount of charge removed on a discharge, expressed as a percentage of the battery's rated capacity.[^1]

The NASA nickel-hydrogen handbook states the measure in ampere-hours against rated capacity, which is fixed by the manufacturer over the cell's life. Using rated rather than measured capacity means a stated percentage removes the same ampere-hours at the beginning and end of life, even though measured capacity changes as the cell ages.[^1]

NASA's small-spacecraft survey lists depth of discharge with temperature, charge and discharge rate and storage conditions as variables that influence battery ageing. It concludes that life testing under mission-specific conditions is needed to confirm a battery will meet its life requirement.[^2]

Other documents may define the percentage against a different capacity basis, so the convention should be stated when a value is quoted. The sources do not give a recommended depth of discharge, and a percentage means little without the orbit, temperature and cell chemistry.[^2][^1]
