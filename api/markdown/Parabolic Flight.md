Parabolic flight is flight within the atmosphere in which an aircraft describes a series of parabolic arcs lasting tens of seconds to simulate microgravity.[^1]

ESA describes a refitted aircraft that flies up and down at 45 degree angles, with passengers and experiments experiencing around 20 seconds of microgravity at the top of each curve and increased gravity, up to 2 g, before and after.[^2]

ESA also states that the aircraft can fly arcs that simulate lunar or martian gravity levels by adjusting the angle of attack, and that the flights suit short-duration investigations, instrument validation and astronaut training. It presents them as preparation for longer-duration platforms such as sounding rockets or the International Space Station.[^2]

Parabolic flight is an analogue: periods are short and are bracketed by higher gravity, so it does not reproduce sustained weightlessness. Physiology literature treats microgravity or partial gravity as one of several simultaneous stressors, which parabolic flights isolate only briefly.[^2][^3]
