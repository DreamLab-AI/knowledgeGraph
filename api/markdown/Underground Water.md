Underground water is subsurface water in both the unsaturated and saturated zones.[^1][^2] The USGS glossary uses the narrower term ground water for the broadest sense of all subsurface water, and more commonly for the part in the saturated zone.[^1]

In the unsaturated zone the voids contain both air and water, and the water there cannot be pumped by wells because it is held too tightly by capillary forces.[^2] The British Geological Survey adds that water in the unsaturated zone may be under pressure less than that of the atmosphere.[^3] In the saturated zone the voids are completely filled with water.[^2]

SWEET makes underground water a subclass of groundwater and restricts it to lie inside the vadose zone, which is narrower than the USGS reading and excludes the saturated zone.[^4] The retained sources do not reconcile the two usages, and none describes how underground water is observed from space.

[^2]: USGS, “Concepts of ground water, water table, and flow systems”. https://pubs.usgs.gov/circ/circ1139/htdocs/boxa.htm
[^3]: British Geological Survey, “OR/15/028 Glossary”. https://earthwise.bgs.ac.uk/index.php/OR/15/028_Glossary
