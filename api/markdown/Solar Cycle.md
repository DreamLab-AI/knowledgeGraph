Sunspot number shows an approximately 11-year repetition known as the solar cycle.[^1] NOAA calls the peak of sunspot activity solar maximum and the lull solar minimum. A new cycle is considered to have begun when sunspot groups emerge at higher latitudes with the leading-spot magnetic polarities opposite to those of the previous cycle, which means the start of a cycle is recognised from observed groups rather than predicted exactly.[^1]

NASA describes the same rise and fall as the Sun's activity cycle, and notes that sunspots rise and fall in number with it.[^2] NASA also reports that the Sun's magnetic poles swap roughly every 11 years, and that solar maximum is a time of greatly increased solar storm activity.[^3]

Sources give the period as approximate. They do not establish that individual cycles share the same length or strength, and they do not make a particular flare or storm certain at any stage of the cycle.[^1][^3]
