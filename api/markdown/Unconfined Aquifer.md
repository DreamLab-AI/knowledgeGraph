An unconfined aquifer is an aquifer whose upper boundary is the water table, so that its water is at atmospheric pressure at that surface.[^1][^2]

The Groundwater Project defines it as an aquifer with the water table as the upper boundary, and gives the alternative name water-table aquifer.[^1] The British Geological Survey glossary describes it as a partially saturated aquifer which contains a water table which is free to fluctuate vertically under atmospheric pressure in response to discharge or recharge.[^2] USGS Circular 1217 adds that hydraulic heads in unconfined aquifers fluctuate freely in response to changes in recharge and discharge.[^3]

SWEET treats the class as equivalent to water table aquifer and as a subclass of aquifer with an unconfined spatial configuration.[^4] That matches the alias recorded for this page.

USGS notes that many aquifers are intermediate between being completely unconfined or confined, and the retained sources give no numerical test that separates the two.[^3] They describe the class from wells and water levels, not from Earth observation data.
