Astronomy is the observational study of the universe and the objects in it, including their positions, motions and brightness.[^1][^2]

NASA describes its astrophysics programme as studying how the universe began and evolved and how it works, using observatories that cover wavelengths from infrared through visible light to X-rays and beyond. Active galactic nuclei, for instance, are detected as light across the electromagnetic spectrum from telescopes in space and on the ground.[^1][^3]

Astrometry is one branch of observation. ESA's Gaia mission charted positions, distances, movements and changes in brightness for a very large number of stars and other objects, and its photometry and spectra gave physical information about them as well.[^2][^4]

Sources do not draw a formal boundary between astronomy and astrophysics. Programme descriptions appear here rather than discipline definitions, so this page keeps to what is observed and measured.[^1]
