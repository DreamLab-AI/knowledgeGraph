An Earth observation Level 2 data product holds derived geophysical variables at the same resolution and location as its Level 1 source data.[^1][^2]

Derivation is algorithmic: NOAA describes Level 1b data being used in scientific algorithms to derive geophysical parameters, which are termed Level 2 data, and the Metrologia review says the defining characteristic of L2 products is that retrieved geophysical variables are presented on the L1 image co-ordinates.[^3][^4] CEOS gives sea surface temperature and leaf area index as examples of such parameters.[^2]

Because a Level 2 product keeps the sampling of the source measurements, the locations of observations do not exactly repeat between orbits and data volumes may be large.[^4] Level 3 products are generated for applications that tolerate lower spatiotemporal sampling, so Level 2 is the form to use where maximum spatial resolution is needed.[^4]

NASA notes that most Earth Observing System instruments have standard data products at Level 2 and Level 3.[^1] The retained sources do not establish the accuracy or uncertainty of any particular Level 2 product, and the Metrologia review warns that sub-categories such as L2P are not fully standardised across agencies and projects, so a product's own documentation governs its content.[^4]
