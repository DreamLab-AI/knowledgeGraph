Marine remote sensing is remote sensing applied to oceans and coasts, in which information about the sea is obtained from a distance, typically from aircraft or satellites.[^1][^2]

NOAA CoastWatch describes its purpose as helping people access and use global and regional satellite data for ocean and coastal applications. ESA's Sentinel-3 mission measures the oceans, land, ice and atmosphere systematically and provides near-real-time information for ocean and weather forecasting.[^2][^3] These sources describe services and one mission, so they do not define the field's boundaries.

Radar sees the sea through its surface roughness. NASA states that calm water appears black because the radar pulse reflects away from the spacecraft, and that wind-roughened water can backscatter brightly when the waves are close in size to the radar wavelength.[^4] Lidar contributes in a different way: NOAA says bathymetric lidar uses water-penetrating green light to measure seafloor and riverbed elevations.[^5]

Sensors that record reflected sunlight, radar and lasers answer different questions, and each source cited describes one of them. They do not give detection limits, depth ranges or accuracy for any marine product.[^1][^4][^5]
