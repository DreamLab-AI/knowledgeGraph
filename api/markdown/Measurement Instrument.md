A measurement instrument is a device used for making measurements, alone or in conjunction with one or more supplementary devices.[^1]

Metrology vocabulary distinguishes an indicating measuring instrument from a material measure, and treats an instrument that can be used alone as a measuring system.[^1] Those categories come from general metrology, and the vocabulary entry does not mention Earth observation.

Earth observation instruments are carried on platforms and are described as passive or active, depending on whether they detect naturally emitted energy or record the return of energy they emit.[^2] A specific instrument is usually characterised by its sensing geometry and spectral coverage. The Landsat 8 Operational Land Imager, for example, is described as collecting nine spectral bands at 30 metres and one panchromatic band at 15 metres.[^3] Scanning imagers can be built with a rotating mirror or with a linear detector array.[^4]

Landsat figures belong to that one instrument and are not general properties of measurement instruments. Sources do not set out how an instrument is calibrated or how its measurement uncertainty is stated.
