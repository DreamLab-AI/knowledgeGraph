Panchromatic imaging records a broad range of visible light in a single band, giving a grey-scale image.[^1]

On Landsat, NASA describes the panchromatic band as covering a broad range of visible light at higher spatial resolution than the other reflective bands on that instrument.[^1] NASA lists the Operational Land Imager as having a panchromatic band alongside multispectral bands at coarser resolution.[^2] The stated resolutions are specific to Landsat and should not be generalised to other missions.

Pansharpening combines the high-resolution grey-scale panchromatic band with a lower-resolution multispectral colour composite to produce a single high-resolution colour image.[^1] The source describes one technique on one page, and other methods exist that it does not cover.

The Canadian tutorial states that spatial resolution of passive sensors depends primarily on the instantaneous field of view.[^3] It does not tie panchromatic bands to that quantity, so the resolution advantage described above is not explained by these sources.
