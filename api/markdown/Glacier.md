A glacier is an accumulation of ice and snow that slowly flows over land under its own weight.[^1]

NSIDC describes alpine glaciers as frozen rivers of ice flowing down mountainsides, and notes that glaciers also exist on the fringes of ice sheets, which are ice masses on land extending more than 50,000 square kilometres.[^1]

The SWEET comment records that the definition of glacier is highly variable, notably over whether a mass of ice must currently show movement and how glaciers relate to ice sheets.[^2] Randolph Glacier Inventory outlines are a snapshot of glacier outlines and is not suitable for measuring glacier-by-glacier rates of area change.[^3]

Retained sources do not establish a minimum size or thickness for a glacier.
