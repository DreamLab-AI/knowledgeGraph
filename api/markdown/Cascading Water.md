Cascading water is groundwater that trickles or pours through cracks or perforations down the casing or uncased borehole above the water level in a well.[^1] It is a well-construction and well-measurement term, not a separate body of water in the ground.

The Iowa Department of Natural Resources warns that cascading water can drip down from higher in the well and give a false water-level reading.[^2] The University of Arizona guide says that when the water table drops below the well casing, flow into the well screen becomes turbulent as the cascading water mixes with air.[^3]

SWEET lists the class as a subclass of both groundwater and hydrosphere feature.[^4] The retained sources do not establish a flow rate or depth at which water counts as cascading, and none connects the term to satellite observation.
