A planetary ring is a band of small particles orbiting a planet, and NASA states that Saturn’s rings are made of billions of small chunks of ice and rock coated with dust.[^1][^2]

ESA describes the particles in Saturn’s rings as composed primarily of water ice and ranging from microns to metres in size.[^2] NASA gives an extent of up to 175,000 miles from the planet and a typical vertical height of about 30 feet in the main rings, so the system is very broad and very thin.[^1]

NASA notes that Saturn is not the only planet with rings, though none is as complex.[^1] The sources opened here describe Saturn only, so composition and structure of other ring systems are not established by them.
