A space probe is an unmanned spacecraft that is not designed to return to Earth.[^1]

NASA's educational page adds that much of what is known about the planets of the solar system came from probes sent into space.[^1] Robotic spacecraft in NASA's classification scheme are divided into eight broad classes by mission, which it describes as an arbitrary grouping: flyby, orbiter, atmospheric, lander, penetrator and rover spacecraft.[^2]

Atmospheric spacecraft, typically described as probes, are carried to their destination by another spacecraft and may have no propulsion or attitude control subsystems, needing only an electric power supply such as batteries and telecommunications equipment.[^2][^3] Such examples include the Galileo atmospheric probe, carried to Jupiter by the Galileo orbiter, and balloon packages designed to float and travel with the wind in a planetary atmosphere.[^2]

No retained source fixes a formal boundary for the term. NASA's StarChild page states a design intent and is not a technical definition, and the classification chapter groups craft by mission and does not define probe as a class of its own. Flyby and orbiter spacecraft carry fuller subsystem sets than the atmospheric case, so statements about missing subsystems should not be generalised to every probe.[^3]
