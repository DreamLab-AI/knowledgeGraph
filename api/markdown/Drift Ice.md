Drift ice is a floating mass of ice that is not attached to land or to land-fast ice and is moved by wind or currents.[^1][^2]

SWEET gives this definition and makes drift ice disjoint from fast ice.[^1] NSIDC notes that most sea ice is constantly moving and changing location, and that the wind blowing on the top surface of the ice produces a drag force that causes it to drift.[^2]

Terminology has shifted. SWEET records that before about 2014 drift ice was synonymous with the grades of pack ice, and that pack ice now refers to drift ice at or above a concentration of 7/10, although other usages remain common.[^1] A reader therefore needs to check which convention a given dataset follows.

Retained sources do not give drift speeds or a satellite method for retrieving ice motion, so the page bounds the term to its physical definition.[^1][^2]
