A stellar core is the central region of a star in which nuclear fusion releases the energy that supports the star.[^1][^2]

For the Sun, ESA describes the core as the place where the Sun generates its energy, with hydrogen nuclei fused to helium under great pressure and density, and NASA gives a core temperature of about fifteen million degrees Celsius, hot enough to sustain fusion.[^2][^3] Those figures are solar and differ for other stars.

A textbook chapter describes two ways of burning hydrogen, the proton-proton chain and the CNO cycle. It associates the first with a radiative core under a convective envelope and the second with a convective core under a radiative envelope.[^4] Lecture notes likewise divide a stellar interior into a nuclear burning core, a convective zone and a radiative zone.[^5] Which arrangement applies to a given star depends on its mass and temperature, and the sources do not give a single boundary.

In the solar interior, the core, radiative zone and convection zone are distinguished, making the core the innermost of these layers.[^3] SWEET classifies the stellar core as a kind of stellar interior associated with nuclear fusion, supporting only that classification.[^6]
