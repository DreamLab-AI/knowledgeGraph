Ground-based remote sensing is remote sensing in which the instrument is carried on the ground or on a ground-level support such as a vehicle, tower or tethered balloon, rather than on an aircraft or satellite.[^1][^2]

Natural Resources Canada defines remote sensing as acquiring information about a surface without contact, by recording reflected or emitted energy, and notes that it includes non-imaging sensors. The UCGIS body of knowledge lists hand-held cameras, cranes, ground vehicles, tethered balloons and towers as ground-based platforms. It says they typically give views from up to 50 metres above ground and are useful for low-altitude imagery with frequent coverage of dynamic phenomena.[^1][^2]

Fixed instruments form networks. NASA Goddard describes the Micro-Pulse Lidar Network as ground-based lidar systems co-located with sun and sky photometer sites of the Aerosol Robotic Network. It also lists satellite sensor calibration and model validation among the uses of its atmospheric profiling instruments.[^3]

None of the sources gives a formal definition of the term, so its boundary with in situ measurement and with airborne work is a matter of usage. The examples here are drawn from the cited pages and do not cover all ground-based sensing.[^1][^2][^3]
