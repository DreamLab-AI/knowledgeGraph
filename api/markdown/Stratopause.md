A stratopause is the upper boundary of the stratosphere, where the temperature rise through the layer ends.[^1][^2] UCAR names the tropopause as the lower boundary of the stratosphere and the stratopause as the upper boundary, and SWEET types the stratopause as a planetary boundary.[^1][^3]

The Royal Meteorological Society places the stratopause around 50 km, where ultraviolet absorption by ozone is greatest and temperatures reach a maximum.[^2] That source gives a range from -30 °C over the winter pole to +20 °C over the summer pole, depending on latitude and season.[^2] Above it, temperatures in the mesosphere decrease with height.[^2]

Retained sources give slightly different heights for the top of the stratosphere: UCAR and the Royal Meteorological Society give about 50 km, while the Met Office gives about 50 to 55 km, so the height of the stratopause is approximate.[^1][^4]
