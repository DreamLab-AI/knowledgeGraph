A digital terrain model is a gridded elevation model of the shape of the ground surface, excluding buildings and vegetation.[^1]

Ordnance Survey describes OS Terrain 5 as a mid-resolution digital terrain model representing the bare-earth surface of Great Britain. It is available at 5m post spacing with 5m interval contours and is produced from photogrammetry. These figures belong to that product and are not general properties of terrain models.[^1]

NASA describes bare-earth elevation models as showing elevation and topography without trees, buildings or other objects, and as a synthesis of several data sources. Its page uses the term DEM rather than DTM. The Copernicus DEM, by comparison, is described as a surface model that includes buildings, infrastructure and vegetation, which is a different product type.[^2][^3]

Vertical datum is not stated for the Ordnance Survey product, and the Ordnance Survey text opened here does not itself say that buildings are excluded. Whether a model is a terrain or surface model should be checked in the producer's documentation.[^1][^2][^3]
