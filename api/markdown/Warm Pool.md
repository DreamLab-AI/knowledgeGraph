A warm pool is an extensive region of the tropical ocean whose surface waters stay warmer than a threshold temperature, and the threshold depends on the source.[^1][^2] NOAA Climate.gov describes the Indo-Pacific Warm Pool as a region where sea surface temperatures remain above 28°C year-round, while NOAA AOML defines both warm pools as the region where sea surface temperature exceeds 28.5°C.[^2][^1]

NOAA AOML names two bodies, the Western Hemisphere Warm Pool and the Indo-Pacific Warm Pool, and says the first lies entirely north of the equator while the second straddles it.[^1] NASA says the waters of the Indo-Pacific pool are warmer than any other open ocean on Earth, and gives the temperature at its edges as 27°C.[^3]

Warm pools matter because warm sea surface temperature drives deep convection, so local precipitation follows the variation of the pool’s mean temperature and extent.[^1][^3] NOAA Climate.gov links expansion of the Indo-Pacific pool to changes in the Madden Julian Oscillation.[^2]

SWEET classes the term as a hydrosphere feature without a definition.[^4] The retained sources use three different thresholds and do not settle which is canonical.
