A Small Solar System Body is, in the IAU 2006 definition, any object orbiting the Sun that is not a planet, a dwarf planet or a satellite.[^1]

IAU definitions separate a planet and a dwarf planet by orbit around the Sun and hydrostatic equilibrium shape, and distinguish them by whether the orbital neighbourhood has been cleared. Remaining Sun-orbiting objects, except satellites, are grouped as Small Solar-System Bodies, so the category is defined by exclusion rather than by size or composition.[^1]

NASA describes asteroids, comets and meteoroids as chunks of rock, ice and metal left over from the formation of the solar system about 4.6 billion years ago.[^2] These are examples that NASA discusses; the NASA page does not itself use the IAU category name, so the mapping of every asteroid or comet to the class follows from the IAU wording.[^1][^2] NASA also reports surveying near-Earth objects to detect, track, catalogue and characterise them.[^3]

Sometimes the category is used loosely as a synonym for asteroid or comet. Retained sources do not give a size boundary, and the IAU wording alone does not settle how particular borderline objects are classed.[^1]
