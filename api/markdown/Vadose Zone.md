Vadose zone, also called the unsaturated zone, is the subsurface zone between the land surface and the water table.[^1][^2] SWEET records the two names as equivalent classes.[^3]

The USGS describes its voids as containing both air and water, and its upper part as the soil-water zone.[^4] The British Geological Survey says it includes the capillary fringe and may contain water under pressure less than that of the atmosphere.[^1] The USGS glossary defines the capillary fringe as the zone above the water table in which water is held by surface tension.[^2]

Water in this zone cannot be pumped by wells because capillary forces hold it too tightly.[^4] The retained sources do not give a thickness, and do not establish how the zone is monitored by satellite.
