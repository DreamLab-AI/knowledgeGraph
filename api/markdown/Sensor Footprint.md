A sensor footprint is the area on the Earth's surface that a sensor views or illuminates at one instant.[^1][^2]

For passive sensors the Natural Resources Canada tutorial describes the instantaneous field of view as the angular cone of visibility of the sensor. The ground area seen is found by multiplying that angle by the distance from the ground to the sensor. This area is called the resolution cell in the tutorial, which says it determines the sensor's maximum spatial resolution. If the field of view stays constant, spatial resolution varies from the image centre to the swath edge.[^1]

For imaging radar the instantaneous area illuminated by each pulse is called the antenna footprint. Its size in range and along-track directions is largely set by the wavelength, the antenna length and the distance from the sensor to the ground.[^2]

A footprint is not the swath, which is the strip imaged as the platform moves along its orbit,[^3] and it is not the pixel size, which the tutorial says should not be used interchangeably with spatial resolution.[^1] For the ERS wind scatterometer, ESA reports a swath 500 km wide made of overlapping 50 km resolution cells on a 25 km grid, which illustrates the difference between cell size and sampling interval for one instrument only.[^4] The terms footprint and resolution cell are used differently between the optical and radar sources, so values should not be compared across them.
