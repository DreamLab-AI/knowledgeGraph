Confined aquifer is an aquifer bounded above and below by confining units of distinctly lower permeability than the aquifer itself, so that its water is held under pressure.[^1][^2]

Western Oregon University adds that confined describes a condition in which the potentiometric surface is above the top of the aquifer, which it treats as synonymous with artesian.[^1] The Groundwater Project says water levels in tightly cased wells rise above the top of the aquifer, and that the surface to which water rises defines the potentiometric surface.[^2]

USGS Circular 1217 attributes the pressure to a geological body much less permeable than the aquifer, and USGS Circular 1186 notes that a confined aquifer stays fully saturated while pumping reduces head and pressure.[^3][^4] That response differs from an unconfined aquifer, where the water table itself falls.[^2][^4]

Many aquifers are intermediate between confined and unconfined, and the retained sources give no numerical threshold separating them. They describe confinement from well observations rather than from Earth observation data.[^3][^4]
