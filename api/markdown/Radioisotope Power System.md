A radioisotope power system is a space nuclear power system that produces electricity from the heat released by the natural radioactive decay of a radioisotope, most often plutonium-238.[^1][^2]

A radioisotope thermoelectric generator has a radioisotope heat source and solid-state thermocouples that convert decay heat into electricity, with no moving parts. NASA describes this as one type of radioisotope power system and lists such generators among spacecraft power generation options alongside photovoltaic arrays.[^3][^1]

NASA's fact sheet gives about 110 watts of electrical power at launch for the current Multi-Mission Radioisotope Thermoelectric Generator and describes useful excess heat that can also be supplied to a spacecraft. That figure is a launch value for one generator type, and the sources do not state a general output for the whole class.[^1]

Layered safety features are described in the same fact sheet, including the ceramic fuel form, metal cladding, graphite sleeves and an aeroshell, that are intended to limit release of nuclear material in accidents. These are statements of design intent by the operating agency and do not by themselves establish outcomes in every accident case.[^1]
