A crew-worn active dosimeter based on direct ion storage technology, the Crew Active Dosimeter is designed for use by spacecraft crewmembers.[^1]

NASA's Space Radiation Analysis Group at the Johnson Space Center investigated several compact, low power, real-time instruments for personal dosimetry, and the Crew Active Dosimeter was chosen as a replacement for the legacy crew passive radiation detectors.[^2] A workshop abstract says it was developed with Mirion Technologies to meet NASA design requirements for exploration missions outside low Earth orbit, and describes it as battery-operated and compact, with a display that tracks mission cumulative dose and dose rate.[^3]

These sources report a technology demonstration on the International Space Station in 2018 and implementation for ISS crew operations since 2020. According to the abstract, it is the designated personal dosimeter for Artemis 2+ missions, and units were delivered to support several Artemis I secondary payloads.[^2][^3]

A 2022 SRAG slide describes it as a crew monitor for use inside the vehicle only, worn continuously to capture crew-specific exposure.[^4] The sources reviewed give no measurement uncertainty or dose-response data, and the ISS and Artemis statements apply as at their 2022 and 2023 dates.[^2][^3][^4]
