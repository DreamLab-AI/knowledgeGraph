Thermosphere is the layer of the atmosphere above the mesosphere, in which temperature rises with increasing altitude.[^1][^2]

Met Office text places it above the mesosphere and notes that the idea of an atmosphere as we understand it is beginning to break down by that altitude.[^1] UCAR places it below the exosphere, calls the boundary between them the thermopause, and says solar activity strongly influences temperature in the layer.[^2] SWEET models the class as an atmosphere layer whose lower boundary is the mesopause and whose temperature gradient is a positive slope.[^3]

Only the Met Office gives a top height: it puts the thermopause between 500 and 1000 km depending on location, time of day and season, and warns that temperature in the usual sense is not very useful in air this thin.[^1] UCAR notes that the ionosphere overlaps with and shares the same space as the thermosphere, so the two terms describe different divisions of one region.[^2]

No retained source establishes a fixed altitude range for the layer or tie it to a particular Earth observation instrument.

[^2]: UCAR Center for Science Education, “The Thermosphere”. https://scied.ucar.edu/shortcontent/thermosphere-overview
[^3]: ESIP SWEET ontology (GitHub mirror), “SWEET realmAtmo.ttl”. https://raw.githubusercontent.com/ESIPFed/sweet/master/src/realmAtmo.ttl
