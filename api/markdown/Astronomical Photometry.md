Astronomical photometry is the measurement of the brightness of astronomical objects, reported for example as fluxes or magnitudes in defined wavelength bands.[^1][^2]

The Gaia mission offers a worked example. Its photometric observations were collected for all objects observed astrometrically, and they allow photometric time series to be built for variable objects. The sources describe this for Gaia only and do not set out a general definition.[^1][^2]

Reported brightness carries stated uncertainty. Gaia publishes standard errors for its integrated mean fluxes in the G, BP and RP bands as standard outputs of its photometric processing, and reports them in each data release.[^1]

Magnitudes are only comparable when the photometric system is stated. Gaia warns that the system for its third data release differs from the systems used in earlier releases, and it publishes passband definitions for its bands. Photometric measurements are also used with astrometric ones, but the sources do not make photometry and astrometry interchangeable.[^2][^3]
