A spaceflight biospecimen is a biological sample collected from an organism or experiment flown in space, or from associated ground studies, and kept for further research.[^1]

NASA’s Space Biology Biospecimen Sharing Programme collects biospecimens to maximise the scientific return from biological spaceflight and associated ground investigations and to broaden participation in space biology research. After dissection, the programme submits the specimens and their metadata to the NASA Biological Institutional Scientific Collection.[^1]

NASA describes that archive as the world’s largest repository of non-human space biology samples from NASA-supported studies, holding specimens predominantly from rodent studies with some quail tissues and microbes.[^2] The sources cover non-human material held by NASA, and the superlative is NASA’s own description.[^1][^2]
