An ice stream is a current of ice within an ice sheet or ice cap that flows faster than the surrounding ice.[^1][^2]

SwissEduc adds that the ice need not flow in the same direction as its surroundings and that zones of strongly sheared, crevassed ice often define the margins.[^2] Bennett's review ties fast flow to topographic corridors, to ice that is rheologically weaker than its surroundings, or to a lubricated bed, and says pure ice streams are today largely restricted to the Siple Coast of Antarctica.[^3]

NSIDC lists two further senses, the confluent sections of a branched valley glacier and an obsolete synonym of valley glaciers, and this page leaves both out.[^1] SWEET types the class only as a kind of ice, with a label and no definition, so the retained sources do not establish a SWEET-side meaning.[^4] They also give no speeds, widths or discharge figures, and the page states none.
