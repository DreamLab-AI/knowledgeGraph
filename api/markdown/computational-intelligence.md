- ### Definition
  - [[Computational Intelligence]] designates a cluster of adaptive, learning-based algorithmic methodologies inspired by biological and cognitive phenomena, including [[Neural Network]] architectures, [[Fuzzy Logic]] systems, and evolutionary algorithms. Unlike traditional rule-based [[Reasoning]] in symbolic AI, CI methods operate effectively under uncertainty and with incomplete data by iteratively adjusting internal parameters. They underpin much of practical [[Machine Learning Discipline]] and provide the [[Optimization Algorithm]] foundations for training modern large-scale models.

- ### Relationships
  - Computational Intelligence is a major sub-discipline of [[Artificial Intelligence]] and encompasses as constituent parts [[Fuzzy Logic]], [[Neural Network]] approaches, and evolutionary computation. It enables practical [[Machine Learning Discipline]] pipelines by supplying adaptive learning mechanisms and depends on [[Optimization Algorithm]] families (gradient descent, genetic algorithms, particle swarm optimisation) to search parameter spaces. It is related to classical [[Reasoning]] paradigms whilst differing in its tolerance for imprecision and stochastic methods.

- ### Content
  - The term "Computational Intelligence" was formalised by Bezdek in 1994 to distinguish biologically motivated, adaptive computing methods from classical symbolic AI. The three canonical pillars—artificial neural networks (Rosenblatt's perceptron, 1958), fuzzy sets (Zadeh, 1965), and evolutionary computation (Holland's genetic algorithms, 1975)—had independent origins but converged as a recognised field through the IEEE Computational Intelligence Society, founded in 1990. Early applications dominated pattern recognition, control systems, and combinatorial optimisation.

  - Technically, computational intelligence methods share the property of being population-based or gradient-guided adaptive processes rather than deductive inference chains. Fuzzy systems represent vague concepts through membership functions and process them with linguistic rules, enabling smooth interpolation between discrete categories. Evolutionary algorithms maintain populations of candidate solutions and apply selection, crossover, and mutation operators to guide search toward optima. Neural networks adjust weighted connections through backpropagation or Hebbian learning to minimise prediction error across training datasets. Hybrid neuro-fuzzy and neuro-evolutionary systems combine strengths of multiple paradigms.

  - The CI ecosystem encompasses IEEE Transactions on Fuzzy Systems, IEEE Transactions on Neural Networks and Learning Systems, and the annual IEEE World Congress on Computational Intelligence. Commercial CI applications are embedded in industrial process control (fuzzy PID tuners), financial trading (neuro-evolutionary portfolio optimisers), and game AI (evolutionary strategy learners). MATLAB's Fuzzy Logic Toolbox and Genetic Algorithm Toolbox remain standard educational platforms; PyGAD, DEAP, and scikit-fuzzy serve modern Python practitioners.

  - In 2024–2025, the boundaries between CI and deep learning continue to blur: neuroevolution approaches such as NEAT and its successors are being used to architect and optimise transformer variants, whilst fuzzy neural networks are gaining traction in explainable AI applications where regulators demand interpretable decision logic. The fusion of reinforcement learning with evolutionary strategies—as in OpenAI ES and CMA-ES variants—is proving effective for high-dimensional robotics control problems where gradient information is noisy or unavailable.
