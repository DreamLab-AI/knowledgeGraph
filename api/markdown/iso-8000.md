- ### Definition
- ISO 8000 is the international standard for [[Data Quality]] and master data, setting requirements for how data is represented, exchanged, and measured.
- It belongs to the family of [[Data Standards]] maintained through formal [[Standardization]] processes.
- The standard underpins [[Master Data Management]] and [[Data Governance]] by giving them an objective, verifiable basis for trusting data.
- ### Overview
- ISO 8000 addresses the problem that data is often unusable across organisations because its meaning, structure, and provenance are unclear.
- It defines portable data that carries its own definitions, so a receiver can interpret it without external context.
- The standard specifies quality characteristics and the metadata required to assess them, enabling repeatable measurement.
- It is structured in parts covering master data, data quality management, and the exchange of characteristic data.
- Conformance lets organisations certify that data meets explicit, agreed quality requirements rather than relying on informal trust.
- ### Key aspects
- Portability — data accompanied by the definitions needed to interpret it.
- Semantic encoding — explicit linkage of values to a shared [[Data Model]] and [[Schema]].
- Provenance — recorded origin and processing history of data.
- Quality measurement — defined characteristics such as completeness, accuracy, and conformance.
- Master data focus — consistent reference data shared across an enterprise.
- ### Applications
- Certifying supplier and product master data exchanged between trading partners.
- Establishing measurable data-quality targets within governance programmes.
- Improving interoperability in supply-chain and procurement systems.
- Providing audit-ready evidence of data quality for regulated industries.
