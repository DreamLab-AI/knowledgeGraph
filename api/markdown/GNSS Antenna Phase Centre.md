GNSS Antenna Phase Centre is the apparent source of radiation of a receiving or transmitting GNSS antenna, the point to which a GNSS measurement is referred, and it is not necessarily the geometric centre of the antenna.[^1]

Phase centre depends on the direction from which the signal arrives and on the signal frequency. Navipedia therefore separates a phase centre offset, a mean position expressed in north, east and up components from the antenna reference point, from phase centre variations, the deviations of individual measurements from that mean. The antenna reference point is typically on the mounting axis of the antenna, and manufacturers publish offsets in their technical sheets.[^1]

Modelling the effect for precise work involves both the receiver antenna and the satellite antenna. The International GNSS Service maintains absolute phase centre corrections for both, distributed in the Antenna Exchange Format, and its antenna working group links the topic to reference frame, clock products and calibration. The US National Geodetic Survey publishes calibrations for many antenna models and says it uses them in its own GNSS processing; it also advises matching the calibration set to the reference frame of the orbits and station coordinates in use.[^1][^2][^3]

These sources describe how corrections are defined and distributed. They do not give a size for the effect on a given receiver, and the Navipedia article is an educational summary from 2011 with later edits, not a calibration standard. A correction file is valid only for the antenna model, frequencies and reference frame it was derived for.
