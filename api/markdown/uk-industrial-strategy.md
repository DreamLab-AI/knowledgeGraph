- ### Definition
  - UK Industrial Strategy is a government [[Public Policy]] framework for productivity and growth.
  - It directs investment toward [[Advanced Manufacturing]] and other priority sectors.
  - It coordinates [[Economic Development]], skills, and [[Regulation]] behind national missions.
  - It shapes regional [[Infrastructure]] and innovation funding decisions.
- ### Overview
  - The strategy sets national priorities to lift productivity and rebalance the economy regionally.
  - It combines sector deals, mission-led funding, and place-based investment.
  - It aligns research, skills, infrastructure, and regulation behind chosen strategic sectors.
  - It is an instrument of directed industrial policy rather than purely laissez-faire economics.
- ### Key aspects
  - Sector prioritisation channels support toward areas of comparative or strategic advantage.
  - Mission framing organises diverse policy levers around concrete long-term goals.
  - Place-based interventions target regional innovation corridors and clusters.
  - Skills and R&D investment underpin the absorptive capacity needed for industrial upgrading.
- ### Applications
  - Funding advanced-manufacturing capacity in northern innovation corridors.
  - Coordinating clean-energy and net-zero transition investment.
  - Supporting digital and AI sector growth through targeted R&D incentives.
  - Aligning regulation and infrastructure to attract strategic foreign investment.
