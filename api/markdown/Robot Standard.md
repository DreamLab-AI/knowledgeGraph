  # RobotStandard
  A Robot Standard defines technical specifications, safety requirements, communication protocols, and interoperability guidelines established by standards organizations to ensure consistency, safety, and compatibility across robotic systems. These standards facilitate technology transfer, reduce integration costs, and establish safety baselines for robot deployment.
  Major standards include ISO 10218 for industrial robot safety, ISO/TS 15066 for collaborative robots defining force limits for human-robot contact, ISO 8373 establishing robotics vocabulary, and IEEE 1872 providing ontology for robotics and automation. Communication standards like OPC UA Robotics specify data exchange formats, while ROS (Robot Operating System) serves as a de facto standard middleware despite not being formally standardized.
  Application domains maintain specialized standards: ISO 13482 for personal care robots, ASTM F3322 for autonomous ground vehicles, and medical robot standards addressing specific safety and performance requirements. Standards development involves industry stakeholders, academic researchers, and regulatory bodies balancing innovation enablement with safety assurance.
  Contemporary standardization efforts address emerging technologies including autonomous vehicles (ISO/SAE standards), drones (ASTM F38 committee), exoskeletons (ISO/TC 299), and AI-enabled systems. Recent work focuses on cybersecurity standards for networked robots, ethical guidelines for autonomous systems, and certification frameworks for learned behaviors. Challenges include standardization pace lagging technological advancement, harmonization across regional regulatory frameworks, and balancing prescriptive requirements with innovation flexibility. Open standards and reference implementations increasingly complement formal standards to accelerate adoption and ensure practical implementability.
