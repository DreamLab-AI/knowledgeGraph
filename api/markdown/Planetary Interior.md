A planetary interior is the structure beneath a body's surface, usually described as layers such as a core, a mantle and a crust.[^1][^2]

NASA describes Earth as having four main layers, an inner core enveloped by an outer core, a mantle and a crust, and says the inner core is a solid sphere of iron and nickel.[^1] For Mars it describes a dense core, a rocky mantle above it and a crust over that.[^2]

Icy moons are described differently. ESA says three of Jupiter's moons are thought to have liquid-water oceans beneath icy crusts, and NASA reports that gravity measurements pointed to an ocean supplying the jets at Enceladus.[^3][^4] Such statements describe interiors inferred from indirect evidence.

Sources give size ranges as source-stated values and do not explain how interiors are measured in detail, so this page does not generalise beyond the bodies named.[^2]
