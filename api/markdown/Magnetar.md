A magnetar is a neutron star with an ultra-strong magnetic field, much stronger than that of an ordinary neutron star.[^1][^2]

Sources give different comparisons for the field strength, so none is repeated here. One account estimates field strength from the ratio of spin rate to spin-down rate, which makes it an inferred value, and the same article reports a direct spectral measurement for one object that it does not treat as settling the case.[^3]

Internally the field is thought to be strong enough to twist or crack the stellar crust. Stresses of this kind are linked to starquakes and to bursts of X-rays and soft gamma rays, observed in objects called soft gamma repeaters, and the textbook describes a rigid crust over a superdense neutron core.[^2][^3][^4]

Only a small number of magnetars were known when the cited pages were written, and counts depend on the date of the source, so no figure is given. The sources present the burst mechanism as the favoured interpretation rather than a closed question.[^2][^3]
