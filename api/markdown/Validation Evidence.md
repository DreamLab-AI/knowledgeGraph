Validation evidence is the documented outcome of comparing a satellite-derived product with reference data, used to state how well the product performs.[^1]

CEOS describes validation in stages. At the stage with no validation, accuracy has not been assessed and the product is considered beta. At higher stages, uncertainties are quantified over many locations and periods against reference data, procedures follow community-agreed good practice, and results are published in the peer-reviewed literature.[^1]

QA4EO adds that products should carry an indicator of quality traceable to reference standards, so that users can judge suitability for their applications.[^2]

No formal SWEET definition of the term could be retrieved, so this description rests on general CEOS and QA4EO practice. The CEOS stages belong to its land product hierarchy and may not carry over to other domains.[^1][^2]

[^1]: CEOS, “Land Product Validation Subgroup”. https://lpvs.gsfc.nasa.gov/
[^2]: CEOS and ESA, “Quality Assurance Framework for Earth Observation (QA4EO)”. https://qa4eo.org/
