- ### Definition
  - [[Gitcoin]] is a blockchain-based platform for funding [[Open Source Software]] and public goods through [[Quadratic Funding]] grant rounds and bounties, and it has expanded into decentralised identity with the [[Gitcoin Passport]] system for [[Sybil Resistance]].
- ### Relationships
  - Gitcoin is a concrete instantiation of [[Public Goods Funding]] mechanisms and is most closely associated with [[Quadratic Funding]], a mechanism it helped bring into mainstream Web3 practice. It operates as a [[DAO]] and supports the broader [[Decentralised Autonomous Organisation]] ecosystem by demonstrating how on-chain governance can direct funding allocation. The [[Gitcoin Passport]] credential system addresses [[Sybil Resistance]] in open grant rounds, while [[Retroactive Public Goods Funding]] programmes complement its prospective [[Grant Programs]].
- ### Content
  - Gitcoin emerged from a simple premise: open-source software underpins enormous economic value yet lacks sustainable funding models. Traditional grants from foundations and corporate sponsors are slow, opaque, and often concentrated in well-connected projects. By placing grant matching pools on a public blockchain and using [[Quadratic Funding]] to determine allocations, Gitcoin created a mechanism where community preference — expressed through the number of unique contributors rather than the volume of capital — drives resource distribution.

  - The quadratic funding model amplifies grassroots support: a project receiving £1 donations from one hundred individuals receives significantly more matching funds than one receiving a single £100 donation, because the matching function squares the sum of square roots of contributions. This mathematical property aligns incentives with broad community benefit rather than concentrated donor influence, addressing a core failure mode of traditional grant-making.

  - Gitcoin's transition to a [[DAO]] governance model (GTC token holders) represented a step towards community self-governance of the platform's parameters — which projects are eligible, how large matching pools are, and how funding rounds are structured. This transition also introduced on-chain accountability: governance decisions and treasury movements are publicly auditable, contrasting with opaque traditional foundation grant processes.

  - The [[Gitcoin Passport]] emerged as a response to Sybil attacks, where malicious actors create multiple pseudonymous identities to capture disproportionate matching funds. Passport aggregates [[Verifiable Credentials]] from multiple identity providers (social accounts, on-chain credentials, biometric attestations) into a composite trust score, allowing grant rounds to weight contributions from verified unique humans more heavily. This identity infrastructure has broader applications across the Web3 ecosystem wherever [[Sybil Resistance]] is required.
