Spatial resolution is the size of the area on the Earth's surface represented by each pixel of a digital image, so a smaller value shows more detail.[^1][^2]

ESA describes the spatial resolution of an instrument as the at-ground representation of an individual detector in a satellite sensor array. It is commonly stated in metres, so that a stated 30 m means pixels of 30 m by 30 m.[^2][^3]

Resolution can differ between bands of one instrument. The NASA page notes that most MODIS bands have 1 km pixels, with some bands at 250 m or 500 m, and the ESA page lists Sentinel-2 bands at 10 m, 20 m and 60 m.[^1][^2]

NASA states that high spatial resolution generally requires a narrower swath and therefore a longer time between observations of one area, so designers trade it against temporal resolution. The sources describe pixel size, and do not claim that it equals the smallest feature that can be identified in an image.[^1][^2][^3]
