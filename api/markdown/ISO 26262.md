- ### Definition
  - ISO 26262 is the automotive adaptation of [[IEC 61508]] that defines ASIL classification and [[Functional Safety]] lifecycle requirements for road vehicle E/E systems, providing the certification framework for [[ADAS]] and [[Autonomous Vehicle]] development.

- ### Relationships
  - ISO 26262 derives from [[IEC 61508]], the generic functional safety standard, adapting its Safety Integrity Level framework to the automotive domain's specific hazard profiles and development practices. It is the automotive counterpart to aviation's [[DO-178C]] and shares the goal of systematic risk reduction through evidence-based development processes. The standard directly enables the safe development of [[ADAS]] features (lane keeping, automatic emergency braking) and is the essential certification framework for [[Autonomous Vehicle]] systems and [[Self Driving Car]] platforms. ASIL D systems require the most rigorous [[Software Testing]] regimes including MC/DC coverage, and may incorporate [[Formal Verification]] for the most critical safety functions.

- ### Content
  - ISO 26262 was published in 2011 as an automotive-specific instantiation of IEC 61508, developed by ISO Technical Committee 22 in response to the growing complexity of vehicle electronics. Earlier automotive safety relied primarily on FMEA (Failure Mode and Effects Analysis) and general quality management (ISO/TS 16949), but the proliferation of safety-critical software in braking, steering, and powertrain systems demanded a more rigorous, systematic framework. The 2018 second edition extended scope to motorcycles (Part 12) and addressed semiconductor development (Part 11), reflecting the increasing integration of complex SoCs in safety-critical functions.

  - The standard organises safety activities across twelve parts covering vocabulary, management of functional safety, concept phase (hazard analysis and risk assessment—HARA), system level, hardware, software, production and operation, supporting processes, ASIL decomposition, guidelines for safety analyses, and semiconductors. The HARA process assigns ASIL ratings based on three parameters: Severity (S0-S3) of potential harm, Exposure (E0-E4) to the hazard situation, and Controllability (C0-C3) by the driver. ASIL D corresponds to the highest risk requiring the most stringent development measures; ASIL A is the lowest; QM (Quality Management) applies where residual risk is acceptable without specific functional safety requirements.

  - For software, ISO 26262 Part 6 defines activities across the software development lifecycle: software safety requirements specification, software architectural design, software unit design and implementation, software unit testing, software integration testing, and software testing. Higher ASIL levels require more extensive use of formal notation, more restrictive coding guidelines (MISRA-C is commonly required), higher structural coverage targets, and stronger independence between development and verification. Tool qualification requirements similar to DO-330 in aviation apply to software tools used in the development process.

  - By 2024–2025 ISO 26262 is being challenged by the AI and machine learning components entering ADAS and autonomous driving systems. Traditional ISO 26262 assumes deterministic software that can be exhaustively verified against specified requirements—a model that breaks down for neural networks trained on data. ISO/PAS 8800 (AI and automotive safety) and SOTIF (ISO 21448, Safety of the Intended Functionality) address the functional insufficiency and performance limitation aspects of AI in vehicles. The convergence of ISO 26262, SOTIF, and cybersecurity standard ISO/SAE 21434 is defining the multi-standard compliance landscape for next-generation autonomous vehicles.
