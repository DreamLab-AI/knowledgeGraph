Polarimetric SAR is a synthetic aperture radar technique in which polarised information is measured in the signals backscattered from the Earth to a satellite sensor.[^1]

Radar can collect signals in different polarisations by controlling the analysed polarisation on both the transmit and receive paths. NASA notes that SAR sensors typically transmit linearly polarised signals, and that horizontal and vertical polarisation are indicated by the letters H and V, so a signal transmitted vertically and received horizontally is labelled VH.[^2]

Signal strength in the different polarisations carries information about the structure of the imaged surface through rough-surface, volume and double-bounce scattering.[^2] ESA describes polarimetric interferometry as using two polarimetric images acquired from slightly different angles to give information on the three-dimensional structure of the scene.[^1]

The ESA story is a dated news item about a polarimetric SAR workshop, and its examples of wetland monitoring, old forest-fire scars and urban three-dimensional mapping come from that date. It names Radarsat-2 and ALOS as the main data sources then.[^1] Neither source establishes which missions currently provide polarimetric data.
