A spacecraft thermal coating is a paint, film, surface finish or adhesive tape applied to an exterior surface to set its solar absorptivity and infrared emissivity, and so how it exchanges heat with the Sun and space.[^1]

Solar absorptivity governs how much incident solar heating a surface absorbs, while infrared emissivity governs how much heat it emits.[^1] The guidebook states that white paints are used to radiate or reflect heat and black paints to absorb it, and NASA describes radiator surfaces as combining high infrared emissivity with low solar absorptivity.[^1][^2]

Coating properties are not fixed. NASA reports that coatings darken in orbit through atomic oxygen and ultraviolet exposure, which raises solar absorptance, so beginning-of-life and end-of-life values differ and thermal models should bias the optical properties accordingly.[^1] The guidebook advises measuring the optical properties of surfaces whenever possible.[^2]

Multilayer insulation and optical solar reflectors are related passive surface technologies but are not coatings in the sense used here. Multilayer insulation uses layers of low-emissivity material, and optical solar reflectors are quartz mirror tiles.[^1][^3] The sources cited here do not rank specific products.
