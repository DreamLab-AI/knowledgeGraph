Habitat mapping is the delineation and classification of habitats on the land or seabed, with Earth observation used to support extent and condition assessment.[^1][^2]

The UK Joint Nature Conservation Committee reports a project that reviewed Earth observation for habitat and biodiversity surveillance and its potential for that use.[^2] A follow-on phase looked at habitat condition, and JNCC says that differences in management of semi-natural habitat can be observed from NDVI responses.[^3] These are findings for UK sites and do not show that a vegetation index identifies habitat types in general.

Land-cover products are sometimes used in place of habitat maps. NOAA's C-CAP dataset is derived from satellite imagery and its page advises use only as a screening tool for local or site-specific decisions, with small features and changes verified against higher-resolution data.[^4] Land cover and habitat type are not the same classification.

Habitat measures also feed biodiversity indicators. GEO BON describes Essential Biodiversity Variables as standardised measurements, which with other information can support indicators such as the proportion of habitat in protected areas.[^5] That is a reporting framework and not a mapping method.
