A natural satellite is a naturally formed body that orbits a planet, and is also called a moon or planetary satellite.[^1]

NASA extends the usage to bodies orbiting dwarf planets, comets, asteroids and trans-Neptunian objects, and reports hundreds of known moons that differ in shape, size and type.[^1] Earth's Moon is Earth's only natural satellite.[^2]

Orbits of planetary satellites can be summarised by mean elements describing the general shape and orientation of an orbit, but JPL states these are not intended for ephemeris computation.[^3]

No source gives a formal size threshold or an exact count of moons.

[^1]: NASA Science, “Moons of Our Solar System”. https://science.nasa.gov/solar-system/moons/
[^2]: NASA Science, “Moon Facts”. https://science.nasa.gov/moon/facts/
[^3]: NASA JPL Solar System Dynamics, “Planetary Satellite Mean Elements”. https://ssd.jpl.nasa.gov/sats/elem/
