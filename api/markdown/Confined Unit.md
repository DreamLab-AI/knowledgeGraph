Confined unit is a hydrogeological unit whose water is held under raised pressure beneath a confining bed, as the Groundwater Project uses the phrase.[^1][^2]

Groundwater Project text says confining beds are not very permeable and water moves slowly through them, so an raised water pressure is maintained in the underlying confined unit.[^1] Western Oregon University defines a confining unit, a different thing, as a hydrogeologic unit of impermeable or distinctly less permeable material bounding one or more aquifers.[^2]

USGS nomenclature guidance calls confining unit the more general term, appropriate especially where more than a single bed makes up the unit, so confined unit should not be read as a synonym of confining unit.[^3][^2] A confined aquifer is the usual case of a confined unit.[^2][^1]

Retained sources give one passing use of the phrase and no formal definition, no figures, and no remote-sensing treatment.[^1]

[^1]: The Groundwater Project, “Aquifers and Confining Units”. https://books.gw-project.org/groundwater-in-our-water-cycle/chapter/aquifers-and-confining-units/
[^2]: Western Oregon University, “Glossary of Hydrologic Terms”. https://people.wou.edu/~taylors/nssi/glossary.htm
[^3]: U.S. Geological Survey, “Aquifer-Nomenclature Guidelines (Open-File Report 86-534)”. https://pubs.usgs.gov/of/1986/0534/report.pdf
