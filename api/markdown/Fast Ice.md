Fast ice is sea ice that forms along a coast and stays attached to the shore, to an ice wall, to an ice front, or between shoals or grounded icebergs.[^1][^2]

SWEET adds a thickness limit of less than 2 m to its definition, and its comment says fast ice thicker than about 2 m above sea level is called an ice shelf.[^1] NSIDC explains the name: near the coast, ice can attach to the shore or a shallow shelf and is pinned in place, whereas most sea ice moves.[^2]

Fast ice is the stationary counterpart of drift ice, and SWEET declares the two classes disjoint.[^1] The same source notes that fast ice may be formed on site from sea water or by pack ice of any age freezing to the shore, and that it may be more than one year old.[^1]

Retained sources do not establish how fast ice extent is mapped from space or how its edge is located in imagery.[^1][^2]
