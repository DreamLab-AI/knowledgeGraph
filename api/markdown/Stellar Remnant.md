Stellar remnant is the compact object, such as a white dwarf, neutron star or black hole, that is left when a star ends its life.[^1][^2]

NASA describes the core of a sun-like star as becoming a white dwarf, a stellar remnant that produces no new heat of its own and cools gradually.[^1][^2][^3] A massive star that runs out of fuel collapses, and the remnant core can be a neutron star or a black hole.[^1][^4] Pulsars are a kind of rapidly rotating neutron star.[^2]

A white dwarf can also be the origin of a Type Ia supernova, which Chandra describes as produced by white dwarfs, the condensed remnants of sun-like stars.[^5] Supernova remnants are the expanding debris clouds of supernovae, distinct from the compact remnants left near the centre.[^5]

No firm mass ranges for each outcome are given in the retained pages, which treat the three types as the main cases, so exotic end states are not covered here.
