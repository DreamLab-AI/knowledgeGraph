A stratosphere is the atmosphere layer above the troposphere in which air temperature increases with altitude.[^1][^2] The Met Office states that it lies above the tropopause and extends up to about 50 to 55 km altitude, and SWEET types it as an atmosphere layer.[^1][^3]

Ozone supplies the warming, heating the layer as it absorbs energy from incoming ultraviolet radiation from the Sun.[^4][^1] Because of this temperature stratification there is little convection and mixing, so the layers of air are quite stable.[^4][^1] UCAR also describes the stratosphere as very dry air containing little water vapor.[^4]

The Royal Meteorological Society contrasts the layer with the troposphere, noting that stratospheric warming results from direct absorption of solar radiation.[^2] Retained sources give slightly different heights for the top of the layer, about 50 km from UCAR and 50 to 55 km from the Met Office, and do not give a fixed figure.[^4][^1]
