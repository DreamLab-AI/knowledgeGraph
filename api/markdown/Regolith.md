Regolith is the layer of loose dust and rocky debris covering a planetary surface, and NASA applies the term to the Moon and to Martian soil.[^1][^2]

NASA states that nearly the entire Moon is covered by a rubble pile of charcoal-grey, powdery dust and rocky debris called the lunar regolith.[^1] On Mars, NASA attributes the reddish colour to oxidation of iron in the rocks, regolith and dust.[^2]

NASA also says the Moon is struck by a steady rain of asteroids, meteoroids and comets that leave craters.[^1] The pages do not state that impacts produce the regolith, and they give no depth or grain-size figures, so formation and properties are not established here.

[^1]: NASA Science, “Moon Facts”. https://science.nasa.gov/moon/facts/
[^2]: NASA Science, “Mars: Facts”. https://science.nasa.gov/mars/facts/
