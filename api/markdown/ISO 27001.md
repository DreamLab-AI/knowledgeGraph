- ### Definition
  - [[ISO 27001]] is the globally authoritative [[Cybersecurity Standard]] for [[Information Security]] management systems, providing a structured [[Risk Management]] and [[Compliance Framework]] that organisations use to protect information assets systematically and demonstrate [[Data Security]] assurance to customers, regulators, and supply chain partners.

- ### Relationships
  - ISO 27001 is classified as a [[Cybersecurity Standard]] within the ISO/IEC family and operates as the implementation standard paired with ISO/IEC 27002 (the control catalogue) and ISO/IEC 27005 (the risk management guidance). It embeds [[Risk Management]] as its central operating principle, requiring the documented treatment of information security risks as inputs to control selection. Its requirements directly enable [[Data Security]] assurance, systematic [[Compliance Audit Trail]] generation, and the application of [[Privacy By Design]] principles within a governed ISMS context. Organisations certified under ISO 27001 frequently cite it as foundational for [[GDPR Compliance]] evidence, and the newer [[ISO IEC 42001]] standard for AI management systems is explicitly structured to be compatible and integrated with ISO 27001 as a sibling management system standard.

- ### Content
  - ISO/IEC 27001 has its origins in the British Standard BS 7799, first published by the British Standards Institution in 1995. BS 7799 was developed in response to the growing reliance of organisations on computerised information processing and the inadequacy of ad hoc security measures for protecting sensitive business and customer data. Part 1 of BS 7799 became ISO/IEC 17799 in 2000 (the code of practice for information security management), and Part 2 — the management system specification — became the first edition of ISO/IEC 27001 in 2005, establishing the certifiable management system framework.

  - The standard's structure follows the high-level structure (HLS) common to all ISO management system standards — the same Clause 4-10 framework used by ISO 9001 (quality), ISO 14001 (environment), and ISO 45001 (occupational health and safety) — enabling integrated management systems. The ISMS lifecycle encompasses: understanding the organisation and its context, defining the scope, establishing an information security policy, conducting a risk assessment using a documented methodology, selecting controls from Annex A (93 controls in the 2022 edition, reorganised into four themes: Organisational, People, Physical, and Technological), implementing a statement of applicability, and undergoing Stage 1 (documentation review) and Stage 2 (audit) certification by an accredited certification body.

  - ISO 27001 certification has become a de facto procurement requirement in technology supply chains, financial services, healthcare, and government contracting. Holding certification signals to customers and partners that an organisation's information security posture meets an independently verified baseline, reducing due-diligence burden and enabling access to regulated markets. Over 70,000 organisations worldwide hold ISO 27001 certificates, with particularly high adoption in the UK, Japan, India, and Germany. Cloud service providers seeking SOC 2 or CSA STAR certifications typically align their control frameworks with ISO 27001 Annex A, making the standard a common reference point even for organisations that pursue alternative certification paths.

  - The 2022 revision (ISO/IEC 27001:2022) introduced significant updates reflecting the evolving threat landscape: the Annex A control set was restructured from 14 clauses and 114 controls to 4 themes and 93 controls, with 11 new controls addressing cloud services security, threat intelligence, data masking, physical security monitoring, ICT readiness for business continuity, and secure coding. In 2024-2025, the intersection of ISO 27001 with AI governance has become a focus: many organisations are implementing ISO 27001 ISMS controls as the operational backbone for their ISO/IEC 42001 AI management systems, using the established audit and risk management disciplines of ISO 27001 to govern AI-specific risks such as training data integrity, model drift monitoring, and adversarial input detection.
