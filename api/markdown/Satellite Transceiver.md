
A satellite transceiver is an electronic unit or subsystem that both transmits and receives signals for a satellite communications link.

A typical transmit path accepts data, applies selected modulation and coding, amplifies the signal and feeds a transmit antenna. A receive path collects the incoming signal and may filter, low-noise amplify and demodulate it. These are functional stages rather than a fixed equipment boundary: the antenna, power amplifier, receiver and modem may be separate units or integrated differently in a spacecraft or ground terminal.[^1]

A spacecraft transponder is related but is not always synonymous with a general-purpose transceiver. It may receive an uplink and return a coherent or frequency-translated signal, and can support functions such as ranging or relay. NASA’s onboard-systems account describes coherent spacecraft transponders; terminology varies across engineering contexts, so the actual functions should be stated.[^1][^2]

Transceiver selection depends on link distance, data rate, frequency, power and mass budgets, pointing and environmental constraints. A component description or laboratory test does not establish link closure, interoperability, redundancy or readiness for on-orbit service. RF is common, while optical systems also use transmit and receive equipment; their naming may differ by mission or provider, and they require precise pointing.[^1][^3]
