A spacecraft heater is an electrical resistance element used as active thermal control to keep components above their lower temperature limits.[^1]

NASA's survey says electrical resistance heaters on small spacecraft are most often Kapton heaters, which are polyimide film with etched foil circuits that produce heat when current flows. They are usually controlled by a thermostat or temperature sensor and used in cold environments to maintain battery temperatures, typically for components with the narrowest temperature limits.[^1]

Some small satellites required a heater on the battery in addition to passive control during eclipse, according to the same survey. A NASA patent document describes thermostat-based heater control, with redundant thermostats wired to guard against a thermostat failing closed or open, as typical prior practice.[^1][^2]

These sources describe general practice and do not set heater power or temperature thresholds for any specific spacecraft.[^1][^2]

[^2]: NASA, *Spacecraft Component Heater Control System*, patent document on the NASA Technical Reports Server. https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/19890019185/downloads/19890019185.pdf
