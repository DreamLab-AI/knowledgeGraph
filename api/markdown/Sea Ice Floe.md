A sea ice floe is a relatively flat piece of floating ice, formed from frozen sea water, that is 20 m or more across.[^1][^2]

SWEET makes the class a subclass of sea ice and equivalent to ice floe, which explains the alias IceFloe.[^1] The Canadian Ice Service defines a floe more broadly as any relatively flat piece of ice, and grades it from small, 20 to 100 m across, through medium, big and vast to giant, greater than 10 km across.[^2]

Floes are the discontinuous pieces that make up pack ice.[^1] NSIDC describes floes colliding with each other and piling into ridges and keels, and leads opening where floes diverge or shear as they move parallel to each other.[^3] In the Canadian grading, open drift is ice whose floes are generally not in contact with one another.[^2]

Retained sources do not establish how individual floes are identified or tracked in satellite imagery, nor a size distribution for any region.[^2][^3]
