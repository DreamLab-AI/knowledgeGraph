Heliopause, the boundary where the tenuous, hot solar wind meets the cold, dense interstellar medium, marks this limit.[^1]

NASA describes the crossing of this boundary by Voyager 2 as the point at which the spacecraft left the heliosphere, the bubble of plasma created by the solar wind. Voyager 1 crossed the same boundary earlier, in 2012, at a different place and time.[^1]

According to the Voyager mission page, the two spacecraft are the only ones ever to operate outside the heliosphere, the protective bubble of particles and magnetic fields generated by the Sun. The heliopause is therefore the outer edge of that bubble as seen by these two crossings.[^2]

Sources describe the boundary but do not give a single distance for it, and the two crossings occurred at different places, so the heliopause should not be treated as a fixed sphere. The sources do not discuss other boundaries such as the planetary magnetopause.[^1][^2]
