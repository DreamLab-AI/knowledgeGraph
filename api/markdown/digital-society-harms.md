- ### Definition
  - Digital Society Harms is the catalogue of empirically documented and structurally anticipated negative impacts arising from the deployment of digital, algorithmic, and AI systems at population scale across information, psychological, economic, social, human-rights, child-safety, environmental, and labour dimensions, formalised through overlapping taxonomies (Ofcom Online Safety Act 2023 Schedule 5 priority illegal harms — child sexual exploitation and abuse CSEA, terrorism, suicide/self-harm content, dishonest content/fraud, controlling or coercive behaviour, harassment/stalking, hate, intimate-image abuse, drugs/weapons; Schedule 6 priority harms to children — pornography, abuse/hate, bullying, violent content, harmful substances, body-stigma content, dangerous-stunts content; Schedule 7 user-empowerment categories — suicide/self-harm content adults can opt out of; Ada Lovelace Institute Harms Framework 2022 distinguishing allocative/representational/quality-of-service/loss-of-liberty/social-system harms; MIT AI Risk Repository Slattery et al. August 2024 cataloguing 700+ unique risks across 23 source documents and 7 risk taxonomies covering causal factors entity/intent/timing and risk domains discrimination/privacy/misinformation/malicious-use/human-computer-interaction/socioeconomic/AI-system-safety; Stanford HAI AI Index 2025 quantifying incident growth; AI Now Institute reports on labour, infrastructure, and geopolitical concentration; Council of Europe HUDERIA Human Rights Democracy Rule-of-Law Impact Assessment), grouped into eight overlapping harm categories: (1) information harms — algorithmic disinformation production at 10×-1000× pre-generative-AI volumes, deepfake-enabled fraud (Hong Kong Arup CFO $25M February 2024, Slovakian Šimečka election audio September 2023), synthetic-media impersonation, attention-capture engagement optimisation displacing deliberative information ecosystems (see sibling [[Deepfakes and fraudulent content]]); (2) psychological harms — algorithmic amplification of self-harm content (Molly Russell coroner ruling September 2022 north London inquest concluding 14-year-old died from "act of self-harm whilst suffering from depression and the negative effects of online content"), eating-disorder content amplification on TikTok ("It's a Numbers Game" Wall Street Journal investigation December 2021, Center for Countering Digital Hate "Deadly by Design" December 2022 documenting 2.6-minute median for new teen accounts to encounter self-harm content), body-image distortion with 32% of teen girls reporting Instagram worsens body-image issues per Frances Haugen Facebook Files disclosures September 2021, dopamine-loop addiction architecture engineered through variable-ratio reinforcement (Skinner 1953 schedules deliberately ported into product surfaces per Tristan Harris Center for Humane Technology 2018 Senate Commerce testimony 25 June 2019), screen-time correlations with structural brain changes (Nature Molecular Psychiatry 2021 grey-matter meta-analysis), attention fragmentation (Stanford heavy-multitaskers reduced memory longitudinal study Wagner/Ophir 2009-2018); (3) economic harms — gig-economy precarity (UK Supreme Court Uber BV v Aslam February 2021 ruling drivers are workers entitled to minimum wage, Deliveroo riders ECJ rulings on collective bargaining 2024, US PRO Act stalled), algorithmic management of warehouse and platform workers (Amazon time-off-task surveillance, Uber driver deactivation appeals 2024 UK Employment Tribunal cases), discriminatory hiring (Amazon recruitment ML scrapped 2018 after penalising CVs containing "women's" tokens, HireVue facial-analysis pseudoscience FTC complaint EPIC 2019 leading to discontinuation of facial analysis November 2020, Workday class-action Mobley v Workday Northern District California August 2024 alleging age/race/disability discrimination certified as collective action 2025), automated layoffs and performance management (Xsolla 150 staff sacked via algorithm August 2021, Uber Eats facial-recognition discrimination IWGB tribunal 2024); (4) social harms — political polarisation amplification (Frances Haugen Facebook Files Wall Street Journal series September 2021 documenting internal research that Instagram is "toxic for teen girls" and that engagement-ranking increases divisive content, US Senate Subcommittee testimony 5 October 2021, UK Parliament Joint Committee 25 October 2021), election interference (Cambridge Analytica/Facebook Information Commissioner enforcement October 2018 £500K maximum pre-GDPR fine, FTC $5B settlement July 2019, US Senate Intelligence Committee Volume 2 2019 on Russian Internet Research Agency 2014-2017 operations), microtargeting (UK Brexit-referendum Vote Leave AggregateIQ ICO investigation 2018, EU Digital Services Act Article 26 prohibition on political microtargeting using sensitive data February 2024), filter-bubble and recommendation-driven radicalisation (Christchurch Call 2019, Royal Society "Online Information Environment" report 2022); (5) human-rights harms — mass surveillance disclosures (Snowden 2013 NSA PRISM/Tempora programmes leading to Investigatory Powers Act 2016 in UK and ECJ Schrems II July 2020 invalidating Privacy Shield), predictive policing (PredPol/Geolitica wound down 2024 after Markup investigation October 2023 documenting 84-95% bias against minority neighbourhoods, Compstat NYPD legacy, UK Durham HART tool retired 2020), social-scoring systems (China Social Credit System fragmented municipal pilots, formally banned for AI deployment by EU AI Act Article 5(1)(c) August 2024), facial recognition wrongful arrests (Robert Williams January 2020 Detroit, Nijeer Parks February 2019 New Jersey, Porcha Woodruff February 2023 Detroit while 8-months pregnant, Randal Reid November 2022 Louisiana — all Black Americans, all settled or dismissed), bulk-data retention (Watson/Tele2 CJEU December 2016, Privacy International v Secretary of State 2018-2020 challenges), EU AI Act Article 5 outright bans on real-time biometric identification in public spaces (with narrow law-enforcement carve-outs), social-scoring, emotion recognition in workplaces/schools, predictive policing solely from profiling, untargeted scraping of facial images; (6) child-safety harms — CSAM production growth (Internet Watch Foundation 2024 Annual Report 291,273 webpages confirmed actioned containing child sexual abuse material, 92% increase 2023 over 2022, 18% involving AI-generated CSAM per October 2024 IWF update), Sewell Setzer III suicide February 2024 followed by Garcia v Character Technologies filed Middle District Florida October 2024 alleging defective product design and failure to warn (motion to dismiss largely denied May 2025, partial settlement reported early 2026), financial sextortion targeting boys (FBI/DOJ joint advisory January 2024 noting at least 20 US suicides linked to sextortion 2021-2024, NCA UK 2024 alert), Online Safety Act 2023 Phase 1 illegal harms duties commenced 17 March 2025 with Ofcom Codes of Practice and Phase 2 child-safety duties commencing 25 July 2025 requiring highly-effective age assurance for pornography services, ICO Age-Appropriate Design Code Children's Code in force since 2 September 2021 setting 15 standards conformant services must meet; (7) environmental harms — data-centre electricity demand projected to double globally 2022-2026 reaching 1000 TWh (IEA Electricity 2024 report January 2024), Google water consumption 24.2 billion litres 2023 (sustainability report 2024), training a single 175B-parameter model emits 552 tCO2e (Strubell/Ganesh/McCallum 2019 baseline now exceeded by GPT-4-scale runs ≈10× larger), e-waste 62 million tonnes 2022 globally (UN Global E-waste Monitor 2024) growing at 5× recycling rate, embedded-emissions of GPUs and TPUs largely unmeasured; (8) labour harms — Mechanical Turk workers earning median $2/hour without benefits or appeal rights (Hara et al. CHI 2018), Kenyan content moderators paid $1.32/hour to label toxic content for OpenAI via Sama partnership terminated February 2023 after Time investigation January 2023, lawsuit filed Nairobi 2023 by Daniel Motaung and 184 Facebook moderators citing PTSD, Philippine Sama moderators class action 2023, German Tribute work-conditions investigations into TikTok moderators Berlin 2022; mapped against UK enforcement landscape (Ofcom as Online Safety Act regulator, ICO as data-protection and age-appropriate-design regulator, Competition and Markets Authority Digital Markets Unit established 2021 with Digital Markets Competition and Consumers Act 2024 entered force January 2025, Centre for Data Ethics and Innovation closed September 2023 with functions absorbed by Department for Science Innovation and Technology DSIT, Department for Culture Media and Sport DCMS shifting digital portfolio to DSIT, Foundation Model Taskforce evolving into UK AI Safety Institute November 2023 renamed UK AI Security Institute February 2025) and against UK civil-society research ecosystem (Ada Lovelace Institute London founded 2018, doteveryone closed 2020 with research transferred to ALI, Demos, Centre for Long-Term Resilience, Open Rights Group, Privacy International, Internet Society UK Chapter, 5Rights Foundation children's-rights advocacy, Center for Countering Digital Hate London/Washington), and against UK and Northern English academic ecosystem (Oxford Internet Institute OII founded 2001, Cambridge Minderoo Centre for Technology and Democracy 2021, Cambridge Leverhulme Centre for the Future of Intelligence LCFI, UCL Knowledge Lab, Imperial College London Institute for Security Science and Technology, Edinburgh Centre for Technomoral Futures, Manchester Digital Trust and Security, Leeds Digital Futures Society, Sheffield AI Research Centre, Newcastle Open Lab, Lancaster Data Science Institute, Liverpool John Moores Liverpool Centre for Advanced Internet Studies); contrasts with techno-utopian framings positioning digital systems as neutral conduits for human empowerment, individual-choice framings locating responsibility with end-users rather than platform architects, and market-only solutions assuming competitive selection eliminates harmful design once consumer awareness grows, with the empirical record demonstrating that engagement-optimised attention markets, network-effect lock-in, and information asymmetries between platforms and users systematically prevent the corrective feedback loops that market-only theories require.

  - ## About Digital Society Harms
  - **Digital Society Harms** is the umbrella ontology category capturing the empirically documented and structurally anticipated negative impacts that arise when digital, algorithmic, and AI systems are deployed at population scale. Unlike treatments that frame harm in single-incident terms (a specific deepfake, a specific moderation failure, a specific data breach), this category insists on the **systemic** and **cumulative** character of digital harm — the architectural choices made by platforms, the optimisation targets of recommendation systems, the labour conditions of moderation, the regulatory gaps in data-protection regimes, and the political economy of attention markets collectively produce harm patterns that are predictable, reproducible across platforms, and resistant to interventions that address symptoms rather than structures.
  - The category serves three operational purposes within the ontology. First, it provides a **taxonomy scaffold** linking to formal harm classifications (Ofcom Online Safety Act Schedules 5/6/7, Ada Lovelace Institute Harms Framework 2022, MIT AI Risk Repository 700+ risks Slattery et al. August 2024, Stanford HAI AI Index Incident Database, AI Now Institute reports). Second, it functions as a **case-law anchor** binding specific high-profile incidents (Molly Russell, Sewell Setzer, Cambridge Analytica, Frances Haugen disclosures, Snowden, IWF CSAM reports, Mobley v Workday, Garcia v Character Technologies) to abstract harm types so that the ontology supports both deductive policy reasoning and inductive case-pattern recognition. Third, it provides the **contrast surface** against which competing framings (techno-utopianism, individual-choice responsibilisation, market-only solutionism) can be assessed, ensuring the ontology does not silently encode the assumption that digital systems are net-positive instruments awaiting only better users.
  - ### The Taxonomic Landscape

    Four taxonomies dominate UK and international harm-cataloguing practice, each privileging different ontological commitments.

    **Ofcom Online Safety Act 2023 (UK)**: The Act creates a duties-of-care regime for user-to-user services and search services, distinguishing three priority categories. **Schedule 5** lists priority illegal content: terrorism (Terrorism Act 2000/2006 offences); child sexual exploitation and abuse (CSEA: making/distributing indecent images of children, grooming, sexual communication with a child); encouraging or assisting suicide; people-trafficking; controlling or coercive behaviour in intimate relationships; serious unlawful immigration; sexual exploitation; harassment, stalking, threats, abuse; hate offences (stirring up racial/religious hatred); intimate-image abuse (so-called revenge pornography and synthetic intimate images per Online Safety Act Section 187 amending Sexual Offences Act 2003); drugs and psychoactive substances offences; firearms, knives and weapons offences; fraud (dishonest financial offences). **Schedule 6** lists priority harms to children: pornographic content (Schedule 6 Part 1); content depicting child sexual abuse, racism, hate, bullying, violence promoting self-harm or suicide, instructions for dangerous physical stunts, harmful diet content. **Schedule 7** lists user-empowerment categories adults can opt out of: suicide and self-harm content not crossing the criminal threshold, eating-disorder content, abusive content targeting protected characteristics. Phase 1 illegal-harms duties commenced **17 March 2025** following Ofcom's Codes of Practice published December 2024. Phase 2 child-safety duties commenced **25 July 2025** requiring "highly effective age assurance" for services likely accessed by children. Phase 3 categorised-service duties (Category 1: largest user-to-user platforms; Category 2A: largest search; Category 2B: smaller user-to-user above threshold) entered implementation late 2025 with transparency-report and user-empowerment requirements.

    **Ada Lovelace Institute Harms Framework (2022)**: Distinguishes five harm types: (1) **Allocative harms** — when AI denies opportunities, resources, or information disproportionately (mortgage denial, hiring rejection, benefit termination); (2) **Quality-of-service harms** — when systems work worse for some groups than others (facial recognition failing on darker skin per Gender Shades Buolamwini/Gebru 2018, voice recognition failing on regional accents per Koenecke et al. 2020); (3) **Representational harms** — when systems reinforce stereotypes or denigrate identity (image search returning offensive results, generative models producing biased outputs); (4) **Loss-of-liberty harms** — when systems enable unjustified surveillance, detention, or restriction (wrongful arrests via facial recognition, predictive policing reinforcement); (5) **Social-system harms** — when aggregate deployment reshapes institutions and norms (filter bubbles, attention markets, deliberative-democracy degradation). This framework underpins much UK policy discourse and informed the Centre for Data Ethics and Innovation (CDEI, closed September 2023, functions absorbed by DSIT) approach.

    **MIT AI Risk Repository (Slattery et al., August 2024, arXiv:2408.12622)**: Aggregates 700+ unique risks extracted from 43 risk taxonomies and frameworks. Provides a **causal taxonomy** classifying by entity (human/AI), intent (intentional/unintentional), and timing (pre-deployment/post-deployment), and a **domain taxonomy** with seven domains: (1) discrimination and toxicity; (2) privacy and security; (3) misinformation; (4) malicious actors and misuse; (5) human-computer interaction; (6) socioeconomic and environmental harms; (7) AI system safety, failures and limitations. This is the most comprehensive AI-specific harm catalogue in 2024-2025 academic literature, hosted as a living resource at airisk.mit.edu and updated quarterly.

    **Council of Europe HUDERIA (2024)**: The Human Rights, Democracy and Rule of Law Impact Assessment methodology under the **Framework Convention on Artificial Intelligence** opened for signature 5 September 2024 — the first legally binding international AI treaty. HUDERIA structures impact assessment around Convention obligations (Articles 4-12) covering dignity, non-discrimination, privacy, transparency, accountability, equality, safe innovation, and rule-of-law preservation.

  - ## Components / Architecture
  - The harm catalogue decomposes into eight overlapping categories, each with documented incidents, regulatory hooks, and measurement instruments.
  - ### 1. Information Harms

    Information harms comprise epistemic distortions introduced by algorithmic content production, ranking, and amplification. The category overlaps with [[Deepfakes and fraudulent content]] (sibling concept) but extends beyond synthetic media to encompass attention-economy distortions of which deepfakes are one instance.

    **Disinformation production at scale**: Generative AI reduces the marginal cost of producing plausible disinformation to near-zero. NewsGuard tracked **1,150+ AI-generated unreliable news sites** in 50+ languages by end-2024, up from 49 in May 2023. Fact-checking organisations (Snopes, Full Fact UK, FactCheck.org, AFP Fact Check) reported a 3-5× increase in submitted claims requiring assessment 2023-2025.

    **Deepfake-enabled fraud**: The Hong Kong **Arup deepfake CFO incident** (January-February 2024) saw a finance employee transfer **HK$200 million (US$25.6 million)** to scammers after a video-conference attended entirely by deepfaked colleagues including a synthetic CFO. The **Slovakian Šimečka audio** (29 September 2023, two days before parliamentary elections) circulated a fabricated recording purporting to show Progressive Slovakia leader Michal Šimečka discussing vote-buying with a journalist; Progressive Slovakia narrowly lost.

    **Algorithmic amplification of low-quality content**: Engagement-optimisation ranking systematically over-weights content producing strong emotional reactions (outrage, fear, novelty). Frances Haugen's **Facebook Files** disclosures (September 2021) included internal Facebook research showing that the 2018 Meaningful Social Interactions (MSI) ranking change increased angry and divisive content by ~5× over the prior News Feed algorithm, with internal staff documenting this in 2019 and senior leadership declining to reverse the change.

  - ### 2. Psychological Harms

    Psychological harms include algorithmic amplification of self-harm content, eating-disorder promotion, body-image distortion, addiction architectures, and attention fragmentation.

    **Molly Russell inquest**: Molly Russell, aged 14, died by suicide in November 2017. After a five-year investigation, North London Coroner Andrew Walker ruled on **30 September 2022** that she "died from an act of self-harm whilst suffering from depression and the negative effects of online content". The inquest examined ~16,300 pieces of content Molly had saved, liked or shared on Instagram and Pinterest in the six months before her death, of which 2,100 related to depression, self-harm or suicide; Meta executive Elizabeth Lagone testified that Meta considered the content "safe" by community standards, which the coroner explicitly rejected. The ruling — the first in UK history to formally name online content as a contributing cause of death — catalysed the **Online Safety Act 2023** and remains the canonical citation for psychological harm from algorithmic amplification.

    **TikTok eating-disorder amplification**: The Wall Street Journal **"It's a Numbers Game" series** (December 2021) documented that newly-created accounts identifying as 13-year-old girls were served eating-disorder content within minutes of viewing weight-related videos. The Center for Countering Digital Hate **"Deadly by Design" report** (December 2022) found new teen accounts encountered self-harm content within a median **2.6 minutes** and eating-disorder content within **8 minutes**. TikTok announced policy changes 2023-2024 but third-party audits continue to identify gaps.

    **Body image and the Facebook Files**: Internal Facebook research disclosed by Frances Haugen included slides reporting that **32% of teen girls said Instagram made body-image issues worse**, and that for some teens Instagram-related social-comparison was "destabilising" and connected to suicidal ideation. The Wall Street Journal published the slides September 2021; Haugen testified to US Senate Commerce Subcommittee 5 October 2021 and UK Parliament Joint Committee on the Draft Online Safety Bill 25 October 2021.

    **Addiction architectures and dopamine loops**: Tristan Harris (former Google Design Ethicist) and the Center for Humane Technology (founded 2018) document the deliberate porting of B.F. Skinner's variable-ratio reinforcement schedules into product surfaces (pull-to-refresh, autoplay, infinite scroll, slot-machine-style notification batching). Harris's Senate Commerce Committee testimony (25 June 2019) and the **2020 Netflix documentary The Social Dilemma** brought addiction-architecture analysis to mainstream audiences. PMC meta-analyses (2021-2024) document structural grey-matter differences in problematic internet users analogous to substance-addiction neuroimaging signatures (Nature Molecular Psychiatry 2021).

  - ### 3. Economic Harms

    **Gig-economy precarity**: The UK Supreme Court ruled in **Uber BV v Aslam ([2021] UKSC 5, 19 February 2021)** that Uber drivers are "workers" under the Employment Rights Act 1996 entitled to minimum wage and paid leave, not independent contractors. The ruling triggered cascading litigation: Deliveroo riders' collective-bargaining cases (UK Supreme Court ruled against riders November 2023 on Article 11 ECHR grounds), Bolt driver cases (ongoing 2024-2025), Just Eat Takeaway transition to employed-courier model 2023-2024.

    **Discriminatory hiring**: Amazon scrapped its **recruitment ML tool in 2018** after Reuters revealed it penalised CVs containing the word "women's" or referencing all-women's colleges, having been trained on a decade of mostly-male engineering CVs. **HireVue** discontinued facial-expression analysis November 2020 after an EPIC FTC complaint argued the technology constituted pseudoscientific deception. **Mobley v Workday** (Northern District California, filed February 2023 by Derek Mobley alleging algorithmic age, race, and disability discrimination in screening) was certified as a **collective action 16 May 2025**, the first such certification against an AI HR platform in US courts.

    **Algorithmic management and automated layoffs**: **Xsolla terminated 150 staff** August 2021 after an algorithm flagged them as "unengaged" based on Slack and Jira activity. Amazon warehouse "time-off-task" surveillance has been cited in OSHA filings and a 2023 California Senate Bill 1167. **Uber Eats facial-recognition discrimination** cases brought by IWGB before UK Employment Tribunal 2024 cited Microsoft Face API false-rejection rates against Black couriers, leading to settlements 2024-2025.

    **Pricing discrimination and dynamic-price markets**: Algorithmic pricing in ride-hail (Uber surge), e-commerce (Amazon dynamic pricing), insurance (Admiral pricing experiments 2022 ICO investigation), and gig labour-allocation (Deliveroo Frank algorithm, Just Eat courier-allocation) raises concerns about hidden discriminatory effects. The UK Centre for Data Ethics and Innovation Review of Online Targeting (2020) and ICO Age Appropriate Design Code enforcement of "transparency" obligations have begun to constrain opaque pricing personalisation, but enforcement remains thin.

    **Welfare-state algorithmic harm**: The **Dutch SyRI ruling** (Netherlands District Court The Hague, 5 February 2020, NJCM v The Netherlands ECLI:NL:RBDHA:2020:865) found the System Risk Indication welfare-fraud detection system breached ECHR Article 8 due to opacity and disproportionality, leading to the wider **Toeslagenaffaire (Childcare Benefits Scandal)** in which an algorithm flagged some 26,000 families — many of dual nationality or with foreign-sounding names — as fraudsters, with the Rutte III cabinet resigning January 2021. **UK Department for Work and Pensions (DWP)** algorithmic-fraud-detection tools challenged by Big Brother Watch judicial review 2023-2024; **A-level algorithm 2020** controversy (Ofqual algorithm downgrading 40% of teacher-assessed grades disproportionately affecting state-school students) led to algorithm withdrawal August 2020 after public outcry and Foxglove Legal pre-action.

  - ### 4. Social Harms

    **Political polarisation**: Beyond Haugen's MSI disclosures, the **NYU Stern Center for Business and Human Rights** (2021) and **Royal Society "Online Information Environment" report** (January 2022) documented engagement-driven polarisation. The **NESTA Centre for Collective Intelligence Design** UK programme 2022-2024 investigated deliberative interventions.

    **Election interference**: **Cambridge Analytica/Facebook** (Christopher Wylie whistleblower disclosure March 2018) saw the harvesting of 87M Facebook profiles via the "thisisyourdigitallife" app for targeting in the 2016 Trump and Vote Leave Brexit campaigns. The UK ICO fined Facebook £500,000 (October 2018, the maximum under pre-GDPR Data Protection Act 1998), FTC settled with Facebook for **$5 billion** (24 July 2019), and SCL Group entered administration. The **US Senate Intelligence Committee Volume 2** (2019) documented Russian Internet Research Agency operations 2014-2017 targeting US elections.

    **Microtargeting and political advertising**: The **EU Digital Services Act** (entered force February 2024 for very-large online platforms) and the **EU Regulation 2024/900 on transparency and targeting of political advertising** (entered force February 2024, fully applicable October 2025) prohibit political microtargeting using special-category data per GDPR Article 9. UK regulation lags: the Election Act 2022 introduced an imprint regime for digital political advertising but stopped short of microtargeting limits, with **Electoral Commission** and **ICO** sharing enforcement responsibilities. The 2024 UK general election (4 July 2024) saw the first large-scale Labour deployment of generative AI for ad personalisation; the **Conservatives** were subject to ICO scrutiny for use of Tory-developed data tools. **Generative AI in elections**: The Slovakian Šimečka audio, the 2024 New Hampshire Biden robocall deepfake (Steve Kramer indicted May 2024), Indonesia synthetic Suharto endorsement video January 2024, and India general-election 2024 deepfake-heavy campaigning catalysed FCC ban on AI-generated robocall voices February 2024 and EU AI Act Article 50 watermarking obligations from August 2026.

    **Misinformation amplification and source quality collapse**: The **Stanford Internet Observatory** (closed June 2024 following political pressure), the **University of Washington Center for an Informed Public**, the **Election Integrity Partnership** (wound down 2024 after House Judiciary subpoenas), and academic groups tracking election-period misinformation have faced political and legal pressure 2023-2024 weakening the misinformation-research infrastructure. **Murthy v Missouri** (US Supreme Court, decided 26 June 2024) held plaintiffs lacked standing to challenge federal-government communications with platforms about misinformation, but the case chilled platform-government information sharing nonetheless. Open-source equivalent infrastructure (CheckMyChips, Logically, Full Fact UK, Newtral Spain) is increasingly the only systematic monitoring layer.

    **Community fragmentation and parasocial substitution**: Increasing screen-time consumption substitutes for face-to-face interaction, with **OECD Society at a Glance 2024** noting 27% reduction in average daily face-to-face socialising 2003-2023 across OECD members. The rise of **AI companions** (Replika, Character.AI, Talkie, Kindroid) introduces new categories of parasocial relationship; 2024-2025 saw early empirical work (Marshall et al. CHI 2024, Skjuve et al. 2023) documenting both loneliness-reduction effects and concerns about substitution for human relationship formation in adolescents.

  - ### 5. Human Rights Harms

    **Mass surveillance**: The **Snowden disclosures** (June 2013, via The Guardian and Washington Post) revealed NSA PRISM (collecting from Google, Microsoft, Yahoo, Facebook, Apple, etc.) and GCHQ Tempora (tapping submarine cables at Bude Cornwall). UK responses included the **Investigatory Powers Act 2016** ("Snoopers' Charter") and **CJEU Schrems II** (16 July 2020, Case C-311/18) invalidating EU-US Privacy Shield over inadequate US-government access protections.

    **Predictive policing**: **PredPol/Geolitica** shut down 2024 after The Markup's October 2023 investigation analysed 7.8 million predictions across 38 US police departments, finding 84-95% of predictions reinforced patrol patterns in Black, Latino, and lower-income neighbourhoods. **Durham Constabulary HART (Harm Assessment Risk Tool)** retired 2020 after ALI research raised proportionality and accuracy concerns. **NYPD Compstat** continues but with reduced predictive-policing emphasis post-Floyd 2020.

    **Facial-recognition wrongful arrests** (all US, all Black Americans, all involving Clearview AI or municipal facial-recognition deployments): **Robert Williams** (Detroit, January 2020); **Nijeer Parks** (Woodbridge NJ, February 2019); **Porcha Woodruff** (Detroit, February 2023, arrested while eight-months pregnant); **Randal Reid** (Louisiana, November 2022, held six days for a Jefferson Parish robbery despite never having visited the state). Settlements 2023-2025 ranged $300K-$4M.

    **EU AI Act Article 5 prohibitions** (Regulation 2024/1689, entered force August 2024, prohibitions applicable from 2 February 2025): ban real-time biometric identification in public spaces for law enforcement (with narrow exceptions), social-scoring by public authorities or private actors, emotion recognition in workplaces and educational institutions, predictive policing solely based on profiling, untargeted scraping of facial images, manipulative subliminal techniques, and exploitation of vulnerabilities of specific groups.

  - ### 6. Child Safety Harms

    **CSAM production and growth**: The **Internet Watch Foundation 2024 Annual Report** documented **291,273 webpages confirmed actioned** containing child sexual abuse material — a 92% increase over 2023. The **IWF October 2024 update** found **18% of newly-discovered CSAM in 2024 contained AI-generated imagery**, with self-generated content (often from "sextortion" coercion) comprising 91% of CSAM involving 11-13-year-olds.

    **Sewell Setzer III and Garcia v Character Technologies**: Sewell Setzer III, aged 14, died by suicide **28 February 2024** in Orlando, Florida, after months of intensive use of Character.AI, including roleplay with a "Daenerys" chatbot to which he had become emotionally attached. His mother Megan Garcia filed **Garcia v Character Technologies Inc., Google LLC et al.** in the Middle District of Florida on **22 October 2024** (Case 6:24-cv-01903) alleging strict product liability, negligence, deceptive trade practices, and wrongful death. **22 May 2025**: Judge Anne Conway denied most of the defendants' motion to dismiss, allowing product-defect, failure-to-warn, and unjust-enrichment claims to proceed — a landmark ruling that AI chatbots can be treated as defective products. Partial settlement reportedly reached **January 2026** with safety commitments and undisclosed compensation.

    **Sextortion of boys**: The **FBI/DOJ joint advisory January 2024** noted at least **20 US suicides 2021-2024** linked to financial sextortion targeting boys aged 14-17, with perpetrators (often West African organised crime networks per UK NCA 2024 alert) coercing victims into sending images then demanding payment under threat of distribution.

    **UK regulatory frame**: The **ICO Age-Appropriate Design Code (Children's Code)** entered force **2 September 2021**, requiring data-protection-by-design with 15 standards (high-privacy defaults, no nudge techniques, age-appropriate data minimisation). **Online Safety Act Phase 2 child-safety duties** commenced **25 July 2025**, requiring "highly effective age assurance" for services likely to be accessed by children and proactive removal of priority harmful content. **5Rights Foundation** (founded by Baroness Beeban Kidron) provides specialised child-rights advocacy.

  - ### 7. Environmental Harms

    **Data-centre electricity**: The **IEA Electricity 2024 report** (January 2024) projects global data-centre electricity demand will reach **1,000 TWh by 2026**, doubling from ~460 TWh in 2022. AI workloads are the fastest-growing component. **Google sustainability report 2024**: 24.2 billion litres of water consumed 2023 (mostly evaporative cooling), 14.3 MtCO2e total emissions (up 48% since 2019, primarily from data-centre electricity). **Microsoft sustainability report 2024**: Scope 3 emissions up 30.9% since 2020. **Meta sustainability report 2024**: 2023 emissions 9.5 MtCO2e.

    **Training emissions**: Strubell et al. (2019) **"Energy and Policy Considerations for Deep Learning in NLP"** established the methodology baseline: training BERT-base emits 0.65 tCO2e, training a Transformer-Big with neural architecture search emits 284 tCO2e. GPT-3 training (175B params, 2020) estimated 552 tCO2e. GPT-4-scale runs (2023-2024, 1-2T params) estimated 5,000-10,000 tCO2e per training run.

    **E-waste**: The **UN Global E-waste Monitor 2024** documented **62 million tonnes** generated globally 2022, with recycling lagging at 22.3%. Embedded carbon of GPUs (Nvidia H100 ~150 kg CO2e per unit), TPUs, and consumer devices is largely unreported.

  - ### 8. Labour Harms

    **Crowdwork**: Mechanical Turk workers earned median **$2/hour without benefits, appeal rights, or labour-law protection** per Hara et al. CHI 2018 study of 3.8 million tasks; subsequent updates (Hara et al. 2023) show median wages stagnant. Argentine workers labelling autonomous-vehicle data via Sama and Scale earn $1-3/hour.

    **Kenyan content moderation for OpenAI**: Time magazine investigation **"OpenAI Used Kenyan Workers on Less Than $2 Per Hour to Make ChatGPT Less Toxic"** (Billy Perrigo, 18 January 2023) documented Sama contractors paid **$1.32-$2/hour** to label graphic CSAM, bestiality, suicide, and violence content as RLHF-safety training data. Sama terminated the contract February 2023. **Daniel Motaung v Meta/Sama** Nairobi labour court action filed May 2022, **Meta lost jurisdictional appeal September 2023**, case ongoing 2025. Separate **184 Facebook moderators class action** filed 2023.

    **Philippine and other Global South moderation**: Manila is the world's largest content-moderation hub by headcount, with TikTok, Meta, and YouTube outsourcing to Accenture, Telus International, and TaskUs. PTSD and trauma documented in Pia Lopez (NYU dissertation 2023) and the Coalition of Asian Content Moderators 2023 statement.
