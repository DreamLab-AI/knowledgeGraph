The International Terrestrial Reference System (ITRS) is the Earth-fixed geocentric reference system that the International Earth Rotation and Reference Systems Service defines, realises and promotes, adopted by the International Union of Geodesy and Geophysics in 2007.[^1]

Its definition requires a geocentric origin at the centre of mass of the whole Earth, including oceans and atmosphere, and a unit of length that is the SI metre, with a scale consistent with Geocentric Coordinate Time.[^1] Its orientation was initially given by the BIH orientation at 1984.0, and its time evolution rests on a no-net-rotation condition for horizontal tectonic motions.[^1]

Primary realisations are International Terrestrial Reference Frames, produced by the ITRS Center of the IERS.[^1] The USNO circular describes the frame as the list of observing stations and their coordinates, expressed as geocentric rectangular vectors, so it does not depend on a reference ellipsoid.[^2]

The IERS chapter retained here is an update of 1 April 2019 whose current realisation is ITRF2014, and the circular is from 2005, so later releases are not stated here.[^1][^2]
