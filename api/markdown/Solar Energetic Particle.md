A solar energetic particle is a charged particle, most importantly a proton, accelerated in the solar atmosphere to a large fraction of the speed of light by a large-scale magnetic eruption.[^1] NOAA describes such eruptions as often producing a coronal mass ejection and an associated solar flare. When the fast protons reach Earth they can penetrate the magnetosphere that shields the planet from lower energy charged particles.[^1]

Energetic protons can penetrate deep into spacecraft or people and damage electronic circuits or biological DNA, according to NOAA, and passengers and crew of high-flying aircraft at high latitudes may face radiation risk during more extreme storms. NOAA does not supply dose figures here.[^1]

NASA more generally notes that solar activity releases energy and particles, some of which affect Earth.[^2] Flares are a separate phenomenon, an eruption of electromagnetic radiation, so a flare and a particle event are related in origin but are not the same thing.[^3]
