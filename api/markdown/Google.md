- ### Definition
  - **Google LLC** (trading as **Google**, parent corporation **Alphabet Inc.**, NASDAQ: GOOGL/GOOG) is an American multinational technology conglomerate founded in September 1998 by Larry Page and Sergey Brin at Stanford University, headquartered in Mountain View, California (Googleplex), that has emerged by 2024-2026 as the most consequential full-stack [[Artificial General Intelligence]] research and deployment organisation on earth — uniquely possessing vertically integrated capability across AI research ([[Google DeepMind]], post-April 2023 merger of Google Brain and DeepMind), frontier model development ([[Gemini]] model family 1.0 through 3.1 as of 2026), on-device silicon ([[Tensor Chip]] G-series SoCs), cloud-scale custom accelerators (TPU v5/v6 Trillium), internet-scale data infrastructure (Search, YouTube, Gmail, Maps, [[Android]] — six products with over two billion users each), cloud hyperscaling ([[Cloud Computing]] Google Cloud Platform, AI Hypercomputer), and consumer AI surfaces ([[Anthropic Claude]] analogue: Gemini assistant, [[NotebookLM]], AI Overviews). Google simultaneously faces the most significant antitrust enforcement actions in the tech industry since United States v. Microsoft (2001): Judge Amit Mehta's August 2024 ruling found Google illegally maintained monopoly power in general search and search advertising, and the European Commission's March 2024 DMA non-compliance proceedings under [[Digital Markets Act]] gatekeeper designation across eight core platform services including [[AI Search]], [[Android]], Chrome and YouTube add to a complex compliance landscape.   - ## About Google / Alphabet

  - **Google LLC**, a wholly-owned subsidiary of **Alphabet Inc.** (NASDAQ: GOOGL, GOOG), founded 4 September 1998 by Larry Page and Sergey Brin in Menlo Park, California (now headquartered at the Googleplex, 1600 Amphitheatre Parkway, Mountain View, CA), has evolved from the world's dominant search engine company into the largest vertically integrated artificial intelligence organisation in existence. Under CEO Sundar Pichai (Alphabet CEO since 2019, Google CEO since 2015) and [[Google DeepMind]] CEO Demis Hassabis, the company's declared "AI-first" strategic pivot — announced at Google I/O 2016 — reached its most consequential expression in 2023-2026 through the merger of Google Brain and DeepMind into **Google DeepMind** (April 2023), the launch of the [[Gemini]] model family, and the deployment of [[Agentic Internet]] capabilities across every major Google product surface.

    Alphabet's corporate structure as of 2025:

    - **Google LLC** (primary operating entity): [[AI Search]], YouTube, Maps, [[Android]], Chrome, Gmail, Google Cloud, all AI products, Google Ads
    - **Other Bets**: Waymo (autonomous vehicles), Verily (life sciences), Wing (drone delivery), Calico (longevity), DeepMind Health
    - **Isomorphic Labs** (wholly-owned DeepMind spin-out): drug discovery via AlphaFold; Eli Lilly ($1.7B) and Novartis ($1.2B) partnerships

    - Key executives: Sundar Pichai (Alphabet/Google CEO), Demis Hassabis ([[Google DeepMind]] CEO), Koray Kavukcuoglu (Chief Science Officer), Liz Reid (Google Search VP), Thomas Kurian (Google Cloud CEO)
    - Employees: ~181,000 worldwide (FY2024); headquarters Mountain View CA with major offices in London, New York, Singapore, Zurich, Seoul
    - Market cap: approximately $2.0-2.1 trillion (2025), third or fourth largest globally alongside Microsoft, Apple, NVIDIA

  - ## Gemini Model Family (2023-2026)

  - Google's [[Gemini]] represents the most extensively deployed frontier model ecosystem in 2026, spanning on-device (Nano), efficiency (Flash, Flash-Lite), standard (Pro), and frontier (Ultra, Deep Think) tiers across versions 1.0 through 3.1. Each generation extended context, multimodal capability, and agentic reasoning.

  - ### Gemini 1.0 (December 2023)

    Three-tier family (Ultra, Pro, Nano) trained natively multimodal from the ground up on text, images, audio, video, and code using Google's Pathways distributed training system. Gemini 1.0 Ultra exceeded GPT-4 performance on 30/32 academic benchmarks (MMLU, HumanEval, GSM8K, BIG-Bench Hard) at launch — though critics noted launch demo staging issues — representing Google's first competitive frontier model response to the OpenAI/Microsoft ChatGPT moment.

    - Gemini 1.0 Pro replaced Google Bard; Bard rebranded to **Gemini Assistant** in February 2024
    - Gemini 1.0 Nano was the first on-device [[Cognitive AI]] deployment on Pixel 8 Pro at 512M-1B parameter scale
    - Architecture: natively multimodal Transformer, trained with Google Pathways distributed framework
    - Context window: 32,000 tokens (Pro), 8,192 tokens (Nano)

  - ### Gemini 1.5 (February 2024)

    The landmark architectural innovation was the introduction of a **1 million token context window** via Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) architecture and Ring Attention mechanisms — later expanded to **2 million tokens**, the longest context of any commercially deployed model at time of release.

    - Gemini 1.5 Pro: processes entire books (~700K tokens), one-hour video files, or 30,000+ lines of code in a single context with strong retrieval accuracy across the full window (98%+ needle-in-haystack)
    - Gemini 1.5 Flash: sub-$0.10/1M-token efficiency tier enabling cost-effective deployment of [[Large Language Models]] at scale
    - MoE architecture: sparse activation of a subset of parameters per forward pass, mirroring DeepMind's Chinchilla-influenced compute efficiency thinking
    - Deployed across [[AI Search]] AI Overviews (US launch May 2024), Google Workspace AI, and Vertex AI

  - ### Gemini 2.0 (December 2024)

    Positioned as the **"agentic era" model**, Gemini 2.0 Flash and 2.0 Pro introduced a step-change in [[Agent Frameworks]] capability:

    - Native tool use and function calling (Google Search, code execution, third-party APIs) without prompt engineering
    - Multimodal output generation: text + images + steerable TTS audio simultaneously in a single response
    - Improved planning and multi-step task completion for extended [[Agentic Internet]] horizons
    - Real-time streaming via Google AI Studio and Vertex AI for low-latency agentic applications
    - 1M token context at Flash tier; roughly 2x inference throughput vs 1.5 Flash at equivalent quality
    - Underpins Project Astra (universal AI assistant), Project Mariner (browser agent), and Jules (AI code agent)

  - ### Gemini 2.5 (June 2025)

    Gemini 2.5 Pro and Flash reached general availability on 17 June 2025; Gemini 2.5 Flash-Lite launched simultaneously as an ultra-low-cost tier.

    - 2.5 Pro **"deep thinking" mode**: extended reasoning via chain-of-thought and self-consistency analogous to OpenAI o1/o3
    - Competitive with [[Anthropic Claude]] 3.7 Sonnet thinking mode on AIME (mathematics), Codeforces (coding), and GPQA (science) benchmarks
    - Flash-Lite: sub-$0.01/1M token pricing targeting high-volume [[AI Search]] and [[AI Adoption]] enterprise workflows

  - ### Gemini 3.0 and 3.1 (November 2025 – April 2026)

    Gemini 3 Pro and 3 Deep Think were announced 18 November 2025, with **Gemini 3.1 Pro** becoming the most capable model in the family by April 2026.

    - **77.1% ARC-AGI-2** — the leading test of general reasoning and novel problem-solving capability
    - ~35% coding accuracy improvement over 2.5 Pro
    - Dynamic thinking: adaptive inference-time compute allocation based on problem difficulty, enabled by default
    - Underpins **Deep Research Max** with MCP (Model Context Protocol) support and native visualisations for long-horizon autonomous research
    - Token pricing: 3.1 Pro at ~$7/1M input tokens (competitive with [[Anthropic Claude]] 4 Sonnet)

  - ## AI Products and Applications

  - ### AI Overviews and AI Mode (Search)
  - Google's transformation of [[AI Search]] from keyword-retrieval to AI-generated synthesis represents the largest single deployment of [[Large Language Models]] in human history. **Search Generative Experience (SGE)** launched in limited preview at Google I/O May 2023, was rebranded to **AI Overviews** and broadly deployed in the US in May 2024, expanding globally through H2 2024. By Q1 2025 AI Overviews reached **1.5 billion monthly users**, making it the most widely used LLM application globally by user count.   - ## Hardware: TPU and Tensor Silicon

    - **TPU v5e**: cost-per-token optimised for inference, enabling economical [[AI Search]] AI Overviews and Gemini API serving

  - ## Antitrust and Regulatory Landscape

  - Google faces the most complex concurrent antitrust and regulatory enforcement environment in the technology sector as of 2026.

  - ### United States v. Google LLC (Search Monopoly, 2020/2024)

    Filed October 2020 by the DOJ, prosecuted through a nine-week bench trial in Autumn 2023. Central allegation: Google's exclusionary distribution contracts violated Sherman Act Section 2 by foreclosing rivals from default distribution channels.

    - Google paid Apple approximately **$20 billion annually** (2022 estimate) for Google Search as default Safari/iOS search engine
    - Similar exclusive revenue-share contracts with Android OEMs (Samsung, LG, Motorola) and browser vendors (Mozilla, Opera)
    - **5 August 2024**: Judge Amit Mehta ruled Google illegally maintained monopoly power in **general search services** and **search text advertising**
    - Google processed approximately 90% of US search queries; exclusionary contracts (not organic superiority) found to be primary causal mechanism
    - **September 2025 remedies ruling**: DOJ requests for structural divestitures (Chrome, contingent Android) rejected as disproportionate
    - Behavioural remedies imposed: prohibition of exclusive default-search distribution contracts; mandatory sharing of search index data and user interaction data with competitors
    - Google announced appeal of liability and remedies; Supreme Court review possible by 2027-2028
    - Commercial risk: Apple Safari default search estimated $15-25B annual revenue contribution if Apple distributes search across multiple providers

  - ### United States v. Google LLC (Ad Tech, 2023)

    Separate DOJ case (filed January 2023, Virginia federal court) targeting advertising technology stack dominance:

    - Defendants: Google Ad Manager/DFP (publisher ad server), AdX (ad exchange), DV360/Google Ads (advertiser buying tools)
    - DOJ theory: simultaneous operation on buyer and seller sides of programmatic advertising constitutes illegal monopolisation
    - Trial proceedings ran through 2024-2025; DOJ sought divestiture of Google Ad Manager publisher components
    - Potential impact: structural disruption of Google's estimated $50B+ annual advertising technology revenue stream
    - [[AI Liability]] implications: AI-powered programmatic advertising raises new questions about algorithmic price-fixing and auction manipulation

  - ### EU Digital Markets Act (DMA) Enforcement

    Google (Alphabet) was designated a **DMA Gatekeeper** in September 2023 — the broadest gatekeeper designation of any company in the DMA framework:

    - **Eight designated core platform services**: Search, YouTube, [[Android]], Chrome, Google Maps, Google Play, Google Shopping, Google Ads
    - DMA obligations applicable from 7 March 2024
    - **25 March 2024**: Commission opened formal non-compliance proceedings against Alphabet, Apple, and Meta
    - **March 2025 preliminary breach findings**: (i) Google Play anti-steering rules blocking app developers from better offers; (ii) Google Search self-preferencing Alphabet's comparison services (Shopping, Flights, Hotels)
    - Google launched [[AI Search]] AI Overviews in Europe one week after the preliminary finding — DMA requires "equivalent opportunity" for third-party services within AI-generated answer cards
    - **July 2025**: Additional DMA complaint lodged targeting user choice restrictions
    - Google's 19-page challenge to enforcement: DMA compliance "degraded some of our services"
    - Maximum fines: **10% of global annual turnover** (~$35B+) per sustained non-compliance finding; 20% for repeat infringements
    - Cumulative EU [[AI Liability]] and [[Asia Pacific Regulation]] exposure across DMA + AI Act could reach $50B+ in worst-case scenarios

  - ### EU AI Act (GPAI Systemic Risk Tier)

    [[Gemini]] 2.5/3.x training compute estimated to exceed the EU AI Act's **10^25 FLOP systemic-risk threshold**:

    - Classified as Tier 2 systemic-risk GPAI provider under EU AI Act (obligations effective August 2025)
    - Required: mandatory transparency reports; adversarial red-teaming by independent bodies; incident notification to EU AI Office; copyright due diligence on training data
    - Google DeepMind joined EU AI Pact (voluntary pre-compliance) in 2024 alongside OpenAI, Microsoft, Anthropic
    - [[California AI bill]] equivalents in multiple US states create additional fragmented compliance obligations
    - [[AI Liability]] frameworks in the EU and UK may impose civil liability for Gemini-generated harm in high-risk domains (healthcare, legal advice)

  - ## Components and Architecture

  - ### Training Infrastructure

    [[Gemini]] 3.x training runs operate on Google's proprietary **Pathways** distributed ML framework:

    - Training a single Gemini 3.1 Pro model involved multi-month runs across tens of thousands of Trillium chips
    - Continuous checkpointing, automated failure recovery, and cross-datacenter gradient synchronisation via Jupiter 13 Petabit/second fabric
