- ### Current Landscape (2026)
  - The stack has consolidated around open interoperability standards: the Alliance for OpenUSD ratified Core Specification 1.0 at the end of 2025, with a Physics Rigid Body 1.0 spec targeting Q2 2026 ratification and a defined "journey to ISO" via a JTC1 PAS submission.
  - Formal metaverse standardisation reached the international tier in 2026, with ISO/IEC FDIS 24931-1 (Metaverse — Part 1: Concepts, characteristics and technology) entering its FDIS ballot in June 2026 under ISO/IEC JTC1 SC24/WG10.
  - The Metaverse Standards Forum (2,600+ members) and RP1 launched Sneeze on 15 June 2026 via the Open Metaverse Browser Initiative — the first open-source (Apache 2.0) metaverse browser engine, built on HTTPS/DNS, Khronos (OpenXR, glTF, ANARI, SPIR-V), W3C (WebAssembly, DIDs) and OGC GeoPose.
  - 3D asset interoperability advanced concretely: the Khronos glTF KHR Gaussian splatting extension reached release-candidate status with ratification expected around Q2 2026, alongside active glTF-to-OpenUSD roundtripping work and OpenPBR/MaterialX material alignment.
  - The hardware and platform layer shifted in late 2025 — Google and Samsung shipped the Android XR operating system (October 2025), Meta released Ray-Ban Display AI glasses (September 2025), and Niantic Spatial spun off (May 2025) as a dedicated spatial/geospatial-AI infrastructure firm.
  - Capital is rotating from consumer VR to the industrial/enterprise metaverse and AI infrastructure: Meta's Reality Labs lost roughly USD 19.2bn in FY2025 (about USD 88bn cumulative by Q2 2026) and moved Horizon Worlds into maintenance mode, while the spatial-computing market (~USD 179.5bn in 2025) saw over USD 47bn in 2025 deal value, led by Siemens' USD 5.1bn Dotmatics acquisition and NVIDIA Omniverse-driven digital twins in airports, hospitals and factories.
  - Open challenges as of 2026 centre on fragmentation and governance: ETSI's 2025 Standards Landscape Report tracked 912 standards across eight Virtual Worlds domains yet found Infrastructure comparatively under-served (only ~20 standards in development), leaving shared reference architectures, cloud-edge orchestration, low-latency (sub-20ms motion-to-photon) networking, persistent identity/DIDs and responsible data governance as the frontier.
