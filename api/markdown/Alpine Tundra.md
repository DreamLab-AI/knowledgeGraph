Alpine tundra is a tundra ecosystem at high altitude where low temperatures and high winds stunt the vegetation.[^1][^2]

The National Park Service places it above the elevational treeline and says that the climate and soils of high-elevation alpine tundra prevent tree growth.[^2] The same page lists the defining traits of tundra in general as frost- or ice-molded landscapes, very low temperatures, little precipitation, poor nutrients and short growing seasons.[^2]

The Park Service distinguishes alpine tundra from Arctic tundra, which lies above the latitudinal treeline, noting that alpine tundra typically lacks permafrost and has more porous soils.[^2] SWEET treats the term as a subclass of tundra within its cryosphere realm.[^1]

Retained sources do not establish a treeline elevation, a vegetation cover threshold or a satellite method for delineating the biome. Both sources are descriptive, and the Park Service page is written from an Arctic Alaska perspective.[^1][^2]
