An aquifer is a geological body with enough saturated, permeable material to yield significant quantities of water to wells and springs.[^1][^2] The University of Arizona guide adds that the water is held in fractures and cracks of rock or in the pores between grains of sand and gravel.[^3]

The British Geological Survey separates an unconfined aquifer beneath a standing water table from one confined by an overlying impermeable or weakly permeable horizon.[^2] SWEET places the class under hydrosphere feature and restricts it to being part of a saturated zone.[^4]

Functionally, the definition rests on yield to a well or spring, so the same rock can count as an aquifer for one use and not for another. No retained source gives a yield figure that fixes the boundary, and none describes how remote sensing identifies an aquifer directly.
