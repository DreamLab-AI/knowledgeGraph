Earth observation is the gathering of data about the planet from space-based sensors, which ESA says gives coverage across both space and time.[^1][^2]

ESA describes several instrument classes. Optical instruments record reflected energy across wavelengths that include visible light and infrared bands. Radar instruments send microwave pulses towards the surface and record the reflections. Which class suits a task depends on what is being measured, and ESA also notes that spatial resolution varies with the orbit and intended function of an instrument.[^1]

Agencies frame the purpose in different ways. NASA's Earth Science Division describes observing the oceans, land cover, ice, atmosphere and life, and the UK Space Agency states that Earth observation supports economic, societal and environmental benefit. CEOS describes its handbook as setting out the capabilities, applications and missions of satellite Earth observation.[^2][^3][^4]

These sources describe satellite observation. They do not define airborne or ground-based observation, and they do not establish how well any one product meets a given need, which depends on the mission, instrument and processing.[^1][^4]
