Spin stabilisation is a method of holding a spacecraft's attitude by setting the whole vehicle spinning so that the gyroscopic action of the rotating mass acts as the stabilising mechanism.[^1]

NASA describes thrusters that are fired only occasionally to change the spin rate or the spin-stabilised attitude, so the attitude is not continuously actuated. Pioneer 10 and 11, Lunar Prospector and the Galileo orbiter and its atmospheric probe are given as examples of spinning spacecraft.[^1] A spinning vehicle also provides a continuous sweeping motion, which the same source says suits fields and particles instruments and some optical scanning instruments.[^1]

Smallsat guidance notes mention that magnetic torquers can slowly spin up a spinner by applying an external torque over time, and that thrusters can supply the large torques used for spin stabilisation. That chapter concentrates on three-axis controlled small spacecraft, so it treats spinners briefly.[^2]

Sources opened here do not give spin rates, stiffness figures or pointing accuracy for spinners, and do not compare spin stabilisation with three-axis control quantitatively. Dual-spin designs are not covered, so the term applies to a vehicle spun as a whole.[^1][^2]
