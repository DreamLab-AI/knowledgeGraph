A space station is a spacecraft capable of supporting a human crew in orbit for an extended period of time.[^1]

The NASA Space Life Sciences Ontology adds that a station lacks major propulsion or landing systems and must have docking ports so that other spacecraft can transfer crew and supplies. It gives Mir as an example of a station that operated in low Earth orbit.[^1]

The International Space Station is described by NASA as the largest humanmade object to orbit Earth. NASA reports that it orbits within a stated altitude band, falls towards Earth through atmospheric friction and needs periodic rocket firings to boost its orbit, and cautions that its dimensions and mass vary as resupply vehicles come and go.[^2]

Stations serve as platforms for research, including human health studies described by NASA and ESA. The sources reviewed do not enumerate all stations or investigations.[^3][^4]
