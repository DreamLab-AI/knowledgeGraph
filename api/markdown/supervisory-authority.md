- ### Definition
  - A supervisory authority is an independent public body empowered by law to monitor, enforce, and advise on compliance with a regulatory regime within a jurisdiction. It is a concrete instrument of [[Regulation]] and a pillar of [[Governance]], exercising statutory powers of investigation and enforcement.
  - In data-protection law it oversees how organisations handle personal data under [[GDPR]], conducts [[Audit]]s, and drives [[Regulatory Compliance]].

- ### Overview
  - Regulation only has effect if some body is responsible for applying it. The supervisory authority is that body: a statutory institution that interprets rules, monitors regulated entities, investigates breaches, and imposes corrective measures or penalties. Its independence from the entities it supervises, and often from day-to-day political direction, is essential to its credibility.
  - The term is most precise in European data-protection law, where each member state designates one or more supervisory authorities to enforce the [[GDPR]], coordinate through the European Data Protection Board, and act as the contact point for complaints. But the concept generalises to financial conduct regulators, competition authorities, and other sectoral overseers.
  - Supervisory authorities combine several functions: ex-ante guidance and rule-making, ongoing supervision and examination, and ex-post investigation and enforcement. The balance between these reflects the maturity and risk profile of the regulated sector.

- ### Key aspects
  - **Statutory mandate** — Authority is conferred by legislation, defining the scope, powers, and independence of the body and grounding it in [[Regulation]].
  - **Supervision and audit** — Ongoing monitoring through reporting requirements, inspections, and [[Audit]] verifies that regulated entities maintain [[Compliance]].
  - **Investigation** — On receiving complaints or detecting risk, the authority can compel information, examine systems, and interview personnel.
  - **Enforcement** — Powers range from warnings and corrective orders to substantial administrative fines, licence revocation, and referral for prosecution.
  - **Guidance and rule-making** — Authorities publish guidelines, codes of conduct, and decisions that clarify how the law applies in practice, shaping [[Regulatory Compliance]] across the sector.
  - **Independence and accountability** — To be trusted, the authority must be independent of the regulated industry yet accountable to the legislature and the public.

- ### Applications
  - **Data protection** — National authorities enforce [[GDPR]] and equivalent laws, protecting individuals' [[Data Protection]] rights and sanctioning unlawful processing.
  - **Financial supervision** — Conduct and prudential regulators, alongside the [[Central Bank]], supervise banks, insurers, and markets for stability and consumer protection.
  - **Competition and markets** — Competition authorities investigate anti-competitive conduct and review mergers.
  - **Sector-specific oversight** — Telecoms, energy, healthcare, and other regulators apply domain-specific rules and licensing.
  - **Cross-border coordination** — Authorities cooperate through boards and networks to handle entities operating across jurisdictions.
