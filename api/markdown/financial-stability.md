- ### Definition
  - Financial stability refers to the condition in which the financial system — comprising banks, markets, payment infrastructure, and intermediaries — can absorb shocks and continue to perform its core functions of credit intermediation, risk transfer, and payment settlement. In the context of blockchain and crypto-assets, financial stability analysis examines how the growth of crypto markets, the interconnections between crypto and traditional finance, and the failure of large participants can propagate systemic risk. Regulatory bodies such as the Financial Stability Board, BIS, and IMF monitor these dynamics and advise on macroprudential policy responses.

- ### Content
  - Financial stability is a macroprudential objective: ensuring that the financial system as a whole continues to function efficiently under stress. It is monitored by central banks, financial stability boards, and international organisations. In the context of digital assets, financial stability concerns arise across several dimensions.
  - First, crypto-asset market volatility can spill over into traditional financial markets when there is significant interconnection — for example, through crypto-native firms holding traditional collateral, or institutional investors with material exposure to crypto assets. Second, stablecoins represent a structural financial stability risk analogous to money market funds: a sudden loss of confidence can trigger runs, as demonstrated in the depegging events of algorithmic stablecoins. Regulators have responded by proposing reserve requirements and redemption rights for stablecoin issuers. Third, the failure of large centralised crypto entities (exchanges, lenders, custodians) can expose retail and institutional creditors to significant losses and erode confidence in the broader ecosystem.
  - Regulatory frameworks designed to address financial stability in crypto include the EU's Markets in Crypto-Assets (MiCA) regulation, which establishes reserve and redemption requirements for e-money tokens and asset-referenced tokens; the Basel Committee's prudential treatment of banks' exposures to crypto assets; and the Financial Stability Board's global regulatory framework recommendations. The intersection of Decentralised Finance with financial stability poses particular analytical challenges due to the absence of identifiable regulated entities and the automatic liquidation mechanisms embedded in DeFi lending protocols.
