An uncrewed spacecraft is a spacecraft that operates without a crew on board, and NASA's spacecraft classification chapter describes the robotic kind as specially designed and constructed systems that can function in specific hostile environments.[^1]

It arbitrarily designates eight broad classes: flyby, orbiter, atmospheric, lander, penetrator, rover, observatory, and communications and navigation spacecraft. Flyby spacecraft follow a continuous solar orbit or escape trajectory and are never captured into planetary orbit, while landers are designed to reach a planetary surface and survive long enough to telemeter data back to Earth.[^1]

NASA's onboard-systems chapter notes that not all classes of spacecraft have the same subsystems. An atmospheric probe, for example, may lack propulsion and attitude control subsystems entirely.[^1][^2]

NASA sources use the word robotic and do not define uncrewed or contrast it with crewed spacecraft. The equivalence drawn here is an editorial reading, and the eight-class scheme is the chapter's own grouping rather than a formal taxonomy.[^1]
