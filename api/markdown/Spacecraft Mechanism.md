A spacecraft mechanism is a mechanical device that deploys assemblies after launch or provides motion on a spacecraft.[^1][^2]

NASA's overview places such equipment in a mechanical devices subsystem. Its motion is often initiated but, once started, is not controlled by feedback. Pyrotechnic devices belong to this group and are used for tasks such as separation from the launch vehicle, and NASA lists single use, mechanical shock and handling hazards among their drawbacks.[^1]

A small-spacecraft review divides mechanisms into passive types, which use spring loading and consume no electrical energy, and active types, which are motorised. It names antennas, radiators, solar panels, gravity gradient booms and science instruments as common deployables.[^2]

Neither source gives reliability figures, and the sources read here do not set out a single classification of mechanisms. Mechanisms are distinct from the structure that carries them.[^1][^2]
