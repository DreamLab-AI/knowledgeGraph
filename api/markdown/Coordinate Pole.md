A coordinate pole is a pole of a spatial coordinate system, such as the north or south pole that defines the direction of the polar axis in a latitude and longitude frame.[^1][^2]

In the SWEET ontology module for space coordinates, pole is a class, magnetic pole is a subclass of it, and north pole and south pole are named individuals. The same module relates the equator to the poles by a perpendicular-to restriction. The file records class structure and labels but gives no prose definition, so it does not define poles geometrically.[^1]

The OGC specification gives a geocentric axis definition that runs from the centre of the modelled Earth parallel to its rotation axis and towards its north pole. It also describes a meridian as the intersection of an ellipsoid with a plane containing the shortest axis, and the term is generally used for the pole-to-pole arc.[^2]

Magnetic poles are a different thing. NOAA describes dip poles as positions where the geomagnetic field is vertical, notes that they migrate over time, and says they need not be antipodal. A coordinate pole should not be read as a magnetic pole unless the source says so.[^3]
