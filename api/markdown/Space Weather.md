Space weather describes changing conditions from the Sun, through the solar wind, to Earth's magnetosphere, ionosphere and upper atmosphere. It includes solar flares, energetic particles, coronal mass ejections and changes in the background solar wind. Their effects form a connected physical system, but they travel at different speeds and do not produce a single, uniform warning time.[^1]

## Effects and observation

Space-weather events can disturb spacecraft electronics, satellite drag, radio links, satellite navigation, aviation and electricity networks. The severity depends on the event, latitude, local geology, orbit, shielding and the design and operation of the affected infrastructure. A bright aurora can accompany enhanced coupling between the solar wind and magnetosphere, but it is not itself a measure of technological damage.

Forecasting combines solar and heliospheric images, upstream plasma and magnetic-field measurements, ground observatories, numerical models and expert interpretation. Products include nowcasts, watches, warnings and alerts. Each answers a different operational question, and all retain uncertainty. In particular, coronagraph observations can estimate a coronal mass ejection's direction and speed, while the magnetic orientation that strongly controls its impact is usually measured only shortly before arrival.[^2]

## UK capability

The Met Office Space Weather Operations Centre provides continuous forecasts and warnings for government, emergency responders, infrastructure operators and the public.[^3] UK capability also includes British Geological Survey geomagnetic services, university instrument teams and observatories, and research delivered through the 2019–2024 Space Weather Instrumentation, Measurement, Modelling and Risk programme.[^4]

The UK National Risk Register uses a severe, Carrington-like event to plan for disruption across several infrastructures.[^5] This is a resilience scenario rather than a prediction or an estimate of event frequency. Live conditions should be taken from current operational products, since a static ontology entry cannot represent a changing forecast.
