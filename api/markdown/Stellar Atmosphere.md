A stellar atmosphere is the outer part of a star, beginning with the photosphere as its bottom layer.[^1]

For the Sun, NASA describes the visible surface as the photosphere, with the thin chromosphere and the huge corona above it, and a transition zone between them.[^2] ESA adds that almost all of the Sun's radiation is emitted from the photosphere, a thin layer at the upper boundary of the convection zone.[^3] This layering is documented for the Sun, which can be resolved; it should not be assumed to be observed in the same detail on other stars.

NASA calls the corona's rise in temperature with distance from the surface one of the Sun's biggest mysteries, and none of the sources cited here establish a heating mechanism.[^2] For stars other than the Sun, reviews describe tracking activity indirectly through photospheric brightness and chromospheric and coronal diagnostics.[^4]

The SWEET ontology classifies photosphere, chromosphere and corona as kinds of stellar atmosphere, which supports the classification but gives no physical definition.[^5]
