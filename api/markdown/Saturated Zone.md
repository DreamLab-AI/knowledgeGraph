Below the water table, the saturated zone is the part of the subsurface in which the voids are completely filled with water.[^1][^2]

USGS gives the water table as the upper surface of the saturated zone, and says that water pressure below it is great enough for water to enter wells.[^1][^3] Water in the zone is referred to as ground water, a usage Physics Today calls traditional, where water flows freely into wells under pore pressure greater than atmospheric pressure.[^1][^2]

Above it lies the unsaturated zone, where the spaces contain both air and water and where water cannot be pumped by wells because capillary forces hold it too tightly.[^3][^2] USGS describes a transition zone called the capillary fringe between the unsaturated zone and the water table.[^1]

SWEET lists the class as a hydrosphere feature without a definition.[^4] The retained sources give no typical depths or thicknesses and say nothing of detecting the zone from space.[^1][^3]
