Multispectral imaging is the collection of image data over several different wavelength ranges, as done by a multispectral scanner.[^1]

The NRCan tutorial calls a scanning system that collects data over a variety of wavelength ranges a multispectral scanner and describes it as the most commonly used scanning system. Across-track scanners use a rotating mirror, while along-track scanners use a linear array of detectors at the focal plane of the lens system.[^1] Scanning systems can extend beyond the visible and near-infrared range of photographic systems into the thermal infrared, and can reach higher spectral resolution.[^1]

The Landsat 8 Operational Land Imager is described as collecting nine spectral bands at 30 metres and one panchromatic band at 15 metres, over a 185 km swath from 705 km altitude.[^2] Multi-band photographic systems that use separate lens systems per band may have bands that are hard to compare spatially and radiometrically.[^1]

The Landsat values describe one instrument and are not general limits. The sources do not define how many bands separate multispectral from other imaging, so that boundary is left open.

[^2]: NASA Landsat Science, “Operational Land Imager (Landsat 8)”. https://landsat.gsfc.nasa.gov/satellites/landsat-8/spacecraft-instruments/operational-land-imager/
