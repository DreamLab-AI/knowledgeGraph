A potentiometric surface is an imaginary surface representing the elevation and pressure head of groundwater, defined by the level to which water rises in a well or piezometer.[^1][^2]

USGS ties the term to confined aquifers, where the surface represents the potential height to which water will rise in wells completed in the aquifer.[^2] BGS says the groundwater level in a confined aquifer is more properly described as a potentiometric surface, the theoretical level to which water would rise if the confining layer were not present.[^3]

BGS adds that the water table is a particular potentiometric surface, and that the term replaces the earlier piezometric surface.[^1] Where several aquifers lie in a vertical sequence there may be several distinct water levels, and the surface may vary with depth where vertical flow is significant.[^3]

SWEET lists the class as a hydrosphere feature without a definition.[^4] The retained sources give no contouring method, no pressure or elevation figures, and nothing on mapping the surface from space, so the page bounds the term to its physical definition.[^1][^3]
