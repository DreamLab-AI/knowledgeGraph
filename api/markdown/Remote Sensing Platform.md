A remote sensing platform is the stable structure that carries a sensor at a distance from the target, such as a ground installation, an aircraft or balloon, or a spacecraft or satellite outside the atmosphere.[^1]

Ground-based sensors are often used to record detailed information about the surface for comparison with aircraft or satellite data. Aircraft, mostly fixed-wing, are often used to collect very detailed images and can collect data over virtually any part of the surface at any time. Cost is a significant factor in choosing among platform options, according to the tutorial.[^1]

Satellites permit repetitive coverage of the surface because of their orbits, and orbits are matched to the capability and objective of the sensors they carry. Geostationary orbits keep a satellite over one region, while near-polar orbits, many of them sun-synchronous, let a satellite cover most of the Earth over a period of time. Unlike aircraft, satellite sensors collect data only over the areas and at the times their orbits dictate.[^2]

Platform and sensor are separate choices. Distance between platform and target affects the detail a sensor can obtain and the total area it images, so a sensor far from its target typically views a larger area but with less detail.[^3] These sources describe platform classes in general terms and do not rank them for any particular task.
