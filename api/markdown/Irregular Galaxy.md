An irregular galaxy is a galaxy that does not have the regular shape of a spiral or an elliptical galaxy.[^1][^2]

Hubble used the term as a catch-all class for galaxies outside the regular categories, so the class is defined by what its members lack. The shapes are described as unusual, from rings to small groupings of stars.[^1][^2]

Some irregular shapes are attributed to interactions or collisions with other galaxies, and some gas-rich dwarf galaxies may have formed from material pulled out in such encounters. The sources say sometimes, so this is not a universal origin.[^1]

Irregular galaxies typically have lower masses and luminosities than spirals, often show relatively intense star formation, and can hold significant gas and dust with a mix of older and younger stars. The Large and Small Magellanic Clouds are the best-known examples in the textbook. These are tendencies, not properties that every member shares.[^1][^2]
