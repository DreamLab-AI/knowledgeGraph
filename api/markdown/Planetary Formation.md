Planetary formation is the process by which planets are thought to grow from dust and gas in the disk surrounding a young star.[^1]

NASA says scientists think planets likely start as dust grains and emerge from a donut-shaped disk of gas and dust around young stars.[^1] Dust clumps become pebbles, and pebbles become larger rocks that grind together and expand.[^1]

This account is a general outline and is stated by NASA as what scientists think. The source does not describe later stages, such as how large planets gather gas, or how any particular planet in the solar system came to have its present orbit.[^1]

NASA links planetary science missions and research to knowledge of the origin and evolution of the solar system, so formation models are tested against observations of existing bodies.[^2]

[^1]: NASA, “How Do Planets Form?”. https://science.nasa.gov/exoplanets/how-do-planets-form/
[^2]: NASA, “Planetary Science”. https://science.nasa.gov/planetary-science/
