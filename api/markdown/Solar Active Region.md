A solar active region is an area of intense and complex magnetic field on the Sun that is a source of solar eruptions.[^1]

Sunspots are the visible component of active regions. NASA attributes their darker, cooler appearance to concentrated magnetic field lines, so a sunspot and the active region that contains it are related but not the same thing.[^1]

NOAA states that solar flares usually take place in active regions, typically those associated with sunspot groups. The wording is hedged, and the source does not claim that every flare originates in an active region.[^2]

Observatories commonly track activity through sunspot counts. NOAA describes a convention in which each sunspot group and each umbra within a group are weighted in the total, though the source does not establish that all observatories apply identical rules.[^3]

[^2]: NOAA Space Weather Prediction Center, “Solar Flares (Radio Blackouts)”. https://www.spaceweather.gov/phenomena/solar-flares-radio-blackouts
[^3]: NOAA Space Weather Prediction Center, “Sunspots/Solar Cycle”. https://www.spaceweather.gov/phenomena/sunspotssolar-cycle
