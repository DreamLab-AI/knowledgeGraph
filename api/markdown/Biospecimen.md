A biospecimen is any material sample taken from a biological entity for testing, diagnostic, propagation, treatment or research purposes.[^1]

According to the space life sciences ontology, a biospecimen covers samples from living organisms and from the biological object after its life functions have stopped. A whole entity cannot be a biospecimen.[^1]

In NASA practice, the Biospecimen Sharing Program collects biospecimens to increase the scientific return of spaceflight and associated ground investigations. After dissection it submits the samples and their metadata to the NASA Biological Institutional Scientific Collection, a biorepository of non-human samples from NASA-funded spaceflight investigations and correlative ground studies.[^2][^3]

NASA reports that the stored tissues are held at several temperature regimes, are preserved in various fixatives and carry descriptive metadata. The collection holds non-human samples only, and the cited pages do not describe how human samples are handled.[^4][^3]
