- ### Definition
  - A [[Smart Contract]] is a self-executing digital agreement encoded on a blockchain where contractual terms are directly translated into code that automatically enforces and executes obligations when predetermined conditions are satisfied, eliminating intermediaries and enabling trustless transactions between parties.

- ### Content

  A smart contract represents a paradigm shift in how agreements are documented, executed, and enforced. Rather than relying on intermediaries to interpret and enforce contractual terms, smart contracts encode business logic directly into code that executes automatically on a blockchain network.

  Smart contracts eliminate intermediary costs, reduce settlement time from days to minutes or seconds, and provide transparent, auditable execution trails. They enable novel applications including decentralised exchanges, automated insurance claims, and programmable payment conditions that would be impractical in traditional systems.

  Smart contracts introduce computational complexity, immutability risks, and security vulnerabilities. Once deployed, code defects cannot be easily corrected, and logic errors may result in irreversible loss of funds. Auditing and formal verification techniques remain essential for high-value contracts.
