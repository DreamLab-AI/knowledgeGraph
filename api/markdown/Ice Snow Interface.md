An ice snow interface is the boundary between a snow layer and the ice beneath it, treated in the retained sources as a height that altimeters try to locate.[^1][^2] NOAA's snow depth product defines it through the geometry of snow-covered sea ice, with the interface height above sea level as one of the quantities.[^1]

In that approach a laser altimeter is assumed to scatter from the air and snow surface, while a Ku-band radar is assumed to scatter from the snow and ice interface, so the difference gives snow depth.[^1] Copernicus's Cryosphere paper notes that laboratory experiments show Ku-band signals can penetrate to the interface under cold and dry conditions.[^2]

SWEET declares the class as a kind of planetary boundary but gives no definition.[^3] The retained sources describe the interface only for snow on sea ice, so they do not establish its meaning on ice sheets or glaciers.

[^2]: The Cryosphere (Copernicus), “Multi-frequency altimetry snow depth estimates over heterogeneous snow-covered Antarctic summer sea ice – Part 2: Comparing airborne estimates with near-coincident CryoSat-2 and ICESat-2 (CRYO2ICE)”. https://tc.copernicus.org/articles/19/4193/2025/
