An elliptical galaxy is a galaxy with a shape ranging from completely round to oval, usually containing little gas and dust and showing very little organised structure.[^1]

NASA states that the stars in an elliptical galaxy orbit the core in random directions and are generally older than those in spiral galaxies, because little of the gas needed to form new stars remains. It describes elliptical galaxies as less common than spiral galaxies.[^1]

NASA says scientists think elliptical galaxies originate from collisions and mergers with spirals. This is stated as a view held by scientists rather than a result for every individual galaxy.[^1] Lenticular galaxies are described there as a cross between spirals and ellipticals, with a central bulge and disc but no arms, so a round or oval outline alone does not settle the type.[^1]

This page retains a single NASA overview of galaxy types. It does not give sizes, masses, subclasses or the numerical classification schemes used in surveys, and this page does not assert them.

[^1]: NASA, “Galaxy Types”. https://science.nasa.gov/universe/galaxies/types/
