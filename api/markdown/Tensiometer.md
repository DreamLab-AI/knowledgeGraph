A tensiometer is a soil instrument made of a sealed water-filled tube with a porous ceramic tip and a vacuum gauge, used to measure how tightly water is held in the soil.[^1][^2][^3]

Installation puts the ceramic tip at the chosen depth in the root zone. Water moves between the tube and the surrounding soil until the two are in equilibrium, and the pressure inside the tube registers on the gauge as soil water tension, also called matric potential.[^1][^3]

A reading is a tension, not a water content. The University of Hawaiʻi leaflet states that the measurement is not a direct measurement of soil water content, and the Florida publication says tension values must be converted through a soil characteristic curve to obtain moisture. A tensiometer reading should therefore not be reported as a moisture percentage without that step.[^1][^2]

To work accurately a tensiometer must stay filled with water and free of air bubbles, and the Oregon State guidance gives a limited working range beyond which air may enter the water column and break hydraulic continuity.[^3] Those retained sources are agricultural extension material. They do not describe tensiometers as an Earth observation product, so any use as an in-situ reference for satellite soil-moisture data lies outside what they establish.[^3]
