Outer space is the region beyond Earth's atmosphere, which the exosphere separates from the rest of the atmosphere.[^1]

NASA states that there is no clear boundary between where the atmosphere ends and outer space begins. Most scientists use the Karman line, located 100 kilometres above the surface, as a delineation for the transition.[^2]

That line is conventional. A peer-reviewed analysis describes it as widely but not universally accepted, records proposals ranging from 30 kilometres to well beyond the Moon, and argues for 80 kilometres as a more appropriate boundary. The sources do not give a legal definition.[^3]

The SWEET ontology models outer space as a class under the realm concept. That is an ontology modelling choice and not an astronomical or legal definition.[^4]
