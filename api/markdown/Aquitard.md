An aquitard is a confining bed that retards but does not prevent the flow of water to or from an adjacent aquifer, which is why it is also called a leaky confining bed.[^1] It does not readily yield water to wells or springs, but it may serve as a storage unit for groundwater.[^1]

Its place in the series is between an aquifer and an aquiclude: the BGS glossary defines the aquifer as yielding significant water to a borehole, well or spring and the aquiclude as unable to transmit it in significant amounts.[^2] The USGS glossary lists confining unit as the preferred term covering aquitard.[^1] SWEET places the class under hydrosphere feature without further axioms.[^3]

No retained source gives a leakage rate that separates an aquitard from an aquiclude, and the BGS glossary retained here has no separate aquitard entry.
