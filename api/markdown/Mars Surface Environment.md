Mars surface environment is the set of physical conditions at the surface of Mars, a cold, dusty desert world with a very thin atmosphere.[^1]

NASA describes the atmosphere as mostly carbon dioxide, nitrogen and argon, with winds that can occasionally raise dust storms.[^1] The Space Life Sciences Ontology places Mars surface environment under space environment, with martian surface environment as an exact synonym, and gives it no definition text.[^2]

Radiation at the surface has its own class. In radiation modelling the Martian atmosphere and regolith are treated as shielding, and one comparison paper reports that their composition details have little effect on surface dose rates in its sensitivity tests.[^3]

None of the retained sources gives site-specific pressure, temperature or dust values, so this page states none.
