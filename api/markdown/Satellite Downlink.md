
A satellite downlink is a transmission from a satellite towards an Earth station or terminal.

Downlink describes direction, not payload content. A spacecraft may send housekeeping telemetry, science or Earth-observation data, video, files, broadband traffic or other mission data. In operations, telemetry commonly returns on a downlink, but the term does not mean that every downlink is TT&C.[^1][^2][^5]

Capacity and availability depend on transmit power, antenna gain and orientation, ground-station sensitivity, bandwidth, propagation, modulation, coding and contact geometry. For low-orbit missions, contact windows and onboard storage affect data scheduling. The ITU handbook opened for this research is from 2011; its pass-planning examples illustrate operational constraints and are not current typical performance figures.[^1][^4][^6]

A downlink carrier can support tracking as well as data recovery. In coherent deep-space operation, a spacecraft can return a frequency related to a received ground uplink, enabling precise Doppler measurements. This is an operating mode, not an inherent property of all downlinks; one-way links may use an onboard reference.[^2][^7]

A relay satellite can forward information to Earth on a separate downlink hop. NASA documents an optical relay demonstration with an Earth-facing ground segment; the downlink remains subject to its own geometry, station access and atmospheric conditions. When describing an end-to-end route, identify the original source and each relay hop.[^3][^8]
