- ### Definition
- ISO 14001 is the international [[Standards]] specification for an environmental management system, published by [[ISO]]. It frames how organisations plan, operate, check, and improve their environmental performance, supporting [[Compliance]] and [[Environmental Sustainability]].
- Certification signals that an organisation runs a recognised, audited EMS.
- ### Overview
- The standard adopts the plan-do-check-act cycle: set objectives, implement controls, monitor results, and act to improve.
- It is generic and applies to organisations of any size or sector, focusing on the management system rather than prescribing specific performance levels.
- Conformance is verified by independent [[Audit]] leading to third-party [[Certification]].
- ### Key aspects
- Environmental policy: leadership commitment to legal compliance and continual improvement.
- Aspects and impacts: identifying how activities affect the environment.
- Objectives and controls: setting targets and operational procedures to manage impacts.
- Continual improvement: iterating the management cycle to raise performance over time.
- ### Mechanisms
- Documented procedures define responsibilities and operational controls.
- Internal and external audits assess conformance and surface non-conformities.
- Management review evaluates the system's effectiveness against objectives.
- Tools such as [[Lifecycle Assessment]] and [[Environmental Impact Assessment]] inform the analysis of impacts.
- ### Applications
- Demonstrating environmental governance to regulators, customers, and investors.
- Underpinning [[ESG]] reporting and [[Carbon Footprint]] reduction programmes.
- Aligning with related management standards such as [[ISO 9001]] and [[ISO 27001]] in integrated systems.
- Reducing environmental risk and operating cost through systematic control.
