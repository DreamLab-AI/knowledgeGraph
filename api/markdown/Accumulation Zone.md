An accumulation zone is the part of a glacier where snow accumulation exceeds melting during the summer.[^1][^2]

SWEET defines it more briefly as an ice accumulation zone that overlaps part of a glacier, and adds that it usually occurs at higher elevations and generally overlaps the conversion of snow to glacial ice.[^2] At its highest elevations a glacier gains new snow each year in this zone.[^3]

Ice then flows downslope from the accumulation zone to the ablation zone, and a glacier advances when more mass is added as new snow here than is lost in the ablation zone.[^3] The Park Service adds that the zone has a snow layer on top, a transitional firn layer beneath it and ice at the base.[^4]

Retained sources do not establish how the zone is delimited from satellite imagery or how its extent is quantified. SWEET and the Park Service pages agree on the position of the zone but give no numerical thresholds.[^2][^4]
