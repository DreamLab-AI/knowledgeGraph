Sea ice is any form of ice found at sea that has formed by the freezing of sea water.[^1][^2]

NSIDC says sea ice forms, grows and melts in salty ocean water, and that most of it is constantly moving.[^3] SWEET describes it as either discontinuous pieces moved by wind and currents, which it calls pack ice, or a motionless sheet attached to the coast, which it calls land-fast ice.[^1]

SWEET calls sea ice less than one year old first-year ice, and perennial ice is sea ice that survives at least one summer.[^1]

Glacier ice is a separate category. The Canadian Ice Service lists ice of land origin as ice formed on land or in an ice shelf and found floating in water, and SWEET states that icebergs are not sea ice.[^2][^1]

NSIDC gives equilibrium thicknesses of approximately 3 to 4 metres in the Arctic and typically 1 to 2 metres in Antarctica.[^3] The retained sources do not establish how sea ice extent or thickness is retrieved from space.[^2][^3]
