Earth observation processing is the staged conversion of raw instrument measurements into calibrated, derived and gridded data products, labelled by processing level.[^1][^2]

NASA processes its Earth Observing System data products at levels from Level 0 to Level 4, where Level 0 products are raw data at full instrument resolution and the data at higher levels are converted into more useful parameters and formats.[^1] Level 1A data are time-referenced and annotated with ancillary information, Level 2 holds derived geophysical variables at the same resolution and location as the Level 1 source data, Level 3 maps variables onto a uniform space-time grid, and Level 4 is model output or the results of analyses of lower-level data.[^1]

Radar providers follow the same idea. The SAR Handbook says that providers offer imagery in a range of processing levels, each progressively simplified and tailored to emphasise different components of the SAR information space.[^2]

A processing level records how far a product has been taken from the raw measurement, so it describes lineage and form rather than quality. NASA also notes that some products, such as near real-time data, are made available more quickly than standard processing.[^1] The retained sources do not establish a single level scheme shared by every agency, mission or sensor type, and the Level 3 and Level 4 descriptions are NASA wording only.[^1][^2]

[^2]: NASA Earthdata (SAR Handbook), “Spaceborne Synthetic Aperture Radar: Principles, Data Access, and Basic Processing Techniques”. https://earthdata.nasa.gov/s3fs-public/2025-04/SARHB_CH2_Content.pdf
