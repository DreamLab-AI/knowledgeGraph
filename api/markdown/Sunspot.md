A sunspot is a cooler, darker region on the Sun's photosphere associated with a concentration of magnetic field lines.[^1][^2]

NASA describes sunspots as the visible component of active regions, from which solar flares and coronal mass ejections typically erupt. SWPC describes the dark core, called the umbra, and a lighter surrounding penumbra, and notes that spots can last from hours to months.[^1][^2]

Sunspot number is a standard measure of solar activity. SWPC states that observatory reports count each sunspot group as ten and each umbra as one, and that the official daily and monthly numbers come from the World Data Center for the Sunspot Index and Long-term Solar Observations at the Royal Observatory of Belgium. The number varies with an approximately eleven-year cycle, and SWPC publishes a monthly-updated prediction for the current cycle.[^2][^3]

Sunspot pairs also carry magnetic polarities that reverse in each hemisphere from one cycle to the next. The sources give different temperature values for sunspots, so the page states none, and none establishes why sunspot numbers vary between individual cycles.[^4][^1][^2]
