Observation provenance is information about the entities, activities and people involved in producing an observation, which can be used to assess its quality, reliability or trustworthiness.[^1]

The W3C describes provenance in general terms and defines a family of documents for exchanging it, including a data model and an OWL2 ontology that maps the model to RDF. This general model is not specific to Earth observation, and the cited overview does not say which mission records an agency would keep.[^1]

For satellite data, the processing history of a product, including how an instrument was calibrated and how the product was validated, is part of what a user needs to judge it. CEOS defines calibration as defining a system's response to known and controlled inputs and validation as independent assessment of derived products, but its page does not describe provenance records.[^2]

Provenance records what was done and does not by itself establish that the result is accurate. Accuracy and uncertainty need their own statements.[^1][^2]

[^2]: CEOS, “Working Group on Calibration & Validation (WGCV)”. https://ceos.org/ourwork/workinggroups/wgcv/
