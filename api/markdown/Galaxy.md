A galaxy is a large group of stars, gas and dust bound together by gravity.[^1]

NASA describes galaxies as ranging from the smallest, with only a few hundred thousand stars, to the largest, with trillions of stars, and as found alone, in small groups or in large clusters.[^1]

Galaxies come in a variety of shapes, mostly spirals and ellipticals together with less orderly irregular forms, and most large galaxies have supermassive black holes at their centres.[^2]

The SWEET ontology models Galaxy as a subclass of astronomical body, which matches the class relation recorded for this page.[^3] These sources are educational and ontology texts and do not supply a formal IAU definition.[^1][^2]
