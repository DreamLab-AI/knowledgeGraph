
Satellite service coverage is the geographic area in which a specified satellite system can provide a defined service under stated radio, terminal, operational and authorisation conditions.

“Coverage” can describe different things. A physical beam footprint is formed by an antenna pattern on Earth and may be expressed using a specified gain or power-flux-density contour. Its edge depends on the contour and design assumptions; it is not simply a line beyond which no signal exists. ITU regulatory procedures also use an administratively defined service area, represented in some satellite-plan processes by test points. That service area need not match a −3 dB antenna contour, and a signal may reach locations outside the area without the same regulatory protection.[^1][^2]

A regulatory filing or licence is a separate matter from physical reach and customer service availability. Ofcom manages UK satellite filings to the ITU for international recognition and coordination. Its NGSO guidance addresses licences for UK gateways and user-terminal networks, spectrum use and interference cooperation. Ofcom states that its earth-station licences authorise spectrum use by UK-based stations connected to satellite systems, not operation of the satellites themselves.[^3][^4][^5][^6]

Even where a terminal lies within a physical footprint or a stated service area, actual service can depend on local authorisation, terminal eligibility, capacity, network status and the provider’s offer. Ofcom’s 2025 Kuiper decision, for example, authorises defined UK service and frequency use; it does not establish identical performance at every location or for every terminal.[^4][^7]

Coverage descriptions should name the system and service, the relevant contour or administrative area, and any date or operating assumptions. A global, regional or spot-beam label alone does not establish continuous availability or a specific service level.[^1][^2][^7]
