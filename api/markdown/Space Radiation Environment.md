A space radiation environment is the field of high-energy ionised particles present in space, which affects spacecraft, components and biological systems.[^1][^2]

NASA groups space radiation into particles trapped in the Earth's magnetic field, particles released during solar particle events, and galactic cosmic rays, and states that all of these are ionising radiation. ESA lists the same three groups in its space weather service description, using the term solar energetic particle events.[^3][^1]

NASA's Space Life Sciences Ontology defines a space radiation environment as one outside a commonly defined boundary marking the beginning of space, frequently the Karman line, and defines region-specific environments in more detail. This is an ontology definition and not the only usage.[^2]

Ionising radiation is described by NASA as much harder to avoid than non-ionising radiation. ESA states that cosmic radiation could increase cancer risk on long missions and that station crews are exposed to more radiation than some Earth workers, a comparison quoted in a feature article from 2017. Dose limits and risk estimates are not asserted here, because the sources reviewed do not set them out in a form suitable for citation.[^3][^4]
