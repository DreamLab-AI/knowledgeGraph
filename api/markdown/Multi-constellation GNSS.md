Multi-constellation GNSS is the use of signals from more than one global or regional navigation satellite system together to produce a combined solution.[^1][^2]

Navipedia cites the International Committee on GNSS definition of interoperability as the ability of systems and augmentations to be used together to give better capability at user level than a single system's open signals would. It describes a combined solution as one that generally shows benefits such as better accuracy or higher availability, and notes that a single system is often not enough in challenging conditions such as urban environments.[^1]

EUSPA states that receivers with multi-constellation capacity can combine signals from different constellations to provide greater positioning accuracy, and that Galileo is interoperable with GPS, GLONASS and BeiDou. Neither source quantifies the improvement, which depends on environment and receiver.[^2][^1]

Systems keep separate time references. For Galileo and GPS, the Galileo to GPS Time Offset is broadcast in the navigation message, which lets a combined receiver form one solution. As an alternative, a receiver can solve the offset as an additional unknown, at the cost of at least one more measurement. Signal-level interoperability holds only in some shared frequency bands, and legacy GLONASS signals use a different carrier arrangement, so combined processing differs by pair of systems.[^1][^2]
