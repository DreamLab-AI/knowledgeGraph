Earth orientation parameters are the quantities that describe the rotation of the International Terrestrial Reference System relative to the International Celestial Reference System, in conjunction with a conventional precession-nutation model.[^1]

In the IERS description they comprise the coordinates of the pole in the terrestrial frame, UT1 expressed as UT1 minus UTC or UT1 minus TAI, and the celestial pole offsets in longitude and obliquity relative to the direction given by the adopted precession and nutation theory. UT1 is the rotation angle about the pole.[^1][^2]

Space-geodetic observations determine the values observations. The IERS Rapid Service publishes Bulletin A with daily determinations of polar motion, UT1 minus UTC and celestial pole offsets, combined from VLBI, satellite laser ranging and GPS analyses, and it also provides predictions for a year ahead. Rapid values and later final values differ in latency, and the sources opened here do not give current accuracy figures.[^2][^1]

Frame transformations depend on these parameters. ESA's Navipedia explains that precession and nutation have analytical expressions, whereas the Earth's rotation and orientation cannot be modelled theoretically and must be updated periodically from observations. The IERS supplement and Navipedia articles predate current IAU and IERS model conventions, so they establish the parameter definitions and their purpose, not the present model details.[^3][^1]
