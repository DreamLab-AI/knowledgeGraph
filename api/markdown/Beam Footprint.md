A beam footprint is the region or contour on a specified surface associated with a defined geometric or radiometric boundary of an antenna beam.

A footprint description should state the surface and boundary criterion: for example, an intersection of an antenna’s relative-gain contour with Earth, or a constant-power-density contour under a stated model. The contour shape and scale depend on antenna pattern, frequency, pointing and viewing geometry. ITU-R S.672-4 is a design objective for geostationary fixed-satellite-service antenna patterns; it is not a universal measured footprint model.[^1][^2][^4]

A plotted footprint does not automatically represent an administrative service area. ITU filing procedures distinguish geographic service-area parameters from satellite beam contours, although a particular plan may relate the two, for example by requiring a specified contour to include a territory. Neither representation by itself proves uniform received quality, customer availability, licensing rights or actual service delivery.[^2][^3]

Multi-beam planning documents show illustrative overlaps and contour changes with off-axis geometry. Those figures are guidance or examples, not a current operator coverage map. Older calculations based on idealised antenna models likewise describe their assumed power-density contours, not actual side lobes or present-day service boundaries.[^1][^4]
