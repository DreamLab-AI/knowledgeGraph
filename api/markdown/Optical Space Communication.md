Optical space communication transfers information through free space on a modulated optical carrier, usually a laser. A link can connect two spacecraft or a spacecraft with an airborne or ground terminal. Current Consultative Committee for Space Data Systems standards specify physical-layer parameters for non-coherent optical links, providing an interoperability baseline rather than a complete terminal design.[^1]

## Link characteristics

An optical terminal concentrates energy into a much narrower beam than a typical radio-frequency system. This can support high data rates with relatively small flight hardware and reduce spill-over into other receivers. The same geometry makes acquisition, pointing and tracking exacting: a terminal must find its counterpart, establish the link and keep the beam aligned while both ends move.[^2] A narrow beam can make interception harder, but it is not encryption and does not by itself secure the data.

Space-to-ground optical links must also cross the atmosphere. Clouds can block the beam, while aerosols and turbulence can attenuate or distort it. Operators can improve availability by choosing suitable sites, monitoring atmospheric conditions and handing traffic between geographically separated optical ground stations.[^3] The result still depends on local climate and correlated weather, so site diversity must be evaluated against a stated service target. These atmospheric constraints do not apply to a crosslink that remains in vacuum.

## UK capability and status

In May 2026, Dstl reported a completed demonstration in which Archangel Lightworks used its deployable TERRA-M ground station to receive many gigabits during a 90-second pass of a low-Earth-orbit satellite.[^4] This established a UK optical-downlink demonstration; it did not establish continuous service or a national ground-station network.

The UK Space Agency's C-LEO programme identifies optical links as a technology for domestic development alongside onboard processing, active antennas, networking and user terminals.[^5] Such programme support, integration studies and test missions show an active industrial base, but each remains distinct from a deployed operational network. Claims about capacity, availability and security therefore need to be tied to a particular terminal, link geometry and service state.
