A meteor is the visible streak of light produced when a meteoroid enters an atmosphere at high speed and burns up.[^1][^2]

NASA calls these fireballs or shooting stars, and the atmosphere may be Earth's or another planet's, such as Mars.[^1] CNEOS defines a fireball as an unusually bright meteor.[^2]

Some meteor showers recur when Earth passes through dusty debris left by a comet, and most of that debris burns up before reaching the ground.[^3]

Meteor names the atmospheric event, not the rock that causes it. The rock in space is a meteoroid, and a piece that reaches the ground is a meteorite.[^1]
