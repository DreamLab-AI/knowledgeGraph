GNSS integrity is the measure of the trust that can be placed in the correctness of the information supplied by a navigation system, including the ability to give users timely warnings when the system should not be used.[^1]

Navipedia specifies integrity through an alert limit, a time to alert, an integrity risk and a protection level. The alert limit is the error tolerance that must not be exceeded without an alert, and the time to alert is the longest allowed delay between the system going out of tolerance and the alert. Navipedia distinguishes integrity from accuracy mainly by where in the error distribution the requirement sits and by the presence of alarms.[^1]

The GPS Standard Positioning Service performance standard defines integrity for the signal in space in the same terms, as trust in the correctness of the information provided together with the ability to give timely alerts. The integrity assured by that service concerns the broadcast signal, which is a narrower thing than the integrity of a user's position solution.[^2]

Augmentation is a common route to integrity. EUSPA says EGNOS provides an integrity message that gives a guarantee on residual positioning errors, for uses such as aviation. The sources do not establish particular alert limits, risks or times for any operation, and these depend on the standard or approval that applies.[^3][^1]
