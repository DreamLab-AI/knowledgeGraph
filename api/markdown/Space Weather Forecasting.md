Space weather forecasting is the prediction of conditions in space, driven by the Sun and the solar wind, that can affect Earth's magnetic field, upper atmosphere, radio systems and spacecraft.[^1][^2]

The Met Office says that coronal mass ejections can take days to reach Earth and that their arrival time is the focus of space weather forecasting. It describes solar flares as hard to predict, with their energy detectable in Earth's atmosphere very soon after the event.[^1]

NOAA SWPC publishes forecast products on a regular cycle. A plain-language three-day forecast gives recently observed and expected conditions with a short rationale, and a separate technical discussion sets out the observed data, analysis and forecaster reasoning.[^3][^4]

SWPC also publishes a solar cycle prediction based on a curve fit to monthly sunspot number and radio flux, updated monthly and shown with stated uncertainty bands. This describes the expected course of the cycle, not individual events. None of the sources opened gives forecast skill, so the page makes no claim about accuracy.[^5]
