Precipitation retrieval is the process of transforming radar reflectivities and passive brightness temperatures measured from space into estimates of precipitation.[^1]

NASA's Global Precipitation Measurement mission uses both kinds of instrument. Active radars such as the Dual-Frequency Precipitation Radar transmit signals and record what is reflected, while passive radiometers such as the GPM Microwave Imager measure natural thermal radiation from the whole scene, including snow, rain, clouds and the surface.[^1] The algorithms are designed by the science team and run by a processing system, and their performance is checked against ground validation data.[^1]

The Core Observatory carries the dual-frequency radar and a multi-channel microwave imager and is described as a reference standard for the estimates from the other sensors in the constellation.[^2] NASA presents an extended ability to measure light rain and solid precipitation as a main advance over the Tropical Rainfall Measuring Mission.[^2]

These pages describe one mission's retrieval chain. They do not quantify errors by region or surface type, so no accuracy is stated here, and the description should not be applied to other precipitation products without checking their inputs.[^1][^2]
