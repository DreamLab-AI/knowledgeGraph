Glacier Terminus is the lowest end of a glacier, the margin whose position is used to say that a glacier has advanced or retreated.[^1][^2]

The USGS glossary gives the terminus as the lower-most margin, end or extremity of a glacier, also called the toe, end or snout.[^2] The SWEET cryosphere ontology words it as an ice mass that forms the part of a glacier with the lowest elevation, and treats snout as an equivalent term.[^1] An older Arctic glossary agrees that the terminus is the lower extremity of a glacier, sometimes called the snout.[^3]

Advance and retreat describe the terminus alone, not the whole glacier.[^4] A glacier terminus advances when less ice is lost to melting or calving than the amount of yearly advance, because the ice keeps moving forward whether or not the terminus does.[^2] Where a terminus ends in a river, lake or ocean it is a calving terminus, and the pieces that break away are treated separately as Ice Calf rather than as part of the terminus.[^2]

Retained sources define the term in glaciological words and do not say how a terminus position is mapped from satellite imagery, so no detection method, accuracy or threshold is stated here.[^1][^4]
