Measurement uncertainty is a non-negative parameter characterising the dispersion of the quantity values being attributed to a measurand, based on the information used.[^1][^2]

The VIM notes that the parameter may be a standard deviation, called standard measurement uncertainty, or a specified multiple of it, or the half-width of an interval with a stated coverage probability. It also notes that uncertainty includes components from systematic effects, such as those associated with corrections and the assigned values of measurement standards.[^1]

The GUM words the concept as a parameter associated with the result of a measurement that characterises the dispersion of the values that could reasonably be attributed to the measurand. NIST Technical Note 1297 is described as a method of evaluating and expressing uncertainty adapted from the GUM, and its contents list Type A and Type B evaluation, combined standard uncertainty and expanded uncertainty.[^2][^3]

Uncertainty is not the same as error or accuracy. The sources cited do not give uncertainty budgets for particular satellite instruments, and any stated value depends on its measurement model and coverage.[^1][^2]
