A trans-Neptunian object is a minor planet whose orbit has a semimajor axis beyond the orbit of Neptune, in the wording of the Minor Planet Center.[^1]

The Minor Planet Center notes that some of these objects have perihelion distances inside Neptune's orbit, and it treats Centaurs, which have semimajor axes inside Neptune's orbit, as a separate list.[^1] NASA describes the Kuiper Belt as a doughnut-shaped region of icy objects beyond Neptune, home to Pluto and most known dwarf planets, and says there may be millions of other icy worlds there.[^2]

Pluto was reclassified by the IAU as a dwarf planet in 2006 and lies in the Kuiper Belt.[^3] Under the IAU definitions, objects that are not planets, dwarf planets or satellites are Small Solar-System Bodies, so a trans-Neptunian object can fall into either of two IAU classes.[^4]

NASA says most of what is known of the Kuiper Belt comes from ground-based telescopes and Hubble, and that only one spacecraft has visited it. The NASA page does not use the term trans-Neptunian, so the link between the term and the region follows from the orbital definition.[^2]
