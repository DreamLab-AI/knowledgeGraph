A white dwarf is the stellar remnant core that remains after a red giant has shed all its atmosphere.[^1]

NASA describes a white dwarf as usually Earth-size but hundreds of thousands of times more massive. It produces no new heat of its own, so it cools gradually over billions of years, and despite the name it can emit visible light ranging from blue white to red.[^1]

Chandra educational material places white dwarfs within stellar evolution: mid-sized stars eject planetary nebulae and leave a white dwarf core remnant, whereas more massive stars explode as supernovae and leave neutron stars or black holes. The outcome therefore depends on the star's initial mass, although neither cited page states mass thresholds.[^2]

Both sources are general-audience explanations. They do not establish detailed cooling models, composition or the behaviour of white dwarfs in binary systems.[^1][^2]

[^1]: NASA Science, “Types of Stars”. https://science.nasa.gov/universe/stars/types/
[^2]: Chandra X-ray Center, “Stellar Evolution”, educational materials, revised 2019. https://chandra.harvard.edu/edu/formal/stellar_ev/
