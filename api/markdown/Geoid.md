In geodesy the geoid is the equipotential surface of the Earth's gravity field that is perpendicular to the direction of gravity and best fits mean sea level, either locally, regionally or globally.[^1]

NOAA gives a plainer account: a model of global mean sea level used to measure precise surface elevations, whose irregular shape follows from variations in the strength of gravity.[^2] Ordnance Survey describes it as the single level surface that best fits the average surface of the oceans over the whole Earth, and explains that heights need a level reference surface because uphill and downhill depend on gravity.[^3]

OGC adds a note that mean sea level in a global context differs from a global geoid by not more than 2 m, so the two should not be treated as the same surface.[^1] Because the allowed fit is local, regional or global, a geoid model is tied to a stated choice.[^1]

GNSS gives ellipsoid heights, which are geometric and say nothing about the gravity field, so a geoid model is needed to obtain orthometric heights.[^3] Different geoid models can give different orthometric heights for the same point.[^3]
