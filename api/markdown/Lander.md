A lander is a spacecraft designed to reach the surface of a planet and survive long enough to send data back to Earth.[^1]

The JPL chapter lists the Soviet Venera landers on Venus, the Viking landers at Mars and the Surveyor landers at the Moon as examples, and describes Mars Pathfinder as a lander that carried its own instruments and also deployed a rover. It treats penetrator and rover spacecraft as separate types, and the Deep Space 2 penetrators, which struck the Martian soil but returned no data, appear under penetrators rather than landers.[^1]

A lander differs from an orbiter or flyby spacecraft in its survival requirement at the surface, and from a rover in not being primarily a mobile platform, although a lander may deliver one. The retained source supports that framing but does not describe entry, descent and landing engineering, landing accuracy or surface operations, so those subjects are outside this page.[^1]

[^1]: NASA Science, *Basics of Space Flight*, chapter 9. https://science.nasa.gov/learn/basics-of-space-flight/chapter9-1/
