Wildfire monitoring is the use of satellite observations, including thermal anomaly products, to follow conditions before a fire starts, track active fires and assess their effects after a burn.[^1][^2]

NASA states that its wildfire data let users monitor conditions before a fire starts, track them once they do, and assess their effects after a burn.[^1] The MODIS Thermal Anomalies/Fire products are primarily derived from MODIS 4- and 11-micrometer radiances.[^2] MODIS data on Terra and Aqua are acquired from each platform twice daily at mid-latitudes, which sets the usual revisit for these products.[^2]

Other instruments add to the picture. The Sentinel-3 SLSTR provides fire radiative power information as an additional product beside its surface temperature measurements.[^3] It is a different product from the detection of a fire by thermal anomaly.[^3]

These retained sources do not give detection limits, false alarm rates or a cloud and smoke caveat, so the twice daily acquisition should be read as a typical schedule and not as a guarantee of coverage. They also do not describe burned area mapping, which has its own page.[^1][^2]

[^2]: NASA MODIS, “MODIS Thermal Anomalies/Fire”. https://modis.gsfc.nasa.gov/data/dataprod/mod14.php
[^3]: ESA and EUMETSAT SentiWiki, “S3 SLSTR Instrument”. https://sentiwiki.copernicus.eu/web/s3-slstr-instrument
