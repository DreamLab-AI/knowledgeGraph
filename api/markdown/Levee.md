A levee is a human-made barrier built to reduce the frequency or probability of flooding in a protected part of a floodplain.[^1][^2] The US Army Corps of Engineers says most levees in the U.S. are earthen embankments made primarily of local soil materials, although many include more than one type of material.[^1]

Environment Agency guidance describes a flood embankment as constructed from earth, possibly with a clay core to reduce seepage, and separates it from a floodwall built of brick, masonry, concrete, sheetpiling or a combination of these materials.[^2] The same guidance reports that such defences rarely exceed 5m in height.[^2]

SWEET declares the class with a label only and lists it among the classes it treats as equivalent to dike, alongside embankment and raised bank.[^3] The retained UK source uses the terms flood defence and flood embankment and never the word levee, and the retained sources do not establish a height threshold, a boundary with a dam, or how a levee is observed from orbit.[^1][^2]
