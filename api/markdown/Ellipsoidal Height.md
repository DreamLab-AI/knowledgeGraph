Ellipsoidal height, also called geodetic height, is the distance of a point from the reference ellipsoid along the perpendicular from the ellipsoid to the point, positive if upwards or outside of the ellipsoid.[^1]

It is the third coordinate in a three-dimensional ellipsoidal coordinate system, alongside geodetic latitude and geodetic longitude. In the OGC text it is a geometric quantity tied to a geodetic reference frame, and it is paired with gravity-related height as a distinct kind of height.[^1]

Satellite positioning typically works in geocentric Cartesian coordinates, which are converted to latitude, longitude and height above the ellipsoid. ESA Navipedia notes that the latitude in this conversion is computed by an iterative procedure, with the height obtained within the same iteration.[^2]

No relation is given between ellipsoidal height and heights measured from a geoid or mean sea level. The value depends on the ellipsoid and frame in use, and heights from different frames should not be compared without checking both.[^1]
