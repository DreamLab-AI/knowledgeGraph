Tundra is a vegetated area of cold, treeless land that forms part of a tundra ecosystem and typically overlies frozen ground.[^1][^2]

SWEET defines it only as a vegetated area which is part of a tundra ecosystem, and models it as land that has a frozen ground realm.[^1] NASA Earth Observatory describes the biome as having almost no trees because of the short growing season and permafrost, with lichens, mosses, grasses, sedges and shrubs, and says the word comes from the Finnish tunturia, meaning treeless plain.[^2]

That NASA page also says permafrost is a defining characteristic of the tundra biome and that tundra is found in regions just below the ice caps of the Arctic, and also at the tops of very high mountains elsewhere in the world.[^2] SWEET handles the mountain case as a subclass, alpine tundra, which it describes as a tundra ecosystem at high altitudes where vegetation is stunted by low temperatures and high winds.[^1]

Retained sources agree on the cold, treeless character but do not give a formal boundary, such as a tree line or a temperature threshold, and do not describe how tundra is mapped from satellite data.

[^2]: NASA Earth Observatory, “Biome: Tundra”. https://earthobservatory.nasa.gov/biome/biotundra.php
