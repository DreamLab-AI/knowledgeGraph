  - ## About Llama 3
  - **Llama 3** is Meta AI's third-generation open-weight large language model family and the most downloaded and deployed open-weight AI model series in history. Launched in April 2024 with 8B and 70B parameter sizes, it rapidly expanded across five major release cohorts over twelve months to include frontier-class 405B dense models, multimodal vision capabilities, on-device edge models, and a Mixture-of-Experts architecture — establishing the paradigm that well-designed open-weight models could match or exceed the performance of closed proprietary systems costing billions of dollars to develop.
  - Prior to Llama 3, a prevailing consensus held that open models would lag frontier closed systems ([[Anthropic Claude]], [[ChatGPT]], [[Gemini]]) by 12-18 months. Llama 3 70B (April 2024) matched GPT-3.5-level performance on major benchmarks. Llama 3.1 405B (July 2024) surpassed GPT-4 Turbo on MMLU (88.6% vs 86.4%), crossing the frontier threshold. Llama 3.3 70B (December 2024) matched 405B quality at 70B scale. Llama 4 Maverick (April 2025) achieved Arena Elo ~1280, competitive with GPT-4o and Claude 3.5 Sonnet.
  - As of May 2026, Llama models account for approximately 65-70% of all open-weight model downloads on Hugging Face, with Llama 3.1 8B Instruct exceeding 1 billion cumulative downloads — the first open-weight model to reach this milestone. The ecosystem spans 350M+ monthly downloads, 15+ enterprise cloud platform integrations, 12M+ Ollama installations, and thousands of derivative fine-tunes.

  - ### Strategic Context and Competitive Position
  - **Meta's Strategic Rationale for Open Release**: Meta's decision to release model weights openly reflects a deliberate competitive strategy: by making powerful AI freely available, Meta accelerates developer adoption of its infrastructure (PyTorch, Llama Stack), increases demand for Meta's cloud services, and undermines the competitive moats of OpenAI and Anthropic whose revenue depends on API pricing. Yann LeCun (Meta Chief AI Scientist) has explicitly argued that open-weight AI is safer than closed AI because vulnerabilities can be independently verified and that intelligence should be a utility rather than a monopoly.
  - **The Frontier Democratisation Thesis**: Llama 3's release validated what many in the open research community had argued but commercial interests had contested — that a well-resourced open-weight effort could achieve frontier-tier performance. This has significant downstream implications for [[Competition in AI]], [[AI Adoption]] patterns, and geopolitical AI competition (state actors that were previously dependent on US API access can now build local capabilities from open weights).
  - **OSI Open Source Controversy**:
    - Meta labels Llama as "open source" but OSI's four-freedom analysis concludes it is "open-weight" not "open-source."
    - Freedom (1) — use for any purpose: FAIL. AUP restricts use to non-weapons, non-election-interference, non-CSAM contexts.
    - Freedom (2) — study how it works: PARTIAL FAIL. Weights available but training data and reproducible training code are not. You can study the trained weights but cannot reproduce the training.
    - Freedom (3) — redistribute copies: PASS. Weights can be freely redistributed under licence terms.
    - Freedom (4) — distribute modified versions: PARTIAL PASS. Derivative models can be distributed under same licence terms with attribution, but 700M MAU restriction applies.
    - OSI Stefano Maffulli (September 2024): "Llama is not open source. It is open-weight. These are different things with different implications for transparency, safety, and democratic oversight."
    - EU AI Act Article 53 implications: GPAI providers have reduced transparency/evaluation obligations if their model is released under open-source licence. Whether Llama qualifies is a live regulatory dispute before the EU AI Office.
    - UK parallel: UK AI Opportunities Action Plan (January 2025) distinguishes "open-weight" from "open-source" AI in its frontier AI governance recommendations, adopting the OSI framework distinction.

  - ## Architecture: Core Technical Framework
  - **Decoder-Only Transformer Baseline**: All Llama 3 dense models share a causal decoder-only transformer architecture with autoregressive next-token prediction pre-training objective:
    - **Causal self-attention**: Each token attends only to preceding tokens via causal mask. No encoder, no cross-attention beyond vision adapters (Llama 3.2). No bidirectional attention.
    - **Autoregressive pretraining**: Next-token prediction loss L = −(1/N) Σ log p(x_t | x_{<t}) over all tokens t in the training sequence. Efficient: single forward pass computes loss for all positions simultaneously via teacher forcing.
    - **Architecture lineage**: GPT-2 (Radford et al. 2019) → GPT-3 (Brown et al. 2020) → PaLM (Chowdhery et al. 2022) → LLaMA 1 (Touvron et al. 2023) → LLaMA 2 (2023) → Llama 3 (2024). Key innovations added at each step: RoPE (replaces sinusoidal at LLaMA 1), GQA (replaces MHA at LLaMA 2 34B, all Llama 3), SwiGLU (replaces ReLU at LLaMA 1), RMSNorm (replaces LayerNorm at LLaMA 1), 128K vocab (new at Llama 3).
    - **Shared across all dense Llama 3 sizes**: Same architectural template (transformer blocks × N layers, GQA, RoPE, SwiGLU, RMSNorm) scaled by width (hidden dim), depth (layers), and head count. MoE (Llama 4) adds expert routing layer replacing standard FFN.
  - **Grouped Query Attention (GQA)**: The central memory-efficiency innovation enabling practical deployment at scale. In standard multi-head attention, every attention head has its own key/value projection matrices — for a 70B model with 64 heads and 8192 hidden dim this produces a KV cache of 64 × 2 × seq_len × 128 × 2 bytes = 33MB per token at BF16, scaling to 4.2GB for 128K context. GQA groups Q heads into G groups, each sharing a single K/V head, reducing KV cache by factor G. Llama 3 70B uses G=8 (8 Q heads share 1 K/V head pair, 64 Q heads → 8 K/V heads), reducing KV cache from 4.2GB to 530MB at 128K context — making 128K-context generation practical on A100/H100 GPUs. Quality degradation versus full MHA is minimal (<0.5% MMLU) because key-value representations capture less information-theoretically distinct content than queries.
  - **Rotary Position Embeddings (RoPE)**: Position encoding via complex multiplication of query and key vectors by rotation matrices R_θ(m) = diag(e^{imθ_1}, ..., e^{imθ_{d/2}}) where position m controls the rotation angle and base frequency θ determines the wavelength spectrum. RoPE enables the model to learn relative positional relationships (attending to tokens k positions apart depends only on k, not absolute position m) and supports context length extension via frequency scaling: dividing base frequency θ from 10,000 (original RoPE) to 500,000 (Llama 3) extends the effective context window without additional training data at longer sequences. NTK-aware scaling and YaRN interpolation enable further extension to 1M+ tokens in derivative fine-tunes.
  - **SwiGLU Activation**: Feed-forward networks use SwiGLU (Shazeer 2020): FFN(x) = (xW₁ ⊙ σ(xW₃)) W₂ where σ is the sigmoid function and ⊙ denotes elementwise multiplication. Hidden dimension set to 8/3 × d_model (rounded to multiple of 256 for hardware efficiency): 8B→14336, 70B→28672, 405B→53248. Compared to standard ReLU FFN (FeedForward(x) = max(0, xW₁)W₂), SwiGLU provides smoother gradient flow via the sigmoid gate, improving convergence and final quality at equivalent parameter count — empirically ~0.5-1.0 MMLU points better than ReLU at matched FLOPs.
  - **RMSNorm Pre-Normalisation**: Each transformer block applies RMSNorm (Root Mean Square Normalisation) before attention and FFN sublayers rather than after. RMSNorm(x) = x / RMS(x) × γ where RMS(x) = √(1/d ∑ xᵢ²) and γ is a learned scaling parameter. Pre-normalisation (applied before the sublayer, not after) provides more stable gradients than post-normalisation, enabling training at larger learning rates. RMSNorm omits the re-centering (mean subtraction) of LayerNorm, reducing computation by 7-10% with equivalent quality.
  - **128K BPE Vocabulary**: Llama 3 uses a 128,256-token tiktoken-style byte-pair encoding vocabulary, a 4× expansion from Llama 2's 32,000-token SentencePiece vocabulary. Larger vocabulary improves tokenisation efficiency for non-English text and code: a typical English sentence tokenises into 25-35% fewer tokens, Chinese and Arabic into 50-70% fewer tokens, code into 20-30% fewer tokens. Fewer tokens per input sequence increases effective context utilisation, reduces processing costs, and improves model quality on multilingual and code benchmarks.
  - **Training Infrastructure**: Llama 3.1 405B trained on 16,384 H100 SXM5 GPUs (80GB HBM3 each) across Meta's AI Research SuperCluster (RSC) interconnected via InfiniBand HDR-400 (3.2 Tb/s bisection bandwidth). Training parallelism: 4D parallelism combining tensor parallelism (TP=8, splitting attention heads across GPUs), pipeline parallelism (PP=16, splitting transformer layers), data parallelism (DP=128, replicating across data shards), and sequence parallelism for long-context activation redistribution. Mixed precision: BF16 forward/backward, FP32 optimizer states. FSDP2 (Fully Sharded Data Parallelism v2) shards optimizer states and gradients across DP replicas. Estimated FLOPs: 3.8×10²⁴ for 405B on 15.6T tokens.

  - ## Training Data: Curation and Composition
  - **Scale**: Llama 3.1 405B trained on 15.6 trillion tokens of multilingual text — approximately 7-8× more than Llama 2 (2T tokens). This deliberate over-training relative to Chinchilla optimal (~8T tokens for 405B) follows the inference-optimal philosophy: train longer to reduce perplexity and improve quality, even at the cost of additional training FLOPs, because inference costs at deployment scale dwarf training amortised over millions of users.
  - **Data Sources and Composition**:
    - **Web Crawl (CommonCrawl derivatives)**: Primary data source, approximately 80% of total tokens after filtering. CommonCrawl is the largest publicly available web corpus (~petabytes of raw HTML). Llama 3 applies FineWeb-style quality filtering: educational content classifier trained on curated educational text (textbooks, Wikipedia, scientific papers) scoring each web document 0-5; documents scoring <3 discarded; domain-specific upsampling for STEM, programming, law, and medicine domains.
    - **Code (The Stack v2)**: Source code from GitHub, GitLab, StackOverflow, and documentation sites covering 350+ programming languages. Approximately 4-5% of total training tokens. De-duplicated via near-duplicate detection using MinHash LSH. License filtering retains permissively licensed code (MIT, Apache, BSD) and excludes copyleft (GPL) to reduce IP risk.
    - **Scientific Literature (S2ORC, PubMed, ArXiv)**: Full-text scientific papers from Semantic Scholar Open Research Corpus (S2ORC), PubMed Central open-access articles, and arXiv preprints covering mathematics, physics, computer science, biology, chemistry. Approximately 2-3% of total tokens. Provides dense factual knowledge, formal reasoning patterns, and technical vocabulary.
    - **Books and Long-Form Text**: Processed ebooks, books from the Books3 corpus, and Project Gutenberg public domain literature. Provides narrative coherence, long-range dependency modelling, and diverse stylistic patterns. Approximately 1-2% of total tokens.
    - **Synthetic Data (Self-Generated)**: 5-10% of total tokens generated by early Llama 3 checkpoints, Llama 2 70B, and Llama 3.1 itself in a bootstrap loop. Covers reasoning traces, step-by-step mathematics, code documentation, and diverse personas. Meta-generated synthetic data improves performance on instruction-following and chain-of-thought reasoning tasks beyond what web-crawled data alone achieves.
  - **Data Quality Filtering Pipeline**:
    - **Language identification**: FastText language classifier filters non-target-language content; English retains largest share (~65%), followed by German, French, Spanish, Portuguese, Hindi, Thai.
    - **Quality scoring**: Perplexity filtering (low-perplexity documents under a language model are more coherent), educational content classifier, and n-gram deduplication at document and paragraph level.
    - **PII scrubbing**: Regex and classifier-based removal of email addresses, phone numbers, government IDs (SSN, NIN, passport), and geographic identifiers. Critical for privacy compliance and reducing memorisation of personal data.
    - **Safety filtering**: Toxic content classifier (trained on human-labelled samples) removes violent, hateful, and sexually explicit content from pretraining data. Imperfect: some toxic content passes filters, motivating post-training safety alignment.
    - **MinHash LSH deduplication**: 5-gram MinHash signatures computed for each document; pairs with Jaccard similarity >0.8 deduplicated by retaining highest-quality version. Removes training data memorisation risk and improves token diversity.
  - **Multilingual Data Strategy**: Llama 3.1 explicitly targets 8 non-English languages for instruction following. Pretraining data distribution: English ~65%, German ~5%, French ~5%, Spanish ~5%, Italian ~3%, Portuguese ~3%, Hindi ~3%, Thai ~2%, other ~9%. Non-English training data is upsampled 2-3× relative to its natural web frequency to ensure adequate multilingual representation given the dominance of English on the web.

  - ## Post-Training Pipeline: SFT, Rejection Sampling, DPO, PPO
  - **Overview**: All Llama 3 instruction-tuned variants undergo a four-stage post-training pipeline transforming the base autoregressive language model into an aligned conversational assistant. Each stage serves a distinct purpose and uses different data and algorithms.
  - **Stage 1 — Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT)**: Fine-tuning on 10M+ instruction-response pairs covering diverse capability domains: general knowledge and reasoning, coding (Python, JavaScript, TypeScript, SQL, Bash), mathematics (chain-of-thought algebra through competition-level proofs), creative writing (stories, poetry, persuasive essays), summarisation, translation (8+ languages), safety refusals, tool-use demonstrations (JSON function calls), and multi-turn dialogue. Data sourced from three pipelines: (a) Human annotator contractors following detailed style guides specifying response length, format, reasoning transparency, and refusal posture; (b) Synthetic generation using Llama 3 pretraining checkpoints to create diverse personas and topic-following prompts; (c) Public instruction datasets (FLAN, Open Assistant, ShareGPT subsets, CodeAlpaca) after quality filtering. Training: full fine-tuning (not LoRA) of all parameters at learning rate 2×10⁻⁵ with cosine decay over 1-2 epochs.
  - **Stage 2 — Rejection Sampling (RS)**: For each prompt in a 1M+ training set, generate 10-20 candidate responses using the current SFT model. Score all candidates with a reward model (RM) trained on human preference data to evaluate helpfulness, accuracy, instruction-following, and format adherence. Select the top-1 candidate per prompt, constructing a cleaned high-quality training set. Fine-tune again on this RS-filtered set. RS iteratively improves response quality without requiring online RL: the reward model acts as an automated quality filter, dramatically reducing the labour cost of curating high-quality demonstrations.
  - **Stage 3 — Direct Preference Optimisation (DPO)**: DPO (Rafailov et al. 2023) trains directly on preference pairs (y_w, y_l) — pairs of preferred/rejected responses to the same prompt — without an explicit reward model or online RL. The DPO objective: L_DPO = −E[log σ(β log(π_θ(y_w|x)/π_ref(y_w|x)) − β log(π_θ(y_l|x)/π_ref(y_l|x)))] where π_θ is the model being trained, π_ref is the SFT model frozen as reference, and β (typically 0.1-0.5) controls deviation from the reference policy. DPO applied to 2M+ preference pairs curated from: (a) human pairwise comparisons from annotation contractors; (b) synthetic preference pairs where Llama 3.1 405B acts as judge ranking responses on helpfulness/honesty/harmlessness (constitutional AI-style). Key advantage over PPO: no reward model required at inference, no online generation loops, 2-3× faster training convergence, lower instability.
  - **Stage 4 — PPO Reinforcement Learning**: Proximal Policy Optimisation (Schulman et al. 2017) applied for safety-critical capability domains where DPO alone insufficient. A separate reward model RM and value network V trained on safety-specific preference data (CBRN refusals, Llama Guard 3 label agreement, demographic fairness probes). PPO clip objective: L_PPO = E[min(r_t(θ)A_t, clip(r_t(θ), 1−ε, 1+ε)A_t)] where r_t(θ) = π_θ(a_t|s_t)/π_θ_old(a_t|s_t) is the probability ratio and ε=0.1. PPO ensures that RLHF updates do not deviate too far from the DPO-trained policy in a single gradient step, preventing reward hacking. Applied most intensively for jailbreak resistance, CSAM rejection, CBRN uplift limitation, and election interference prevention.
  - **Llama Guard 3 Integration**: At inference time, [[Llama Guard 3]] (8B classifier fine-tuned on MLCommons AI Safety taxonomy) screens both user inputs and model outputs against 12 hazard categories. Deployed as a "safety shield" in [[Llama Stack]], wrapping every inference call. False positive rate 4.2% on benign creative writing; false negative rate 5.0% on adversarially crafted red-team prompts. Llama Guard 3 1B variant available for resource-constrained mobile/edge deployments where 8B GPU overhead is prohibitive.

  - ## Release History: The Llama 3 Herd
  - ### Llama 3 8B and 70B (April 2024)
  - Released April 18, 2024 as Meta's initial Llama 3 launch. Two sizes: 8B (suitable for consumer GPUs, fine-tuning on single A100) and 70B (requiring 4×A100 80GB for inference).
  - **8B Architecture**: 32 transformer layers, 4096 hidden dimension, 32 attention heads, 8 KV heads (4:1 GQA), 14K FFN dimension, 14T training tokens, 8K native context (128K with RoPE frequency scaling).
  - **70B Architecture**: 80 transformer layers, 8192 hidden dimension, 64 attention heads, 8 KV heads (8:1 GQA), 28672 FFN dimension, 15T training tokens, 8K native context.
  - **Benchmarks**: 8B achieves MMLU 66.6%, HumanEval 60.4%, MBPP 68.1%, GSM8K 79.6%. 70B achieves MMLU 79.5%, HumanEval 81.7%, MBPP 82.3%, GSM8K 93.0%. Both significantly outperforming Llama 2 (70B MMLU 68.9%) and competitive with GPT-3.5 Turbo.
  - **Key Improvements over Llama 2**: 4× larger vocabulary (128K vs 32K tokens); GQA replacing multi-head attention for KV cache efficiency; 15T vs 2T training tokens; improved instruction fine-tuning data quality; longer pre-training context (8K vs 4K); tiktoken-style BPE vs SentencePiece tokenisation.
  - **LMSYS Arena Context**: Llama 3 70B Instruct achieved Arena Elo ~1207 at launch (May 2024 analysis), competitive with Claude 3 Sonnet (~1200) and significantly above GPT-3.5 Turbo (~1112), validating open-weight competitive parity at the Sonnet/Turbo tier.

  - ### Llama 3.1 8B, 70B, and 405B (July 2024)
  - Released July 23, 2024 as a major capability expansion. The 405B model crossed the frontier threshold, becoming the first open-weight model to match or exceed GPT-4 Turbo performance on multiple flagship benchmarks.
  - **405B Architecture**: 126 transformer layers, 16384 hidden dimension, 128 Q heads, 16 KV heads (8:1 GQA), 53248 FFN dimension, 15.6T training tokens, 128K native context. Parameter count: 405,021,326,336 (~405B).
  - **Context Extension to 128K**: RoPE base frequency increased from 500K to enable native 128K-token context across all sizes (8B, 70B, 405B). Critical for document analysis, code understanding over large codebases, and multi-document reasoning tasks.
  - **Multilingual Instruction Tuning**: Explicit instruction-following training in 8 languages — English, German, French, Italian, Portuguese, Hindi, Spanish, Thai — with native speaker evaluation for each language. Prior Llama versions were English-dominant despite multilingual pretraining.
  - **Tool Use and Function Calling**: Standardised via JSON schema function definition. Model outputs structured JSON tool calls that can be parsed and executed by application code. Enables [[AI Agents]] and [[Agent Frameworks]] integration without custom parsing.
  - **Frontier Benchmarks**: MMLU 88.6% (vs GPT-4 Turbo 86.4%), GPQA 51.1% (vs GPT-4 Turbo 35.7%), HumanEval 89.0% (vs GPT-4 Turbo 87.1%), MATH 73.8% (vs GPT-4 Turbo 64.5%), GSM8K 96.8%. First open-weight model clearly exceeding GPT-4 Turbo across multiple capability dimensions.
  - **Technical Report**: Dubey et al. (2024) "The Llama 3 Herd of Models" arXiv:2407.21783, 92 pages. Most detailed public documentation of a frontier model training pipeline released to date, covering data curation, architecture choices, post-training methodology, safety evaluations, and benchmark results across all sizes.

  - ### Llama 3.2 1B, 3B, 11B, and 90B (September 2024)
  - Released September 25, 2024 as the multimodal and edge expansion. Two capability directions: vision-language models (11B, 90B) for image understanding; edge-optimised text models (1B, 3B) for on-device deployment.
  - **Vision Architecture (11B, 90B)**: Vision encoder is ViT-H/14 — Vision Transformer with patch size 14×14 pixels, 1280 hidden dim, 16 attention heads, 32 layers, 632M parameters. Image tokens are projected through a 4-layer MLP adapter into the language decoder's residual stream via cross-attention. Supports: visual question answering (VQA), document understanding (OCR, layout analysis, table extraction), chart analysis, image captioning, and multi-image reasoning.
  - **Vision Benchmarks (11B)**: DocVQA 88.4%, ChartQA 83.4%, AI2D 91.1%, VQAv2 75.2%, MMMU 50.7%. Competitive with GPT-4V on document and chart understanding tasks whilst running locally at no API cost.
  - **Edge Text Models (1B, 3B)**: Derived via structured pruning of the 8B Llama 3.1 model (removing low-importance attention heads and FFN neurons identified by Taylor expansion importance scores) followed by knowledge distillation from a 70B teacher model. 1B achieves ~60% of 8B MMLU performance at 12.5% parameter count. 3B achieves ~80% of 8B MMLU performance at 37.5% parameter count — the efficiency-quality frontier for <10B models in late 2024.
  - **On-Device Target**: 1B and 3B designed for Apple Neural Engine, Qualcomm Snapdragon NPU, and MediaTek Dimensity inference at 20-40 tokens/second on flagship smartphones. 3B can run in 4-bit quantised GGUF format at ~2GB memory footprint, fitting within mobile memory constraints alongside the OS and applications.
  - **Llama Guard 3 1B**: Released alongside 3.2 as a companion safety classifier for mobile deployments. Distilled from the 8B Llama Guard 3. Achieves 88% of 8B precision/recall at 12.5% parameter count, suitable for on-device safety filtering in mobile applications where 8B GPU overhead is unavailable.

  - ### Llama 3.3 70B (December 2024)
  - Released December 2024 as a proof-of-concept for algorithmic post-training efficiency: matching Llama 3.1 405B quality at Llama 3.1 70B parameter count, thereby eliminating the primary motivation for deploying the resource-intensive 405B in most production scenarios.
  - **Teacher-Driven Synthetic SFT**: Llama 3.1 405B generates 5M+ high-quality instruction-response pairs across domains, used as SFT training data for 3.3 70B. Bootstrapping from a larger teacher model provides higher-quality demonstrations than human-written data alone, transferring 405B's reasoning patterns into the smaller 70B model.
  - **Constitutional DPO**: Preference pairs curated with explicit constitutional principles (helpfulness, harmlessness, honesty, instruction-following) encoded as scalar reward dimensions. Multi-objective DPO trains on 2M+ preference pairs sampled to represent balanced coverage across capability and safety dimensions.
  - **Extended Post-Training Compute**: 3× more RL compute versus 3.1 70B — extended PPO training with improved reward model calibration and additional safety fine-tuning passes. Post-training compute scaling (Snell et al. 2024) proves as effective as pretraining scaling for capability improvement at fixed inference cost.
  - **Benchmarks**: MMLU 86.0% (vs 405B 88.6% — within 3%), GPQA Diamond 50.5% (vs 405B 51.1%), HumanEval 88.4% (vs 405B 89.0%), MATH 77.0% (vs 405B 73.8%), LiveBench 56.3%. Arena Elo approximately matching 405B. The de facto production recommendation for most deployments as of early 2025, replacing 405B except for highest-stakes tasks.

  - ### Llama 4 Scout, Maverick, and Behemoth (April 2025)
  - Released April 5, 2025 as Meta's first Mixture-of-Experts architecture in the Llama lineage. Three models targeting different deployment contexts from single-GPU to datacenter-scale.
  - **MoE Architecture Overview**: Each transformer layer contains a set of N expert FFN networks. A routing network (gating function) selects k experts for each input token (top-k routing). Only activated experts compute on a given token, so active parameters << total parameters. This enables "parameter scaling" (more total parameters) without proportional "compute scaling" (active FLOPs per token remain bounded). Llama 4 uses learned per-token routing with load balancing loss to prevent expert collapse.
  - **Scout (17B active / 109B total)**: 16 experts per MoE layer, top-2 routing (2 experts activated per token), 17B active parameters. Context: 10M tokens (longest production context at April 2025 launch, 78× longer than Llama 3.1). Inference compute equivalent to a 17B dense model. Designed for single-H100-GPU deployment (17B active fits in 80GB HBM). Use cases: long-document analysis (legal contracts, academic literature review, code repository understanding), very long conversation context, archival research.
  - **Maverick (17B active / 400B total)**: 128 experts per MoE layer, top-2 routing. Total parameters 400B but only 17B activate per token, giving inference compute equivalent to a 17B dense model whilst benefiting from knowledge encoded across 400B parameters. MMLU 86.5%, Arena Elo ~1280. Competitive with GPT-4o and Claude 3.5 Sonnet. Context: 1M tokens.
  - **Behemoth (288B active / 2T total)**: 1024 experts per MoE layer, top-k routing with k=32 (288B active). Still in training at April 2025 announcement, with preliminary benchmarks showing above GPT-4.5 on STEM domains. Targeted for datacenter deployment at 8×H100 node minimum. Estimated training FLOPs: 10²⁵-10²⁶ — crossing the EU AI Act Article 53 GPAI threshold of 10²⁵ FLOPs.
  - **MetaP Initialisation**: Meta-learned parameter initialisation derived from training smaller proxy models and extrapolating optimal hyperparameters (learning rate schedule, weight initialisation scale, attention temperature) to the full model size. Reduces hyperparameter tuning compute by 80% and improves training stability for the first 10K gradient steps — critical at Behemoth scale where hyperparameter instability is costly.
  - **Multimodal Pretraining**: Llama 4 models trained on interleaved image/text/video sequences from the start of pretraining (not post-hoc vision adapters as in Llama 3.2). This enables native visual grounding — the model jointly reasons over visual and textual content without the modality switching artifacts of adapter-based approaches.

  - ## Inference Ecosystem: llama.cpp, Ollama, vLLM
  - **llama.cpp (Georgi Gerganov, C++, January 2023–present)**:
    - Origins: Gerganov reverse-engineered Llama 1 from the leaked model weights (February 2023) within days of the leak, implementing pure C++ inference with no Python/PyTorch dependency — making LLM inference accessible on any platform supporting a C compiler.
    - GGUF format: Gerganov Universal Format (GGUF) replaced the original GGML format in August 2023. Encodes model architecture, vocabulary, and quantised weights in a single binary file with metadata header for automatic configuration. GGUF files are single-file model artifacts analogous to Docker images — pull and run.
    - Quantisation levels: Q2_K (2.6 bits/weight, ~50% MMLU degradation — unusable for reasoning), Q4_K_M (4.55 bits/weight, <0.5% MMLU degradation — recommended for quality/size tradeoff), Q5_K_M (5.7 bits/weight, ~0.2% MMLU degradation), Q8_0 (8 bits/weight, ~0.1% degradation — near-lossless), F16 (16 bits/weight — full BF16). Q4_K_M is the community standard for local deployment.
    - Memory requirements at Q4_K_M: Llama 3.2 3B = 2.0GB, Llama 3.1 8B = 4.9GB (fits 8GB RAM), Llama 3.1 70B = 42GB (fits 48GB VRAM or Apple M2 Ultra 192GB unified memory), Llama 3.1 405B = 245GB (requires 8× RTX 3090 or equivalent).
    - Performance on Apple Silicon: M2 Max (96GB) runs 70B Q4_K_M at 12 tokens/second via Metal GPU acceleration. M2 Ultra (192GB) runs 70B at 25 tokens/second. Apple Silicon's unified memory architecture eliminates CPU-GPU transfer bottleneck, making MacBook Pro competitive with small GPU servers for local LLM inference.
    - Server mode: llama.cpp provides an OpenAI-compatible REST API (‐‐server flag) enabling drop-in replacement for OpenAI API endpoints in local environments without code changes.
    - GitHub: 70K+ stars, 3,000+ contributors, updated within days of every Llama release. Community maintains GGUF quantised models for every Llama variant on Hugging Face (TheBloke/bartowski repositories, 500M+ cumulative downloads).
  - **Ollama (Go, 2023–present)**:
    - Architecture: Thin Go wrapper around llama.cpp providing Docker-style model lifecycle management. `ollama pull llama3.1:70b` downloads GGUF model; `ollama run llama3.1:70b` starts interactive session; `ollama serve` starts OpenAI-compatible REST server.
    - Model library: Pre-packaged GGUF models with automatic configuration for 100+ models including all Llama 3 variants, Mistral, Gemma, Qwen, Phi. No manual GGUF file selection — Ollama selects optimal quantisation for available hardware.
    - Deployments: 12M+ installations by 2026 across Linux (native systemd service), macOS (native app), and Windows (WSL2). Default installation includes automatic GPU detection for NVIDIA/AMD/Apple Silicon.
    - Enterprise use: Supports multi-user concurrent access via HTTP API. Integrations with LangChain (langchain-ollama package), LlamaIndex (OllamaLLM class), Continue (VS Code extension), Open WebUI (ChatGPT-like interface), and AnythingLLM (document RAG with local LLMs).
    - Privacy advantage: All inference runs locally; no data leaves the device or network. This is the primary driver of NHS, HMRC, and financial sector adoption where data sovereignty requirements prohibit cloud API use.
  - **vLLM (PagedAttention, 2023–present)**:
    - Architecture: Python/CUDA high-throughput serving framework with PagedAttention memory management — paging KV cache across non-contiguous GPU memory blocks (analogous to OS virtual memory) enabling concurrent batching of 100s of requests with minimal memory waste.
    - Throughput: 2-4× throughput improvement over naive batching for the same GPU hardware, critical for serving 405B at production scale.
    - Continuous batching: Dynamically adds new requests to in-progress batches without waiting for current batch to complete, maximising GPU utilisation.
    - OpenAI-compatible API: Drop-in replacement for OpenAI API endpoints. `vllm serve meta-llama/Meta-Llama-3.1-70B-Instruct` provides /v1/completions and /v1/chat/completions endpoints.
    - Multi-GPU tensor parallelism: `--tensor-parallel-size 8` distributes Llama 3.1 405B across 8 H100s with automatic tensor parallelism, enabling frontier-tier open-weight serving without custom distributed inference code.
  - **Hugging Face Transformers**:
    - Primary integration path for research and fine-tuning. `from transformers import AutoModelForCausalLM` provides unified loading for all Llama 3 variants via Hugging Face Hub model IDs (meta-llama/Meta-Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct, etc.).
    - PEFT integration: transformers + peft library provides LoRA (Hu et al. 2021) and QLoRA (Dettmers et al. 2023) fine-tuning via `get_peft_model()` — reducing 70B fine-tuning from 140GB to 48GB VRAM.
    - Accelerate library: Multi-GPU and multi-node training via `accelerate launch`, enabling Llama 3 distributed fine-tuning without custom FSDP/DDP code.
    - TGI (Text Generation Inference): Hugging Face's production serving solution equivalent to vLLM; used by AWS SageMaker JumpStart, Hugging Face Inference Endpoints, and Azure ML Model Catalog to serve Llama 3 as managed APIs.
    - Datasets integration: Hugging Face Datasets library provides one-line access to instruction fine-tuning datasets (OpenHermes-2.5, Alpaca, ShareGPT4, FLAN v2) for Llama 3 fine-tuning workflows.

  - ## Academic Context
  - **Mechanistic Interpretability**:
    - Llama 3's open weights enabled circuit-level analysis unavailable for closed models — a major research accessibility advantage.
    - McDougall et al. (Anthropic, 2024): Identified induction heads (2-layer circuits enabling in-context copying), previous-token heads, and attention sink heads in Llama 3 via activation patching — consistent with GPT-2 mechanistic findings, suggesting universal circuit motifs across architectures.
    - EleutherAI TransformerLens Llama 3 support: logit lens analysis (tracking token probabilities evolving across transformer layers — early layers handle syntax, middle layers semantics, final layers output formatting), attention pattern visualisation, direct logit attribution, and activation steering experiments for controllable generation.
    - Representation analysis: Platonic Representation Hypothesis (Huh et al. 2024) used Llama 3 alongside other open models to demonstrate convergence of internal representations across architecturally different models trained on different data — Llama 3 as key empirical test case.
    - Sparse autoencoder (SAE) feature extraction: Anthropic's superposition hypothesis research extended to Llama 3 by independent researchers; SAEs trained on Llama 3.1 8B residual stream activations identify 100K+ linear features representing concepts, emotions, syntax patterns — enabled by weight access.
  - **Alignment Research Impact**:
    - DPO deployment in Llama 3 post-training established it as canonical open-weight alignment reference. Major 2024-2025 alignment papers benchmarking against Llama 3 DPO: SimPO (Meng et al. 2024), ORPO (Hong et al. 2024), RLVR (Shao et al. 2024), IPO (Azar et al. 2024), KTO (Ethayarajh et al. 2024), REBEL (Gao et al. 2024).
    - Constitutional AI-style training: Llama 3's use of Llama 3.1 405B as judge model for DPO preference generation is the largest-scale deployment of AI-assisted alignment data generation, validating that larger teacher models can replace expensive human preference collection for sub-frontier alignment.
    - [[Bias in Large Language Models]]: Llama 3 BBQ (Bias Benchmark for QA) scores: 8B: 69.2%, 70B: 80.4%, 405B: 84.7% — significant improvement over Llama 2 but still exhibiting systematic biases in gender, ethnicity, and religion domains under adversarial prompting.
  - **Inference-Time Scaling**:
    - Llama 3 primary testbed for "test-time compute scaling" (Snell et al. 2024; Brown et al. 2024; Anthropic 2024).
    - Self-consistency voting: Llama 3.1 70B with 32-sample majority voting on MATH 500 matches Llama 3.1 405B zero-shot — suggesting 405B advantage partially recapturable via inference compute.
    - Process Reward Models (PRMs): Training a Llama 3.1 8B PRM on step-level mathematical reasoning correctness, then using it to score beam search over 70B generation, achieves +15% MATH accuracy versus 70B greedy decoding — matching 405B at 70B cost + 32× inference budget.
    - Implication: The Llama 3.3 70B efficiency result (matching 405B quality) is partially attributable to inference-time compute allocation during post-training RS and PPO stages — not purely pretraining parameter scale.
  - **Scaling Laws Validation**:
    - Llama 3.1 405B training run provided community validation of Hoffmann et al. (2022) Chinchilla predictions.
    - 405B on 15.6T tokens is ~2× over Chinchilla optimal (~8T tokens for 405B parameters), deliberately so.
    - Meta's inference-optimal philosophy: train longer (higher token:parameter ratio) to reduce inference perplexity and improve quality even at superoptimal training FLOPs cost, because at deployment scale inference compute (millions of users × tokens/query) dwarfs amortised training compute.
    - Adopted across the field: Mistral 7B/8×7B, Qwen 2.5, Gemma 2, Phi-3 all follow inference-optimal over-training strategy.
  - **Benchmark Saturation and New Evaluations**:
    - Llama 3.1 405B at 88.6% MMLU (near human expert ~89.8%) accelerated community shift to harder evaluations: GPQA Diamond (graduate-level science), MATH 500 (competition mathematics), AIME proofs, LiveBench (monthly-refreshed, contamination-controlled), FrontierMath (expert-level mathematical problem sets).
    - LiveBench (launched March 2024): Monthly-refreshed problems from current events, recent papers, new competitions — prevents training data contamination by construction. Llama 4 Maverick ~62-65% vs GPT-4o ~65-68%.
    - FrontierMath (2025): Problems requiring multiple hours for PhD mathematicians to solve. Llama 4 Maverick ~8-12% — all frontier models below 20%, indicating enormous unsolved reasoning capability gap.
  - **Data Contamination Research**: Zhang et al. (2024) found statistically significant overlap between CommonCrawl training data and certain MMLU subjects (history, humanities) — suggesting modest contamination inflation — but no evidence for GPQA Diamond or HumanEval contamination. Motivated LiveBench and custom held-out evaluation adoption as community standard.
  - **Open vs Closed Debate**: Sebastian Raschka (Lightning AI) proposed tiered openness: "open-weight" (weights only), "open-base" (weights + model card + training config), "open-source AI" (weights + training data + reproducible training code). Cambridge CFI February 2025 working paper argued EU AI Act open-source carve-out creates regulatory asymmetry incentivising weight release over full open publication. Llama 3/4 are the canonical empirical test cases for this policy debate.

  - ## Future Directions (2026-2030)
  - **Llama 5 Extended Thinking (Expected 2026-2027)**:
    - Native chain-of-thought with extended thinking compute budgets is the primary capability gap: OpenAI o3 and [[Anthropic Claude]] extended thinking maintain 10-15 point advantages on AIME/GPQA Diamond via multi-step internal reasoning.
    - Training requirement: "Thinking traces" — long internal reasoning chains before final answers, similar to GPT-o1's chain-of-thought self-distillation. Requires process reward models (PRMs) scoring correctness of intermediate reasoning steps, not just final outputs.
    - Expected architecture: Llama 5 likely retains MoE from Llama 4 with added "reasoning mode" toggling extended compute allocation; may integrate speculative decoding draft model for efficiency.
    - Timeline: Public Meta statements and FAIR research publication cadence suggest Q4 2026 - Q2 2027 target.
  - **Video-Native Multimodal (Llama 5 Target)**:
    - Llama 4 Behemoth supports video at limited frame rates via multimodal pretraining pipeline with interleaved image/text/video sequences.
    - Llama 5 target: Full-motion video understanding with temporal reasoning — causality across frames, action recognition, physics reasoning, long-form video comprehension (hours, not seconds).
    - Competing with GPT-4o video capabilities (32-frame sampling at 512×512) and Gemini 1.5's long-video understanding (1 frame/second for 1-hour videos via 1M context).
    - Practical applications: Surgical video analysis for medical training, manufacturing inspection from continuous video feed, sports analytics, security surveillance with natural language querying.
