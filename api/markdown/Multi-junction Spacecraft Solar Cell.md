A multi-junction spacecraft solar cell is a photovoltaic cell made of stacked semiconductor layers, each tuned to absorb a different part of the solar spectrum.[^1][^2]

In ESA's description the junctions are stacked in series by sensitivity, with the top junction responding to the highest-energy light and passing the rest on to the layers below. Because the junctions are in series, the layers must be matched in current to avoid efficiency losses.[^2]

Space radiation degrades the individual junctions at different rates, so the design aims to keep the junctions matched at the end of the satellite's life, and the cells are covered with glass. NASA's survey contrasts these cells with single-junction cells and reports higher spectral efficiency for the stacked design, while noting that real performance is limited by optical, thermal and material losses.[^1][^2]

A cell with only two semiconductor materials is called a tandem-junction cell in the NASA survey. The sources here do not give a single efficiency for a flight cell, and published efficiency values depend on the cell design and on whether they refer to beginning or end of life.[^1][^2]

[^2]: ESA, “Inside a solar cell”. https://www.esa.int/Enabling_Support/Space_Engineering_Technology/Inside_a_solar_cell
