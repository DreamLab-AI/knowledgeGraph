A seepage face is the part of a sloping boundary where groundwater leaves the saturated zone at atmospheric pressure and discharges to the surface.[^1][^2] A hydrology paper describes a slope segment where groundwater discharges from the aquifer and flows overland and downslope toward a downstream water body, and a Wageningen University text defines a seepage surface as the boundary where water leaves the soil mass and continues to flow in a thin film along its surface.[^3][^1]

A seepage face typically forms at steep lateral boundaries of the aquifer, where groundwater debouches into atmospheric pressure.[^2] In modelling terms the hydraulic pressure head there becomes zero.[^2] Seepage surfaces can also occur on the downstream face of dams.[^1]

SWEET types the class as a kind of boundary.[^4] Because the retained sources are modelling and engineering texts, they do not establish how a seepage face is observed from space or how it appears in satellite imagery.
