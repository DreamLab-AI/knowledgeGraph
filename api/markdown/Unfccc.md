The United Nations Framework Convention on Climate Change (UNFCCC) is an international environmental treaty, adopted in 1992, that establishes the institutional framework for intergovernmental cooperation to limit greenhouse gas emissions and address climate change. It convenes the annual Conference of the Parties and provides the legal and procedural basis for subsequent agreements including the Kyoto Protocol and the Paris Agreement. The convention defines reporting, review and finance mechanisms that govern global climate action.

- The [[Unfccc]] (United Nations Framework Convention on Climate Change) is the 1992 international treaty that frames intergovernmental cooperation on climate change. It is an instrument of global [[Governance]].
- It convenes the annual Conference of the Parties and provides the legal basis for the [[Paris Agreement]] and earlier protocols, defining the mechanisms behind [[Climate Finance]].

### Definition note

- Although commonly written UNFCCC, the page label follows the source verbatim. The convention itself is the parent framework for modern climate diplomacy.

### Overview

- The UNFCCC set the objective of stabilising greenhouse gas concentrations at a level that prevents dangerous human interference with the climate system, without itself imposing binding emission limits.
- Binding commitments and detailed rules are negotiated under the convention: the Kyoto Protocol introduced binding targets for developed countries, and the Paris Agreement established nationally determined contributions for all parties.
- The convention institutionalises annual negotiation, transparency and reporting, and the channelling of finance and technology to developing countries.

### Key aspects

- Conference of the Parties: the supreme decision-making body meeting annually.
- Common but differentiated responsibilities: a principle apportioning obligations by capability.
- Reporting and review: parties submit inventories and progress reports.
- Finance mechanisms: funds and facilities support mitigation and adaptation.
- Subsidiary bodies: scientific and implementation committees advise negotiations.

### Applications

- Negotiation venue for the Paris Agreement and its implementation rulebook.
- Framework governing international [[Climate Finance]] flows.
- Reference for national [[Sustainability]] and [[ESG]] reporting commitments.
- Coordination mechanism for collective climate mitigation.
