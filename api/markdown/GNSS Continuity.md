GNSS continuity is the ability of the total navigation system to perform its function without interruption during the intended operation, stated more specifically as the probability that specified performance is maintained for the duration of a phase of operation, given that the system was available at its start.[^1]

The GPS Standard Positioning Service performance standard defines continuity for the signal in space as the probability that a trackable and healthy signal continues to be trackable and healthy without unscheduled interruption over a specified time interval. Navipedia, adapting that standard, groups the causes of lost continuity at constellation level into hard failures, wear-out failures, soft failures and satellite operation and maintenance activities.[^2][^1]

In Navipedia's account a hard failure does not necessarily cost continuity: if the control segment can warn in advance of the interruption there is no loss, whereas a sudden loss without warning is a loss of continuity. A second satellite in the same orbital slot can also avoid the loss. The distinction between scheduled and unscheduled interruptions is therefore part of the definition.[^1]

Continuity is conditional on availability at the start of the period, so it should not be confused with availability, which is a percentage of time. The sources define the term and its failure categories; they do not give continuity probabilities, and values depend on the standard, service and interval named.[^1][^2]
