Frazil ice is the first stage of ice formation in turbulent water, consisting of small needle-like or disc-shaped crystals that exist before a coherent ice sheet forms.[^1][^2]

The American Meteorological Society describes frazil ice as platelets or discs roughly 1 mm in diameter, and NSIDC describes needle-like crystals that appear as ocean water begins to freeze.[^1][^2] Because salt does not freeze, the crystals expel salt into the water and consist of nearly pure fresh water.[^2]

Frazil ice may form in open water leads and around the ice margins in the Arctic, is most common in the Antarctic, and may also form in turbulent rivers in winter.[^1] Crystals float to the surface, accumulate and bond together into sheets of sea ice, or in rough water gather into pancake ice.[^2]

Retained sources do not establish how frazil ice is detected from orbit, nor give a thickness at which it stops counting as frazil.
