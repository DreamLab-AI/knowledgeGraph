Ground deformation monitoring is the measurement of changes in the position of the land surface over time, here by repeated satellite radar observations.[^1][^2]

NASA describes interferometric synthetic aperture radar as combining two or more radar images of the same region to reveal surface topography or surface motion.[^1] If the ground moves between passes, the phase difference is proportional to the ground deformation along the line of sight.[^1] Only the line-of-sight component is therefore observed, and the NASA page notes that other phase contributions must be removed before motion remains.

The European Ground Motion Service was created in response to user needs voiced at the Copernicus User Forum, and the Copernicus page presents it as supporting decisions about where to build infrastructure by assessing the likelihood of hazards such as landslides or subsidence.[^2] That is a statement about intended use.

A general accuracy figure is not given by the retained sources, and results depend on the data and processing of each product. A measured displacement is a surface motion estimate and does not by itself identify its cause.[^1][^2]

[^2]: Copernicus Land Monitoring Service, “European Ground Motion Service”. https://land.copernicus.eu/en/products/european-ground-motion-service
