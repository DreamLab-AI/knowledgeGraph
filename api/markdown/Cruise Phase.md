Cruise phase is the part of a planetary mission bounded by the launch phase at the start and the encounter phase at the end.[^1]

NASA/JPL says cruise may last as little as a few months or span years, for example where the spacecraft loops the Sun to perform gravity-assist flybys. After launch the spacecraft is commanded to configure for cruise, and appendages stowed to fit the launch vehicle are deployed fully or to intermediate cruise positions.[^1]

Routine cruise operations are run from stored command sequences, and quiescent cruises between planets need relatively few activities, so one command load may stay valid for several weeks or months. Real-time commands can still be sent when new activities become desirable, and onboard fault protection may put the spacecraft into safing or contingency modes that have to be recovered.[^1][^2]

Phase definition comes from a JPL description of planetary missions. Sources do not establish how other agencies name or bound the phase, and do not give typical durations for specific missions beyond the general range.[^1]
