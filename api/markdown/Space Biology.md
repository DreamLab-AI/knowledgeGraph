Space biology is research into how spaceflight affects living systems, whether in spacecraft such as the International Space Station or in ground-based experiments that mimic aspects of spaceflight.[^1]

NASA's Space Biology Program uses the space environment to learn how gravity affects the design and function of living organisms and how biological systems accommodate to spaceflight environments.[^2] NASA also lists radiation and microgravity among the space stressors whose effects on biological and physical phenomena it studies.[^2]

Results from spaceflight and ground studies can be reused, because NASA's Open Science Data Repository gives open access to biological and physical science datasets.[^3] Deposit in that repository is not shown to be complete for every study.

Quoted here are the aims of one agency's programme. They describe NASA's scope and do not claim to define the field for other space agencies, and the retained pages give no experimental findings.[^1][^2]
