An iceberg is a piece of ice that has broken off from the end of a glacier that terminates in water.[^1]

SWEET is broader on origin, since its tabular iceberg class is derived from an ice shelf, ice tongue or floating tidewater glacier via detachment.[^2] SWEET also states that icebergs are not sea ice and that they add fresh water to the ocean when they melt.[^2]

USNIC, the United States body that names and tracks Antarctic bergs, confirmed one as calved from the Vanderford Glacier in the Wilkes Land Region of Antarctica, and derives iceberg names from the Antarctic quadrant in which each was first sighted.[^3] The retained sources do not give a size threshold that the page can adopt, so it states no size classes or dimensions.
