End-of-life disposal is the planned action taken when a spacecraft's mission ends to limit its continuing presence in useful orbits and the debris it can generate.[^1][^2]

In a 2015 ESA presentation of its requirements, presence in the low-Earth-orbit protected region is limited to a maximum of 25 years from the end of mission. Listed options include controlled re-entry, a manoeuvre to an orbit with a shorter lifetime, augmenting decay with a deployed device, natural decay, or raising perigee above the protected region for a long period.[^1]

ESA's presentation also treats disposal as something to be made reliable, setting a threshold probability of successful disposal at the time it is executed. It records adoption of ISO 24113 as ECSS-U-AS-10C for agency projects.[^1]

NASA's Orbital Debris Program Office describes its 2001 standard practices as aimed at limiting new long-lived debris, including postmission disposal of space structures.[^2] Those pages do not state the thresholds here, and the ESA material is dated 2015, so later policy changes are not covered by the sources opened.[^1][^2]
