In situ observation, in Earth observation practice, is ground-based or near-surface measurement used to calibrate and validate satellite data and the products derived from them.[^1][^2]

The Copernicus In Situ Component describes in situ data, specifically Fiducial Reference Measurements, as vital for calibrating and validating Copernicus satellite products. The retained page does not say how those measurements are characterised or traced, so no further property is asserted here.[^1]

A 1990s FAO workshop report records the CEOS definitions of calibration, as defining the system response to known controlled signal inputs, and of validation, as assessing data quality by independent means. It adds that product validation needs independent measurement at a spatial scale appropriate for the sensing system. The report is historical and is used here for the principle, not for current practice.[^2]

These sources describe the satellite calibration and validation use. They do not define in situ measurement in general, and the term is also used outside Earth observation, so this page does not claim a wider scope.[^1][^2]
