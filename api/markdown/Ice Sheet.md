An ice sheet is a glacier of land ice covering more than 50,000 square kilometres.[^1][^2] Its place as a kind of glacier matches the SWEET hierarchy, where the class sits below glacier.[^2]

NSIDC says ice sheets are constantly in motion, spreading out under their own weight in broad domes.[^1] Earth now has just two, in Greenland and Antarctica, and together they hold more than 99 percent of the land ice.[^1]

Smaller dome-shaped masses below the same threshold belong on the ice cap page. The retained sources agree on the area figure and on the count of two. They do not give thickness, volume or mass balance, and neither source says how those are measured from orbit.

[^1]: National Snow and Ice Data Center, “Ice Sheets”. https://nsidc.org/learn/parts-cryosphere/ice-sheets
[^2]: ESIP SWEET ontology (GitHub mirror), “SWEET realmCryo.ttl”. https://raw.githubusercontent.com/ESIPFed/sweet/master/src/realmCryo.ttl
