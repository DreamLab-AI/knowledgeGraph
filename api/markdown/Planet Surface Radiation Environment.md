A planet surface radiation environment is the natural radiation environment of a region of a planetary surface.[^1]

On Mars, the Radiation Assessment Detector on the Curiosity rover measures and identifies high-energy radiation at the surface, including protons, energetic ions of various elements, neutrons and gamma rays.[^2] A published analysis of its first observations reports absorbed dose and dose equivalent from galactic cosmic rays and solar energetic particles over about 300 days near solar maximum.[^3]

Outside Earth's protective atmosphere, NASA describes the radiation environment as energetic protons and heavy nuclei that are hazardous to living organisms.[^4] That statement concerns space generally. The sources retained here do not establish how much a planetary atmosphere or ground reduces exposure, and they do not give surface values for bodies other than Mars.

A surface environment is therefore specific to a place and a period. The Mars measurements describe one rover, one site and one stretch of the solar cycle, and they should not be read as a general figure for the planet or for other bodies.[^2][^3]
