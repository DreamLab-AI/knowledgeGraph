Sea surface height measurement is the determination of the height of the ocean surface from a satellite altimeter, which times a microwave pulse sent to the sea and back.[^1][^2]

NASA describes the method as bouncing microwave pulses off the ocean surface and measuring how long the signals take to return, then differencing the resulting height against the geoid.[^1] The quantity measured varies because waves, tides, currents and variations in atmospheric pressure all raise or lower the sea surface.[^1] The calculation relies on precise satellite tracking and detailed models of the forces on the satellite path.[^1]

For Sentinel-3, ESA and EUMETSAT state that the main objective of radar altimeters is to determine the Earth's surface topography beneath the satellite.[^2] Because the radar is active and works in the microwave range, it can operate night and day, whatever the meteorological conditions.[^2] A microwave radiometer on board corrects the altimeter range for the excess path delay caused by water vapour in the troposphere.[^2] Over sea surfaces the parameters estimated include sea surface height, significant wave height and wind speed, so height is one product among several.[^2]

Responsibility for products is split by surface. EUMETSAT generates the Level-2 products covering marine surfaces, including coastal areas, while ESA covers continental surfaces and the cryosphere.[^2] The retained sources do not give an accuracy figure for sea surface height, and they do not set out the full list of geophysical corrections applied beyond the water vapour delay.[^1][^2]

[^2]: ESA and EUMETSAT SentiWiki, “S3 Altimetry Instruments”. https://sentiwiki.copernicus.eu/web/s3-altimetry-instruments
