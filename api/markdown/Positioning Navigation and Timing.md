
Positioning, navigation and timing (PNT) are three related service functions: determining location and orientation; determining a current position and supporting movement towards a desired position; and acquiring or maintaining accurate time against a reference.

A PNT solution is an estimate derived from measurements, models and reference information. For example, EUSPA describes Galileo receivers as processing satellite signals to determine location, velocity and precise time. The calculated user solution is distinct from the received radio observables used to form it, and position, velocity and time are distinct outputs with different performance needs.[^2][^8]

Global Navigation Satellite Systems (GNSS), including Galileo, are important space-based sources of PNT, but PNT is not another name for GNSS or satellite navigation alone. UK policy defines separate positioning, navigation and timing functions and calls for resilience beyond reliance on GNSS. The UK Space Agency and National PNT Office describe a system-of-systems approach that integrates space and terrestrial technologies; the European Commission Joint Research Centre also considers terrestrial timing, communication-based and hybrid PNT approaches.[^1][^2][^3][^7]

Timing can be useful independently of a user’s position fix, while a navigation service uses position information to support movement or direction. Accuracy, availability, continuity and integrity should be stated for the particular service and use case rather than inferred from the general label PNT.[^1][^2][^7]

Galileo is one GNSS that provides positioning, navigation and timing information through defined services. EUSPA’s overview distinguishes its operational management role from the European Commission’s programme-management role and ESA’s system-design role. Its service-status page reports that several services are delivered in an initial configuration while the full infrastructure continues to be deployed. The UK’s space-based PNT concepts are assessments of possible architectures, not evidence that a new UK system is already operational.[^4][^5][^6]
