  - The NICVE was a major academic VR research centre in the late 90's and and 2000's. It followed on from the pioneering work by [Bob Stone](https://www.linkedin.com/in/prof-bob-stone-21b86918/), [Andy Connell](https://www.linkedin.com/in/andrew-connell-vr/),  in the National Advanced Robotics Research Centre (NARRC) at The University Of Salford.
  - I was a student in the first year of the [Virtual Reality Master Programme](https://www.timeshighereducation.com/features/salford-offers-virtual-reality-masters/98524.article) at the NICVE, under [Nick Avis](https://www.linkedin.com/in/nick-avis-885177211/), [Karen Padmore](https://www.linkedin.com/in/karen-padmore-a061961/), [Terrence Fernando](https://www.linkedin.com/in/terrence-fernando-6972411/), and [Rob Aspin](https://www.linkedin.com/in/professor-rob-aspin-85655340/). I later worked in the centre under [David Roberts](https://www.linkedin.com/in/david-roberts-0b6b181b/) as it evolved until it was disbanded early in this decade as interest in extended reality and telepresence waned.
  - This was from our first paper, published by another of our lecturers; [Ruth Aylett](https://www.linkedin.com/in/ACoAAAACrVwB204Ab7f4c5tb6XmlXPpd9ov_mrA), which we did as the project at the end of the masters programme. Every single triangle in that was hand drawn separately, one at a time, by me. Modelling wasn't really a proper thing back then. It was CAD, or Medit on an SGI O2. You can see the resemblance to N64 games of the era which were all the same hardware/software pipelines. [https://www.aaai.org/Papers/Symposia/Fall/1999/FS-99-01/FS99-01-014.pdf](https://www.aaai.org/Papers/Symposia/Fall/1999/FS-99-01/FS99-01-014.pdf)

We took this to Farnborough airshow in support of [Euravia Engineering & Supply Co. Limited](https://www.linkedin.com/company/euravia/)

  - The NICVE 4 sided system, which was powered by a three rack SGI Onyx2 IR2 "supercomputer". It was made of wood because the tracking systems was electromagnetic (flock of birds). When trains went past everything wobbled a bit. I used to sit in the dark reconverging the CRT guns every week with music blaring. It was a good task. It was I think the first such system outside of the USA, [where it was pioneered](https://ualr.edu/eac/cave/).
