The Earth-centred Earth-fixed frame is a terrestrial coordinate frame whose origin is the Earth's centre of mass and which rotates with the Earth, so that points fixed to the crust keep nearly constant coordinates.[^1][^2]

ESA's Navipedia describes the conventional terrestrial system with its z axis along the conventional terrestrial pole and its x axis at the intersection of the equatorial plane with the mean Greenwich meridian. The y axis completes a right-handed set. Because the frame follows the Earth's diurnal rotation it is not an inertial frame.[^1]

The IERS defines the International Terrestrial Reference System with its origin at the centre of mass of the whole Earth, including the oceans and the atmosphere. Its orientation evolves so that it has no residual rotation relative to the Earth's crust, and the reference meridian is defined implicitly through the coordinates of the stations that realise the reference frame.[^2]

Converting between this frame and the celestial frame uses rotations for precession, nutation, Earth rotation and polar motion. The polar-motion and rotation steps require Earth orientation parameters that are updated from observations. The Navipedia text names the older conventional terrestrial pole, so details of modern realisations are not established by the sources cited here.[^3][^1]
