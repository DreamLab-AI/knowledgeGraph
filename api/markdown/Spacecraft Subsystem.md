A spacecraft subsystem is a functional part of a spacecraft, or flight system, that sits between the whole system and its individual assemblies in NASA's ideal hierarchy.[^1]

NASA's Science chapter expects a system to comprise subsystems and subsystems to contain assemblies. Its example is an attitude control subsystem containing reaction wheel assemblies and reference assemblies. The chapter also says that the words system and subsystem are often used arbitrarily, so a given project's boundaries may differ from the ideal hierarchy.[^1]

Subsystems are not the same across spacecraft. The chapter says that not all classifications of spacecraft have the same subsystems, and that an atmospheric probe may lack propulsion and attitude control subsystems entirely. The chapter mainly addresses the subsystems typical of complex flyby or orbiter-class spacecraft.[^1][^2]

Structure is one named subsystem example, and a small-spacecraft report notes that structural design is influenced by subsystem integration. That points to coupling between subsystems, but the sources retained here do not give a complete or standard list of subsystems.[^1][^3]
