Escape velocity, more precisely escape speed, is the speed that gives zero specific orbital energy in an ideal two-body model at a stated radius from a central body.

For a point-mass body with gravitational parameter μ, the threshold is vₑₛc = √(2μ/r), where r is measured from the body's centre. At exactly this speed, the ideal trajectory is parabolic and approaches zero speed only at infinite distance. Greater speed gives positive specific energy and a hyperbolic trajectory; its hyperbolic excess speed describes the limiting speed at infinity in the two-body model. Escape is therefore an energy condition, not a direction: angular momentum and burn direction determine the path, and another body can change the outcome.[^1235][^1242][^1243]

Every quoted value needs a central body, gravitational parameter and radius. A surface value also depends on which reference radius is used; Earth’s equatorial, polar and mean radii differ, and irregular bodies may have more complex shapes. Altitude above a reference surface must be converted to centre-to-centre distance before it is used in the equation. State the adopted constants and units, since published parameters belong to particular reference solutions.[^1246][^1247][^1248]

A surface vacuum escape speed is a scale for a ballistic object, not a launch requirement. A vehicle can build energy through continuous thrust without receiving one instantaneous velocity jump equal to that speed. Its trajectory must account for thrust direction and duration, mass change, gravity, drag and steering losses. Rotation already gives a surface vehicle inertial velocity that depends on latitude; atmosphere, heating and structural limits add further constraints. Reaching the scalar threshold in an unsuitable direction can still produce a collision or atmospheric passage.[^1225][^1228][^1252][^1253]

Earth escape does not mean escape from the Solar System. In a patched-conic description, departure from Earth produces an Earth-relative hyperbolic excess vector that combines with Earth’s heliocentric velocity to set the next leg. The vector, reference frame and epoch matter, and multi-body propagation may be needed when the two-body boundary approximation is inadequate. Near zero specific energy, state and force-model uncertainty can change whether a fitted trajectory is classified as bound or unbound; report the covariance and model scope with the result.[^1234][^1241][^1242]
