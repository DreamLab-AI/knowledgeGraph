- ### Definition
  - A Digital Product Passport (DPP) is a standardised digital record that captures and communicates information about a product's material composition, origin, manufacturing process, environmental footprint, repair history, and end-of-life options throughout its lifecycle. Mandated by EU ecodesign and product sustainability legislation, DPPs are designed to be machine-readable and accessible to consumers, regulators, recyclers, and supply chain actors via data carriers such as QR codes or RFID. They are a foundational instrument of circular economy policy and are frequently implemented using blockchain or distributed ledger technology to ensure provenance integrity and tamper-evident audit trails.

- ### Content
  - The Digital Product Passport is a policy instrument emerging from the EU's Sustainable Products Regulation and the Ecodesign for Sustainable Products Regulation (ESPR). It aims to ensure that information about a product's sustainability, repairability, and end-of-life treatment is available in a structured, machine-readable format throughout the product's lifecycle — from manufacture through sale, use, repair, refurbishment, and recycling.
  - A DPP is associated with a specific product or product batch via a unique identifier (such as a serialised QR code, NFC tag, or RFID) that links to a digital record accessible over the web. The record is expected to contain material composition (including hazardous substances), carbon footprint data, repair and spare part information, warranty conditions, and recycling instructions. For some product categories, such as batteries (governed by the EU Battery Regulation), DPP requirements are already legally mandated with detailed technical specifications.
  - Blockchain and Distributed Ledger Technology are being evaluated as technical substrates for DPPs because they provide tamper-evident, decentralised ledgers capable of recording Supply Chain Traceability events across multiple participants without a trusted central authority. Provenance Tracking using blockchain enables each custodian in the supply chain to append authenticated records of their handling of the product. Standards bodies including GS1 (which manages global barcode standards) and FIWARE are active in defining interoperability standards for DPP data models and APIs, ensuring that passports can be queried across jurisdictions and product categories.
