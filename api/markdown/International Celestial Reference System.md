ICRS (the International Celestial Reference System) is an idealised barycentric coordinate system to which celestial positions are referred, kinematically non-rotating with respect to distant extragalactic objects.[^1]

It has no intrinsic orientation. It was aligned close to the mean equator and dynamical equinox of J2000.0 for continuity with earlier systems, but its orientation is independent of epoch, ecliptic and equator.[^1][^3] A 1997 resolution of the International Astronomical Union established it, with an origin at the solar system barycentre.[^1]

ICRS is realised by adopted coordinates of extragalactic sources. The International Celestial Reference Frame is that realisation, and the US Naval Observatory states that the precision of a quasar-based frame led to its formal adoption by the IAU as the instantiation of the ICRS.[^1][^2]

That circular dates from 2005 and describes the original radio catalogue of 212 defining sources, so later realisations are not covered here. The ICRS is not a terrestrial system and is not the equator and equinox of date.[^1][^2]
