A digital surface model is a gridded elevation model of the Earth's surface that includes buildings, infrastructure and vegetation.[^1]

According to the European Space Agency's Copernicus Data Space Ecosystem, the Copernicus DEM is a digital surface model. It is provided in three instances, EEA-10, GLO-30 and GLO-90, from TanDEM-X data acquired between 2011 and 2015, with older elevation models used where gaps were filled.[^1]

NASA describes a bare-earth digital elevation model differently: it shows elevation and topography without trees, buildings or other objects. The contrast is in what the surface includes, although producers use the abbreviations DEM, DSM and DTM with some variation, so a product should be read against its own description.[^1][^2]

Vertical reference of the Copernicus product is not stated, and accuracy for any particular area is not established. Those details belong in the product documentation rather than in the general term.[^1][^2]
