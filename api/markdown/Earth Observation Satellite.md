An Earth observation satellite is a spacecraft that carries instruments for observing the planet, which ESA says can stay in place for long periods and so show gradual environmental change.[^1]

Sentinel-1 is one example. ESA describes each satellite in the mission as carrying a radar instrument that supplies imagery of the surface in all weather, day and night. The ESA page lists several satellites in the series and a recent retirement story, so the number operating at a given time should be checked against the mission page.[^2]

NASA states that its Earth Science Division operates more than 20 satellites in orbit, which gives a sense of scale for one agency but is not a count of all such satellites. ESA also notes that the detail an instrument can resolve differs with orbit and intended function, so no single resolution applies to the class.[^1][^3]

Satellites in these sources are described through their instruments and data rather than through their bus design or launch vehicles. Commercial constellations are outside the scope of these retained pages.[^1][^2][^3]
