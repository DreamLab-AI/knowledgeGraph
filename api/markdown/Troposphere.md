Troposphere is the lowest layer of the atmosphere, extending from the surface to the tropopause, in which air generally becomes colder with increasing altitude.[^1][^2]

Met Office text says nearly all weather takes place in the troposphere and that 99% of all the water vapour in the atmosphere is found there, so almost all clouds form in it.[^1] MetLink explains that its thermal profile is largely the result of the heating of the Earth’s surface by incoming solar radiation, with heat transferred upward by convective and turbulent transfer.[^2] SWEET models the class as an atmosphere layer bounded below by the planetary surface and above by the tropopause, with a negative temperature gradient.[^3]

Two atmospheric sources give different shares of atmospheric mass: the Met Office says about 75% and MetLink says about 80%.[^1][^2] Its top also varies, between about 9 km and 17 km in the Met Office account.[^1] This page therefore states no single mass fraction or depth.

No retained source establishes how the layer is observed from space.

[^2]: Royal Meteorological Society MetLink, “Atmosphere”. https://www.metlink.org/resource/atmosphere/
