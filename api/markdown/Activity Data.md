- ### Definition
  - Activity data is the time-ordered record of discrete events generated by the interaction between humans, machines, and digital systems, collected by [[Tracking System]] and [[Telemetry & Analytics]] infrastructure. It forms the primary input to [[Analytics Engine]] systems for understanding behaviour, and when combined with identity context it creates [[Audit Log]] records that support [[Compliance]] monitoring. Its collection, retention, and use are subject to privacy regulations that balance utility against individual rights.

- ### Relationships
  - Activity data is a product of [[Data Collection]] processes and relies on [[Tracking System]] and [[Telemetry & Analytics]] infrastructure to capture events. It enables [[Analytics Engine]] processing and serves as the basis for [[Audit Log]] generation. It supports [[Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback]] by providing labelled examples of human behaviour. Its handling is governed by [[Compliance]] obligations, and its appropriate disclosure is a matter of [[Transparency]] policy for data controllers.

- ### Content
  - The systematic collection of activity data predates digital computing—railway companies recorded train movements and post offices logged mail volumes in the nineteenth century. The digital era transformed activity data collection from manual sampling to continuous automated capture. Web server logs, introduced in the early 1990s, were the first large-scale digital activity datasets, recording IP address, timestamp, requested URL, and response code for every HTTP request. Clickstream analysis emerged as a discipline in the late 1990s as e-commerce companies sought to understand user journeys and conversion paths.

  - Modern activity data architectures use event streaming platforms—Apache Kafka, Amazon Kinesis, Google Pub/Sub—to handle millions of events per second. Events are typically encoded in structured formats (JSON, Avro, Protocol Buffers) with standardised schemas such as the xAPI (Experience API) specification for learning systems or OpenTelemetry for software observability. Data lakes built on object storage (S3, GCS, Azure Blob) retain raw event streams indefinitely, whilst downstream systems apply transformation and enrichment pipelines to produce analytical datasets. Session stitching—linking events across devices and sessions to a single user identity—is a complex probabilistic inference problem central to activity data utility.

  - Activity data is foundational to personalisation, recommendation systems, fraud detection, and operational intelligence. It is the substrate from which collaborative filtering models (Netflix, Spotify), sequential recommendation models, and anomaly detection systems are trained. In workplace analytics, activity data from productivity tools, calendars, and communication platforms feeds into employee experience platforms, raising significant ethical concerns around surveillance. In healthcare, patient activity data from wearables and clinical systems is used for disease management and clinical trial monitoring.

  - In 2024–2025, the use of activity data to train AI systems—particularly large language models and recommendation systems—has attracted intense regulatory scrutiny. The EU's GDPR and its enforcement actions have established that activity data constitutes personal data when linked to identifiable individuals, requiring legal basis, purpose limitation, and data minimisation. Synthetic activity data generation has emerged as a privacy-preserving alternative for model training. The growing use of activity data in agentic AI systems, where AI agents log their own actions and decisions as activity streams, is creating new classes of activity data with novel provenance and accountability implications.
