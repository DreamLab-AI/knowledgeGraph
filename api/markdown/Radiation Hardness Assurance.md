Radiation hardness assurance is the set of activities undertaken to ensure that the electronics and materials of a space system perform to their design specifications after exposure to the mission space environment.[^1][^2]

NASA Goddard authors describe the work as a methodology that includes defining the radiation environment, assessing the radiation sensitivity of parts, worst-case analysis of the impact of radiation effects, and part acceptance decisions that are likely to include mitigation measures. The process is described as iterative, starting from top-level estimates of the environment and refining them as needed.[^1]

Testing is part of the evidence. NASA Goddard test compendia state that flight parts must be tolerant to total ionizing dose and displacement damage, or be mitigated by shielding or other methods, and that the effects need to be evaluated by test to determine the risk to a spaceflight application.[^3]

A common conflation is treating a radiation hard label as blanket protection. Barth, LaBel and Poivey note that such a label usually refers to total ionizing dose characteristics and does not usually imply hardness to non-ionizing dose or single event effects.[^1] The sources are NASA Goddard documents from 2004 to 2016 and do not set acceptance thresholds for any particular mission.
