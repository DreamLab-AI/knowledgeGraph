Sample return is the practice of bringing material collected from a body in the solar system back to Earth for laboratory study.[^1]

NASA describes sample return as one purpose of its planetary missions, allowing detailed study and analysis on Earth.[^1] The OSIRIS-REx mission delivered a capsule of material from the asteroid Bennu on 24 September 2023, and NASA gives the delivered total as 121.6 grams.[^2] Six Apollo missions between 1969 and 1972 brought back 382 kilograms of lunar rocks, core samples, pebbles, sand and dust.[^3]

Returned material is curated rather than consumed at once. NASA's Johnson Space Center houses the Bennu capsule, where curation began after the canister lid was removed, and the Apollo collection is held at the same centre, with samples distributed each year for research and teaching.[^2][^3]

Retained sources describe NASA programmes only. They do not establish how other agencies handle curation, nor do they give a general comparison of sample return with remote sensing or in situ analysis.[^1][^2][^3]
