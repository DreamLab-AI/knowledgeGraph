- ### Definition
  - Ethan Mollick is an American academic and professor at the [[Wharton School]] of the [[University of Pennsylvania]], recognised as one of the most influential public voices on the practical implications of [[Generative AI]] for work, education, and innovation. His research examines how [[Large Language Models]] and related AI tools augment human capability, reshape [[Knowledge Work Augmentation]], and alter the economics of [[Entrepreneurship]] and [[Innovation Management]]. He is notable for treating AI adoption as an empirical, hands-on practice rather than pure speculation.

- ### Overview
  - Ethan Mollick holds an academic position at the Wharton School with a dual focus on management science and entrepreneurship. His scholarly work addresses how technology — particularly AI — reshapes organisational behaviour, startup formation, and educational outcomes.
  - From approximately 2022 onward he became a prominent public commentator on [[Generative AI]], specifically [[ChatGPT]] and subsequent [[Large Language Models]], publishing accessible analyses on his Substack newsletter "One Useful Thing" and in mainstream media.
  - His core argument is that AI tools, particularly those based on [[Large Language Models]], represent a genuine and consequential shift in the nature of [[Knowledge Work Augmentation]] — comparable in magnitude to previous general-purpose technologies. He advocates direct, experimental engagement with AI tools rather than passive observation.
  - His 2024 book *Co-Intelligence: Living and Working with AI* synthesises his research and public writing into a framework for understanding [[Human-AI Collaboration]], presenting both opportunities and risks with empirical grounding.
  - He is associated with the concept of [[AI Literacy]] as an active, practice-based skill rather than merely a declarative understanding of AI technology.

- ### Key Contributions and Mechanisms
  - **Empirical AI Experimentation**: Mollick is known for conducting and publishing structured informal experiments on AI-assisted tasks — writing, coding, analysis — making his findings accessible beyond academic journals.
  - **Co-Intelligence Framework**: The concept of treating AI as a collaborative partner rather than a tool or replacement, discussed in depth in his book and central to debates about [[Human-AI Collaboration]] and [[Future of Work]].
  - **AI in Education**: Significant body of work on how AI affects pedagogy, assessment, and student learning outcomes, engaging with debates around [[AI in Education]] and academic integrity.
  - **Entrepreneurship and Innovation**: His foundational academic work covers startup dynamics, [[Innovation Management]], and the role of technology in enabling new ventures — brought together with AI research to examine how [[Generative AI]] lowers barriers to entrepreneurship.
  - **"One Useful Thing" Substack**: His public newsletter through which he disseminates research findings, experiments, and commentary on [[AI Adoption]] to a broad audience of practitioners and policymakers.
  - **[[Prompt Engineering]] Practice**: Advocates for developing practical [[Prompt Engineering]] skills as a component of professional AI literacy, demonstrating techniques through worked examples.
  - **Organisational AI Adoption**: Research on how firms and teams integrate AI tools into workflows, examining productivity effects and [[Organisational Learning]] dynamics.

- ### Applications and Use Cases
  - **Professional Upskilling**: His writing directly influences how professionals approach [[AI Adoption]] in their own work, providing actionable frameworks for experimenting with [[Generative AI]] tools.
  - **Educational Policy**: Contributions to debates on AI use in universities and schools, shaping thinking on assessment reform and [[AI in Education]] integration.
  - **Management Practice**: His research informs organisational strategies for deploying AI tools in knowledge-work settings, intersecting with [[Future of Work]] planning.
  - **AI Policy Discourse**: As a respected academic voice, his work informs [[AI Policy]] debates about societal readiness, workforce transformation, and ethical deployment of AI.
  - **Startup and Venture Ecosystems**: His background in [[Entrepreneurship]] means his AI adoption research informs venture strategy and product development within AI-adjacent startups.
  - **[[Behavioural Economics]] of AI**: Work examining how humans psychologically engage with AI outputs, trust formation, and decision-making when augmented by AI systems.
  - **[[Educational Technology]] Integration**: Practical guidance for educators integrating AI into classroom and curriculum design.
