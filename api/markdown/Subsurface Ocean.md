A subsurface ocean is a body of liquid water beneath the solid surface of a planetary body, most often beneath an ice shell on an icy moon.[^1]

NASA states that scientists strongly suspect a salty subsurface ocean beneath Europa's icy crust, and that Cassini discovered a global ocean of liquid water beneath the icy shell of Enceladus. The Europa case is therefore inferential in the wording of the source, while the Enceladus case is reported as a discovery.[^1]

Cassini flew through the Enceladus plume and detected organic and nitrogen-bearing molecules, and silica grains in the plume are of a size that likely formed in hydrothermal vents.[^1] NASA frames the Europa Clipper mission around whether Europa has the ingredients to support life as we know it, which is a mission question and not a finding.[^2]

An ocean beneath ice is not shown by these sources to be habitable or inhabited. Its depth, extent and composition are not established beyond what the sources state, and the retained pages do not settle which other bodies hold one.[^1][^2]

[^2]: NASA, “Planetary Science”. https://science.nasa.gov/planetary-science/
