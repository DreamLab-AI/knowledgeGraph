Radiation dosimetry is the measurement of exposure to ionising radiation in terms of defined quantities and units, such as the absorbed dose and dose equivalent reported for space missions.[^1][^2]

The International Commission on Radiation Units and Measurements publishes recommended definitions of the fundamental quantities and units for ionising radiation.[^1] A dose figure is only interpretable when the quantity and unit are named, because absorbed dose and dose equivalent are reported as separate results.[^2]

NASA monitors crews partly with dosimeters, sometimes worn as badges, which measure radiation and are returned to the ground for analysis. Its dosimetry laboratory handles thermoluminescent dosimeters, optically stimulated dosimeters and plastic nuclear track detectors.[^3]

The Space Radiation Analysis Group works to keep astronaut exposure below established safety limits, and NASA's Space Radiation Element studies how ionising radiation affects the human body.[^4] The retained sources do not state the limits themselves, and a measured dose is a physical quantity distinct from any estimate of biological risk.[^3][^4]
