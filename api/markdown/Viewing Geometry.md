Viewing geometry is the set of angles that describe the directions from an observed point on the Earth to the sensor and to the sun, such as the viewing zenith angle and the solar zenith angle.[^1]

Solar zenith angle is the angle between the local zenith and the line of sight from the ground point to the sun. Viewing zenith angle is the corresponding angle between the local zenith and the line of sight to the satellite. The BIRA-IASB page states that the solar zenith angle depends on the position on the Earth and on the local date and time.[^1]

Instrument modes are also described by their geometry. In nadir mode the instrument points downward and measures sunlight reflected from the surface or scattered in the atmosphere, whereas in limb mode it observes scattered sunlight at almost horizontal viewing angles.[^2]

Both retained sources come from atmospheric-composition retrieval rather than land imaging. They do not define azimuth angles or the relative azimuth, and they do not establish how surface reflectance changes with angle, so those points are left to other sources.[^1][^2]
