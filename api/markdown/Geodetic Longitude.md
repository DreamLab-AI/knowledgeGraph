Geodetic longitude is the angle from the prime meridian plane to the meridian plane of a given point, with eastward treated as positive.[^1]

OGC gives ellipsoidal longitude as a synonym.[^1] Ordnance Survey describes the prime meridian as one meridian of the ellipsoid that is chosen and assigned zero longitude, with the longitude of a point being the angle between its meridian and that one.[^2] Which meridian is used belongs to the coordinate reference system definition, and the opened sources do not discuss alternatives.

Longitude and latitude together define a point on the surface of a stated ellipsoid, and a third coordinate is needed to place points above or below it.[^2] For that reason longitude is only meaningful alongside the ellipsoid and datum it refers to.[^2]

Sign convention is part of the definition. The OGC wording treats eastward as positive, and other applications may state a different convention.
