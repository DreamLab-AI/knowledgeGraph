A laser communication terminal is optical communications hardware that transmits or receives modulated light and acquires and points towards a compatible optical endpoint.

A terminal may combine an optical modem, laser and detector, telescope or other beam-forming optics, pointing-acquisition-tracking sensors and actuators, and control electronics. This is a functional description rather than a fixed equipment boundary: implementations can distribute these functions differently. LCRD, for example, has optical modems, two optical terminals and optical ground stations; the terminal is an endpoint subsystem, not the end-to-end link, network architecture or service.[^1][^2]

Acquisition and pointing must account for the narrow optical beam and the motion of the endpoints. The applicable accuracy, beacon arrangement and control design are system-specific; a terminal description alone does not establish a link data rate, interoperability or availability.[^2][^3]

Evidence spans different maturity levels. ESA’s PATROL work describes completed development of a PAT controller breadboard, while Alphasat records a project-specific optical relay demonstration from low Earth orbit to geostationary orbit followed by a Ka-band ground link. NASA’s LCRD is an experimental relay platform. These demonstrations establish their stated engineering activities, not a general operational service. NASA’s pointing explainer dates from 2018 and carries an outdated-page caveat, so it is used only for basic mechanism, not current specifications.[^1][^2][^3][^4]
