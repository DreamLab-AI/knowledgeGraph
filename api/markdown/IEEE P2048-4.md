- ### Content

  ## Definition

  **IEEE P2048-4** is the interchange-format part of the [[IEEE P2048]] series on virtual and augmented reality. Immersive video cannot be treated as ordinary video with a wider lens: a player must know the projection used (equirectangular, cubemap, fisheye), whether the content is monoscopic or stereoscopic and how the eye views are packed, the initial and permitted viewports, spatial audio bindings, and — for volumetric content — the geometry representation being carried. P2048-4's scope is to standardise how this information is expressed in file containers and streaming manifests so that content mastered once plays back correctly across heterogeneous headsets, mobile players, and web runtimes.

  The part therefore sits at the boundary between coding and experience. It does not define compression algorithms — those come from codec standards such as HEVC, VVC, and AV1, and from volumetric coding work in MPEG — but it defines the signalling wrapped around coded payloads: projection and packing metadata, region-wise quality indications for viewport-adaptive streaming, synchronisation of video, audio, and haptic tracks, and profile designations that bound what a conformant player must support. In this it parallels what MPEG's OMAF does within the ISO base media file format family, providing the IEEE series with a format vocabulary aligned to the taxonomy of IEEE P2048-2.

  The contrast with [[IEEE P2048-3]] is one of subject matter within the same series: P2048-3 standardises person identification — how users and their avatars are identified and authenticated in immersive environments — while P2048-4 standardises the media artefacts those environments consume. The two meet in deployed systems, where identity governs entitlement to protected immersive content, but their normative scopes are disjoint.

  ## Current Landscape

  Immersive media packaging is converging across bodies: MPEG's OMAF and V3C carriage specifications, the DASH and CMAF streaming ecosystems, and WebXR-adjacent web delivery all address overlapping needs, with IEEE P2048-4 contributing the VR/AR-community view of required signalling. Practical adoption pressure comes from 360° video platforms, live immersive sport and events, and early volumetric streaming trials, where viewport-adaptive delivery can halve bandwidth but only if projection and region metadata are interoperable. The open front is volumetric and neural-rendered content — point clouds, meshes, and Gaussian-splat scenes — whose streaming formats are still stabilising, which keeps this part, like its siblings, at emerging maturity.
