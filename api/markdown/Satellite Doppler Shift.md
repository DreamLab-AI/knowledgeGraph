Satellite Doppler shift is the change in received carrier frequency caused by relative line-of-sight motion between a satellite and another endpoint.

For radial relative speed small compared with the speed of light, the first-order relation is Δf/f₀ ≈ −vᵣ/c when vᵣ is defined positive for recession; reversing the radial-velocity convention reverses the sign. The shift is tied to changing propagation phase and path length, so the sign and reference frequency must be stated when reporting a value.[^1][^2]

In radiometric tracking, a Doppler count over an interval measures accumulated phase change and is used to estimate line-of-sight range change, hence topocentric range rate. It is not by itself a direct three-dimensional velocity measurement; orbit determination combines observations over time with geometry and a dynamical model.[^1][^2]

A measured frequency residual is not automatically physical Doppler. With one-way non-coherent tracking, the received frequency is compared with an onboard oscillator estimate; its frequency-reference error can resemble a range-rate error. Coherent two-way tracking relates the downlink to the uplink frequency standard and can reduce that independent-reference uncertainty, but station, transponder, propagation and modelling errors remain. Deep-space light time or station visibility may also prevent a same-station two-way observation.[^1][^2]
