Partial gravity is the reduced gravity of planetary bodies, treated in spaceflight research alongside weightlessness.[^1]

Partial gravity of planetary bodies appears in NASA's Biological and Physical Sciences programme page, next to weightlessness, as a spaceflight condition that its physical sciences research examines. The page names the concept but gives no numerical definition, so this page gives none.[^1]

Partial gravity can be simulated briefly on parabolic flights, which ESA says can fly arcs approximating lunar or martian gravity levels. Those simulations last seconds, and the cited source does not establish the effects of sustained partial gravity.[^2]

In space physiology, microgravity or partial gravity is described as one of several stressors acting together on the human body, alongside ionising radiation, altered atmospheric composition, circadian disruption and confinement.[^3]
