An ocean world is a planet or moon that holds substantial liquid water, a category NASA uses in its search for life beyond Earth.[^1]

NASA describes water on other worlds as occurring in diverse forms on moons, dwarf planets and comets, so the term does not imply a single kind of body or a single kind of ocean.[^1] At Enceladus, material from the ocean is sprayed into space where a spacecraft can sample it, and NASA reports that the samples indicate most of the chemical ingredients needed for life.[^2]

For several other bodies the evidence is less direct. ESA describes Ganymede, Europa and Callisto as thought to have oceans of liquid water beneath icy crusts, and that wording is a hedge rather than a confirmation.[^3]

No source establishes that an ocean world is inhabited. Having liquid water and chemical ingredients is a condition studied in relation to habitability, not a detection of life.[^1][^2]

[^1]: NASA, “Ocean Worlds: Water in the Solar System and Beyond”. https://science.nasa.gov/solar-system/ocean-worlds/
[^2]: NASA, “Enceladus”. https://science.nasa.gov/saturn/moons/enceladus/
[^3]: ESA, “Juice factsheet”. https://www.esa.int/Science_Exploration/Space_Science/Juice/Juice_factsheet
