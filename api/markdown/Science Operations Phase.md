A science operations phase is the part of a mission in which the spacecraft carries out its main scientific observations, for a planetary orbiter by exploring a planetary system or mapping a planet.[^1]

NASA's encounter chapter describes orbital science-gathering as having at least two broad categories, system exploration and planetary mapping. A system exploration tour makes observations of the planet, its satellites, rings, atmosphere and magnetosphere over typically a few years or more, whereas mapping concentrates on the planet itself.[^1]

During either activity the orbiting spacecraft is in an extended encounter period requiring continuous or dependably regular support from the flight team, the Deep Space Network and other institutional teams.[^1] Phase names can differ between missions, and no standard name is defined for flyby encounter phases; phase boundaries therefore depend on the mission's own plan. Because the source describes planetary missions rather than Earth-orbiting or astronomy missions, its applicability to those domains is unclear.

[^1]: NASA Science, *Basics of Space Flight*, Chapter 16: Encounter. https://science.nasa.gov/learn/basics-of-space-flight/chapter16-1/
