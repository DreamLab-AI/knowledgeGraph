Geocoding is the process of taking an address and returning a latitude and longitude coordinate for it.[^1][^2]

The US Census Bureau defines geocoding as taking an address and returning an actual or calculated latitude/longitude coordinate, with the achievable granularity depending on which parts of the address are provided.[^1] The New York State GIS programme describes it as the use of technology and reference data to return a geographic coordinate when an address location is entered, and defines reverse geocoding as the opposite conversion into a human-readable address.[^2]

SWEET lists Geocode as a subclass of GeospatialService.[^3] Natural Resources Canada notes that a gazetteer system may include the capability to do geo-coding, meaning conversion of a street address to a geographic location.[^4]

Retained sources are about address matching against reference data. They do not establish the separate use of geocoded in SAR product names for imagery resampled to a map grid, and the Earth observation sense of the word needs its own source.
