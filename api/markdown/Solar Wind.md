Solar wind is a continuous, variable flow of charged particles from the Sun's outer atmosphere. It carries the Sun's magnetic field through interplanetary space; solar rotation winds the large-scale field into a spiral, while waves and transient structures create local variation.[^1]

## Structure and interaction with Earth

Solar-wind speed, density, temperature and magnetic-field direction change over time. Fast streams can overtake slower plasma and form compressed interaction regions. A coronal mass ejection is a transient magnetised structure travelling through this background wind, rather than another name for the wind itself.

At Earth, changing pressure compresses or expands the dayside magnetosphere. Magnetic coupling can transfer solar-wind energy and particles across its boundary, driving aurora, radiation-belt changes and geomagnetic currents. The response depends on both external conditions and the magnetosphere's prior state.

## Measurement and warning

Spacecraft near the Sun–Earth L1 point make direct upstream measurements. NOAA's DSCOVR supplies real-time plasma and magnetic-field observations, with up to about an hour between sampling at L1 and arrival at Earth; the actual interval varies with speed and spacecraft position.[^2] This short lead is especially valuable because remote images do not yet provide a reliable operational measurement of an approaching CME's magnetic orientation.

UK teams contribute instruments across the measurement chain. UCL's Mullard Space Science Laboratory leads the Solar Wind Analyser suite on Solar Orbiter, which measures electron, proton and heavy-ion populations near the Sun.[^3] Imperial College London leads the magnetic-field instrument on the Interstellar Mapping and Acceleration Probe, measuring field strength and direction and releasing rapid data products.[^4] Each instrument measures part of the environment; neither alone constitutes a complete forecast.

Future observations from the Sun–Earth L5 region are intended to improve the side-on view of solar structures. Until those streams are operational and validated, they should be described as planned capability rather than current warning performance.
