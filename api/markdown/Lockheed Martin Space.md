Lockheed Martin Space is the Lockheed Martin business area that describes itself as a mission integrator responsible for the research, design, development, engineering and production of spacecraft, instruments, robotics and integrated systems.[^1]

According to the business area page, its work includes spacecraft for the scientific study of the solar system, systems for returning to the Moon and future Mars missions, national security space systems, and spacecraft for weather forecasting and environmental observation.[^1] Customers named on the page include the U.S. Space Force, the NRO, NASA and the Missile Defense Agency.[^1]

Orion is the best-documented programme in the retained sources. A Lockheed Martin release of 31 August 2006 says NASA selected the Lockheed Martin team to design and build Orion with an initial contract value of approximately $4 billion, with Lockheed Martin as prime contractor leading an industry team.[^2] A 2021 company release says Lockheed Martin is prime contractor and built the crew module, crew module adaptor and launch abort system for the Artemis I spacecraft.[^3] NASA separately says crew modules for Artemis missions are built by prime contractor Lockheed Martin, with the pressure vessel welded at the Michoud Assembly Facility in New Orleans.[^4]

The 2006 figure is an initial contract value only, and later contract changes are not covered. The Orion role is a programme-level relationship and does not establish Lockheed Martin's role in other programmes. The business area page is a company self-description and gives no structure or financial detail.
