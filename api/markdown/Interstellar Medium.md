Interstellar medium is the material that fills the space between the stars of a galaxy, made up mostly of gas with a small share of dust.[^1][^2]

Its gas is chiefly hydrogen and helium with other elements in smaller amounts, and the solid grains are described as silicate or graphite cores with a mantle of ices. These are averages, because the material is patchy and some clouds are far denser than the mean, although even these are closer to a vacuum than anything made on Earth.[^1]

Stars form from interstellar matter, and forming stars and young clusters are found with interstellar clouds. Cool gas and dust are observed at radio and infrared wavelengths respectively, and neutral hydrogen can be detected through a radio line that dust does not absorb.[^2]

Educational descriptions often stress the cold clouds, but the term covers more than the cold clouds that dominate the educational descriptions: the sources treat the medium as a mixture whose properties vary from place to place, so a single temperature or density should not be taken from them.[^1][^2]
