Formation flying is the operation of two or more spacecraft in a controlled relative arrangement, which the NASA small-spacecraft survey treats as a research area needing relative navigation, guidance and actuation.[^1]

NASA's survey describes precision formation flying together with rendezvous and proximity operations. Autonomous versions need sensors to feed onboard navigation filters that determine relative positions and attitudes, guidance laws that maintain formations within constraints and avoid conjunctions, and actuation to control relative states.[^1]

ESA presents Proba-3 as a precision formation flying mission in which a pair of satellites fly together in a fixed configuration, acting as a single large solar coronagraph. The page is archived, describes the mission before launch and reports no results, so it supports the concept rather than any achieved precision.[^2]

The NASA text discusses formation flying and rendezvous as related research areas without separating them formally. This page therefore does not claim a fixed boundary between them, nor an accuracy standard for either.[^1]
