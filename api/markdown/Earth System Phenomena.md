Earth system phenomena are observable events, processes and features arising from the interaction of the atmosphere, ocean, land, ice and life that make up the Earth system.[^1][^2]

NASA's description of Earth system science says the continents, atmosphere, oceans, ice and life are ever changing and ever interacting, and that these interconnected processes comprise the Earth system.[^2] The NASA Earth Science programme asks how the global Earth system is changing, with human-induced and natural changes both influencing productivity and the availability of natural resources, and its research areas include the cryosphere, hydrosphere and biosphere.[^1]

This corpus uses the term as a grouping for observed phenomena such as sea ice types and flood-control structures, which sit across the cryosphere and the hydrosphere. Retained sources describe the Earth system and its spheres but do not define the phrase Earth system phenomena itself, so the grouping is an editorial choice.[^1][^2]

Retained sources do not establish a closed list of phenomena or a formal boundary with Earth Observation, to which the page is related.[^1][^2]
