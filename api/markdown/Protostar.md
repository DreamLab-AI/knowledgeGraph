A protostar is an early stage of star formation in which a collapsing clump of gas and dust has heated up but is not yet a hydrogen-fusing star.[^1][^2]

NASA describes stars as forming inside cold molecular clouds, where gas clumps into dense pockets and some clumps collapse under gravity. Friction heats the collapsing material, and a protostar develops.[^1]

At first most of the protostar's energy comes from heat released by its collapse. After millions of years, pressures and temperatures in the core are described as high enough for hydrogen fusion to begin, and the star then balances gravity with fusion pressure.[^1][^3]

The Chandra stellar-evolution material calls the point at which a protostar becomes a main-sequence star the zero-age main sequence. The sources give only a qualitative account; they do not state how long the stage lasts for stars of different masses, and the page makes no such claim.[^2][^3]
