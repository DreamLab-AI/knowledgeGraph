Medium Earth Orbit (MEO) is the broad region of geocentric Earth orbits between low Earth orbit and the geosynchronous altitude range, under a stated altitude convention.

MEO is commonly associated with navigation constellations, but the label has no single boundary that fits every catalogue or operational use. NASA's SCaN glossary gives an altitude range from 2,000 to 36,000 km, while an ITU overview places most MEO satellites between 8,000 and 20,000 km; ESA describes the band broadly as lying between LEO and GEO. State the altitude datum and adopted boundary when classifying an object, especially if the orbit is eccentric.[^1391][^1398][^1400]

GPS provides a familiar near-circular MEO example: NASA lists a nominal altitude of about 20,200 km, a 12-hour period and six orbital planes inclined by 55 degrees. ESA lists Galileo's nominal circular shell at 23,222 km and 56 degrees. These are constellation designs, not definitions of MEO. The orbit height supports broad visibility and longer orbital periods than LEO, while inclination and plane spacing shape coverage and revisit geometry.[^1392][^1393]

MEO mission design must account for the trapped-particle radiation environment. ESA reports that Galileo's orbit crosses the outer Van Allen belt. The UK-built GIOVE-A carried monitors from QinetiQ and the University of Surrey; its 23,600 km, 56-degree orbit returned measurements showing variable particle flux and electron events. British Antarctic Survey researchers later modelled outer-belt variability and validated results against Galileo in-orbit measurements. These observations describe changing environments, not a fixed dose for every MEO mission.[^1395][^1396][^1403]

Radiation estimates therefore need the spacecraft's trajectory and duration, particle energies and directions, shielding, materials and solar or geomagnetic conditions. ESA's MEO-V3 is one trapped-electron model with specified inputs and a recorded version; mission analyses should validate its use and include other relevant particle populations. UK SDA requirements also treat MEO as a distinct surveillance region, reinforcing that an operational region is a declared convention rather than a natural boundary.[^1394][^1397]
