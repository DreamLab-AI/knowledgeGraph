Universal Time 1 is the angle of the Earth's rotation about the CIP axis, defined by a conventional linear relation to the Earth Rotation Angle, and it can be regarded as a time determined by the rotation of the Earth.[^1]

UT1 is determined by observation, currently from very long baseline interferometry of the diurnal motions of distant radio sources, and it is obtained from the uniform scale UTC through the quantity UT1 minus UTC, which the IERS provides.[^1] The US Naval Observatory describes it as mean solar time at the prime meridian and says it measures UT1 minus UTC daily with short VLBI sessions.[^2]

Earth's rotation is irregular, so leap seconds are occasionally added to or removed from UTC to keep it close to UT1.[^2] Navipedia distinguishes UT0, which is corrected for polar motion to give UT1, and UT2, which adds a seasonal correction.[^3] The broadcast quantity DUT1 is the same difference given with a precision of 0.1 seconds.[^4]

The IERS explanatory supplement dates from 2004 and describes some definitions as they stood before 2003, so the Conventions are the better reference for the definition. These sources do not establish how the permitted UT1 minus UTC difference may change in future.[^1][^4]
