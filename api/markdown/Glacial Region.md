A glacial region is a geographic division used to group glaciers for regional assessment, the standard scheme being the Global Terrestrial Network for Glaciers regions of glaciers outside the ice sheets.[^1][^2]

That scheme defines 20 first-order glacier regions and more than 90 second-order regions covering all of Earth's glaciers outside the ice sheets.[^1] Standardised regions support regional assessments of glacier change and the regional aggregation of glacier data.[^1] The Randolph Glacier Inventory packages its outlines globally and by region, with regions based on this network.[^2]

Retained sources describe glacier regions rather than a region called a glacial region, so this page treats the term as that grouping. They do not establish whether the term also covers areas shaped by past glaciation, and they give no boundary rule beyond the region lists themselves.

[^1]: Global Terrestrial Network for Glaciers, “Glacier Regions”. https://www.gtn-g.ch/glacreg/
[^2]: NSIDC, “A Dataset of Global Glacier Outlines, Version 4”. https://nsidc.org/data/nsidc-0770/versions/4
