Right ascension is the celestial equivalent of longitude, measured on the celestial sphere.[^1]

It is usually expressed in hours, minutes and seconds of time rather than degrees, because the sky appears to turn 360 degrees in 24 hours. NASA states that an hour of right ascension equals 15 degrees of sky rotation. It is paired with declination, the celestial equivalent of latitude.[^1]

Vernal equinox, also called the first point of Aries, marks the zero point; NASA describes it as the point where the Sun crosses into the northern hemisphere at the start of spring.[^1]

Epochs are not discussed in the page opened for this entry. Because the equinox itself moves with precession, a stated right ascension needs a reference epoch; that point is drawn from the precession discussion in the same NASA chapter and is not stated on the page for this concept.
