A planetary rover is a mobile, electrically powered spacecraft that drives across the surface of another world to explore it.[^1]

NASA's spacecraft classification chapter records that the first Mars rover, Sojourner, was carried by the Mars Pathfinder lander, and that its rocker-bogie mobility system was scaled up and used on all Mars rovers up to the date of that text. The same chapter says rover craft need to be semi-autonomous, because radio delay between Earth and Mars means they must make some decisions on their own as they move.[^1]

JPL's account of the Mars Exploration Rovers gives a concrete example. In autonomous navigation mode the vehicles used cameras to look ahead and avoid hazards, and they were the first to demonstrate visual odometry, which tracks nearby terrain features to measure slip on soft or sloped ground. JPL reports that the rovers combined human-directed drives with autonomous drives.[^2] Design of rovers on the Moon or other bodies and the general autonomy threshold for classification are not covered by these sources.
