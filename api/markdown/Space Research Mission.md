A space research mission is a set of coordinated tasks and operations undertaken to meet specific scientific or technical objectives within defined time and location constraints.[^1]

That wording comes from the NASA Space Life Sciences Ontology, which notes that it is modified from a NASA Science Mission Directorate catalogue definition. It is a formal definition for the ontology and may not match every agency's use of the word mission.[^1]

In the life sciences, such missions study the effects of space stressors. NASA's biological and physical sciences programme describes studying the effects of radiation and microgravity on biological and physical phenomena as a means of promoting mission success.[^2]

NASA's Human Research Program and ESA both describe running investigations on the International Space Station and in ground analogue studies. The sources do not list every mission or set out how missions are selected.[^3][^4]
