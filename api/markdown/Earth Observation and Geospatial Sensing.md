Earth observation and geospatial sensing is the domain covering satellite observation of the planet and the use of the resulting data, as set out for satellite Earth observation in the CEOS handbook.[^1]

The CEOS handbook describes capabilities, applications and missions, and its 2015 edition looks at disaster risk reduction. CEOS itself was set up in 1984 to coordinate and harmonise Earth observations so that users could access and use the data, with early work on interoperability, common data formats and inter-calibration of instruments.[^1][^2]

National and agency sources give examples of use. NASA names the oceans, land cover, ice and atmosphere, and the UK Space Agency names climate response, extreme weather and resource management among the benefits it expects from Earth observation data.[^3][^4]

None of the retained sources defines geospatial sensing as a field in its own right or describes geographic information systems. Those links are drawn in the ontology and are not established by these pages.[^1][^2][^3][^4]
