An Earth observation data product is a packaged dataset derived from satellite or other Earth observation measurements and issued at a stated processing level.[^1]

NASA describes its standard data products as an internally consistent, well-calibrated record of Earth’s geophysical properties to support science. It also notes that some products, such as near real-time data, are released more quickly than standard processing. Products are made at distributed archive centres or science investigator-led processing systems, so one instrument can feed products at several levels.[^1]

Missions define their own product classes. Sentinel-1 documentation lists Level-0 raw SAR products in several classes and Level-1 products such as single look complex and ground range detected data, and Sentinel-2 documentation describes products at Level-0, Level-1A and Level-1B stages.[^2][^3] The SAR Handbook describes product types in terms of the processing level they correspond to.[^4]

This term should not be read as a statement about quality or readiness for analysis. The sources opened here describe what each product type contains and how it is processed, but they do not establish a common product definition across agencies, and the status of a product depends on its own documentation.[^1][^3][^4]
