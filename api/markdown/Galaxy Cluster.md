A galaxy cluster is a gravitationally bound structure of many galaxies, and NASA says clusters may contain thousands of galaxies.[^1]

Chandra describes a cluster as made of galaxies, hot gas that fills the space between the galaxies, and dark matter that supplies additional gravity.[^2] It says astronomers think clusters form as clumps of dark matter and associated galaxies are pulled together by gravity, with groups of dozens of galaxies merging to form larger clusters, and that the gas is heated as the cluster forms.[^2]

NASA separates clusters from smaller groups, which hold about 100 or fewer members, and from superclusters, which are arrangements of groups and clusters that are not gravitationally bound.[^1]

The Chandra page is a field guide and states formation as what astronomers think, so the sequence is a model and not a measured history.[^2]

[^2]: Chandra X-ray Observatory, “Field Guide to X-ray Sources: Groups & Clusters of Galaxies”. https://chandra.harvard.edu/xray_sources/galaxy_clusters.html
