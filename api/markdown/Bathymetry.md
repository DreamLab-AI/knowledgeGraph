Bathymetry is the study of the beds or floors of water bodies, including oceans, rivers, streams and lakes.[^1][^2]

NOAA states that the term originally referred to ocean depth relative to sea level and has come to mean submarine topography, the depths and shapes of underwater terrain.[^1] It is described as the underwater equivalent of topography, and bathymetric maps represent relief in the way topographic maps do on land.[^1][^2]

Bathymetry is the foundation of hydrography, which also covers the shoreline, tides, currents, waves and the physical and chemical properties of the water.[^1] Hydrography is therefore wider than bathymetry. Ocean mapping uses acoustics, satellites, light and imaging among other technologies.[^2] NOAA uses sonar for nautical charts, hazards to navigation and seafloor objects, and explains that sound waves travel farther in water than radar and light waves.[^3]

These pages are outreach descriptions from one agency. They do not give accuracy, resolution or coverage figures for any method, and the choice of vertical datum for depths is not covered by them.[^1][^2][^3]
