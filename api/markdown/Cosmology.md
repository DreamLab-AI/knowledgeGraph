Cosmology is the study of the origin, evolution and nature of the universe, and of how it is conceptualised.[^1]

NASA describes cosmology as transformed by ideas and discoveries of the twentieth century, while stating that much remains unknown. For example, scientists are not sure what came before cosmic inflation or what powered it.[^1]

Much of the evidence is observational. ESA describes the cosmic microwave background as the furthest back in time that can be explored using light, formed about 380,000 years after the Big Bang and carrying traces of the seeds of later stars and galaxies.[^2] NASA says its WMAP measurements of small temperature variations in that radiation reveal the size, matter content, age, geometry and fate of the universe.[^3]

Survey missions test the picture at later epochs. ESA states that Euclid observes how the Universe has expanded and how structure has formed, so that properties of dark energy, dark matter and gravity can be inferred.[^4] These sources describe the current account rather than a settled one, and they do not establish what lies beyond the observable universe.
