An ice field, written icefield by NSIDC, is a mass of glacier ice somewhat similar to an ice cap but usually smaller and lacking a dome-like shape.[^1] Compared to an ice cap, it is more strongly influenced by the underlying terrain.[^1]

This page follows the glacier sense. SWEET defines the class differently, as an ice field primarily composed of sea ice floes greater than 10 kilometres across.[^2] The two usages describe different things, one a land-ice body and one a sea ice expanse, and the retained sources do not reconcile them.

Retained sources give no area range for the glacier sense and no method for detecting an icefield from space. A single glaciological source, NSIDC, supports the definition, so the boundary with an ice cap rests on shape and terrain control rather than on a stated number.

[^1]: National Snow and Ice Data Center, “Glaciers”. https://nsidc.org/learn/parts-cryosphere/glaciers
[^2]: ESIP SWEET ontology (GitHub mirror), “SWEET realmCryo.ttl”. https://raw.githubusercontent.com/ESIPFed/sweet/master/src/realmCryo.ttl
