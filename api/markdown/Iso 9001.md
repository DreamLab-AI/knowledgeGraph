ISO 9001 is an international standard that specifies the requirements for a quality management system (QMS), enabling organisations to demonstrate their ability to consistently provide products and services that meet customer and regulatory requirements. Published by the International Organization for Standardization, it is built on principles such as customer focus, process approach and continual improvement. Organisations can be independently certified against ISO 9001 to evidence conformity.

### Overview

- ISO 9001 sets the requirements for a quality management system that organisations can adopt and certify against.
- It rests on quality-management principles including customer focus and the process approach.
- Continual improvement, exemplified by the plan-do-check-act cycle, is central to the standard.
- Independent certification bodies audit conformity and issue accredited certificates.

### Key aspects

- A documented quality management system covering scope, policy and objectives.
- Process approach mapping inputs, activities and outputs across the organisation.
- Risk-based thinking applied to planning and operational control.
- Internal audit and management review to drive corrective action.
- Continual improvement embedded through measurement and feedback loops.

### Applications

- Manufacturing and service organisations seeking consistent quality.
- Supplier qualification and contractual quality assurance.
- Regulatory and customer assurance through accredited certification.
- Foundation for sector-specific standards that extend the QMS model.
