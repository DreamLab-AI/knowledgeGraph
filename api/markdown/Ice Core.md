An ice core is a cylindrical sample drilled from accumulated snow and ice that preserves trapped air bubbles from earlier periods.[^1][^2] It is a physical sample of an ice mass, not a mass of ice in the landscape, so it differs from the glacier-type pages in this batch.

Cores usually come from ice sheets such as those of Antarctica and Greenland, or from high mountain glaciers.[^1][^2] NASA likens a core to a marine sediment core, a vertical timeline of past climates, and says the trapped bubbles record past atmospheric composition.[^3][^1]

The British Antarctic Survey briefing reports that the oldest continuous records extend 123,000 years in Greenland and 800,000 years in Antarctica.[^2] That briefing dates from 2010, and the retained sources do not establish whether those ages have since been exceeded. None of them covers satellite observation, so the retained sources do not establish any direct Earth observation use.
