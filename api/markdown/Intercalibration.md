Intercalibration is the comparison and harmonisation of calibration between Earth observation instruments so that observations from different satellites can be used together.[^1]

The WMO Global Space-based Inter-Calibration System is an example. It is described as a collaborative effort, begun in 2005 with the Coordination Group for Meteorological Satellites, to monitor, improve and harmonise the quality of observations from operational weather and environmental satellites. Its stated strategy combines monitoring instrument performance, operational inter-calibration, tying measurements to absolute references and recalibrating archived data.[^1]

Calibration, in the CEOS working group's wording, is the process of defining a system's response to known and controlled signal inputs. Validation is a separate step in which independent means are used to assess the quality of derived data products. Intercalibration is therefore a calibration activity between instruments and does not by itself validate a product.[^2]

These sources describe programme aims and do not give a single method or an achieved accuracy. Whether two instruments are consistent depends on the quantity, the reference and the period compared, and the pages used here do not state those details.[^1][^2]

[^2]: CEOS, “Working Group on Calibration & Validation (WGCV)”. https://ceos.org/ourwork/workinggroups/wgcv/
