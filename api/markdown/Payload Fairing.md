A payload fairing is the shell at the top of a launch vehicle that encloses the payload and protects it from aerodynamic, thermal and acoustic conditions during ascent through the atmosphere.[^1][^2]

ESA describes the Ariane 5 fairing as positioned at the very top of the launcher and as jettisoned once the launcher had left the atmosphere, which lightened the remaining vehicle. NASA similarly describes an Atlas V fairing for the Mars Science Laboratory spacecraft as protecting it during ascent and being jettisoned once the spacecraft is outside the atmosphere.[^1][^2]

In the Ariane 5 case the fairing consisted of two half shells joined by a vertical separation system, with carbon-covered aluminium honeycomb outer panels. A double pyrotechnic system separated it, one part splitting the shell and the other freeing it from the launcher. These details belong to that vehicle, and general sizing rules, load limits and jettison timings applicable to every launcher should be taken from the relevant launch provider's documentation.[^1]
