- ### Definition
  - The psychological state in which a human user accepts vulnerability to an automated system's actions based on expectations that the system will perform appropriately to achieve the user's goals, despite uncertainty and the possibility of negative consequences. It represents the user's confidence in the system's reliability, competence, and integrity.

  - **Dimensions of Trust**
  - Competence (ability to perform task)
  - Reliability (consistency over time)
  - Predictability (behavior expectations)
  - Transparency (understandability)
  - Benevolence (system acts in user's interest)
  - Integrity (adherence to principles)

  - **Factors Influencing Trust**
  - **System-Related Factors**
    - Performance accuracy and reliability
    - Consistency of behavior
    - Predictability of actions
    - Transparency and explainability
    - Error handling and recovery
    - Response time
  - **User-Related Factors**
    - Prior experience with automation
    - Technical expertise
    - Individual differences (age, personality)
    - Expectations and mental models
    - Cultural background
  - **Context-Related Factors**
    - Task complexity and criticality
    - Workload and time pressure
    - Consequences of failure
    - Social influences
    - Organizational culture

  - **Trust Calibration**
  - Over-Trust (excessive reliance, complacency)
  - Under-Trust (disuse, manual override)
  - Appropriate Trust (calibrated to system capability)
  - Misuse (using beyond capability)
  - Disuse (rejecting capable system)

  - **Trust Development Over Time**
  - Initial Trust (based on expectations, reputation)
  - Learned Trust (based on experience)
  - Dynamic Trust (adjusts with interactions)
  - Damaged Trust (after failures)
  - Repaired Trust (recovery after failures)

  - **Measuring Trust**
  - Subjective Measures
    - Self-report questionnaires (e.g., Trust Scale)
    - Interviews and focus groups
  - Behavioral Measures
    - Reliance behavior
    - System usage patterns
    - Override frequency
    - Verification behaviors
  - Physiological Measures
    - Eye tracking
    - Heart rate variability
    - Galvanic skin response

  - **Trust and Safety in Robotics**
  - Essential for human-robot collaboration
  - Affects acceptance of cobots
  - Influences safety compliance
  - Impacts productivity
  - Critical for autonomous systems

  - **Building Trust in Robotic Systems**
  - Demonstrate competence through performance
  - Provide transparency (explain decisions)
  - Ensure predictable behavior
  - Communicate limitations clearly
  - Implement graceful error handling
  - Enable user control and intervention
  - Design intuitive interfaces
  - Provide training and familiarization

  - **Consequences of Trust Misalignment**
  - **Over-Trust**
    - Automation complacency
    - Reduced monitoring
    - Slower error detection
    - Safety risks
  - **Under-Trust**
    - System disuse
    - Excessive manual override
    - Lost efficiency benefits
    - User frustration

  - **Applications in Robotics**
  - Collaborative robots (cobots) in manufacturing
  - Autonomous vehicles
  - Medical robots (surgical assistants)
  - Service robots (caregiving)
  - Military and defense robots
  - Social robots

  - **Design Implications**
  - Balance autonomy and human control
  - Provide appropriate feedback
  - Design for graceful degradation
  - Enable explainability
  - Support situation awareness
  - Implement trust calibration mechanisms

  - **Research Challenges**
  - Modeling dynamic trust
  - Cross-cultural trust differences
  - Long-term trust evolution
  - Trust repair after failures
  - Trust in AI decision-making
  - Ethical implications of trust manipulation

  - Explainable AI (XAI)
