A spacecraft habitat is the crewed living and working volume of a spacecraft or ground analogue in which people live under conditions of isolation and confinement.[^1][^2]

No source reviewed gives a formal definition of the term, so this description follows how NASA uses it. NASA lists isolation and confinement among the hazards of human spaceflight, with behavioural responses arising among groups confined in a small space over a long period.[^2]

Ground habitats are used as analogues. NASA describes the Human Exploration Research Analog as a habitat built to serve as an analogue for isolation, confinement and remote conditions in exploration scenarios. An analogue reproduces some conditions of flight and not others, and the source does not claim equivalence.[^1]

On orbit, the International Space Station supplies pressurised volume for crew and experiments, though NASA notes that its figures change with visiting vehicles. NASA's biological and physical sciences programme treats the effects of space stressors as part of its research remit.[^3][^4]
