Cone of depression is the inverted-cone lowering of the water table or potentiometric surface that forms around a pumped well.[^1][^2]

Kansas Geological Survey and Western Oregon University glossaries both describe it as a depression, roughly conical, produced by extraction from a well, and the second places it on the potentiometric surface.[^1][^2] USGS Circular 1217 calls the area of water-level decline the cone of depression and says its size is controlled by the rate and duration of pumping, the storage characteristics of the aquifer and the ease with which water reaches the well.[^3]

Theis described water levels being drawn down in the vicinity of a pumped well, with the shape of the cone set principally by how easily water flows through the aquifer and by its coefficient of storage.[^4] In a confined aquifer, pumping lowers head and water pressure within the cone while the whole thickness stays saturated, and a USGS worked example gives the confined cone about 2,000 times the volume of its unconfined counterpart.[^5]

That comparison is a hypothetical infinite aquifer, not a measurement, and the retained sources give no field figures. They also describe the feature from well and head observations and do not establish how, or whether, it is observed from space.[^3][^5]
