As the outer boundary of the plasmasphere, the plasmapause marks the edge of the region of cold plasma around Earth.[^1][^2] Originally defined as a steep gradient in electron density that separates the relatively dense plasmasphere close to Earth from the depleted region beyond, called the plasmatrough, it is described by NASA as the outer boundary of the co-rotating ionospheric plasma region.[^2][^3]

Its position is not fixed. ESA reports from Cluster data that the plasmapause location varies with geomagnetic conditions and moves closer to Earth as activity rises.[^1] A published study also notes that a steep gradient is not always observed, for example after long refilling of the plasmasphere or when a spacecraft passes through a plasmaspheric plume, and it selects events using its own chosen density-drop threshold.[^3]

It can therefore mean a density gradient as measured by spacecraft, and the sources do not establish one universal numerical criterion. NASA also notes that space weather events which disturb the plasmapause can raise ionospheric electron content and disrupt satellite radio signals, in a visualisation of one such event.[^2]
