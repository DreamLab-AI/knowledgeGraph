A swath is the area of the surface imaged by a satellite sensor as the satellite moves along its orbit.[^1]

Natural Resources Canada says spaceborne swaths generally vary between tens and hundreds of kilometres wide. Mission pages give specific values: Sentinel-2 states an orbital swath width of 290 km, and the Landsat 9 page lists a swath width of 185 km. These are examples for named missions, not typical values.[^1][^2][^3]

Swaths of consecutive orbits cover new ground as the Earth rotates beneath the satellite. Where adjacent swaths overlap, which increases at high latitudes for near-polar orbits, areas are imaged more often.[^1]

Where the field of view per pixel is constant, ground resolution varies from the image centre to the swath edge. The sources describe this qualitatively, and swath width is a different quantity from scene size or pixel size.[^4]
