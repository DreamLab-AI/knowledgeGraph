An astronomical body is an individual object studied in astronomy, such as a planet, star or black hole.[^1][^2][^3]

Sources here use celestial body and astronomical object loosely and do not give a formal general definition. Surveys such as Gaia treat stars and other objects as things whose positions, distances, movements and changes in brightness can be charted.[^2]

Some categories have formal definitions for particular settings. NASA reports that the International Astronomical Union passed a resolution in 2006 that defined the term planet and set up the dwarf planet category for the Solar System. That definition is specific to the Solar System and is not a general rule for every kind of object, and the original resolution text was not consulted for this page.[^1]

Black holes show how the term can stretch. They neither emit nor reflect light, so they are detected through their effects on surroundings rather than seen directly, and the event horizon is a boundary rather than a material surface.[^3]
