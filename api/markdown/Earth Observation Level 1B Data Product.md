An Earth observation Level 1B data product is Level 1A data that have been processed to instrument units.[^1]

NASA adds that not all instruments have Level 1B source data. Level 1B therefore follows Level 1A where it exists, and the NASA scheme places Level 1C and the derived Level 2 variables above it.[^1]

Sentinel-2 gives a mission-specific example. Its Level-1B processing generates top-of-atmosphere calibrated radiance from the Level-1A radiance data through radiometric corrections and image compression, and the refinement of the physical geometric model is not applied at that level. Orthorectification and reflectance products come later, in the Level-1C stage.[^2]

That phrase, instrument units, has no single meaning across missions, so the units, calibration and geolocation state of a Level 1B product must be taken from its own product documentation. The sources opened here do not set a common definition beyond the NASA wording and the Sentinel-2 description.[^1][^2]

[^1]: NASA Earthdata, “Data Processing Levels”. https://www.earthdata.nasa.gov/learn/earth-observation-data-basics/data-processing-levels
[^2]: ESA Copernicus SentiWiki, “Sentinel-2 Processing”. https://sentiwiki.copernicus.eu/web/s2-processing
