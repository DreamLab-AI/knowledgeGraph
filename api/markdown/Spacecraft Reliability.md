Spacecraft reliability is the degree to which a spacecraft system ensures mission success by functioning properly over its intended life, with a low and acceptable probability of failure.[^1]

The NASA glossary ties this property to simplicity, proper design and the proper application of reliable parts and materials. A Goddard seminar describes the same idea as the ability to perform the necessary functions within expected life-cycle conditions for a required period, and separates mission reliability from design reliability. A reliability statement therefore needs a stated function, a duration and the conditions under which it applies.[^1][^2]

Failure tolerance is a related attribute rather than a synonym. The NASA glossary defines it as the ability to sustain a certain number of failures and still retain capability. One example in the NASA Science onboard-systems chapter is a fault-protection response that swaps to redundant hardware to try to re-establish command reception. That example does not establish a general redundancy rule for spacecraft.[^1][^3]

The Goddard seminar notes that reliability testing of parts does not in general establish long-term reliability. The sources retained here do not give a required reliability value, a prediction method or a mission-specific allocation, so a quoted reliability figure needs its own source and its own assumptions.[^2]
