Most atmospheric ozone sits in a zone of the stratosphere called the ozone layer, which absorbs much of the ultraviolet radiation from the Sun.[^1][^2] The Met Office says the zone holds about 90% of atmospheric ozone, and UNEP describes it as a region of high ozone concentration in the stratosphere.[^1][^3]

Ozone is produced there when oxygen molecules absorb ultraviolet light from the Sun and split into single atoms, and NASA states that stratospheric ozone absorbs most of the Sun's ultraviolet radiation.[^1][^2] UNEP specifically names UV-B as the radiation that ozone absorbs.[^3] The term does not cover ozone near the ground, which NASA notes is toxic to people and plants at high concentrations.[^2]

The Met Office dates the Montreal Protocol to 1987.[^1] The sources give differing altitudes for the layer, with the Met Office and UNEP quoting 15 to 35 km and NASA placing the stratosphere between about 10 and 50 kilometres, and NASA Ozone Watch publishes images and data for atmospheric ozone, but the retained sources do not describe satellite instruments or how the layer is mapped.[^1][^2]
