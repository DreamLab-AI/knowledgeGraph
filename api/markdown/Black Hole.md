A black hole is an extremely dense object whose gravity is so strong that nothing, not even light, can escape it.[^1][^2]

Beyond the event horizon, escape is impossible. NASA emphasises that it is a boundary containing all the black hole's matter, not a surface like Earth's. Black holes neither emit nor reflect light, so they are studied through their effects on surroundings: accretion disks, stellar orbits, gravitational waves and gravitational lensing.[^2][^1]

Black holes grow by taking in matter and by merging with other black holes, and the gas that settles into an accretion disk is the main source of light. ESA describes them as either stellar mass or supermassive, and says how the supermassive ones formed is still being investigated.[^3][^1]

Much is not established. NASA says astronomers do not know whether the singularity predicted at the centre is a physical structure or a purely mathematical one, and the sources note that the jets some black holes produce are not fully understood.[^3][^2]
