A planetary atmosphere is the envelope of gas held around a planet, whose composition and pressure differ from one planet to another.[^1][^2]

Near the surface, Earth has an atmosphere that NASA describes as mostly nitrogen and oxygen, and it affects long-term climate, short-term weather and the radiation that reaches the surface.[^1]

Venus has an atmosphere mostly of carbon dioxide with dense clouds of sulfuric acid, and NASA states that surface pressure there is 93 times that at sea level on Earth.[^2] The comparison shows how widely atmospheres vary, and the retained sources give no single formal definition of the term.

NASA places the study of planetary atmospheres within planetary science, which examines the history of the solar system and the chemistry of unfamiliar worlds.[^3] The sources used here do not cover atmospheric structure, escape or circulation in detail.

[^1]: NASA, “Facts About Earth”. https://science.nasa.gov/earth/facts/
[^2]: NASA, “Venus: Facts”. https://science.nasa.gov/venus/venus-facts/
[^3]: NASA, “Planetary Science”. https://science.nasa.gov/planetary-science/
