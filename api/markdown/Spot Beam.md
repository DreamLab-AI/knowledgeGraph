A spot beam is a relatively focused satellite antenna beam that illuminates a smaller surface region than a broader coverage beam in the same system.

“Spot” is relative to the antenna and mission: the term specifies no universal footprint size, gain, data rate or service area. The footprint depends on beam pattern, frequency, antenna aperture, pointing and the chosen contour criterion. ITU-R S.1782-1 gives illustrative multi-beam characteristics and overlap patterns for fixed-satellite broadband planning, not a current operator coverage map.[^1]

Spatially separated spot beams can support selective capacity allocation or frequency reuse when the antenna design, spectrum plan and interference constraints allow it. A spot beam by itself does not imply frequency reuse, a particular reuse factor, beam hopping, higher user throughput or an offered service. These are network-level choices that need evidence for the specific system.[^1][^3]

ESA’s Hylas-1 account describes one historical Ka-band payload with eight focused spot beams. ITU-R M.2205 gives an older illustrative mobile-satellite architecture in which spot beams may be activated dynamically. ESA’s MARIS page presents a proposed architecture distinguishing user-link beams from feeder-link beams. Together these are bounded examples; they do not establish that all spot-beam systems reconfigure capacity or use the same network architecture.[^2][^3][^4]
