X-ray astronomy is the study of astronomical sources through their X-ray emission, which comes from very hot regions of the Universe.[^1][^2]

Chandra is described by NASA as a telescope designed to detect X-ray emission from regions such as exploded stars, clusters of galaxies and matter around black holes. Hot gases in the Universe also emit X-rays, and X-ray photons carry more energy than ultraviolet photons.[^1][^2]

Because Earth's atmosphere absorbs X-rays, Chandra orbits above it, and most electromagnetic radiation from space does not reach the surface at all. X-ray observatories are therefore space missions.[^1][^2]

ESA's XMM-Newton is a second observatory that studies X-ray sources, including the centres of galaxies, stars at all stages of their lives and black holes. These mission descriptions give examples of targets and do not establish instrument performance or a complete list of X-ray source classes.[^3]
