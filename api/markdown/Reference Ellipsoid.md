A reference ellipsoid is a smooth, bi-axial surface defined to approximate the surface of the Earth.[^1]

The OGC standard describes it as a reasonable approximation of the geoid, which undulates about its surface, and calls the separation between the two the geoid undulation or geoid height.[^1] NOAA describes the geoid as the irregular surface used to define zero elevation, so heights above the ellipsoid and heights above the geoid differ.[^2]

Ordnance Survey explains that local best-fitting ellipsoids were meant for use in the region of best fit and do not fit the Earth elsewhere, yet many remain built into national mapping systems.[^3] It also treats an ellipsoid as distinct from a datum, and the OGC model attaches an ellipsoid to a geodetic reference frame.[^3][^1]

Where a reference frame has an ellipsoidal coordinate system, the OGC standard requires one associated ellipsoid to be described.[^1] The sources here do not list the ellipsoids in current use.
