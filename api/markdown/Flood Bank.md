A flood bank is a raised earthfill embankment beside a river or other water body that contains high water levels and so reduces the probability of flooding in the land behind it.[^1][^2]

Retained UK guidance describes the flood embankment as built from earth, sometimes with a clay core to reduce seepage, and gives its basic form as trapezoidal in cross section.[^1][^2] Embankments are normally set back from the river edge, which leaves some floodplain for storage and lowers the risk of undermining by bank erosion.[^1] Such raised defences let river levels rise without flooding only until the water exceeds the design height, which can happen in extreme rainfall.[^2]

Both retained sources are English flood-defence guidance and speak of flood embankments and raised defences rather than flood banks. They do not establish how the term is used outside the United Kingdom, whether it is interchangeable with a levee, or how such banks are detected or mapped from space.
