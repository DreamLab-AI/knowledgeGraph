Ground support equipment is the equipment on the ground used to build, test, handle and prepare a spacecraft and its payload before and during launch operations.[^1][^2]

NASA's small-spacecraft survey names electrical ground support equipment, abbreviated EGSE, as a category used in spacecraft and payload development and describes tools that connect to spacecraft data interfaces such as CAN bus, MIL-1553, SpaceWire and RS422. It adds that mission operations and ground support suites use software to test, monitor and command the spacecraft, and that smaller missions often share that software across operations, integration and testing.[^1]

The JPL launch chapter describes the wider setting: spacecraft are integrated and tested using command and telemetry programmes much like those used in flight, then shipped to the launch site, where further testing, propellant loading and mating to the launch vehicle follow. Mechanical handling equipment is not described in retained sources, so the page does not characterise it.[^1][^2]

Ground support equipment is distinct from the operational ground segment that tracks and commands a spacecraft in flight, although the survey notes the two can reuse software. The sources give no single formal definition of the term, and categories vary between programmes.[^1]
