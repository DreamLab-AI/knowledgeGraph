Space life sciences is the study of how conditions beyond Earth, such as radiation and altered gravity, affect living organisms and human health, together with the data that research produces.[^1][^2][^3]

NASA groups its biological and physical research under one programme that studies the effects of space stressors such as radiation and microgravity on biological and physical phenomena.[^1] Within it, space biology addresses living systems in spaceflight and in ground-based analogues.[^2]

Radiation health is a separate NASA effort. Its Space Radiation Element examines how ionising radiation from missions beyond Earth's protective atmosphere affects the human body.[^3] Boundaries between these efforts are organisational, and the sources do not give a single scientific definition of the field.

Findings from spaceflight and ground studies are shared as open datasets through NASA's repository.[^4]

[^2]: NASA, “Space Biology Program” https://science.nasa.gov/biological-physical/programs/space-biology/
