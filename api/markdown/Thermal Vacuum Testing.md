Thermal vacuum testing is a test that demonstrates the capability of a test item to operate satisfactorily in vacuum at temperatures based on those expected for the mission.[^1]

Goddard's environmental verification standard states that the test is performed in the simulated thermal and vacuum environments of space, and that the temperature cycling can also uncover latent defects in design, parts and workmanship. Thermal balance testing is described there as phases of thermal vacuum testing used to verify thermal design performance, including heater operation and radiator sizing, and to provide data for correlating thermal models.[^1]

A Goddard workshop course describes thermal vacuum cycling as one of the environmental tests done for flight projects, usually performed last in the campaign after the mechanical tests. It names thermal vacuum testing as the preferred approach compared with cycling at ambient pressure, partly because convective heat transfer paths change the temperature distribution.[^2]

These sources describe Goddard practice for Goddard projects. Cycle counts, dwell times and temperature margins vary with assembly level and programme, and other agencies or customers apply their own standards, so no figures are given here.[^1][^2]
