A space supply chain is the network of organisations that supply, build, integrate and support space systems, from component suppliers up to the prime contractor and the customer.[^1][^2]

The UK Enterprise Space Category groups the market into segments such as space and ground segment manufacturing, enabling services and engineering services.[^1] That tree is a procurement classification and does not describe how firms are linked to each other.

In ESA spacecraft development, contributions from industry across Europe are integrated by the prime contractor.[^2] A UK Government round table note says government buyers sit at the top of complex supply chains with poor visibility of structure, risks and vulnerabilities, and that the buyer has relied on primes to manage lower tiers.[^3] The note records industry concern about export controls, critical materials and bottleneck suppliers, and describes how one programme sets UK design authority and critical technology requirements. These are reported perceptions and programme mechanisms, not measured sector-wide risk.[^3]

UK Space Agency reporting counts supply chain jobs separately from direct sector employment, but the figures are tied to a stated year and are not reproduced here.[^4]
