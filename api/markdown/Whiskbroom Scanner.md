A whiskbroom scanner is an imaging instrument in which an oscillating scan mirror sweeps back and forth across the field of view, roughly perpendicular to the direction of travel, so that the motion of the platform builds the image strip by strip.[^1][^2]

On Landsat's Thematic Mapper and Enhanced Thematic Mapper Plus the mirror projects the scene through a telescope onto a small set of detectors on the focal plane, four in the Thematic Mapper and eight in the Enhanced Thematic Mapper Plus.[^1]

As the platform advances during each sweep, the Enhanced Thematic Mapper Plus carried a scan line corrector that compensated for the forward motion and returned adjacent sweeps to a parallel alignment.[^1][^2] eoPortal records that the corrector failed on 31 May 2003, after which individual scans overlapped and left gaps near the edge of each picture.[^3] The published figures for the share of missing data differ between sources, so none is given here.

These sources document the Landsat designs. They do not establish a general comparison with pushbroom sensors, or how view angle varies across a whiskbroom swath, so the page makes no claim on either.
