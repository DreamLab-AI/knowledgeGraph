A main-sequence star is a star that is fusing hydrogen to helium in its core.[^1]

Its name also refers to a band on the Hertzsprung-Russell diagram, where most stars are found when luminosity is plotted against temperature. The Sun is given as an example of a main-sequence star, and stars on the band vary widely in luminosity, colour and mass.[^1][^2]

Sources describe main-sequence stars as the large majority of stars, and as spending most of their lives in this phase. Fusion releases energy that heats the star and produces pressure that balances its gravity.[^1][^2]

When core hydrogen runs out a star leaves the main sequence, and what follows depends on its mass; the cited page outlines red giants, white dwarfs, neutron stars and black holes as possible outcomes. The main sequence is a phase of a star's life, not a separate kind of object, and the cited pages do not give a precise boundary for when it ends.[^1]
