An Earth science data record is a unified and coherent set of observations of a given parameter of the Earth system, optimised to meet specific requirements in addressing science questions.[^1]

NASA uses this definition under its MEaSUREs programme, which is concerned with generating ESDRs and making them available and useful. The programme page states that such records support assessing variability, long-term trends and change in the Earth system, and provide input and validation for models.[^1]

A closely related but separate term is the climate data record, which a National Research Council committee defined as a time series of measurements of sufficient length, consistency and continuity to determine climate variability and change. The same report divides satellite records into fundamental records, which are calibrated and quality-controlled sensor data, and thematic records, which are geophysical variables derived from them.[^2]

These two definitions come from different bodies and should not be treated as identical. Records are built from data at higher processing levels, where calibration and georeferencing information has been applied to instrument data.[^3]
