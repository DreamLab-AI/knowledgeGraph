Lunar surface environment is the space environment at the surface of the Earth's Moon.[^1]

NASA's Space Life Sciences Ontology gives this class a label and places it under the space environment class, without further definition text, so the description below rests on other sources.[^1] NASA states that gravity on the lunar surface is one-sixth of Earth's, that the atmosphere is very thin and weak, and that nearly the whole Moon is covered by regolith, a rubble pile of dust and rocky debris produced by impacts.[^2] NASA also reports a large difference between surface temperatures in full sunlight and in darkness.[^2]

Because the atmosphere does not shield the surface from the Sun's radiation or from impacts, radiation is part of the surface environment.[^2] Measurements by a lander on the lunar farside identify galactic cosmic rays, a small anomalous cosmic ray part and sporadic solar energetic particles as the charged-particle environment, plus albedo protons released from the regolith.[^3] A National Academies report adds that secondary radiation, including energetic neutrons, may contribute significantly to exposure at a planetary surface.[^4]

These sources do not give site-specific conditions such as the properties of permanently shadowed regions, dust behaviour, or the depth of regolith, and the radiation findings come from a single farside site.
