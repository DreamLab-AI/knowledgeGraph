A solar flare is a large eruption of electromagnetic radiation from the Sun lasting from minutes to hours.[^1]

NOAA states that flares usually take place in active regions, areas marked by strong magnetic fields and typically associated with sunspot groups. NASA likewise describes active regions as the source of solar eruptions.[^1][^2]

A strong enough flare produces ionisation in the lower, denser D-layer of the ionosphere. Radio waves interacting with electrons there lose energy through collisions, which NOAA associates with the absence of HF communication.[^1]

Large magnetic eruptions that produce a flare may also cause a coronal mass ejection and accelerate energetic particles. NOAA words this as often occurring together, so a flare should not be treated as identical to either of the other phenomena.[^3]

[^2]: NASA Science, “Sunspots”. https://science.nasa.gov/sun/sunspots/
