A polar coordinate system locates a point in a plane by its distance from a fixed point, the pole, and by an angle measured from a reference ray.[^1][^2]

OpenStax identifies the centre point as the pole, or origin, corresponding to a radial coordinate of zero.[^1] NASA Glenn describes the radial and angular pair as an orthogonal coordinate system, like the rectangular system, with functions for converting between the two descriptions.[^2]

Because the pole lies at zero distance, the angle does not identify it, so a convention is needed where a unique representation is wanted.[^1] These sources leave angle units and direction to the context; OpenStax measures positive angles anticlockwise.[^1]

These sources cover the plane case only. They do not establish how polar coordinates relate to latitude and longitude on an ellipsoid, where the OGC standard uses the term polar axis for the axis of rotation of the ellipsoid.[^3]
