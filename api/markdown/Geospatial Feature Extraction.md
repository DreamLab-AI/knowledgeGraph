Geospatial feature extraction is the derivation of descriptive variables or discrete objects from georeferenced Earth observation data so that they can be classified, mapped or analysed.[^1][^2]

A remote sensing review of wetland classification places feature extraction and selection as a workflow step covering spectral features such as vegetation and water indices, spatial relationship features such as texture, and temporal sequence features.[^1] It reports multi-feature fusion as a key strategy and notes that convolutional networks extract local spatial features such as texture and shape.[^1] NASA defines Level 2 products as derived geophysical variables at the same resolution and location as the Level 1 source data, which is one product-level outcome of derivation.[^2]

ESA Φ-lab says machine learning is critical to accelerating insight extraction from data while domain experts remain essential to interpret it.[^3] The retained sources do not give a general standard definition, and the review is a single wetland-focused witness. The term also covers two senses, raster variables and vector objects, that the sources do not separate.
