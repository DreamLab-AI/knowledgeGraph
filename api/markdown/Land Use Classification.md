Land use classification assigns areas to classes describing the arrangements, activities and inputs people undertake on a land cover type to produce, change or maintain it [^1].

USGS separates the two ideas by saying that land use describes human activities and management, while land cover describes a physical feature on the land [^2]. FAO adds that defining land use this way links land cover to the actions of people in their environment, and that many classification legends confuse the two [^1].

Image classification alone reaches cover more directly than use. The Natural Resources Canada tutorial classifies pixels into themes such as water, forest or crop types, and leaves the analyst to judge how spectral classes match the information classes of interest [^3]. The retained sources do not state how use is inferred from such cover classes, and they give no accuracy figures.

[^2]: USGS, “An interagency perspective on improving consistency and transparency of land use and land cover mapping”. https://pubs.usgs.gov/publication/cir1549/full
[^3]: Natural Resources Canada, “Image Classification and Analysis”. https://natural-resources.canada.ca/maps-tools-publications/satellite-elevation-air-photos/image-classification-analysis
