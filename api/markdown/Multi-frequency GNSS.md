Multi-frequency GNSS is the reception of signals from the same navigation satellites on more than one carrier frequency, so that a receiver can combine the observables and reduce or estimate frequency-dependent effects such as ionospheric delay.[^1][^2]

First-order ionospheric effects on code and carrier-phase measurements depend on the inverse square of the signal frequency. A dual-frequency receiver can form a linear combination of code or carrier measurements in which that first-order term is eliminated.[^1][^2] Navipedia presents this as removal of the first-order effect only, so higher-order ionospheric terms, multipath and receiver noise are not covered by the combination.[^1]

Other combinations of the same observables serve different purposes. A geometry-free combination leaves the frequency-dependent effects and can be used to estimate ionospheric electron content or detect cycle slips, and a wide-lane combination creates a signal of long effective wavelength that helps with cycle-slip detection and ambiguity fixing.[^1]

On the signal side, the GPS programme describes new civil signals at L2C, L5 and L1C being fielded alongside the legacy L1 C/A signal, and states that a dual-frequency receiver using L1 C/A with L2C can apply ionospheric correction. It also states that users must upgrade their equipment to benefit.[^3] The sources do not give current satellite counts or a general definition of the term, and the examples are dual-frequency; the same reasoning is not shown here for three or more frequencies.[^1][^3]
