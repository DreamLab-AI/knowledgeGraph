Off-nadir angle describes how far a sensor's viewing direction departs from nadir, the point on the Earth's surface directly below the satellite.[^1][^2]

The Canadian tutorial defines the nadir point in its account of orbit cycles and refers to pixels away from nadir as off-nadir. It does not define the angle formally or fix a reference for measuring it, so the sign convention and the vertical reference remain unresolved here.[^1][^2]

For a scanner whose instantaneous field of view stays constant, the tutorial states that pixels at nadir represent a smaller ground area than those off-nadir, so spatial resolution varies from the image centre to the swath edge.[^2] The statement applies to that scanning geometry and does not describe every sensor or pointing mode.

ESA notes that Sentinel-2 areas are revisited every five days under the same viewing conditions in the nominal configuration, and that overlap between swaths of adjacent orbits can produce revisits under different viewing conditions.[^3] The page does not call this off-nadir pointing.
