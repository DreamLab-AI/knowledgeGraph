A prime meridian is the meridian of a reference ellipsoid that is chosen to carry zero longitude, from which the longitude of other points is measured.[^1]

Ordnance Survey states that the longitude of a point on the ellipsoid is the angle between its meridian and the prime meridian, and that the X axis of a geocentric Cartesian system passes through the equator at the prime meridian.[^1]

Several zero-longitude meridians exist. Ordnance Survey notes that they generally pass somewhere near Greenwich, and the OGC model records the prime meridian as part of a geodetic datum description, taking a default when none is given.[^1][^2] Greenwich is therefore the usual choice, not a universal one.

These sources do not describe how a given realisation of the meridian is fixed or maintained in practice.
