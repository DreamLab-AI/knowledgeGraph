- ### Definition
  - The Frontier Model Forum is an industry body founded in 2023 by leading AI development organisations to advance AI safety research, share knowledge on safety best practices, and promote responsible development and deployment of frontier AI models. It provides a venue for cross-company collaboration on technical safety, evaluation methodologies, and engagement with governments and civil society. The Forum focuses specifically on the most capable general-purpose AI models and the risks that may arise from their development and deployment.

- ### Content
  - The Frontier Model Forum is a cross-industry initiative formed to address the safety challenges posed by the most capable AI systems, commonly referred to as frontier models. It was established in 2023 with founding members including major AI laboratories and technology companies, and operates as a collaborative forum rather than a standards body or regulatory authority.
  - The Forum's primary workstreams involve advancing technical AI safety research — particularly around evaluation methodologies for identifying dangerous capabilities, red-teaming practices, and interpretability — and facilitating information sharing on safety-relevant model behaviours and mitigation techniques. A stated goal is to develop a shared library of safety evaluations that can be applied consistently across frontier models to assess potential for harm in areas such as biological, chemical, and cyber risks.
  - The Forum also engages with governments and international bodies to inform AI policy and regulation, representing an industry perspective on how regulatory frameworks should be designed to support responsible AI development without unnecessarily constraining beneficial applications. It operates alongside other governance initiatives including national AI Safety Institutes (in the UK, US, and other jurisdictions), the OECD AI Policy Observatory, and the Global Partnership on AI. The Forum's work on AI Alignment, safety benchmarking, and Red Teaming methodologies contributes to the broader ecosystem of responsible AI practices.
