A stellar active region is a region of a star's surface and overlying atmosphere with intense, complex magnetic fields, of which sunspots are the visible component in the case of the Sun.[^1][^2]

On the Sun, active regions are the sites from which solar flares and coronal mass ejections typically burst.[^1] The magnetic activity behind them is described as a feature of stars with convective outer layers, sustained by a dynamo driven by differential rotation and convective flows.[^2][^3]

Stars other than the Sun cannot be resolved in the same way. Reviews state that their magnetic features can only be observed indirectly, apart from a few interferometric cases, so spot coverage and distribution are inferred from disc-integrated diagnostics such as brightness variations.[^2] For cool stars, broad-band variability is attributed mainly to spots in active regions and to faculae.[^2] The reviews describe starspots and stellar active regions in terms borrowed from solar observation, and the detailed structure of stellar active regions is not directly measured.

The SWEET ontology places an active-region class under its stellar realm class but gives no definition, so it supports the classification and nothing about physical content.[^4]
