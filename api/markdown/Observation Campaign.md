An observation campaign is a coordinated effort in which a set of sensors is deployed to make observations for a shared purpose.[^1][^2]

The W3C sensor vocabulary mentions a network of sensors deployed for an observation campaign as an example of a deployment, and it does not define the term itself. NASA describes its Arctic-Boreal Vulnerability Experiment as a field campaign that linked field-based studies with geospatial data products derived from airborne and satellite sensors.[^1][^2] Neither source sets out how long a campaign runs or how its instruments are chosen.

Satellite data collected in a campaign pass through the same stages as other remote sensing data: an energy source, passage through the atmosphere, interaction with the target, recording by a sensor, transmission and processing, interpretation, and application.[^3] The tutorial presents these stages as a general framework, not as a campaign protocol.

Such usage extends to operational phases of satellite missions. ESA describes a Sentinel-2A extension campaign as a tailored operations scenario that improves revisit frequency for some areas.[^4] That sense concerns orbit operations and should not be read as a field campaign.
