Spacecraft environmental testing is ground testing of space hardware against the environments it is expected to meet, to demonstrate performance and workmanship before launch.[^1][^2]

The ECSS testing standard covers verification by testing of space segment elements and equipment on the ground before launch. It applies to qualification models, flight models tested at acceptance level and protoflight models, and it includes requirements for environmental tests and for test margins and durations. It does not cover in-orbit testing or testing of space segment subsystems.[^1]

NASA's GEVS standard gives Goddard programmes a baseline for demonstrating, by test and analysis, that payloads, subsystems and components perform in the expected mission environments. It is not a NASA mandatory standard.[^2]

Testing is one route within wider verification. The ECSS verification standard applies from single equipment to the overall system and leaves detailed testing requirements to the testing standard. Photographs of the MESSENGER spacecraft being prepared for a vibration test show the practice, and NASA notes that rideshare interfaces must keep spacecraft within dynamic and environmental constraints. The sources read here do not state test levels or durations.[^3][^4][^5]
