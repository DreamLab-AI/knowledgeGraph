A variable star is a star whose observed brightness changes, and the General Catalogue of Variable Stars sorts such stars into types by the astrophysical reason for the change.[^1]

In its fourth edition, the catalogue groups types as eruptive, pulsating, rotating, cataclysmic, eclipsing binary and intense variable X-ray sources. Eruptive variables vary because of violent processes and flares in their chromospheres and coronae, while pulsating variables show periodic expansion and contraction of their surface layers. Cepheids are listed as one radially pulsating class.[^1]

This grouping is one catalogue's scheme, and the cited text is a classification description rather than a physical model of each class. Period and amplitude ranges belong to individual classes and should not be generalised to all variable stars.[^1]

NASA describes CE Camelopardalis as a variable supergiant star. The image caption labels the star but does not define variability or assign a catalogue type, and its remarks about the star's eventual fate are the caption's own statements.[^2]
