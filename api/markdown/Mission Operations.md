Mission operations are the activities that run a spacecraft and its payload during flight, including commanding, monitoring and data handling by a ground team.[^1][^2]

NASA assigns the conduct of the prime mission and the support needed to maintain it to Phase E of its life cycle. In small-satellite practice, the operations team receives tasking requests, builds command plans, simulates the satellite response, and manages downloads and contact requests with ground stations.[^1][^2]

Distance limits what a ground team can do. NASA Basics notes that light time prevents immediate response to anomalies on a robotic spacecraft, so fault protection runs onboard. Teams also prepare in advance: ESA describes simulation campaigns that practise nominal and contingency scenarios including spacecraft anomalies and ground segment failures.[^3][^4]
