Water table is the upper surface of the saturated zone.[^1][^2] The USGS glossary words it as the top water surface of an unconfined aquifer at atmospheric pressure, and the British Geological Survey as the surface of a body of unconfined groundwater at which the pressure equals that of the atmosphere.[^3][^2]

The British Geological Survey adds that the water table is a particular potentiometric surface.[^2] The USGS notes that its configuration indicates the approximate direction of ground-water flow at any location on it.[^1]

It separates the unsaturated zone above from the saturated zone below, and the USGS glossary places the capillary fringe above it.[^3][^1] SWEET classes it as a hydrosphere feature.[^4]

Retained sources define the surface itself and do not establish a remote-sensing retrieval or an accuracy figure.
