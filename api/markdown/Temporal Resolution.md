Temporal resolution is the ability of a remote sensing system to collect imagery of the same area of the Earth's surface at different times, and it is tied to the revisit period of the sensor.[^1]

Natural Resources Canada equates absolute temporal resolution with the revisit period for the same area at the same viewing angle. The actual value depends on satellite and sensor capability, swath overlap and latitude, and pointable sensors can view an area between passes.[^1][^2]

Mission pages give mission-specific figures. Sentinel-2 documentation states a revisit frequency of 5 days at the Equator for its two-satellite design, and the Landsat 9 page lists 16 days. These figures describe named missions and configurations as retrieved.[^3][^4]

Frequent coverage matters where clouds persist, where short-lived events need imaging and where multi-temporal comparison is wanted. Temporal resolution is separate from spatial, spectral and radiometric resolution.[^1]
