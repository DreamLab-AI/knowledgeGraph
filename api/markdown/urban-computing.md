- ### Definition
  - [[Urban Computing]] integrates and analyses city-scale data from [[Sensor Network]] and [[Internet of Things]] infrastructure using [[Data Analytics]] and [[Geospatial Data]] to improve [[Traffic Management]] and energy use, closely linked to [[Smart Cities]].

- ### Overview
  - Urban computing treats the city as a vast, instrumented system whose flows of people, vehicles, energy, and information can be sensed, modelled, and optimised.
  - It fuses heterogeneous data — GPS traces, mobile signalling, environmental sensors, social media, and municipal records — to reveal patterns invisible to any single source.
  - The discipline spans the full pipeline: sensing infrastructure, data integration and storage, analytical and machine-learning models, and actuation through services and policy.

- ### Key aspects
  - Sensing: pervasive IoT and sensor networks capture continuous, spatially distributed urban data.
  - Integration: fusing modalities with differing resolution, latency, and reliability into coherent geospatial datasets.
  - Analytics: spatial-temporal mining and machine learning model mobility, demand, and environmental dynamics.
  - Actuation: insights feed traffic control, energy management, and public-service optimisation.

- ### Applications
  - Adaptive traffic signal control and congestion forecasting.
  - Air-quality and noise monitoring for public health.
  - Demand-responsive transport and urban energy optimisation.
