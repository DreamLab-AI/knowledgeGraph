The Normalised Burn Ratio is a normalised difference of near-infrared and shortwave-infrared reflectance designed to highlight burnt areas in large fire zones.[^1][^2]

UN-SPIDER explains that healthy vegetation reflects strongly in the near-infrared and weakly in the shortwave infrared, whereas recently burnt areas show the opposite, so a high value indicates healthy vegetation and a low value indicates bare ground or recently burnt land.[^1] Sentinel Hub implements the index for Sentinel-2 with bands 8 and 12 and says it highlights burnt areas in large fire zones greater than 500 acres.[^2]

Burn severity is estimated from the difference between pre-fire and post-fire values, written dNBR, and a higher dNBR indicates more severe damage.[^1][^2] UN-SPIDER cautions that dNBR values vary from case to case and that interpretation should, where possible, be supported by field assessment.[^1]

UN-SPIDER and Sentinel Hub describe the ratio as an index used to highlight burnt areas rather than as a complete burned-area product, and the retained text does not give its accuracy. The USGS severity class boundaries cited by UN-SPIDER are held in an image table that is not part of the opened text, so none are stated here.
