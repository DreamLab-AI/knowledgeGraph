An Earth observation spectrometer is an instrument that records the spectrum of light reflected or emitted from the Earth, resolving it into many wavelength bands.[^1][^2][^3]

NASA's AVIRIS delivers calibrated images of upwelling spectral radiance in 224 contiguous spectral channels covering 400 to 2500 nanometres, and has flown on four aircraft platforms.[^2] EMIT is a NASA imaging spectrometer mounted on the exterior of the International Space Station, built to measure the wavelengths of light from minerals on dust-source surfaces and so determine composition.[^3]

Remote sensing generally frames a sensor as a device that records reflected or emitted energy, which is the part of the process a spectrometer performs.[^1] The retained sources describe imaging spectrometers specifically. They do not describe non-imaging spectrometers, atmospheric trace-gas spectrometers or instrument design, so this page does not cover them.
