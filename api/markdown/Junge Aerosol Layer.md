The Junge aerosol layer is the stratospheric aerosol layer, a distinct layer of primarily aqueous sulfuric acid aerosol first observed by Christian Junge and coworkers.[^1]

Kremser and colleagues report that this aerosol occurs between 15 and 25 km altitude with a peak near 20 km, and that the first observation was made in a period not associated with volcanic activity.[^1] They note that the layer is often referred to as the Junge layer.[^1]

SWEET types the class as an atmosphere layer whose substance is aerosol.[^2] The retained sources do not establish how the layer responds to volcanic eruptions, and the page relies on one review for its physical description, so it should be read as a bounded definition and not as a complete account of the layer.

[^1]: Reviews of Geophysics (Kremser et al. 2016, White Rose repository), “Stratospheric aerosol: Observations, processes, and impact on climate”. https://eprints.whiterose.ac.uk/97280/7/Kremser_et_al-2016-Reviews_of_Geophysics.pdf
