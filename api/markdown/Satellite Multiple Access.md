
Satellite multiple access is the set of techniques and procedures by which multiple transmitters or users share satellite communications resources.

A shared resource may be separated by frequency, time or code, and systems can combine these methods. The ITU-R handbook describes FDMA, TDMA and CDMA as foundational access families and notes that hybrid combinations are possible. The handbook dates from 2002, so it supports terminology rather than a survey of current deployments.[^1]

Access resources may be pre-assigned or allocated dynamically through demand-assignment or scheduling procedures. Such channel assignment is a network or operator decision; it is distinct from an ITU frequency allocation and from a national administration’s authorisation for a radio station to use a frequency.[^1][^3][^4]

Non-orthogonal multiple access (NOMA) allows users’ signals to share resources through a different separation approach from conventional orthogonal access. ESA’s NOTES project reported a completed power-domain NOMA return-link demonstration at TRL 5 in February 2026 and assessed possible fit with existing frameworks. That status records a demonstrator, not an adopted standard or routine commercial deployment.[^2]

Regenerative payloads may support onboard processing and resource management; ESA’s D2SAT page described an ongoing demonstrator in July 2026. Actual scheduler placement and access policy depend on system architecture. Multiple access remains distinct from modulation, coding and spectrum permission.[^2][^5]
