Ice sheet monitoring is the repeated satellite observation of the Greenland and Antarctic ice sheets to track changes in their mass, extent and surface elevation.[^1][^2]

An ice sheet is described as a mass of glacial land ice larger than a stated area threshold, and Earth has two of them. Net gain or loss of an ice sheet's mass is called mass balance.[^1] Gravity measurements from the GRACE and GRACE Follow-On missions have been used to show that both ice sheets have been losing mass, with a break in the record between the two missions.[^2]

Altimetry is a second approach. ESA describes CryoSat's radar altimeter as designed to measure changes at the margins of large ice sheets and of floating ice in polar oceans, and NASA lists ICESat-2 as a laser altimeter that measures ice sheet elevation.[^3][^2] The sources do not say how an elevation change is converted to mass change, so the two measurement types should not be treated as interchangeable.

These sources are explanatory pages without an edition or a stated uncertainty, so they support the description of methods and the direction of change reported there rather than a specific loss rate.[^1][^2][^3]
