Earth observation data assimilation is the use of observations together with short-range model forecasts to determine a best possible estimate of the state of a system and its uncertainties.[^1]

ECMWF describes the procedure as typically sequential: a previous model forecast is compared with newly received observations, the model state is updated, and a new forecast is started. The update step is called the analysis and the short forecast used to produce it is called the background.[^1] That description is given for atmospheric forecasting, and the sources reviewed do not describe ocean, land or other assimilation systems.[^1]

NASA lists model output and results from analyses of lower-level data as its highest processing level.[^2] The NASA page does not name assimilation for that level, so the link between assimilation products and that level is an inference and not a statement of the source.[^2]

ESA describes a Digital Twin Earth as a digital replica of the planet that is constantly fed with Earth observation data, combined with in situ measurements and artificial intelligence.[^3] The article reports ideas and precursor activities and does not establish that assimilation is the mechanism of any particular twin, so the connection should be read as a shared use of observations and models.[^3] Assimilation is a method of combining data with a model and is not the same as the observations it uses or the forecast it starts.
