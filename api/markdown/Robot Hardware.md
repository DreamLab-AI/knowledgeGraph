  # RobotHardware
  Robot Hardware encompasses the physical realization of robotic systems including mechanical structures, actuators, sensors, power systems, embedded computers, and communication networks that constitute the material substrate enabling robot operation. This domain addresses electromechanical design, component integration, and physical implementation considerations.
  Hardware systems integrate diverse technologies: mechanical engineering for structural design and kinematics, electrical engineering for power distribution and motor control, electronics for sensing and computation, and thermal management for reliable operation. Modern robot hardware balances performance, reliability, cost, weight, and power consumption through careful component selection and integrated design.
  The category includes robot manipulators with serial or parallel kinematics, mobile bases providing locomotion, gripper mechanisms enabling manipulation, actuator modules driving motion, sensor suites providing perception, onboard computers executing control and planning algorithms, and power systems supplying energy. Industrial-grade hardware prioritizes reliability and repeatability, while research platforms emphasize modularity and reconfigurability.
  Contemporary hardware development leverages advanced manufacturing including additive manufacturing for complex geometries, integrated actuator-sensor modules reducing wiring complexity, high-power-density motors enabling compact designs, and energy-efficient electronics extending operational duration. Recent trends include soft robotics using compliant materials for safe interaction, wearable robotics assisting human activities, bio-hybrid systems incorporating living tissues, and neuromorphic hardware implementing brain-inspired computation. Standardization efforts including ROS 2 Control and Orocos enable hardware abstraction, while digital twins facilitate virtual commissioning before physical deployment.
