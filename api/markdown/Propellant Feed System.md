A propellant feed system is the part of a liquid propulsion system that delivers propellants from the tanks to the thrust chamber at the required flow rate and pressure, and in a liquid rocket engine it comprises the tanks, feed lines, valves and pressurisation devices.[^1]

A NASA Marshall chapter classes feed systems as pressure-fed or pump-fed. A pressure-fed system relies on tank pressure and is described as simple, typically used for space and auxiliary propulsion that needs low system pressures and small quantities of propellant. A pump-fed system uses a turbopump and is used for high-pressure, high-performance applications.[^1] The choice follows trade studies at engine and vehicle level.[^1]

In a pressure-fed layout, gas held in a pressurant tank is supplied to the propellant tanks at a regulated pressure through valves, with ducting carrying the propellant to one or more thrust chambers.[^1] The chapter notes that pressure-fed systems are limited to relatively low chamber pressures because high pressures make the tanks too heavy, and that a pump-fed system still needs tank pressurisation to prevent pump cavitation.[^1]

Smaller thrusters have their own variants. NASA's small-spacecraft survey counts feed systems among the elements of a complete propulsion system and notes that electrospray thrusters can be fed by pressurant gas or by capillary forces, and that propellant management devices can be needed to deliver propellant in low gravity.[^2] The sources do not give a general rule for choosing between feed types beyond the trade described.[^1][^2]
