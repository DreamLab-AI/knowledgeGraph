Ice Base is the lower boundary of an ice mass, where grounded ice meets the bed or floating ice meets the water beneath it.[^1][^2]

Radar sounding work uses the phrase for a surface to be mapped: the PASIN2 airborne radar is described as enabling polarimetric 3D estimation of the ice base in a single pass.[^1] SWEET lists ice base only as a class under planetary boundary, with no definition.[^3]

For floating ice, the Bedmap3 authors refer to the free-floating ice shelf base and exclude radar thickness data that coincided with mapped basal marine ice, because marine ice is opaque to radar and biases the thicknesses low.[^2] The base of a shelf is therefore not always the surface that radar detects.[^2]

None of the retained sources gives a formal definition of the term, so the scope above is inferred from usage. They do not establish a vertical accuracy for the ice base.[^1][^2][^3]

[^2]: Scientific Data, “Bedmap3 updated ice bed, surface and thickness gridded datasets for Antarctica”. https://www.nature.com/articles/s41597-025-04672-y
[^3]: ESIP SWEET, “realmCryo.ttl”. https://raw.githubusercontent.com/ESIPFed/sweet/master/src/realmCryo.ttl
