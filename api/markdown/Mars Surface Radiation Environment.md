Mars surface radiation environment is the natural radiation on or adjacent to the surface of Mars, shaped by free space radiation that the planet partly shields and the atmosphere modulates, albedo radiation from the crust and decay of radioactive elements in the crust.[^1]

It is a subclass of both the Mars radiation environment and the general surface radiation environment in the ontology. The ontology entry contains a wording slip that names the lunar surface inside the Mars definition, so only the Mars sense is used here.[^1]

Curiosity's RAD instrument measured the dose rate at the surface during the Artemis-I period, and the model results in the comparison paper were within 7% to 12% of that measurement. The paper notes that the rover is powered by a radioisotope thermoelectric generator, which adds a near-constant contribution to one detector.[^2]

NASA describes a thin carbon dioxide atmosphere, which is the shielding the calculations represent. The sources establish a model-to-measurement comparison for one mission period, not a surface dose for every site or solar condition.[^2][^3]
