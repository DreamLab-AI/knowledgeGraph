
Effective isotropic radiated power (EIRP) is the power supplied to an antenna multiplied by its gain relative to an isotropic antenna in a specified direction.

For a single carrier, a common dB calculation is EIRP in dBW = transmitter power at the antenna input in dBW + antenna gain in dBi, with feeder losses subtracted when they lie between the transmitter reference plane and antenna input. ITU's definition is directional: gain towards the receiver, not simply maximum antenna gain, belongs in the calculation.[^1]

A NASA study provides an illustrative 32 GHz deep-space budget: 25 W is stated as 14 dBW, the feed loss is 1 dB, and antenna gain is 71 dBi, giving an EIRP of 84 dBW. Those values describe that historical design case, not an operational spacecraft's current output.[^2]

EIRP describes the transmitted signal at a defined antenna reference and direction. It is not received carrier power: path loss, other propagation and equipment losses, receiving gain and the receiving reference plane determine the received level. EIRP also does not establish link closure, achieved data rate or permission to transmit; those require separate engineering and regulatory assessments.[^1][^3]
