Sea surface temperature retrieval is the estimation of the temperature of the sea surface from the measurements of a satellite radiometer, as done by the Sentinel-3 Sea and Land Surface Temperature Radiometer.[^1][^2]

ESA and EUMETSAT state that the principal objective of SLSTR products is to provide global and regional sea and land surface temperature to a very high level of accuracy.[^1] The instrument design rests on the heritage of the Along-Track Scanning Radiometers flown on ERS-1/2 and Envisat, so its record continues earlier sea surface temperature series.[^1] Aerosol retrieval and fire radiative power information are offered as additional products, which shows that one instrument serves several applications.[^1]

NASA presents sea surface temperature data as essential measurements for studying aquatic habitats and the ocean's role in weather and climate processes.[^2] Its catalogue includes gridded analyses such as the UK Met Office OSTIA foundation product, which is derived from multiple satellite and climatology observations.[^2] A blended analysis of that kind is a further processing step on top of a single-instrument retrieval and should not be read as one.[^2]

These retained sources do not describe the retrieval algorithm, the atmospheric correction or any measured validation result. The accuracy language on the SLSTR page is a design objective and not a statement about any particular scene.[^1]
