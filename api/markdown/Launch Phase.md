Launch phase is the first of the mission operations phases in the JPL account of interplanetary missions, covering preparation at the launch site and powered flight until the spacecraft separates from the launch vehicle.[^1]

JPL lists four phases of interplanetary mission operations: launch, cruise, encounter and, depending on spacecraft health and funding, extended operations. It describes these as subcategories of a flight project's operations and data analysis phase. Other agencies and programmes name and divide phases differently, so this is one convention.[^1]

Powered flight is described as ending when the last stage burns out and the spacecraft separates and continues in freefall. Before that, at the launch site, additional testing takes place, propellants are loaded, pyrotechnic devices are armed and the spacecraft is mated to its upper stage and then to the launch vehicle. After launch, tracking passes from the launch-site station to the Deep Space Network and the cruise phase is set to begin.[^1]

This educational text cites launch data from 2013, so vehicle descriptions should not be read as current capability. Formal entry or exit criteria for the phase are not set out.[^1]

[^1]: NASA Science, *Basics of Space Flight*, chapter 14. https://science.nasa.gov/learn/basics-of-space-flight/chapter14-1/
