A planetary nebula is an expanding cloud of dust and gas formed when a dying star blows away its outer layers.[^1][^2]

NASA describes this as a late stage for stars like the Sun, after fusion in the core has used up its fuel and the star has expanded. The outer material is expelled, and what remains of the core becomes a white dwarf.[^1][^3]

Despite the name, it does not refer to planets. The retained sources describe only the physical process of shell ejection and do not explain the origin of the term, so no claim is made here about its history.[^1][^2]

Durations, ejected masses and nebula shapes are not given in these sources. They describe the sequence in outline for low-mass stars, so the page does not extend the account to more massive stars, whose endings the sources treat differently.[^2][^3]
