An embankment is a raised bank of earth or similar material, built to carry or hold back something such as water.[^1][^2]

For this corpus the term is bounded to water control. The National Weather Service describes a levee as a long, narrow embankment usually built to protect land from flooding, and notes that levees and floodwalls confine streamflow within a specified area.[^1] The USDA NRCS standard defines a dike as an embankment constructed of earth or other suitable materials to protect land against overflow or to regulate water, which makes embankment the broader structure and dike one of its uses.[^2]

SWEET asserts embankment as equivalent to dike, so the ontology treats the two as the same class.[^3] Retained sources use embankment as a generic structural term inside their definitions of dike and levee, and none defines embankment on its own account.[^1][^2][^3]

Retained sources do not cover road, railway or dam embankments, nor how embankments are observed or surveyed by remote sensing.[^1][^2]
