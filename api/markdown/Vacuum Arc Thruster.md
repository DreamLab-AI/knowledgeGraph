A vacuum arc thruster is a pulsed plasma thruster in which one electrode is sacrificial and supplies the propellant.[^1][^2]

In the survey description, acceleration is predominantly electromagnetic, which gives a characteristically high specific impulse and low thrust, and one variant adds a downstream electrostatic grid. In the NASA micro-cathode arc thruster, vacuum arc discharges ablate the cathode material and form cathode spots that release plasma, and the titanium cathode of the prototype is also the propellant.[^1][^2]

These devices are pulsed and discharge energy stored in capacitors on each pulse, so the average power drawn from the spacecraft bus depends on the stored energy and the pulse frequency. The survey describes them as simple and compact, with a total impulse small compared with other forms of electric propulsion.[^1]

Metal propellant ejected by the thruster can accumulate and cause unintended shorting between electrodes, so shielding or other physical features are typically needed. A specific impulse figure from one prototype paper should not be applied to every vacuum arc thruster, and the sources do not establish a flight reliability rate.[^1][^2]
