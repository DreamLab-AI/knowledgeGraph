A neutron star is the dense remnant left when the core of a massive star collapses and the core is stopped by neutrons.[^1]

NASA describes the collapse as crushing protons and electrons together into neutrons. If the collapsing core is between about 1 and 3 solar masses, the neutrons can halt the collapse and a neutron star remains, while more massive cores continue to collapse into stellar-mass black holes. The range is approximate.[^1]

A neutron star is described as about 20 kilometres across, with a mass comparable to the Sun. Many are likely undetectable because they emit too little radiation. Those that are seen are often pulsars or magnetars, or members of binary systems that accrete material from a companion.[^1][^2]

Neutron star mergers also produce gravitational waves. GW170817 was identified as a binary neutron star inspiral, and its association with a gamma-ray burst was reported as the first direct evidence linking such mergers to short gamma-ray bursts. This rests on one event.[^3]
