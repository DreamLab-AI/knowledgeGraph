A raised bank is an embankment built up above the surrounding ground to hold back or regulate water, which SWEET treats as equivalent to a dike.[^1][^2]

SWEET declares raised bank equivalent to dyke, embankment, flood bank and levee, all under dike.[^1] The USDA NRCS standard defines a dike as an embankment constructed of earth or other suitable materials to protect land against overflow or to regulate water.[^2] The National Weather Service describes a levee as a long, narrow embankment usually built to protect land from flooding.[^3]

English flood-defence guidance describes a flood embankment as built from earth, possibly with a clay core to reduce seepage, and trapezoidal in cross section.[^4]

No retained source defines raised bank on its own account, so the term adds a label rather than a distinct structure.[^1][^4] The retained sources do not establish heights, construction standards or any satellite method for detecting such banks.[^2][^3]
