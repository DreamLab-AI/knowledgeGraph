A telescope is an instrument that gathers and focuses light so that faraway objects can be seen.[^1]

NASA Space Place says early telescopes used lenses, while most telescopes today use curved mirrors, and that larger optics gather more light. The page is an educational overview and does not cover every telescope type or wavelength regime.[^1]

The European Southern Observatory describes telescopes at its sites for observations in visible and infrared light, among them the Very Large Telescope at Cerro Paranal. This shows ground-based practice at one operator rather than a general account.[^2]

The SWEET ontology classes a telescope as a kind of optical instrument. That is a classification, and it does not define how a given telescope is built or used for Earth observation.[^3]

[^1]: NASA Space Place, “How Do Telescopes Work?”. https://spaceplace.nasa.gov/telescopes/en/
[^2]: European Southern Observatory, “Telescopes and Instruments”. https://www.eso.org/public/teles-instr/
[^3]: ESIP Federation, SWEET ontology instrument module, *matrInstrument.ttl*. https://raw.githubusercontent.com/ESIPFed/sweet/master/src/matrInstrument.ttl
