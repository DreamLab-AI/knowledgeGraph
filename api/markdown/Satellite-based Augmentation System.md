A satellite-based augmentation system, or SBAS, is a regional overlay that improves an underlying GNSS. Reference stations at surveyed locations observe satellite, clock and propagation errors; processing centres calculate corrections and integrity information; geostationary satellites then broadcast the messages to compatible receivers.[^1]

## Correction and integrity

Corrections improve the user's estimate of ranging error. Integrity adds a bound on residual position error and a timely warning when the service must not be used for its intended operation. This distinction matters in safety-critical navigation: better average accuracy does not show that a solution is safe at a particular moment.

Europe's EGNOS Safety of Life service provides accuracy, integrity and continuity information for authorised aviation and maritime uses. Its signal-in-space was designed to comply with ICAO SBAS standards, while operational use still depends on service authorisation, compatible equipment and published procedures.[^2]

An SBAS augments GNSS rather than replacing it. It also differs from a ground-only real-time kinematic network and from precise point positioning, although emerging services can combine regional integrity messages with precise orbit and clock corrections.

## UK status

UK access to EGNOS Safety of Life for localiser performance with vertical guidance approach minima ended on 25 June 2021. The Civil Aviation Authority recorded that the signal remained receivable and could be used in other flight phases when adequate integrity was indicated, while the affected UK approach minima were withdrawn.[^3]

The UKSBAS test bed demonstrated an ICAO-compliant L1 signal through an Inmarsat transponder, with uplink from Goonhilly and augmentation processing in Nottingham. Later work examines architecture, regulation and trials across aviation, maritime, rail, road and timing, with participants including Viasat UK, Ordnance Survey, CGI UK, Goonhilly and GMV NSL.[^4] This is verified test-bed and development activity, not evidence of a certified, continuously operated national SBAS.

Ordnance Survey's OS Net is a complementary network of ground reference stations.[^5] It can support centimetre-level positioning services, but it is not itself a satellite-based augmentation system.
