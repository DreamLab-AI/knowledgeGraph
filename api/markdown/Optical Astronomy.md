Optical astronomy is the area of astronomy that observes and analyses light from the Universe within the wavelength range to which the human eye is sensitive, also called visible light.[^1]

The IAU Office of Astronomy for Education notes that observations in that part of the electromagnetic spectrum are grouped under a single term. Neither glossary entry fixes the wavelength limits, so the boundary with the near-infrared and near-ultraviolet is left open here.[^2][^1]

Visible-light work is done from space as well as from the ground. NASA's TESS surveys the sky in visible light to discover planets orbiting nearby stars, and NASA lists optical observations alongside infrared, X-ray and other bands in its astrophysics programme.[^3]

Optical astronomy is one wavelength window among several. The sources opened here do not describe instruments or observing methods in detail.
