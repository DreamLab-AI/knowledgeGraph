A planet is, in the International Astronomical Union definition reported by NASA, a celestial body in orbit around the Sun that is massive enough to have a nearly round shape and has cleared the neighbourhood around its orbit.[^1]

The 2006 resolution that set this definition also created a separate dwarf-planet category, and NASA reports that the change reduced the Solar System count to eight planets, with five officially recognised dwarf planets. Pluto, previously counted as the ninth planet, moved to the dwarf category.[^1][^2]

This resolution is specific to bodies orbiting the Sun. NASA uses the separate term exoplanet for worlds orbiting other stars and describes them as varying widely in size and orbit, including some that wander without a star. The sources retained here do not give a formal IAU definition for exoplanets.[^1][^3]

Coming from a Greek word for wanderer, planet's modern scope is presented by NASA as a product of later discoveries, such as icy bodies beyond Neptune. The definition is therefore a convention that has changed, and the criterion of having cleared an orbit is the one that separates planets from dwarf planets.[^1][^2]
