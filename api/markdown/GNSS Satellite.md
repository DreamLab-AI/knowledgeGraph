A GNSS satellite is a spacecraft in the space segment of a satellite-navigation system. It broadcasts precisely timed, coded radio signals and navigation data that allow a receiver to estimate signal travel time and the satellite's position. Several satellites, a ground control segment and a user receiver are required for a complete navigation solution.[^1]

## Navigation payload

Navigation payloads contain stable clocks, signal-generation electronics, radio-frequency amplifiers and antennas. Galileo satellites use passive hydrogen masers as master clocks and rubidium atomic clocks as an independent source.[^2] Other systems use different clock and payload designs.

Ranging codes allow a receiver to distinguish satellites sharing a carrier band and measure delay. The navigation message carries orbit and clock data. Ground stations track the constellation, estimate orbit and clock errors, synchronise system time and upload corrections.[^3] Clock performance is central because a timing error becomes a ranging error when multiplied by the speed of light.

A supporting satellite bus supplies power, thermal control, attitude control, propulsion, command and data handling. Orbit maintenance and accurate antenna pointing support predictable signal geometry. Telemetry, tracking and command links remain separate from the user-facing navigation signal.

## Constellation context

Galileo's baseline design uses 24 nominal medium-Earth-orbit satellites in three orbital planes with control centres, sensor stations and uplink stations.[^1] This is an architecture, not a live count of healthy spacecraft. Operational status changes and should be taken from a dated constellation-status service.

## UK capability

Surrey Satellite Technology Ltd reports manufacturing and delivering 34 Galileo deployment-phase navigation payloads in Guildford between 2010 and 2020.[^4] The payloads combined clocks, signal generators, amplifiers, antennas, uplink equipment, search-and-rescue relay hardware and laser retroreflectors. This is UK industrial heritage within a European system, rather than UK ownership of Galileo.

ESA's European Centre for Space Applications and Telecommunications at Harwell hosts navigation and resilience teams, providing a UK base for European research and industrial collaboration.[^5]
