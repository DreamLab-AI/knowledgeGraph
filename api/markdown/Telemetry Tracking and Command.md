
Telemetry tracking and command (TT&C) is the mission function that reports spacecraft status, supports knowledge of its position, and sends commands between a spacecraft and its operators.

In routine operations, telemetry carries spacecraft measurements and status towards the ground, tracking supplies observations used to determine where the spacecraft is, and command carries instructions towards the spacecraft. ESA’s usage includes platform status, ranging signals and uplinked commands; its Galileo control example also combines telemetry collection, command uplink and two-way radio and Doppler ranging.[^1][^4]

TT&C is delivered by a system that can span spacecraft transponders and command-and-data-handling equipment, radio links, ground stations, network operations and mission control. ESA distinguishes the TT&C role from payload-data transmission, while noting that command and data handling may also carry science data over the same link infrastructure.[^1][^2] The term therefore names an operational function, not a single box or a complete radio design.

Usage of the acronym varies by mission. ESA describes links to Earth and also gives orbiter–lander and inter-satellite links as TT&C examples. State the endpoints when the boundary matters. ESA’s Estrack network supports scheduled contacts and radiometric data collection across mission phases.[^1][^3][^5][^6] A ground-station pass provides a radio path; mission-control systems prepare commands and spacecraft command handling routes or executes them.[^2][^3]

TT&C is related to spacecraft telemetry, telecommand and spacecraft ranging, but those terms name different information or measurement functions. Payload science delivery may share infrastructure with TT&C while remaining a separate mission service.[^1][^2]
