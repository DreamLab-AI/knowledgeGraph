Stellar interior is the part of a star beneath its atmosphere, including the core where fusion takes place and the layers through which energy travels outward.[^1][^2]

In the SWEET ontology the stellar interior is a subclass of the stellar realm. The convection zone, the radiative zone, the tacocline and the stellar core are its subclasses, and the core is tied to nuclear fusion as its chemical reaction.[^1] The Sun is the worked example in the retained NASA source, which lists its interior regions as the core, the radiative zone and the convection zone, and says that energy from the core is carried outward by radiation.[^2]

Fusion in the core releases energy that heats the star and holds it against further collapse under gravity.[^3] The retained sources do not describe how interior structure is inferred, and they do not give layer boundaries for stars other than the Sun, so statements about other stars are not made here.

Distinction from the atmosphere is important: in the ontology the corona belongs to stellar atmosphere, a separate subclass of the same parent realm.[^1]
