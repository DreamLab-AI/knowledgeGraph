A microwave radiometer is a passive sensor, typically built as a radiometer or scanner with an antenna, that detects naturally emitted microwave energy within its field of view.[^1]

Energy a passive microwave sensor records can be emitted by the atmosphere, reflected from the surface, emitted from the surface or transmitted from the subsurface.[^1] Tutorials describe the microwave region as roughly 1 cm to 1 m in wavelength, note that the available energy is small compared with optical wavelengths, and state passive microwave sensors are therefore characterised by low spatial resolution.[^1] Passive instruments in general are those that detect energy emitted from the natural environment.[^2]

ESA's Soil Moisture and Ocean Salinity mission carries MIRAS, a two-dimensional interferometric L-band radiometer operating at 1.4 GHz, which is a 21 cm wavelength.[^3] The tutorial lists meteorology, hydrology and oceanography as application areas, including atmospheric profiles and soil moisture.[^1]

Low-resolution statements are the tutorial's general characterisation and are not figures for every instrument. Sources do not establish how recorded brightness is converted into geophysical variables, which needs retrieval methods outside them.
