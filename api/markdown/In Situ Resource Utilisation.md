In situ resource utilisation is the use of local natural resources at a mission destination, instead of taking all needed supplies from Earth.[^1]

NASA describes the aim as enhancing the capabilities of human exploration.[^1] The idea therefore concerns where supplies come from, not a single technique, and the NASA page used here does not catalogue processes or their maturity.

Resources are tied to a destination, so what is usable depends on the body visited. The source gives water and carbon dioxide extracted from volcanic terrain as an illustration of local material, which should not be read as a general inventory of what any site offers.[^1]

Resource utilisation differs from stockpiling cargo launched from Earth. The source does not establish costs, quantities or schedules, so these remain open here.

[^1]: NASA, “In-Situ Resource Utilization (ISRU)”. https://www.nasa.gov/isru/
