A vertical datum is a reference frame that describes how gravity-related heights or depths relate to the Earth, and in most cases it is related to mean sea level.[^1]

Under the OGC specification, vertical datums include sounding datums used in hydrography, in which heights may be negative or expressed as depths. NOAA describes a vertical datum as realised through points of known height above or below a nationally defined reference surface such as mean sea level.[^1][^2]

Geodetic vertical datums generally express land elevations, whereas water-level datums serve as the reference for bathymetric soundings on nautical charts. The geoid, which NOAA describes as a model of global mean sea level, is the surface used to measure precise elevations. Because it is a model, a revised geoid can change heights published against a datum.[^2][^3]

NOAA's VDatum software transforms data among tidal, orthometric and ellipsoidal vertical datums. Its notices record regions where transformation uncertainty is higher than usual, attributing some of this to subsidence, newly established datums and changed geoid versions. The datum in use should therefore be stated with any height or depth, and the NOAA pages describe United States practice only.[^2][^4]
