Magnetic reconnection is a process in which magnetic field lines from different magnetic domains merge and change the overall field topology, mixing plasmas that were previously separate and converting magnetic energy into particle energy.[^1][^2]

In Earth’s magnetosphere, ESA describes reconnection at the magnetopause as occurring when Earth’s field lines interconnect with interplanetary field lines carried by the solar wind, which lets solar material enter along the connected lines.[^1] Observations of the magnetopause in one reported interval showed repeated, sporadic events rather than one steady process, so the sources do not support treating dayside reconnection as continuous.[^1]

On the nightside, reconnection in the magnetotail is reported to heat local plasma and to drive plasma jets towards Earth, in connection with a magnetic substorm.[^2] The same account notes that the process acts within very small diffusion regions even though its consequences are large, which is why the microphysics matters for prediction.[^2]

NOAA states that a southward solar wind magnetic field at the dayside is the condition most effective for transferring energy into the magnetosphere, though that page does not name reconnection as the mechanism.[^3] The sources describe a general mechanism and individual observed events; they do not give a forecast rule for when or where reconnection will start.[^1][^2][^3]
