Geodesy is the science of accurately measuring and understanding the Earth's geometric shape, orientation in space and gravity field.[^1]

NOAA describes geodesists as assigning coordinates to points all over the Earth, and links the field to mapping shorelines, land boundaries and navigation. Its public explainer also frames geodesy as measuring where things are and how those locations change over time.[^1]

In the OGC referencing standard, a datum in modern geodesy is a reference frame that ties an abstract coordinate system to the Earth or another object. Dynamic frames add time evolution, so coordinates may need an epoch.[^2]

Frames are realised and published, for example as ITRF2020, and satellite positioning converts between Cartesian and ellipsoidal coordinates. These are examples of geodetic practice, not a full account of the field, and the sources opened do not cover physical geodesy or gravity-field methods in detail.[^3][^4]
