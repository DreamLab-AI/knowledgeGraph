- ### Definition
  - ISO 10218 is a two-part international safety standard published by the International Organisation for Standardisation that specifies requirements for the design, construction, and safeguarding of industrial robots and robot systems. Part 1 addresses the robot itself and requires manufacturers to conduct risk assessments and implement safety-rated stop functions, speed and force limitation, and workspace monitoring. Part 2 addresses the integration of robots into workcells, covering safety-rated control functions, safeguarding devices, and the responsibilities of system integrators.

- ### Content
  - ISO 10218 is the principal safety standard governing industrial robot installations worldwide and is aligned with the EU Machinery Directive and the ANSI/RIA R15.06 standard. Part 1 (ISO 10218-1) defines the essential safety requirements that robot manufacturers must satisfy before placing a product on the market: these include mechanical stops, control-reliable safety stop circuits, speed monitoring, and workspace limiting devices. Part 2 (ISO 10218-2) covers integration and installation, requiring a formal risk assessment in accordance with ISO 12100 and documentation of the safety control architecture using the categories defined in ISO 13849.
  - The companion technical specification ISO TS 15066 extends ISO 10218 specifically to collaborative robot (cobot) applications, defining four modes of collaborative operation: safety-rated monitored stop, hand guiding, speed and separation monitoring, and power and force limiting. Compliance with ISO 10218 and ISO TS 15066 is a prerequisite for CE marking of robot workcells in European markets.
  - Robot system integrators must demonstrate conformance through documented risk assessments, validation testing, and a Declaration of Conformity. The standard directly shapes the design of collaborative robots from manufacturers such as Universal Robots and FANUC, and influences the safety architecture of frameworks such as ROS 2 via its MoveIt Safety features and hardware abstraction layer constraints.
