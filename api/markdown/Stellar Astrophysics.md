Stellar astrophysics is the branch of astrophysics that studies how stars form, are structured, generate energy and evolve.[^1][^2][^3]

NASA's astrophysics programme describes investigating the origins and evolution of galaxies, stars and planets.[^1] That is a programme statement and not a definition of the sub-field, and the scope given here is drawn from how the cited sources treat stars.

Topics covered by the sources include formation from molecular clouds, hydrogen fusion in a stellar core, and the stages of a star's life that follow.[^2][^3] Stellar structure is described in terms of a core where energy is produced and zones where it is carried outward by radiation or convection, with the arrangement depending on how hydrogen is burned.[^3]

Outer layers and magnetic activity form a further topic. Reviews describe magnetic activity as a feature of stars with convective outer layers and note that, for stars other than the Sun, magnetic features are observed only indirectly.[^4] These sources do not give a formal boundary between stellar astrophysics and neighbouring fields such as solar physics or cosmology.

[^2]: NASA Science, “Stars”. https://science.nasa.gov/universe/stars/
