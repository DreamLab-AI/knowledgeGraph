Multi-messenger astronomy is the study of astrophysical sources through more than one kind of signal, namely electromagnetic radiation, gravitational waves, neutrinos and cosmic rays.[^1]

Astro2020's white paper treats it as a thematic area covering the identification of the sources of gravitational waves, neutrinos, cosmic rays and gamma rays, together with coordinated follow-up across wavelengths. It is a community planning document and not a formal definition.[^1]

GW170817 is the best-documented case, a gravitational-wave signal from a binary neutron star inspiral. Fermi detected a gamma-ray burst shortly after the coalescence, and transient counterparts were later identified across the electromagnetic spectrum in the same location. That letter reports the gravitational-wave measurement and cites the follow-up; it does not report the full electromagnetic campaign.[^2]

Astro2020 names sensitive non-electromagnetic detectors, full electromagnetic coverage with fast response or deep surveys, and better notification platforms as requirements. NASA's astrophysics programme covers the electromagnetic side of such work, from infrared to X-rays, but its page does not define the field.[^1][^3]
