Confined zone is not a defined term in the retained hydrogeological sources, which describe confinement through confining units, confined aquifers and the confined condition of the potentiometric surface.[^1][^2]

Western Oregon University defines confined as a modifier for a condition in which the potentiometric surface is above the top of the aquifer, and defines a confined aquifer as one bounded above and below by confining units of distinctly lower permeability.[^1] The Groundwater Project says the overlying and underlying confining units constrain water from leaking out of the aquifer and direct flow parallel to its boundaries.[^2]

This page uses confined zone as a spatial qualifier for any part of the subsurface where that condition holds. The reading is an inference from the sources, not a quotation, and the extent of such a zone is set by the confining units and not by any retained definition.[^1][^2]

Retained sources give no formal definition, no figures and no remote-sensing treatment of a confined zone.[^1][^2]

[^2]: The Groundwater Project, “6.3 Confined Aquifers”. https://books.gw-project.org/hydrogeologic-properties-of-earth-materials-and-principles-of-groundwater-flow/chapter/confined-aquifers/
