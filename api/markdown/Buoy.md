A buoy is a floating platform that carries sensors to observe conditions at the sea surface and in the air above it.[^1][^2]

NOAA National Data Buoy Center calls its moored buoys the weather sentinels of the sea and deploys them in coastal and offshore waters. They measure and transmit barometric pressure, wind direction, speed and gust, air and sea temperature, and wave energy spectra from which wave height and period are derived.[^1]

Its fleet includes four hull types: three sizes of discus hull and a boat-shaped hull. These details describe one operator's network, and other agencies and drifting buoys use different designs.[^1]

Wind reported by a buoy is an eight-minute average, and the centre publishes a method for estimating wind speed at reference heights, which gives a modelled value rather than an observation. The W3C sensor ontology treats a buoy as one kind of platform, alongside posts, vehicles, ships and aircraft, that can host sensors.[^3][^2]
