A low Earth orbit spacecraft is a spacecraft whose operating orbit is an Earth-centred orbit within the near-Earth region conventionally called LEO.[^1438][^1439][^1441]

Platform and mission regime answer different questions. A launch vehicle can pass through LEO without becoming a LEO spacecraft, and a spacecraft may change orbit during its mission. State the operating orbit when that distinction matters. NASA and ESA use slightly different practical lower limits, while about 2,000 km is a common upper taxonomy boundary.[^1438][^1439][^1441]

LEO platforms include crewed stations and robotic observatories, as well as satellites for Earth observation, communications, navigation, science and technology demonstration. ESA identifies both the International Space Station and the uncrewed Hubble Space Telescope as LEO spacecraft. Operator, funding model and crew status are separate attributes of the platform.[^1439][^1441][^1451]

Design and operations must account for residual atmospheric drag, debris impact risk and radiation exposure. Which effects dominate depends on altitude, inclination, spacecraft shielding and mission duration. A LEO spacecraft is not outside the radiation environment, and not every orbit experiences the same drag or debris flux.[^1436][^1441][^1444]

For a UK-licensed operator, orbital licensing follows activity and jurisdiction rather than an LEO-only category. The CAA states that an orbital operator licence can cover activities anywhere in outer space and distinguishes activity carried out from the UK under the Space Industry Act from relevant UK-entity activity conducted overseas under the Outer Space Act. Current CAA guidance also requires an end-of-life plan for orbital operator applications submitted after 31 January 2026; it does not apply to licences granted before that date.[^1435][^1436]

The UK Space Agency's 2025–26 annual report describes public funding for LEO connectivity projects focused on technology development, market analysis and commercial readiness. This documents commercial-sector development, not the operational status of any particular satellite service.[^1451]
