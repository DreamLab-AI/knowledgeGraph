In the solar atmosphere, the chromosphere lies above the photosphere and below the transition region and the corona.[^1][^2]

NASA lists the photosphere, chromosphere and corona as the parts of the Sun's atmosphere, with the transition region as the thin layer where the chromosphere heats rapidly into the corona. ESA describes the chromosphere as a layer where plasma density drops dramatically, and NASA notes that temperature there rises with distance from the Sun, unlike the layers below.[^1][^2][^3]

Extent figures differ by source. NASA places the layer between about 400 and 2100 km above the photosphere, while ESA gives a thickness of roughly 1000 to 2000 km, so any quoted height is approximate.[^1][^3] Faintly glowing under normal conditions, the chromosphere is usually outshone by the photosphere, and during a total solar eclipse it appears as a fine red rim around the Sun.[^2]

These sources describe the Sun. They do not establish how the chromosphere is heated or how the term carries over to other stars.
