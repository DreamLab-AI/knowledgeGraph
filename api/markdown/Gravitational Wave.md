A gravitational wave is a ripple in the fabric of space-time produced by systems such as binary white dwarfs and merging massive black holes.[^1][^2]

Direct detection by LIGO and Virgo collaborations came from a transient signal seen simultaneously by both LIGO detectors on 14 September 2015, matching the waveform predicted by general relativity for binary black hole inspiral, merger and ringdown.[^3]

NASA describes LISA as a trio of spacecraft in which arriving waves will minutely change the lengths of the triangle's arms, with ground computers searching the measurements for individual events.[^1] ESA describes LISA as a future first space-based observatory dedicated to gravitational waves.[^2]

NASA states that gravitational waves provide different information about their sources than light.[^1] LISA is described as planned in these sources, with no launch date established.
