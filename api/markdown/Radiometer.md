A microwave radiometer is a passive sensor that uses an antenna to detect and record naturally emitted microwave energy.[^1]

Natural Resources Canada states that passive microwave sensors are typically radiometers or scanners, that the emitted energy relates to the temperature and moisture of the surface, and that the energy available is small. The fields of view must therefore be large, so most passive microwave sensors are characterised by low spatial resolution.[^1]

ESA states that the main purpose of the ERS microwave radiometer was measuring the tropospheric path delay for the altimeter, and describes it as a passive microwave multi-spectral radiometer.[^2] NASA describes AMSR-E on Aqua as a twelve-channel, six-frequency, total power passive-microwave radiometer system.[^3]

Sources opened for this page deal only with microwave radiometers. They do not establish how radiometers are defined in the optical or thermal infrared, and a radiometer is not an imaging radar.
