Solar physics is the study of the Sun as a star made of plasma, covering its interior, its atmosphere, its magnetic field and the particles and energy it releases into space.[^1][^2]

NASA describes the Sun as a ball of plasma with no solid surface. The part seen from Earth is the photosphere, and above it lie the thin chromosphere and the corona, where prominences, flares and coronal mass ejections occur.[^2]

The Sun's surface and atmosphere change under magnetic forces generated by its moving plasma. Sunspots, which mark concentrations of magnetic field lines, are used to track a natural cycle, and the magnetic field itself follows a reversal pattern from cycle to cycle.[^1][^3][^4]

Several spacecraft currently investigate the Sun, according to NASA's fact page at retrieval. NASA places this work within heliophysics, which also covers the Sun's influence on space, Earth and other planets. The sources used here do not draw a formal boundary between solar physics and heliophysics, and the page does not either.[^2][^1]
