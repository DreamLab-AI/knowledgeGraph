A quasar is an extremely luminous active galactic nucleus, powered by a supermassive black hole accreting matter at the centre of a galaxy.[^1][^2]

NASA explains that in an active galaxy the central black hole is accreting material from the dense central region, and that this accounts for emission well beyond what the stars would produce. Chandra states that the power of a quasar depends on the mass of the central black hole and the rate at which it swallows matter.[^1][^2]

Chandra reports that X-rays arise when infalling matter is heated as it spirals towards the black hole, and that some quasars are bright enough to be seen at very great distances. NASA groups quasars with radio galaxies and blazars as active galactic nuclei with strong jets, and says that the angle of view affects how the galaxy appears.[^1][^2]

Quasar is one kind of active galactic nucleus, and the retained sources do not give a precise luminosity threshold that separates quasars from other active galaxies.[^1][^2]
