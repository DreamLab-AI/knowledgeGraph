
A spacecraft ground segment is the coordinated ground-based infrastructure and operations that support spacecraft control, communications, tracking and mission data handling.

Its components can include mission-control and flight-dynamics systems, ground stations, communication networks, planning tools, simulators, data processing and links to science or user facilities. ESA describes a mission data system that processes telemetry and prepares commands, while its wider ground segment connects station networks and specialist control centres and is tested end to end with the spacecraft.[^1]

Ground stations receive telemetry and radiometric data and transmit telecommands during scheduled contacts. Networks may be agency-operated, supplemented by institutional or commercial partner stations, and configured for different mission phases. ESA’s Estrack and Network Operations Centre pages describe one agency network; NASA’s Deep Space Network is another specific example, not a service architecture available by default to every mission.[^2][^3][^4] ESA’s Galileo example likewise separates sensor, uplink and TT&C station functions within its larger ground system.[^7]

A ground segment can extend beyond spacecraft command and control. Depending on the programme, it may include mission planning, telemetry storage, payload operations, science processing, archiving and distribution. ESA examples place some science and payload functions in connected specialist centres, while an educational student mission shows a smaller arrangement linking a mission-control computer, operations team and remote stations.[^1][^5][^8] Deep-space antennas also support TT&C through mission phases and can carry out radio-science work.[^9]

Ground-segment boundaries and responsibilities vary by mission and provider. A station network can supply communication and tracking services, but network service does not itself confer mission command authority. In the UK, Ofcom authorises use of radio spectrum and provides licence routes for satellite earth stations; that radio authorisation is one part of the ground operation’s regulatory context.[^6]

Ground Segment is related to spacecraft systems engineering because its interfaces and end-to-end validation must work with the spacecraft and mission operations. It remains distinct from the space segment, even when both are managed as one mission system.[^1][^2]
