
A spacecraft transponder is an onboard communications unit that receives a signal and produces a corresponding transmission on an outgoing link.

A transponder may filter, amplify, frequency-convert, channelise or switch received signals. Its boundary varies between spacecraft designs: some descriptions focus on the RF receive-and-transmit chain, while others include channel or information processing.[^1][^2]

A transparent transponder relays the signal without demodulating and decoding the user information into data for a new link. A regenerative transponder demodulates and decodes incoming information, may switch or process it, then re-encodes and remodulates an outgoing signal. Processing can occur at different levels; regeneration does not by itself imply full network-layer routing.[^1][^2][^5]

Architecture affects where link functions occur and whether uplink and downlink schemes can be chosen separately. ESA’s REDSAT page records a flown onboard regenerative processor example. Other ESA pages describe later project work: NOTES reported a completed non-orthogonal multiple-access demonstration at TRL 5, while D2SAT was still an ongoing scaled demonstrator in July 2026. These examples show specific heritage and development states, not universal deployment across satellite fleets.[^3][^4][^5]

A transponder is one component of a spacecraft communications system. Its architecture should be described separately from the waveform, channel code, user-access method and spectrum permissions used by the complete link.[^1][^2]
