Look angle is the angle between the direction in which a radar antenna is pointing and the nadir.[^1]

NASA Earthdata gives this definition in the context of synthetic aperture radar, and Copernicus documentation for Sentinel-1 calls the off-nadir angle the look angle between the satellite's nadir position and the SAR beam. NASA states that the look angle significantly affects the behaviour of the backscatter and is one of the main parameters setting the viewing geometry. The sources do not quantify that dependence.[^1][^2]

Incidence angle is a separate quantity. NASA defines it as the angle between the radar beam centre and the normal to the local topography, while ESA defines it as the angle between the incident beam and the axis perpendicular to the local geodetic ground surface. Sentinel-1 beams are tied to incidence and off-nadir angles in its documentation, so a mode's angle range depends on the beam chosen.[^1][^2]

NASA notes that the two angles are sometimes used synonymously, and that this is only valid if the geometry is simplified to neglect Earth's curvature and local topography. Treating them as interchangeable is the common error. The sources cited cover radar viewing geometry only; they do not establish how the term is used for optical sensors or ground-station pointing.[^1][^2]
