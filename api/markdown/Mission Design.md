Mission design is the early engineering work that defines a mission concept, its architecture and its operating approach so that requirements and later development have a basis.[^1][^2]

In the NASA life cycle, Phase A develops a proposed mission and system architecture that responds to programme expectations, requirements and constraints, including resources. The concept of operations is part of this work: it is meant to cover nominal and off-nominal operations from integration and test through launch to disposal, and it informs requirements and architecture.[^1][^2]

Some design constraints come from the launch. A launch window is the span of time in which a launch can take place while satisfying safety and mission constraints, and for an interplanetary launch it depends on the positions of Earth and the target planet.[^3]

These sources are NASA material and describe NASA phase vocabulary and interplanetary examples. They do not set out a complete mission design method, trajectory optimisation or the practice of other agencies.[^1][^2][^3]
