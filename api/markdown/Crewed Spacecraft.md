A crewed spacecraft is a spacecraft that carries human occupants and therefore has to provide them with a living area and life support.[^1]

NASA's Orion booklet describes its crew module as a transportation capsule giving a living area for the crew and storage for supplies and research instruments. Its environmental control and life support system is described as controlling atmospheric pressure, fire detection and suppression, oxygen levels, waste management and water recycling.[^1]

For Orion, a separate European service module supplies consumables for the astronauts, namely water, oxygen and nitrogen, alongside propulsion, thermal control and solar-array power.[^2] This shows one way of dividing crew support between a crew module and a service module; the sources do not claim that every crewed vehicle uses the same split.[^1][^2]

NASA's spacecraft classification chapter states that spacecraft carrying human occupants are not considered in it, so that classification does not define crewed vehicles. The sources opened here also do not give crew capacity, mission duration or safety requirements as general rules, and the Orion booklet predates its first flight test.[^1][^3]
