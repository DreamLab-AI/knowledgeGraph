Extragalactic astronomy is the study of galaxies and other structures beyond the Milky Way.[^1][^2]

OpenStax traces the field's start to the demonstration that the Andromeda galaxy lies well outside the Milky Way, which it describes as the birth of a new scientific field.[^1] NASA describes galaxies as occurring in groups and in clusters that may hold thousands of members, with superclusters, voids and walls forming the cosmic web.[^2]

NASA's astrophysics programme frames its work as investigating the origins and evolution of galaxies, stars and planets, so galaxy studies sit within a wider science effort.[^3] The sources are teaching and programme texts, so they describe the subject matter instead of fixing a formal definition of the subfield.[^1][^3]

Superclusters are described as not gravitationally bound, which distinguishes them from clusters.[^2]
