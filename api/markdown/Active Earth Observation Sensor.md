An active Earth observation sensor is a sensor that supplies its own illumination, emitting radiation toward a target and measuring the radiation reflected back to it.[^1]

Natural Resources Canada contrasts this with passive sensors, which record energy that is naturally available. Because an active sensor carries its own source, it can obtain measurements at any time of day or season and can use wavelengths, such as microwaves, that the sun does not supply well. Synthetic aperture radar and laser fluorosensors are the examples the tutorial gives.[^1]

Energy is the cost of this independence: the tutorial notes that active systems must generate a fairly large amount of energy to illuminate targets adequately. The source does not claim that active sensing removes atmospheric or target effects, only that it removes dependence on the sun.[^1]

W3C’s Semantic Sensor Network ontology defines a sensor generally as a device, agent or software involved in or implementing a procedure. It does not itself divide sensors into active and passive, so that distinction rests on the remote-sensing literature rather than the sensor vocabulary.[^2]
