A planetary magnetosphere has a part pushed in the direction of the solar wind, called the magnetotail.[^1] NOAA explains the shape: the pressure of the solar wind compresses Earth’s field on the dayside and stretches it into a long tail on the nightside, which extends to hundreds of Earth radii.[^2] ESA lists the magnetotail, together with the polar cusps, as a key region its Cluster spacecraft visited to study how the solar wind interacts with the magnetosphere.[^1]

Magnetic reconnection occurs at the magnetotail as well. ESA reports that on the nightside reconnection heats local plasma and sends plasma jets towards Earth, and one event it describes was associated with a magnetic substorm.[^3]

Available sources describe the tail’s structure and one observed process in general terms. They do not give a fixed length or a rule for when tail reconnection will occur.[^1][^2][^3]
