Interplanetary space environment is the set of regions of the solar system where physical and biological phenomena are relatively unaffected by the properties of planets and their moons, compared with the influence of the Sun.[^1]

NASA's Space Life Sciences Ontology gives this definition and lists interplanetary space as a synonym, and it places the class under the general space environment class.[^1] The ontology names the Sun's gravitational pull, solar wind, magnetic field and electromagnetic radiation as the dominant influences, and contrasts them with the gravity, atmospheres, magnetic fields and albedo radiation of planetary bodies.[^1]

For crews and spacecraft, the most documented feature is the radiation field. Galactic cosmic rays come from outside the solar system and are modulated by solar activity, while solar energetic particles are accelerated by solar flares or by shocks associated with coronal mass ejections and are hard to predict.[^2][^3] A third natural source, particles trapped in a planetary magnetic field, applies near planets rather than in open interplanetary space.[^3]

These sources do not quantify the solar wind, interplanetary magnetic field or gravity along any particular trajectory, and a mission environment depends on the route and the phase of the solar cycle. Interplanetary space is also not the same as deep space in general, since the definition above is tied to the Sun's influence within the solar system.
