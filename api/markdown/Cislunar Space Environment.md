NASA's Space Life Sciences Ontology defines cislunar space environment as the region of space between the Earth and the Moon.[^1]

Other sources define cislunar space more broadly. According to the US National Cislunar Science and Technology Strategy, it is the three-dimensional volume beyond geosynchronous orbit that is mainly under the gravitational influence of Earth and/or the Moon, and includes Earth-Moon Lagrange point regions, trajectories using those regions and the lunar surface.[^2] A NASA conference paper defines the cis-lunar domain as deep space under the gravitational influence of the Earth-Moon system, listing Earth-centred orbits, libration points and low lunar orbit within it.[^3]

These three definitions differ on the inner boundary and on whether orbits around Earth and the lunar surface are counted. The strategy states that its wording is for the purposes of that document, and the ontology class is marked deprecated in the cited release, so neither establishes a universal boundary.[^1][^2]

Sources describe the region by gravitational influence and by named orbit types rather than by fixed coordinates, and none gives a single agreed boundary. Radiation conditions in the region are a separate class in the ontology.[^1][^3]
