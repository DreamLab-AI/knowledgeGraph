Mars radiation environment is the natural radiation in the vicinity of Mars, comprising free space radiation, radiation produced by its interactions with the Martian atmosphere and crust, and radiation from naturally occurring radioactive elements in the crust.[^1]

This environment, the ontology states, varies with location and time, for example with proximity to the surface, shielding by the planetary body, and the solar cycle and periodic solar particle events. It can include ionising and non-ionising radiation, although the term is often used for biologically damaging ionising radiation such as galactic cosmic radiation and solar particles.[^1]

Mars orbit and Mars surface are separate subclasses in the same ontology, so statements about one should not be transferred to the other without stating the location. At the surface the atmosphere acts as part of the shielding in the radiation models, and the Artemis-I comparison paper reports that Martian atmosphere and regolith composition details have little impact on surface dose rates in its prior sensitivity tests.[^1][^2]

That paper compares model calculations with measurements in low Earth orbit, deep space and at the Mars surface over one period. That comparison supports the models for the stated detectors and mission interval; it does not give a dose for a future crew or a general Mars value.[^2]
