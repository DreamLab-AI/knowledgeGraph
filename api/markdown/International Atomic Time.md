International Atomic Time (TAI) is a continuous atomic time scale computed by the BIPM from clock and time-transfer data contributed by participating laboratories.[^1]

BIPM describes TAI as based on realisations of the SI second, and as a realisation of Terrestrial Time with the same rate.[^1] An explanatory circular from the US Naval Observatory describes it as based on the SI second at sea level on the rotating geoid, resulting from BIPM analysis of data from atomic standards of many countries under an agreed algorithm.[^2]

TAI is an intermediate step in computing UTC. Adding leap seconds to TAI keeps UTC in agreement with time based on the rotation of the Earth, so TAI itself carries no leap seconds.[^1]

That circular dates from 2005 and does not describe the current algorithm, so the pages here do not state one. TAI should not be confused with UTC or with a GNSS system time scale.[^1][^2]

[^1]: BIPM, “UTC”, Time Department. https://www.bipm.org/en/time-department/utc
[^2]: G. H. Kaplan, *The IAU Resolutions on Astronomical Reference Systems, Time Scales, and Earth Rotation Models*, USNO Circular No. 179, 2005. https://arxiv.org/pdf/astro-ph/0602086
