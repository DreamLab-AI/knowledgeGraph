An ice cap is a dome-shaped glacier covering less than 50,000 square kilometres that flows outward in all directions.[^1][^2] It sits below an ice sheet on the land-ice size scale.

NSIDC says an ice cap covers much of the underlying topography, which is what separates it from an ice field.[^1] SWEET adds that an ice cap usually covers a highland area.[^2] Ice sheets use the same 50,000 square kilometre figure as their lower bound, so the area threshold alone places a mass on one side of the line.[^3][^1]

Retained sources give a size ceiling and a shape but no mechanism for how a dome forms, and none says how an ice cap is detected from orbit. The 50,000 figure is a convention: NSIDC and SWEET both use it, and neither explains why that number was chosen.

[^1]: National Snow and Ice Data Center, “Glaciers”. https://nsidc.org/learn/parts-cryosphere/glaciers
[^2]: ESIP SWEET ontology (GitHub mirror), “SWEET realmCryo.ttl”. https://raw.githubusercontent.com/ESIPFed/sweet/master/src/realmCryo.ttl
[^3]: National Snow and Ice Data Center, “Ice Sheets”. https://nsidc.org/learn/parts-cryosphere/ice-sheets
