Surface water is an open body of water on the Earth's surface, such as a lake, river or stream.[^1] The U.S. Geological Survey states that surface water occurs as streams, lakes and wetlands, and also includes the solid forms of water, snow and ice.[^2]

Reservoirs, which are human-made lakes designed primarily to control the flow and distribution of surface water, fall within the same class.[^2] Scope differs between retained sources: the USGS glossary gives a short definition limited to open water bodies, while the USGS circular also counts bays, oceans, snow and ice.[^1][^2]

For remote sensing, NASA describes the SWOT mission as the first satellite mission to observe nearly all water on Earth's surface, measuring the height of water in lakes, rivers, reservoirs and the ocean.[^3] CNES says its radar acquires surface water height in rivers wider than 100 metres and helps determine temporal variations in surface water stocks.[^4][^3] No retained source establishes a universal minimum size for a surface water body, and SWEET records the class only as a hydrosphere feature.[^5]
