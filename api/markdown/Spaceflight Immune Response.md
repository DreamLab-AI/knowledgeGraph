Spaceflight immune response is the altered functioning of the human immune system during spaceflight. One review reports persistent dysregulation during a six-month orbital flight.[^1]

That review indicates distinct aspects of adaptive immunity appear dysregulated in flight while some aspects of innate immunity are heightened. This is a mixed pattern, not a single suppression.[^1]

A review of herpesvirus reactivation states that changes in immune cells, together with decreased cell-mediated immunity, contribute to the reactivation of latent herpes viruses in astronauts.[^2] Herpes reactivation marks altered immunity but does not prove clinical disease.

NASA states that high stress hormone levels and altered immune function increase susceptibility to allergies or infection.[^3] This language is conditional, and ground analogues do not perfectly reproduce spaceflight, so the sources do not establish how far ground findings transfer.[^3]

[^2]: Frontiers in Microbiology, “Herpes Virus Reactivation in Astronauts During Spaceflight and Its Application on Earth”. https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fmicb.2019.00016/full
[^3]: NASA, “The Human Body in Space”. https://www.nasa.gov/hrp/bodyinspace
