A spacecraft structure is the subsystem that provides the overall mechanical integrity of a spacecraft.[^1]

NASA describes it as ensuring that all spacecraft components are supported and can withstand handling and launch loads, flight in freefall and the operation of propulsive components. The spacecraft bus is described as a major part of the structure subsystem, giving places to attach components internally and externally and housing delicate modules that need some thermal and mechanical stability.[^1]

A NASA small-spacecraft report distinguishes primary structures from secondary structures such as solar panels and thermal blankets, which are attached to the primary structure and transmit minimal critical structural loads. It states that failure of a primary structure typically results in catastrophic mission failure, and that failure of a secondary structure may still significantly affect mission performance. The same report says the two categories can be hard to distinguish on small spacecraft.[^2]

Structural design is influenced by subsystem integration, launch environments, mission application and the operating environment. Primary structural components may also serve other functions, such as thermal management or radiation shielding. These statements come from a report on small spacecraft and from a chapter written mainly for flyby and orbiter-class spacecraft, and neither fixes a universal structural layout.[^1][^2]
