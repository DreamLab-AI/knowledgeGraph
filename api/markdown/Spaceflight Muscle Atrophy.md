Spaceflight muscle atrophy is the loss of size and strength of skeletal muscle that NASA reports during spaceflight, which it attributes to the absence of Earth's gravity.[^1][^2]

NASA states that weightlessness can cause muscle deconditioning as a result of chronic disuse, and that the extent of physiological deconditioning varies per individual with starting fitness level, age, mission duration and gravity level.[^2] A peer-reviewed analysis of two astronauts suggests that loss of muscle mass and force in the absence of gravity might be mostly caused by unloading.[^3] The study is small and the interpretation is the authors' own, so it supports a discussion of mechanism and not a population estimate.[^3]

Exercise is prescribed to astronauts as a countermeasure. NASA says current exercise programmes appear to moderate changes in the musculoskeletal system while individual results vary, and that current regimens likely cannot transfer directly to longer exploration missions.[^1][^2]

Such NASA pages treat bone and muscle loss together, so a statement about bone does not stand in for one about muscle. No figures for the size of muscle loss are given in these sources, and none are asserted.[^1][^2]
