
Satellite spectrum allocation is the international designation of a frequency band for use by one or more radiocommunication services under specified conditions.

In the ITU Radio Regulations, an allocation is an entry in the frequency-allocation table. An allotment places a channel in an agreed plan for use by administrations in identified areas, while an assignment authorises a particular radio station to use a frequency under stated conditions. These terms are not interchangeable with an operator’s internal channel scheduling.[^1]

International allocation, satellite-network filings and national station licensing are separate steps. Ofcom acts as the UK notifying administration for eligible satellite filings to the ITU; a filing supports international recognition and coordination but is not proof that a satellite has launched or that a service is operating. Ofcom’s earth-station licences authorise specified UK station deployments under their own terms.[^2][^3]

UK permissions depend on the station class, service, band and licence conditions. Ofcom’s March 2026 Q/V decision makes up to 10 GHz available for specified satellite gateway use in predominantly low-density areas, subject to licence applications and coordination. The retrieved page still marked proposed high-density-area access “Pending Statement (Closed)”; that proposal must not be represented as a decided permission.[^4]

An allocation does not prescribe a satellite’s modulation, channel coding or multiple-access implementation, and it does not itself grant a particular station a right to transmit. The applicable Radio Regulations, filing and coordination status, national authorisation and technical conditions must be considered for the actual network and station.[^1][^2][^3][^4]
