An Essential Climate Variable is a physical, chemical or biological variable, or a group of linked variables, that critically contributes to the characterisation of Earth's climate.[^1]

The Global Climate Observing System specifies the list of variables and describes their datasets as providing empirical evidence for understanding and predicting the evolution of climate. The list is revised, so the current GCOS pages are the authority for which variables are included.[^1]

Producing a variable as a long record is a separate task. A climate data record is defined by a National Research Council committee as a time series of sufficient length, consistency and continuity to determine climate variability and change, and NASA describes an Earth system data record as a set of observations of one parameter optimised for specific science questions.[^2][^3]

Being listed as an ECV does not, on these sources, mean that the variable is observed from space or that any one dataset meets the GCOS observing principles.[^1][^2]
