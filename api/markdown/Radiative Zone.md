A radiative zone is the region of the Sun between the core and the convection zone in which energy is carried outward by radiation.[^1]

NASA Marshall describes the energy generated in the core as carried by light that bounces from particle to particle through this dense material. The zone extends from the outer edge of the core to the tachocline, the interface layer at the base of the convection zone.[^1]

According to this source, the zone extends between about one quarter and about seven tenths of the distance to the surface, with density falling across it. It states that an individual photon takes about a million years to reach the interface layer. These are approximate descriptive figures, and the source does not give uncertainties.[^1]

This description refers to how energy moves, not a separate material. Only one retained source covers the zone, so the page limits itself to this broad picture of a solar structure, and it does not extend the description to other stars.[^1]

[^1]: NASA Marshall Space Flight Center, “NASA/Marshall Solar Physics”. https://solarscience.msfc.nasa.gov/interior.shtml
