Carbon cycle monitoring tracks carbon stored in reservoirs and exchanged among the land, ocean, inland water and atmosphere. It combines several kinds of quantity: atmospheric concentration, biomass or other carbon stocks, and fluxes between reservoirs. These are connected but not interchangeable. A satellite measurement of reflected or emitted radiation must be retrieved into a geophysical variable, while a surface flux often requires atmospheric transport or ecosystem modelling.

## A combined observing system

The UK's National Centre for Earth Observation combines field measurements, satellite observations, models, data assimilation and large-scale computation. Its work includes forest biomass, atmospheric composition and ocean carbon, with ground data used to calibrate and validate satellite products.[^1] This integration is necessary because no platform measures the whole carbon cycle at every scale.

UKCEH's greenhouse-gas flux network measures exchanges of carbon dioxide, water and energy at ecosystem sites, with some work also covering methane. The data support analysis of long-term trends and year-to-year variation, studies of land management, and model calibration. Automated uploads can provide near-real-time flux calculations.[^2] A tower nevertheless samples a local footprint. Fast processing does not make a limited site network spatially complete, particularly across peatland and agricultural systems where UKCEH reports substantial uncertainty.

Surface sites can provide precise process evidence but are sparse and unevenly distributed. Satellites provide much wider and more internally consistent sampling, though only at their overpass times and under the conditions their instruments require. CEOS therefore calls for an integrated, sustained system: radiances must be converted into carbon-related products, linked with surface and airborne observations, and maintained long enough to distinguish trends from natural variability.[^7]

## Atmospheric carbon from MicroCarb

MicroCarb is a joint UK Space Agency and CNES mission launched in July 2025. It measures oxygen and carbon dioxide bands in reflected sunlight from a 650 km orbit, with a nominal 21-day repeat cycle. UKSA gives a standard pixel size of about 4.5 by 9 km, a 2 by 2 km city mode and a concentration-precision design figure of about one part per million.[^3]

MicroCarb returned its first Earth observations from the Amazon on 28 August 2025. Dark forest, persistent cloud and the South Atlantic Magnetic Anomaly made the region difficult to observe. Initial comparisons indicated that the instruments behaved as expected, after which fine calibration and algorithm tuning continued.[^4] Launch and first light are completed milestones. They do not yet establish a validated, operational global flux product: converting column concentration into sources and sinks adds retrieval, transport-model and prior-information uncertainty.

## Forest biomass

Above-ground biomass is the dry mass of woody and other plant material above the soil; it excludes roots and is not itself the whole ecosystem carbon stock. ESA's Biomass satellite carries P-band synthetic-aperture radar, whose longer wavelength is intended to provide more information about trunks and branches beneath dense forest canopy. Tropical forests are a main target because existing biomass estimates there have high uncertainty.[^5]

ESA Climate Change Initiative version 7 is separate from the satellite's future contribution. It provides annual 100 m above-ground-biomass maps for 2005-2012 and 2015-2024, with per-pixel uncertainty, quality flags and selected-year change products. Its algorithm combines radar and spaceborne lidar information, and the maps are assessed against ground and airborne data.[^6] Converting dry biomass into carbon or estimating change requires stated conversion factors, compatible versions and propagated uncertainty.

CEOS governance has long identified consistent calibration, cross-mission comparison, validation data, uncertainty guidance and durable access as requirements for useful carbon time series. Its CARB-08 implementation record closed in 2017, so it documents the action framework rather than certifying all current products.[^8]
