Congelation ice is sea ice that grows on the underside of an existing ice sheet, producing columnar crystals as it thickens downwards.[^1][^2]

SWEET defines it as sea ice formed on the submerged base of an existing mass of sea ice and composed of columnar crystals through downward growth into the water.[^1] NSIDC describes the same process: once the ice has become a stable sheet with a smooth underside, crystals grow directly on the bottom, and they are long and vertical because they grow more slowly than frazil ice.[^2]

Congelation is a formation mechanism, not an age or thickness class. NSIDC reports that congelation growth continues to add thickness to the ice cover, and it treats the age categories such as first-year and multiyear ice separately.[^2] SWEET adds a looser comment that congelation ice usually forms through fusion of platelets into solid ice, a wording that differs in emphasis from its own formal definition.[^1]

Retained sources do not establish how congelation ice is distinguished from other sea ice in satellite imagery, nor any typical growth rate or thickness.[^1][^2]
