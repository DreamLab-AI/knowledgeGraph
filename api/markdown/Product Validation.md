Product validation is the quantitative assessment of the accuracy of a satellite-derived product by comparison with reference data.[^1]

The CEOS Land Product Validation subgroup coordinates this work for land products, with a focus on standardised intercomparison and validation across products from different satellites, algorithms and agencies. It describes a validation hierarchy of stages, starting with no validation, where product accuracy has not been assessed, and rising through assessment at small sets of locations to results that are updated when new product versions are released.[^1]

Validation differs from screening. Screening flags individual observations, as in the per-pixel metadata of the CARD4L specification, whereas validation concerns how well a product agrees with reference data. The sources do not state this contrast directly, and the distinction here is a reading of the two.[^1][^2]

The CEOS hierarchy applies to land products. The sources opened here do not set validation stages for other Earth observation domains, so a stage number should be attributed to CEOS and read with the product version it refers to.[^1]

[^2]: CEOS, *CARD4L Product Family Specification: Surface Reflectance*, version 5.0. https://ceos.org/ard/files/PFS/SR/v5.0/CARD4L_Product_Family_Specification_Surface_Reflectance-v5.0.pdf
