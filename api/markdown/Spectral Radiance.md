Spectral radiance is radiant energy crossing a unit area per unit time, solid angle and wavelength interval, the directional quantity an optical sensor records in each band [^1] [^2].

Radiance counts energy passing through a surface from a particular direction, and the spectral form restricts the count to photons within a narrow wavelength range [^1]. Teaching material for optical remote sensing defines spectral radiance as the energy within a wavelength band radiated by a unit area per unit solid angle, and separates it from reflectance, which is a ratio to the incoming energy [^2].

In NASA's processing-level scheme, Level 1A data are unprocessed instrument data annotated with ancillary information that can include radiometric and geometric calibration coefficients, and Level 1B data are those data processed to instrument units [^3]. That places measured radiance at the lowest processed levels, although the retained sources do not say that NASA's levels page defines a radiance product or names spectral radiance at all.

None of the retained sources establishes sensor-specific units, calibration procedures or the atmospheric corrections that turn at-sensor radiance into surface quantities.
