- ### Definition
  - ISO/TS 15066:2016 is an ISO Technical Specification that supplements ISO 10218-1 and ISO 10218-2 by providing detailed guidance and biomechanical data for the safety of collaborative robot applications in which industrial robots and human workers share a common workspace without a physical separating guard. It defines four collaboration modes — safety-rated monitored stop, hand guiding, speed and separation monitoring, and power and force limiting — along with body-part-specific pain thresholds that govern permissible contact forces and pressures. The specification is a foundational reference for cobot risk assessment and CE marking under the EU Machinery Directive.

- ### Content
  - ISO/TS 15066 addresses a gap in prior robot safety standards (ISO 10218 series, which governed traditional industrial robots behind fencing) by providing the technical and biomechanical basis for human-robot collaboration (HRC). The document draws on published medical and ergonomic data to derive transient contact limits — expressed as maximum permissible force and pressure — for 29 body regions, from the skull to the feet. These thresholds underpin the power-and-force limiting (PFL) collaboration mode, in which the robot reduces speed or stops upon detecting contact within defined force limits.
  - The four collaboration modes defined in ISO/TS 15066 — safety-rated monitored stop (SRMS), hand guiding (HG), speed and separation monitoring (SSM), and power and force limiting (PFL) — may be used individually or combined within a single application. SSM requires a real-time distance measurement system (e.g., laser scanner or 3D vision) to enforce a protective separation distance between the robot and any detected person, with speed scaled inversely to proximity.
  - The standard is used by cobot manufacturers such as Universal Robots, FANUC, and ABB as the technical basis for safety-rated specifications of their collaborative mode configurations. Risk assessors and system integrators reference it when completing CE-marking declarations under the EU Machinery Directive 2006/42/EC. Ongoing work in ISO TC 299 WG3 is evolving the standard toward a full international standard (ISO 10218-3) that will incorporate lessons from field deployments and updated biomechanical research.
