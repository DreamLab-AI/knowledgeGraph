Outermost layer of the solar interior, the convection zone carries heat outward to the photosphere via rising and sinking plasma.[^1][^2][^3]

Energy from the core crosses the radiative zone by radiation. In the convection zone, ESA says plasma is always moving and carrying heat outward, with hot plasma rising and cooler plasma sinking in a turbulent pattern that it compares to a boiling pan of water.[^1][^2]

Visible at the surface, the convective motion appears as granules and supergranules, which NASA identifies as the signature of convection, and as the granulation patterns ESA describes in the photosphere.[^1][^3] ESA gives the temperature at the base of the zone as about two million degrees Celsius.[^1]

These sources describe the Sun only. They do not cover the convective regions of other stars, and the pan-of-water comparison is an analogy rather than a model.

[^3]: NASA Science, “The Heliopedia”. https://science.nasa.gov/reference/the-heliopedia/
