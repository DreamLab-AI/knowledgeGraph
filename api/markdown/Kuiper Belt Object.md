A Kuiper Belt object is an icy body in the Kuiper Belt, a doughnut-shaped region beyond the orbit of Neptune.[^1]

NASA states that the belt holds Pluto, most known dwarf planets and some comets, and that astronomers think it holds millions of small icy objects.[^1][^2] Objects contain rock and water ice with other frozen compounds such as ammonia and methane, and some, including Pluto, are over 1,000 kilometres wide.[^2]

Spacecraft observation is limited. New Horizons flew past Arrokoth in 2019, the first time a second Kuiper Belt object was explored up close, so one object cannot stand for the whole population.[^3]

These sources treat the belt as a region and its members as a mixed group, so size, composition and orbit vary by object.
