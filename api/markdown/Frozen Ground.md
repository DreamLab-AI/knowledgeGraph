Frozen ground is soil or land held at or below the freezing point of water, covering both seasonally frozen ground and permafrost.[^1][^2]

NSIDC gives a temperature at or below 0°C for frozen soil, and separates seasonally frozen ground that freezes for more than 15 days per year from permafrost, which is ground frozen for at least two years.[^1] Permafrost is defined solely by temperature, not by moisture content, snow cover or location.[^1]

SWEET adds, in a comment, that perennially and seasonally frozen ground can be partially to extensively frozen depending on the extent of the phase change.[^2] Frozen ground is therefore a thermal state of land, and the state can change through the year.[^1][^2]

Retained sources do not establish how frozen ground is mapped from satellite observations or which freeze threshold a given product applies.
