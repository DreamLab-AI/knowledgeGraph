An imaginary line around the middle of a planet or other celestial body, halfway between its north and south poles at zero degrees latitude, defines the equator.[^1]

In geodesy the equatorial plane serves as the reference for latitude. Geodetic latitude, as defined by the OGC specification, is the angle from the equatorial plane to the perpendicular to the ellipsoid through a given point, with north treated as positive.[^2]

Geocentric Cartesian systems, also described in the OGC specification, place the X axis within the equatorial plane and align the Z axis with the Earth's rotation axis. Which plane counts as equatorial therefore follows the model or axis in use.[^2]

National Geographic presents the equator as a general planetary term, with Earth as its main example. It is a defined line on a model of a body, not a physical feature, and the sources do not say how precisely its position is realised for a given frame.[^1]
