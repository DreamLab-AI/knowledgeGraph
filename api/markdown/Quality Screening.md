Quality screening is the application of quality checks and flags to Earth observation products so that users can exclude or weight observations according to their suitability.[^1][^2]

The CARD4L surface reflectance specification includes overall data quality in its general metadata and lists per-pixel items such as no data, saturation and cloud, so quality information appears at both product and pixel level. Sentinel-2 processing runs an on-line quality control on each product, with results presented as reports and quality masks, and the mask format changed between processing baselines.[^1][^2]

Programme status labels are a separate matter. The Harmonized Landsat and Sentinel-2 project describes its data as research quality once a provisional tag is removed. That is a statement of product status by the programme and not a measurement of the quality of any observation.[^3]

No source opened here gives a single standard definition separating quality screening from pixel screening, and the product-level and pixel-level split used here is an editorial reading. The definition therefore rests on the CARD4L specification and on operational product documentation.[^1][^2]
