A solid rocket motor is a rocket engine whose propellant is a mixture of oxidiser and fuel stored in solid form as a propellant grain.[^1][^2]

NASA Glenn describes the propellant as packed into a solid cylinder with a hole through it that serves as the combustion chamber. Combustion takes place on the surface, a flame front burns into the mixture, and designers use a variety of hole shapes to control how thrust changes with time.[^2]

Hot exhaust gas passes through a nozzle that accelerates the flow, with the flow choked at the throat. Because the oxidiser is mixed into the propellant, the motor can generate thrust in a vacuum.[^2]

According to the NASA small-spacecraft survey, solid motors are described in the context of impulsive manoeuvres for small satellites.[^1] The Glenn page is a general teaching description that also mentions missiles, model rockets and launcher boosters, and neither source gives performance figures for a particular spaceflight motor.[^2]

[^2]: NASA Glenn Research Center, “Solid Rocket Engine”, *Beginner’s Guide to Propulsion*. https://www.grc.nasa.gov/www/k-12/airplane/srockth.html
