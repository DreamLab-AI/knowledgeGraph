- ### Definition
  - EIP-1559 is an Ethereum Improvement Proposal implemented in the London hard fork that fundamentally reformed Ethereum's transaction fee market. It replaced the simple first-price auction mechanism with a protocol-determined base fee that automatically adjusts each block based on demand relative to a target block gas usage. The base fee is burned rather than paid to validators, creating a deflationary pressure on ether supply. Users optionally add a priority fee (tip) to incentivise timely inclusion. The mechanism improves fee predictability for users and reduces the miner extractable value (MEV) captured through fee sniping.

- ### Content
  - EIP-1559, authored primarily by Vitalik Buterin and deployed on Ethereum in August 2021 as part of the London hard fork, replaced Ethereum's original first-price fee auction with a two-tier fee structure. In the original model, users submitted a gas price and miners selected transactions greedily by price, creating volatility and overpayment during congestion. EIP-1559 introduced a protocol-computed base fee that is common to all transactions in a block and adjusts deterministically based on whether the previous block used more or fewer than the target gas limit.
  - The base fee is burned — removed from circulation — rather than paid to block producers. This makes ether a partially deflationary asset under conditions of sustained network demand, since burned fees reduce the net issuance of ether. Block producers receive only the priority fee (tip), which users include to incentivise preferential inclusion during high-demand periods. The Gas Fee Market becomes more predictable: wallets can estimate the base fee for the next block with high accuracy, and the worst-case overpayment is bounded.
  - EIP-1559 has broad implications for Blockchain Economics and tokenomics. The burn mechanism aligns the interests of ether holders with network usage — high activity benefits holders by reducing supply. During periods of intense network demand (such as NFT mints or DeFi activity), fees have exceeded block rewards, making Ethereum net-deflationary. EIP-1559 also partially mitigates certain MEV strategies that relied on transaction ordering by fee. Subsequent protocol development, including the transition to Proof of Stake via The Merge, builds on the EIP-1559 fee model.
