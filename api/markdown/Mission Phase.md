A mission phase is a defined stage in a project or flight, separated from the next by a decision or a change in operations.[^1][^2]

NASA separates a space flight project into phases divided by key decision points, which are the events where the decision authority judges readiness to progress. Phase E is the operations and sustainment phase. NASA Basics adds that interplanetary mission operations may be considered as launch, cruise, encounter and extended operations, and places these as subcategories of that phase.[^1][^2]

Other organisations use their own terms. ESA material refers to the launch and early orbit phase, during which dedicated teams and rehearsals prepare the first operations after launch.[^3]

Phase names, numbering and boundaries are conventions of each agency or project, and the interplanetary scheme should not be applied to every mission type.[^1][^2]
