A mission control centre is the facility where a spacecraft is commanded, ground station use is managed and telemetry is archived, staffed by teams that operate the mission.[^1][^2]

NASA small-satellite guidance describes a mission operations centre as the place where commanding is generated, ground station control is managed and telemetry is archived, usually in a controlled-access room with terminals for several subsystem specialists. Its size follows mission complexity, with more people present during critical events or while an anomaly is being resolved.[^1]

An ESA account of a joint launch preparation at the European Space Operations Centre lists flight control, ground station operations, flight dynamics and project teams as parts of the operating community, and describes rehearsals of nominal and contingency scenarios. ESA also describes the centre as having controlled satellites since its inauguration, and the same page lists the missions it operates.[^3][^2]

These sources describe a small-satellite facility and one agency centre, so staffing, layout and the split between mission operations and science operations differ between programmes. The sources do not establish a single standard configuration.[^1][^3]
