Burned area mapping is the satellite-based identification of land surfaces affected by fire, reported as where and when burning occurred.[^1][^2]

One operational product, MODIS MCD64A1, combines surface-reflectance imagery with active fire observations and assigns a date of burn to each grid cell within a MODIS tile.[^1] The ESA Climate Change Initiative fire project follows a related approach, aiming for more consistent global burned-area products and using active fire observations as a supplemental variable to improve detection across varying biomes.[^2]

Burned-area products describe land that has already burned, whereas active fire observations describe fires as they are detected. In both programmes the two kinds of observation appear together, with active fire used as an input or supplement rather than as the result.[^1][^2]

Operational fire services use this kind of information within a wider cycle. The European Forest Fire Information System covers pre-fire conditions and the assessment of post-fire damage, although its page does not say which mapping method supports the damage assessment.[^3] The sources retained here do not state product accuracy, so a map should be read with the confidence, uncertainty or quality information supplied with the specific product.
