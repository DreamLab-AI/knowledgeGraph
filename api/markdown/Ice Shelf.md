An ice shelf is an extension of thick land ice that flows out over a cold coastal ocean.[^1] SWEET sets four conditions: it is attached to the coast, at least 2 metres thick, forms where a glacier or ice mass flows onto the ocean surface, and grows by snow accumulation or the seaward extension of land glaciers.[^2]

NSIDC reports that shelves range in thickness from about 50 to 600 metres and that most of them fringe the Antarctic coast.[^1] The 2 metre figure is a classification floor in an ontology and the 50 to 600 metre range describes typical shelves, so the two numbers do not conflict.[^2][^1]

Retained sources give no melt or calving rates and say nothing on remote sensing of shelves.

[^1]: National Snow and Ice Data Center, “Ice Shelves”. https://nsidc.org/learn/parts-cryosphere/ice-shelves
[^2]: ESIP SWEET ontology (GitHub mirror), “SWEET realmCryo.ttl”. https://raw.githubusercontent.com/ESIPFed/sweet/master/src/realmCryo.ttl
