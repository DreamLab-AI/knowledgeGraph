An optical instrument is a sensing device that measures light in the visible, near-infrared or shortwave infrared parts of the spectrum.[^1][^2]

NASA states that the Operational Land Imager aboard Landsats 8 and 9 measures visible, near-infrared and shortwave infrared light. ESA states that each Sentinel-2 satellite carries a single optical payload, the Multi-Spectral Instrument, which samples spectral bands at several spatial resolutions.[^1][^2] These are two examples and do not delimit the class.

The Canadian tutorial gives the visible range as approximately 0.4 to 0.7 micrometres, with infrared wavelengths beyond it.[^3] Which bands a particular instrument covers depends on its design and is stated in its own documentation.

In the W3C sensor vocabulary, a sensor is a device, agent or software involved in implementing a procedure, and it responds to a stimulus and generates a result.[^4] That vocabulary does not define an optical instrument class, so the link between the two is a reading across sources.
