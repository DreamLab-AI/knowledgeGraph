Tropopause is the boundary between the troposphere and the stratosphere, marked by the point where temperature stops falling with height.[^1][^2]

Royal Meteorological Society material describes the usual division of the atmosphere into layers called spheres, each boundary marked by a pause where the temperature profile changes abruptly.[^1] Met Office text states that air in the troposphere becomes colder at higher altitudes up to its upper boundary, called the tropopause, and that the stratosphere above it warms with altitude.[^2] SWEET types the tropopause as a planetary boundary rather than as a layer.[^3]

Its height and temperature vary with latitude. MetLink gives the troposphere as around 16 km high at the equator with a tropopause temperature around -80 °C, and around 8 km high at the poles with a tropopause around -40 °C in summer and -60 °C in winter.[^1] It adds that the move into the stratosphere is marked by an abrupt change in the concentrations of variable trace constituents.[^1]

No retained source gives a formal definition by lapse rate, so this page does not state one. They also do not describe how the tropopause is located from satellite data.
