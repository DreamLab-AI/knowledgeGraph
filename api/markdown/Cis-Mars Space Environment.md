Cis-Mars space environment, as defined in NASA's Space Life Sciences Ontology, is the region of space between the Earth and Mars under the space environment class.[^1]

NASA's ontology gives this definition in a single line, and the cited release marks the class as deprecated. It supplies no boundary, distance or trajectory, and none of the other sources reviewed here defines a cis-Mars region, so the term is best read as an ontology label for the Earth to Mars transit space and not as a formally bounded zone.[^1]

NASA material treats this space mainly through crew hazards. It states that outside Earth's protective magnetic field and atmosphere, ionising radiation is a serious risk to astronauts travelling to Mars, and that crews on a Mars mission will accumulate exposure from galactic cosmic radiation and solar particle events.[^2][^3] A 2017 NASA briefing contrasts deep space, where it lists no protection from either source, with low Earth orbit and the Mars surface.[^4]

These radiation statements describe Mars missions in general, including time at Mars, and they do not measure any defined cis-Mars volume. Cislunar space and the radiation environments of both regions are separate classes in the same ontology.[^1]
