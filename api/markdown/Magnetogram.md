A magnetogram is a map of the magnetic field, such as the line-of-sight and vector maps of the solar magnetic field produced by the Helioseismic and Magnetic Imager on the Solar Dynamics Observatory.[^1]

NASA lists magnetograms among the HMI observables, alongside continuum images of the solar photosphere near the Fe I absorption line and maps of line-of-sight velocity. The same page distinguishes line-of-sight from vector magnetograms. The source gives the term only in parentheses and does not define it more fully, so it is best read as the name of a data product rather than of an instrument.[^1]

Ground-based networks produce magnetograms too. The US National Solar Observatory says that GONG data products include magnetic-flux images of the Sun every minute, and that high-cadence, high-sensitivity magnetograms are available.[^2] Both sources concern the Sun, so what they establish about measurement is specific to solar observation.

Usage of the word can also cover records from ground magnetic observatories, but the sources retained here do not cover that sense and this page does not assert it. The sources also do not give field strengths, calibration practice or how magnetograms are used in space-weather products.[^1][^2]

[^2]: US National Solar Observatory, “Global Oscillation Network Group (GONG)”. https://www.nso.edu/telescopes/gong/
