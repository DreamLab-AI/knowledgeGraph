Temporal coverage, in Earth observation practice, describes how often and over what period a place is observed, commonly expressed as a revisit interval.[^1][^2]

Revisit interval depends on the satellites flying and on latitude. ESA states that the two-satellite Sentinel-2 constellation revisits the same spot over the equator every five days, and that this is faster at higher latitudes.[^1]

Adding a satellite shortens the interval. NASA states that Landsat 9 flying with Landsat 8 observes the same location every 8 days.[^2]

These figures are for the named constellations and are not general values. A revisit interval is a property of the orbits and swaths. The cited pages do not say how many of those passes give usable data once cloud or illumination limits apply, so it should not be read as the number of usable observations.[^1][^2]

[^1]: ESA, “Sentinel-2 satellite constellation”. https://www.esa.int/Applications/Observing_the_Earth/Copernicus/Sentinel-2/Satellite_constellation
[^2]: NASA, “Landsat 9”. https://landsat.gsfc.nasa.gov/satellites/landsat-9/
