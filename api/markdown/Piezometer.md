A piezometer is a pressure- and head-measuring device, which in groundwater hydraulics takes the form of a tightly cased well of small diameter.[^1]

In the USGS study guide, the hydraulic version is a tube that taps a fluid system at one end and is open to the atmosphere at the other, and the pressure at the tap is proportional to the height of the fluid column above it.[^1] The guide adds that piezometers are usually installed to measure pressure and head at the point of measurement, and that pressure head can be measured directly in a piezometer or observation well.[^1]

Water level in a borehole or piezometer is used to define the potentiometric surface, which the BGS glossary describes as representing the elevation and pressure head of groundwater. Piezometric surface is given there as an older term for the same thing, and the water table is treated as a particular potentiometric surface.[^2]

A piezometer is an in situ instrument that records a value at the point where it is installed. Sources opened here do not describe satellite or airborne uses, so any link to Earth observation in the catalogue is a relation between topics rather than something these sources establish. The USGS guide also notes that elevation head depends on an arbitrary datum, so only the pressure component is a directly measured quantity.[^1]
