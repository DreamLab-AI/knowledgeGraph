Galactic astronomy is the study of the structure and contents of the Milky Way, the home galaxy of the Sun.[^1][^2]

OpenStax describes the Milky Way as a thin rotating disk of stars with a central bulge and a spherical halo of very old, faint stars, with most globular clusters in that halo.[^1] NASA describes the Milky Way as a spiral galaxy with a disk of stars spanning more than 100,000 light-years, within the Local Group.[^2]

Galaxy mass extends beyond its luminous stars, with dark matter detected only through its gravitational effects on the motion of luminous matter, such as the orbits of distant star clusters and dwarf galaxies.[^1] Composition of the dark matter remains unknown.[^1]

Observation takes place from inside the system, and the cited sources do not define the subfield formally, so the description here follows how they treat the Milky Way.[^1][^2]
