Solar irradiance is the solar energy arriving at the top of Earth's atmosphere, with total solar irradiance being the spectrally integrated value at a base mean distance of one astronomical unit from the Sun.[^1]

NOAA's climate data record assembles total solar irradiance from individual space instruments, including those flown on SORCE, TCTE and TSIS-1 and a compact monitor demonstration. It tracks the spectrally integrated energy rather than individual wavelengths.[^1]

LASP states that space-based monitoring with electrical substitution radiometers began with Nimbus 7 in November 1978. It reports that total solar irradiance variations follow the passage of sunspots across the solar disk with an amplitude of about 0.2%, and that long-term solar cycle variations are only of the order of 0.1%.[^2]

These figures come from a single mission data page and earlier measurements. The sources do not establish current values or the composition of the latest composite record, and solar irradiance should not be conflated with solar flare output or particle events.[^1][^2]

[^2]: LASP, “SORCE TSI Data”. http://lasp.colorado.edu/sorce/data/tsi-data/
