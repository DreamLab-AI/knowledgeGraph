A reservoir of very cold, fairly dense plasma of ionospheric origin fills the inner magnetosphere and co-rotates with Earth as the plasmasphere.[^1][^2] NASA describes it as made of low-energy particles that drift up from the ionosphere, and as carried around with the planet by the magnetic field lines.[^1][^3] ESA calls it the innermost part of the magnetosphere, a doughnut-shaped region of cold plasma whose toroidal shape is determined by Earth’s magnetic field.[^2]

Its outer boundary, the plasmapause, varies with geomagnetic conditions, so the size of the region changes.[^2] It partly overlaps the Van Allen radiation belts but is distinct from them, because the belts hold highly energetic trapped electrons and protons while the plasmasphere holds cold plasma.[^1][^2] NOAA lists it with the ring current and the radiation belts among the regions that form inside the magnetosphere.[^4]

Available sources give qualitative descriptions of structure and composition. They do not give a single fixed extent or density for the region.[^1][^2][^3][^4]
