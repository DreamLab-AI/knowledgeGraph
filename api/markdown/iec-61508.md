- ### Definition
  - IEC 61508 is a foundational international functional safety standard published by the International Electrotechnical Commission that specifies requirements for the design, development, validation, and lifecycle management of electrical, electronic, and programmable electronic (E/E/PE) safety-related systems across all application domains. It defines Safety Integrity Levels (SIL 1–4) as quantitative risk-reduction targets and prescribes the development methods—fault trees, FMEAs, formal methods, and software design constraints—appropriate to each SIL. Sector-specific derivative standards including ISO 26262 (automotive) and EN 50128 (rail) inherit its SIL framework and lifecycle requirements.

- ### Content
  IEC 61508 (second edition 2010) comprises seven parts covering general requirements, hardware requirements, software requirements, definitions, risk assessment methods, guidelines for application of Parts 2 and 3, and an overview of techniques and measures. The standard applies to safety functions implemented in E/E/PE systems—covering PLCs, microcontrollers, fieldbuses, and FPGA-based safety controllers—wherever a failure could lead to hazards to people, the environment, or property.

  Safety Integrity Level (SIL) is the central risk-reduction concept: SIL 1 requires a probability of dangerous failure on demand (PFDavg) between 10⁻² and 10⁻¹; SIL 4, the highest level, requires PFDavg between 10⁻⁵ and 10⁻⁴. SIL assignment results from hazard and Risk Assessment using methods such as HAZOP, fault tree analysis (FTA), and failure mode and effects analysis (FMEA). For each SIL, the standard specifies both quantitative failure-rate targets and qualitative constraints on development processes—coding standards, design diversity, testing depth, and independence of verification.

  Software requirements in IEC 61508-3 prescribe techniques that are "highly recommended" versus "recommended" at each SIL, including formal specification methods (Z, B, Statecharts), structured programming, and dynamic testing. Embedded Systems implementing IEC 61508-compliant designs must demonstrate traceability from safety requirements through architecture, detailed design, code, and test evidence in a safety case.

  The standard's influence extends broadly: ISO 26262 for Autonomous Vehicle and road-vehicle electronics, EN 50128 for railway software, IEC 62061 for machinery, and IEC 62443 for industrial cybersecurity all derive their SIL-equivalent concepts from IEC 61508. As AI and machine learning components are considered for safety functions, the AI-specific functional safety guidance (e.g., ISO/IEC TR 5469) references IEC 61508 as the baseline framework, highlighting gaps in traditional SIL methodology when applied to probabilistic, data-driven systems.
