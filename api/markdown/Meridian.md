A meridian is the intersection of an ellipsoid by a plane containing the shortest axis of the ellipsoid, usually taken as the pole-to-pole arc.[^1]

Longitude is measured from the prime meridian, the meridian from which the longitudes of other meridians are quantified. The OGC description requires a prime meridian for a geodetic reference frame and takes Greenwich with zero longitude as the default, though other prime meridians may be declared.[^1]

Geodetic longitude is the angle from the prime meridian plane to the meridian plane of a given point, taken as positive eastward.[^1]

Local meridians fix north in site frames. The NAIF documentation defines a topocentric frame whose x-axis points north along the local meridian. This is a worked example in a software toolkit, not a general definition, and a meridian here is a geometric line rather than a magnetic one.[^2]
