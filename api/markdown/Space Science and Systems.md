Space science and systems is the combined field of scientific investigation beyond Earth and the engineering of the spacecraft and supporting systems that make such investigation possible.[^1][^2][^3]

NASA presents its science programme as a set of missions around the Earth, the Sun, the Moon, Mars and other destinations, and treats the spacecraft that carry instruments as part of that activity.[^1][^3] Systems engineering is handled as a separate discipline with its own handbook, which describes a broad view of the system as opposed to a single-discipline view.[^2]

In flight-project usage a spacecraft is also called a flight system, to separate it from the ground system. The same chapter points out that the words system and subsystem are often used loosely, so a single system may span hardware aboard a spacecraft and on the ground.[^3]

None of the sources opened here defines the combined phrase itself. It is used in this corpus as a domain label that groups science, spacecraft and engineering practice, and the grouping should be read as organisational rather than as an established term of art.[^1][^2][^3]
