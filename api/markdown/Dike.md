A dike, also spelt dyke, is an embankment built to confine or regulate water and protect adjacent land against overflow.[^1][^2]

The USDA Natural Resources Conservation Service standard describes a dike as an embankment of earth or other suitable materials that protects land against overflow or regulates water, and applies to both dikes and levees.[^1] SWEET records dike as equivalent to dyke, embankment, flood bank, levee and raised bank, and as a subclass of bank.[^2] National Geographic gives a glossary entry for dike as a barrier, usually a natural or artificial wall used to regulate water levels.[^3]

Usage is not uniform. The National Weather Service glossary treats levee and dike as one entry, but restricts dike to an embankment that blocks a reservoir or lake rim lower than the top of the dam.[^4] The retained sources therefore agree on the core idea of an embankment that holds water back, and differ on whether a dike is the same thing as a levee.[^2][^4]

Retained sources do not establish how dikes are detected or monitored from space, nor any standard height or construction dimension.[^1][^2][^4]
