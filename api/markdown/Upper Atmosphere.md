Upper atmosphere is the high part of Earth’s atmosphere, above the layers closest to the surface, and the retained sources draw its lower limit differently.[^1][^2] SWEET types it as a subclass of atmosphere layer.[^3]

NASA lists the mesosphere, thermosphere and exosphere as the upper layers and adds that the ionosphere, identified by its population of ionized particles, is also part of the upper atmosphere.[^1] NOAA instead describes the thermosphere alone as known as the upper atmosphere, and treats the stratosphere and the mesosphere together as the middle atmosphere.[^2]

The Met Office places the boundary of outer space in the lower thermosphere, and says the top of the thermosphere is usually where the top of the atmosphere is defined.[^4] NASA states that Earth’s atmosphere does not have a fixed outer limit.[^1] The retained sources therefore agree that the upper atmosphere grades into space but not on which layers it contains, and the page states no altitude figures.
