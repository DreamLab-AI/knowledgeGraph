Part 2 ·

What is an agent?

A working definition

  • Chatbot: you ask, it answers. One turn at a time.
  • Agent: you give it a goal. It plans, uses tools (your email, files, browser, apps), checks its own work, and keeps going.

Think of agents as sheepdogs. You are no longer putting sheep in a pen one by one. Sometimes the best path is a long arc round the field; sometimes it is a structured play with more work up front. That part depends on you.

Find out more

  • New this month: agents that live in the cloud. Claude, Microsoft’s Autopilot and Grok Bot all keep working after you close the laptop, and come back to you for approval
  • That makes the question less “what can it do?” and more “what have I let it touch?” (Part 6)
  • Anthropic’s Building effective agents is a clear, vendor-neutral explanation of agent patterns

The capability overhang

  • Very few people use more than a fraction of what current AI can already do
  • The unlock is agents: giving the AI your tools, your files and a goal
  • It is still fiddly. That is what these sessions and the Agentics community are for
  • We have too little time today, just signposts and signals

Agents running longer

  • METR measures how long a task an AI can finish on its own
  • That length has doubled roughly every 4.3 months since 2023
  • The best models now succeed half the time on tasks that take an expert a full working day or more
  • Caveat: half the time is not hand it over. Reliability lags capability.

Part 3 · Agents Now

What launched in the last few weeks

Microsoft Copilot: one app, now with Autopilot

  • The new Copilot (25 September 2026) brings everything into one app, in three parts:
  • Autopilot: a persistent agent in Microsoft’s cloud with its own identity, memory and inbox. It keeps working while you are offline and you can @mention it in Teams like a colleague

Find out more

  • Home: chat and Cowork together, with Word, Excel and PowerPoint built in. Hand it a whole task, such as a tender response
  • Code: describe an app, tracker or dashboard in plain English and it builds it, inside your own Microsoft 365
  • Copilot Business £16.10 per user per month (£13.80 until 31 December, excluding VAT). Agent work is billed by usage

When can I have it?

  • Home and Code: rolling out now through Microsoft’s Frontier early-access programme. Code reaches Microsoft 365 Premium and Pro subscribers in preview later this year
  • Autopilot: private preview from the end of September; not self-service yet
  • Copilot Chat (without the agents) is free with eligible Microsoft 365 business plans

How a small business turns it on

  • You need at least one Microsoft 365 Copilot licence (Copilot Business is the SME version, up to 300 users)
  • Your admin opts in: Microsoft 365 admin centre → Copilot → Settings → Frontier → choose who gets access (allow about three hours)
  • Personal, Family and Premium subscribers can opt in themselves from Word, Excel or PowerPoint on the web

What it costs

  • Everyday Copilot stays on the flat per-user price. Cowork, Code and Autopilot use Copilot Credits: $0.01 each, or $200 a month for 25,000 (Microsoft prices credits in dollars)
  • Usage billing is off by default on existing tenants; admins set spending limits
  • From 2 November 2026, new Copilot Business licences bought through a reseller (CSP) have pay-as-you-go switched on by default. Check yours
  • Bundles: Business Standard with Copilot £18.10, Business Premium with Copilot £22.10–£24.60

Before you let Autopilot loose

  • Each Autopilot is effectively a new member of staff with its own account and email address. Microsoft has not yet published how it asks for approval or how its actions appear in audit logs
  • Microsoft’s governance layer is Agent 365, which lists every agent in your tenant with its identity and permissions

Part 4 · Where UK small businesses are

Adoption: a third, and shallow

  • 35% of UK firms with 10+ staff now use AI, up from 12% in 2023 (ONS, July 2026)
  • 28% of micro firms (0–9 staff), 49% of large firms
  • Only 1 in 10 adopters use AI “extensively”
  • Sector matters: information and communication 58%, construction 13%
  • Business surveys run higher (BCC 54%, Lloyds 61%) because they ask differently. The direction is the same.

Find out more

  • The government’s own AI Adoption Research (January 2026) found 16%: the spread comes from how “using AI” is defined
  • Lloyds (March 2026): the North West ranks third for AI investment
  • ONS: AI in UK businesses, 2023 to 2026

What is holding firms back

  • “No identified need” (71%) and limited skills (60%): DSIT, January 2026
  • Concerns rated serious: ethics 80%, cost 76%, unclear regulation 72%
  • Agentic AI is the hardest to implement: 32% of firms report significant barriers
  • 92% of small firms still worry about data security, copyright and liability (FSB, 2026)
  • Only 7.5% of SMEs are “very confident” they understand the benefits (Lloyds, September 2026)

Find out more

  • The same FSB survey found 55% of small firms now use AI, up from 20% in 2023. Worry and use are rising together
  • DSIT AI adoption research · Lloyds Business Barometer

What the evidence says it is worth

  • UK government trials: 19–26 minutes a day saved on email, drafting and searching for information
  • HMRC’s randomised trial: 2–3% of the working week, about an hour
  • Customer support: new staff 34% more productive with an AI assistant (Quarterly Journal of Economics, 2025)
  • The sober view: a Danish study of 25,000 workers found 2.8% of hours saved and no change in pay

Real, modest, and largest for the least experienced. Not magic.

Find out more

  • DWP’s Copilot evaluation (January 2026) measured 19 minutes a day; the cross-government trial (June 2025) found 26 minutes, self-reported
  • The Department for Business and Trade found “no robust evidence” of higher productivity, and some tasks (scheduling, images) took longer
  • DWP trial · HMRC trial · Brynjolfsson et al. · Humlum and Vestergaard

The catch: AI can intensify work

  • Harvard Business Review, February 2026: people using AI worked faster, took on more tasks and worked longer hours
  • Juggling several AI threads at once feels productive but adds strain
  • The fix is a team “AI practice”: agree what you use it for, and when you stop

Find out more

  • The study followed one US tech firm of about 200 people, so treat it as a warning sign rather than a general rule
  • HBR: AI doesn’t reduce work, it intensifies it

Part 5 · What agents can do for your business

Admin and email

  • Triage the inbox, draft replies, chase invoices, summarise long threads
  • A weekly report assembled from your spreadsheets on a schedule
  • Meeting notes turned into actions and follow-up emails
  • Evidence: the UK government trials’ biggest savings were email drafting (25 minutes) and finding information (26 minutes)

Find out more

  • Try it this week: Claude’s scheduled tasks, ChatGPT Work’s Gmail triggers, or Copilot Cowork in Outlook can each run a “Monday morning summary” for you
  • Start with read-only access to your inbox. Let it draft; you press send

Quotes, tenders and compliance

  • Pull together a bid pack from past proposals, CVs and case studies
  • Check a tender against its requirements before you submit
  • Keep policies, risk registers and method statements up to date
  • Locally: Sellafield’s supply chain (the LINC scheme, the quarterly SME Forum) runs on exactly this kind of paperwork

Find out more

  • These are illustrations: there is no rigorous study of SMEs using agents for tenders yet
  • Microsoft’s own example for Copilot Cowork is an RFP response. Keep a human reading every page before it goes out
  • How to do business with Sellafield Ltd

Marketing and customers

  • Draft posts, newsletters and product descriptions in your voice
  • Answer routine customer questions with a human signing off
  • Research competitors and suppliers, with sources
  • Evidence: marketing is the top use among BCC members who use AI (72%)

Find out more

  • If a chatbot talks to customers in the EU, the EU AI Act now requires it to say it is an AI (Part 6)

Finance, bookkeeping, HR and operations

  • Categorise transactions, reconcile, and flag anomalies for your accountant
  • First drafts of job adverts, onboarding packs and rotas
  • Stock, suppliers and orders: agents that watch and nudge rather than decide
  • Keep a human signature on money, contracts and people decisions

Find out more

  • Microsoft’s Code, Zapier and Make can all build a simple “watch this and tell me” tracker without programming
  • Using AI to screen CVs is “high-risk” under the EU AI Act, and a new automated-decisions regime applies in the UK

Part 6 · Risks and rules

Data protection: you stay responsible

  • The Data (Use and Access) Act 2025 is fully in force from 19 June 2026, with a new regime for automated decisions
  • ICO on agentic AI: you are responsible for what your agent does
  • Do not give an agent data “just because it might be useful”
  • Do a data protection impact assessment (DPIA) where the risk is high
  • ICO agentic AI guidance: consultation opening now, final version due this autumn

Find out more

  • The ICO’s words: “AI agency does not mean the removal of human, and therefore organisational, responsibility for data processing”
  • ICO on the Data (Use and Access) Act · ICO Tech Futures: Agentic AI · ICO guidance pipeline

Security: agents can be tricked

  • Prompt injection: hidden instructions in an email or web page can hijack an agent. The UK’s National Cyber Security Centre says it “may never be totally mitigated”
  • Real example: EchoLeak, a single email that made Microsoft 365 Copilot leak data without a click
  • OWASP’s Top 10 for agentic applications: goal hijack, tool misuse, privilege abuse, memory poisoning
  • The principle is least agency: give an agent the fewest permissions that do the job, and a human sign-off on anything that matters

Find out more

  • It matters more now that agents run in the cloud under their own identities (Autopilot) or log in as you (Grok Bot, Claude in Chrome)
  • The NCSC and its Five Eyes partners published joint advice on adopting agentic AI carefully in April 2026: least privilege, human in the loop
  • Short and free: NCSC: prompt injection is not SQL injection · OWASP Top 10 for Agentic Applications · NCSC: AI and cyber security

Selling into Europe? The EU AI Act

  • Applies to UK firms whose AI systems or their output are used in the EU
  • Since 2 August 2026: chatbots must say they are AI; deepfakes must be labelled
  • High-risk uses (CV screening, credit scoring) are delayed to December 2027
  • In the UK there is still no AI bill. Watch this space: pressure for one returned this month.

Find out more

  • On 14 September Parliament’s Joint Committee on Human Rights called for a UK AI law; the government still prefers sector regulators
  • EU AI Act overview

Copyright and where your data lives

  • The government dropped its proposed text-and-data-mining exception in March 2026: there is no copyright reform coming soon
  • Do not assume AI output belongs to you. Check the vendor’s indemnity.
  • Cheap providers can mean your data leaves the UK: ask where it is processed and whether it is used for training
  • Manus’s ownership changed hands twice in a year. Know who you are handing your files to.

Part 7 · How to start

Three first steps

  1. Pick one painful, repetitive task. Not “AI strategy”: the invoice chasing, the weekly report, the tender pack.
  2. Start in a tool you already pay for. Microsoft 365 Copilot or Google Workspace Gemini first; Claude or ChatGPT for the heavier lifting.
  3. Write the rules down. What it may touch, what a human signs off, what never goes in.

Find out more

  • On Microsoft 365? Ask your IT provider about the Frontier programme, and set an agent spending limit before 2 November
  • Write your rules on one page: which accounts the agent may use, what it may send without asking, and who checks its work

A ladder, not a leap

  1. Chat: ask questions and draft things
  2. Assistant in your tools: Copilot in Outlook, Gemini in Gmail
  3. Agent: Claude with Cowork, ChatGPT Work, Copilot Cowork. Give it a goal and your files.
  4. Automation: Autopilot, Grok Bot, Zapier or Make agents that run without you
  5. Agentic engineering: your own agents, built properly and tested

Most of this room will get real value from rungs 2 and 3.
