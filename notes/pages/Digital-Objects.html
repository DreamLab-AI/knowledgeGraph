About Digital Objects

  • Digital Objects is a deliberately ambiguous term that compresses three independent traditions of infrastructure design — scholarly persistent identifiers (Digital Object Architecture), interoperable spatial-computing assets (glTF/USD/VRM), and tokenised blockchain assets (NFTs and their successors) — into a single ontological category. This page treats all three senses as legitimate facets of the same underlying concept: an addressable, typed, transferable unit of digital content bound to a globally resolvable identifier, with the differences between senses reduced to choices about who operates the identifier registry, how rights are encoded, and what governance model underwrites long-term persistence.
  • The corpus context (jjohare/logseq, immersive-systems and Web3 research) places primary emphasis on senses (2) and (3) — spatial digital objects and NFT digital objects — because these are the active workstreams of the underlying research portfolio (VisionFlow XR system, AR-AI-Knowledge-Graph, DreamLab AI publishing tools). Sense (1) — Digital Object Architecture proper — is treated as foundational background and convergence target, since 2024-2026 sees real bridges forming between DOA-style persistent identifiers and metaverse/blockchain digital object infrastructures (DOI-NFT minting, Handle-resolved IIIF as NFT provenance backing, ISO/IEC JTC 1/SC 24 metaverse identifier drafts).

Sense 1: Digital Object Architecture (DOA)

Digital Object Architecture was proposed by Robert E. Kahn (co-inventor of TCP/IP with Vint Cerf, founder of CNRI — Corporation for National Research Initiatives, Reston Virginia) and Robert Wilensky (UC Berkeley) in their 1995 white paper A Framework for Distributed Digital Object Services, originally as a generalisation of the URN concept that would survive institutional change, format migration, and provider failure. The motivating problem was link rot: HTTP URLs encode location, not identity, so when a journal moves servers, an institution rebrands, or a hosting provider exits the market, the references in millions of scholarly papers silently break.

Core abstraction: A digital object is a typed data structure consisting of:

  • A handle — globally unique persistent identifier of the form prefix/suffix (e.g. 10.1109/5.771073), where the prefix is administered by a Multi-Primary Administrator and the suffix by the prefix owner

  • Type-bound data — the payload, typed against a registered data type

  • Metadata — Dublin Core or schema-specific descriptive metadata, mutable

  • Handle records — resolution information (URLs, public keys, email addresses, alternative handles), updatable independently of the payload

    Resolution flow: A client queries the Global Handle Registry (GHR) for the prefix owner’s service endpoint, then queries that service for the specific handle’s records. This two-stage resolution allows prefix owners to migrate hosting infrastructure without invalidating identifiers — the GHR points to the current service, not the current data location.

    Standardisation: The Handle System is specified in IETF RFC 3650 (Handle System Overview), RFC 3651 (Namespace and Service Definition), RFC 3652 (Protocol Specification) all published November 2003. ITU-T Recommendation X.1255 (Framework for discovery of identity management information, September 2013) elevates DOA to an intergovernmental standard, framing Handles as a security-identifier framework alongside X.509 PKI and SAML. ISO 26324:2022 standardises DOIs as a specific Handle-based application.

    Governance: The DONA Foundation (Digital Object Numbering Authority, headquartered Geneva, Swiss non-profit established 2014 as Kahn’s successor governance for the GHR) operates the Global Handle Registry through thirteen Multi-Primary Administrators (MPAs):

  • CNRI (USA, prefix 0.NA/0)

  • IDF/DOI Foundation (UK/USA, prefix 0.NA/10)

  • ITU (Switzerland, prefix 0.NA/11)

  • CAS-Computer Network Information Centre (China)

  • KISTI (Korea Institute of Science and Technology Information)

  • CNIC (China Internet Network Information Centre)

  • GWDG (Germany, Göttingen)

  • Coalition for Networked Information (USA)

  • CNNIC (China Internet Network Information Centre)

  • National Library of Russia

  • National Library of Korea

  • National Centre for Scientific Research, Demokritos (Greece)

  • Public.Resource.Org (USA)

    This intergovernmental cost-recovery governance model is the central distinguishing feature of DOA versus blockchain-based digital object systems. There is no token, no speculation, no decentralised consensus — only a federated registry operated by non-profit institutions under intergovernmental oversight.

    Production deployments:

  • DOI (Digital Object Identifier): >270 million registered DOIs as of December 2025, allocated by 10 Registration Agencies: Crossref (160M+ scholarly DOIs, 1−1.50 per DOI), DataCite (60M+ research data DOIs), mEDRA (European publications), JaLC (Japan), KISTI (Korea), Airiti (Taiwan/China), ISTIC (China), Public.Resource.Org (legal), EIDR (Entertainment Identifier Registry, 3M+ film/TV identifiers used by Netflix/Disney/Warner), Crossref-managed funder DOIs

  • ARK (Archival Resource Key): Operated by California Digital Library, 8 billion+ identifiers allocated to 1,000+ Name Assigning Authorities (NAAs) including the British Library, Internet Archive, Stanford University Libraries, BnF (Bibliothèque nationale de France), no centralised registration fee — pure namespace allocation

  • ORCID (Open Researcher and Contributor ID): 22M+ researcher IDs across 200+ countries, 25K−75K annual institutional membership for premium API access, founding members include Wellcome Trust, Sloan Foundation, NIH, MIT, CERN, Nature Publishing Group

  • ROR (Research Organization Registry): 110K+ institutional identifiers, free open-licence successor to GRID and ISNI for research organisations, joint Crossref/DataCite/CDL governance

  • ITU-T Y.4459 (IoT-DOA): Adopts DOA for Internet of Things device identification, prototype deployments in Chinese industrial IoT (Sany Heavy Industry, Haier connected appliances) and EU Industry 4.0 pilots

Sense 2: Digital Objects in Spatial Computing and the Metaverse

In the spatial computing / metaverse sense, a digital object is an interoperable 3D asset (mesh, texture, material, animation, scene graph, avatar) bound to a portable identifier and equipped with metadata sufficient to render it consistently across platforms — Apple Vision Pro, Meta Quest, Microsoft HoloLens, Unity, Unreal Engine, NVIDIA Omniverse, mobile AR (ARKit/ARCore), and emerging open-web XR (WebXR, Three.js, A-Frame, Babylon.js).

Format ecosystem:

  • glTF 2.0 (GL Transmission Format, Khronos Group 2017): Royalty-free open standard, “JPEG of 3D”, supported by 150+ engines/tools. KHR_xmp_json_ld extension binds glTF assets to Dublin Core/Schema.org/W3C RDF metadata, enabling DOA-style identifier binding. Sketchfab (8M+ models), Khronos sample model repository, NASA 3D models, BBC Reality Labs digital twins all distribute glTF

  • USD/USDZ (Universal Scene Description, Pixar 2016 open-sourced, Apple ARKit binding 2018): Production-grade scene graph standard, used in Pixar/Disney/DreamWorks/Industrial Light & Magic film pipelines, NVIDIA Omniverse (40K+ enterprise users), Apple Reality Composer Pro, Autodesk Maya/Houdini/Unreal. USDZ is the AR Quick Look format for Apple Vision Pro and iOS AR. Alliance for OpenUSD (AOUSD, founded August 2023) governs the standard with Pixar/Adobe/Apple/Autodesk/NVIDIA as founding members

  • OpenXR Spatial Objects (Khronos OpenXR Working Group, draft 2024): Cross-platform spatial anchoring and scene understanding API, exposing platform-native digital objects (Apple ARKit ARWorldMap, Meta Quest Spatial Anchors, Microsoft HoloLens Scene Understanding) through a single OpenXR extension

  • VRM (VR Model, VRM Consortium Japan 2018): Avatar-specific glTF extension, used by VRChat, Vket Cloud, Cluster, REALITY, and pixiv. 12M+ VRM avatars distributed via VRoid Hub

  • Ready Player Me (Wolf3D Estonia, founded 2014, $80M+ raised): Cross-platform avatar service, 11M+ users, integrations with 9,000+ apps including Roblox, Sandbox, Decentraland, VRChat, Cluster, and enterprise platforms (Accenture metaverse continuum, Deloitte Unlimited Reality)

  • MPEG-I Scene Description (ISO/IEC 23090-14, 2022): glTF-derived ISO standard for immersive media, deployed in 5G/6G XR streaming pilots (Nokia Bell Labs, Ericsson XR Streaming, NTT DoCoMo metaverse trials)

  • C2PA Content Credentials (Coalition for Content Provenance and Authenticity, founded 2021 by Adobe, Microsoft, Intel, BBC, Truepic): Cryptographically-signed provenance manifests attached to digital objects, increasingly used to bind 3D assets to DOI/Handle-style identifiers. Adobe Firefly, OpenAI DALL-E 3, NVIDIA Picasso, Sony Alpha cameras all embed C2PA

    Identity-bound digital twins:

  • Apple Vision Pro Persona (released February 2024): GAN-trained photorealistic avatar generated from front-facing camera scan, bound to Apple ID, used in FaceTime, Microsoft Teams, Webex, Zoom. ~600K Vision Pro units shipped 2024-2025

  • Meta Codec Avatars (Meta Reality Labs, research demos 2019-2024, beta 2025): Hyperrealistic neural-radiance-field avatars trained from 100-camera lightstage capture, target deployment Meta Quest 4 (rumoured 2026)

  • NVIDIA Omniverse digital twins: Factory/building/city-scale USD scene graphs with photorealistic real-time path-traced rendering. Customers: BMW (Plant Debrecen factory digital twin, $1B+ project), Mercedes-Benz, PepsiCo, Lowe’s, Heineken, Siemens (rail digital twin programmes)

  • Microsoft Mesh: Avatar and spatial-collaboration platform integrated into Teams, deployed across Accenture, Mercy Ships, Mitsubishi Heavy Industries

  • Decentraland/Sandbox/Roblox/Fortnite: Platform-locked digital objects (avatars, wearables, LAND parcels, UEFN Fortnite Creative islands). 2024-2025 sees increasing demands for cross-platform portability — the central unsolved problem of Web3 metaverse design

    Metaverse Standards Forum (MSF): Founded June 2022 by Khronos Group, 2,400+ members as of 2025 including Adobe, Alibaba, Autodesk, Avataar, Epic, Huawei, Ikea, Meta, Microsoft, NVIDIA, Pixar, Samsung, Sony, Unity, Wayfair. Active working groups (2025): Interoperable Avatars, 3D Asset Interoperability, Digital Object Portability, Identifier Reference Architecture, Real/Virtual World Integration, Privacy & Identity. The Identifier Reference Architecture working group is explicitly building on Handle System / DOA principles for metaverse digital object identification — the clearest evidence of convergence between Sense 1 and Sense 2.

Sense 3: NFT and Blockchain Digital Objects

Since 2017 the term “digital object” has been heavily appropriated by the NFT industry, where it denotes a blockchain token (ERC-721, ERC-1155, Solana SPL Token Metadata, Bitcoin Ordinal Inscription, RGB stateful contract, Taproot Asset) recording ownership and conditional rights over a digital payload that may be stored on-chain, on IPFS/Arweave, or on a centralised server.

Token standards:

  • ERC-721 (Dieter Shirley et al., Ethereum 2018, finalised EIP June 2018): Non-fungible token standard, each token has a unique tokenId, mandatory ownerOf, transferFrom, tokenURI methods. ~85% of NFT market 2024-2025

  • ERC-1155 (Enjin 2019, Witek Radomski et al.): Multi-token standard supporting both fungible and non-fungible balances per id, dramatically more gas-efficient for batch operations. Used by OpenSea Shared Storefront, Sandbox in-game items, gaming asset packs

  • ERC-4907 (Dual Rental Standard, 2022): NFT rental with separate owner and user roles, used by NFT lending protocols (BendDAO, ParaSpace)

  • ERC-6551 (Token Bound Accounts, 2023): Every NFT can own an Ethereum account, enabling NFTs to hold other tokens, used by Sapienz, Tokenbound

  • ERC-7589 (Soulbound tokens, draft 2024 building on Vitalik Buterin/Glen Weyl/Puja Ohlhaver 2022 SBT paper): Non-transferable attestations

  • Bitcoin Ordinals (Casey Rodarmor, January 2023): Inscriptions of arbitrary data onto individual satoshis, enabled by 2021 Taproot upgrade. 65M+ inscriptions by end of 2024, peaked at $300M+ daily volume during Ordinals NFT boom

  • Runes (Casey Rodarmor, April 2024 launch coinciding with Bitcoin halving block 840000): UTXO-based fungible token protocol on Bitcoin, designed to replace BRC-20. 250K+ etched runes within first three months

  • RGB Protocol (LNP/BP Standards Association, mainnet alpha 2024): Client-side validated smart contract protocol for Bitcoin and Lightning Network, supports complex digital objects with privacy through zero-knowledge proofs. Used by DIBA, Bitfinity, Iris Wallet

  • Taproot Assets (Lightning Labs, formerly Taro, mainnet 2024): Asset issuance protocol leveraging Taproot for Lightning Network-compatible digital objects. Backed by USDT issuer Tether for Bitcoin-native stablecoin issuance

    Major NFT digital object families (2024-2026):

  • Profile Picture NFTs (PFPs): Bored Ape Yacht Club (Yuga Labs, 10K NFTs, floor 12-25 ETH 2025), CryptoPunks (Larva Labs/Yuga 2017, 10K NFTs, floor 35-65 ETH 2025), Pudgy Penguins (8.8K NFTs, floor 8-15 ETH 2025), Azuki, Doodles, Moonbirds, Milady Maker

  • Generative art: Art Blocks (Erick Calderon, Tyler Hobbs Fidenza, Snowfro), fxhash on Tezos, Bright Moments, Sotheby’s Gen Art platform

  • Music NFTs: Sound.xyz (artists release song NFTs with revenue splits), Catalog (one-of-one music), Royal (fractional song royalties), MintSongs. Daniel Allan, RAC, 3LAU, Snoop Dogg (Stash House)

    Continuing challenges:

  • Off-chain storage problem: ~70% of pre-2023 NFTs reference IPFS or centralised URLs that have suffered link rot; the “permanent” digital object becomes ephemeral if the pinning service exits. Solutions: Bitcoin Ordinals (full on-chain), Arweave (200-year storage endowment), Filecoin pinning

  • Rights ambiguity: As Galaxy Digital documented in 2022, NFT ownership rarely conveys IP rights over the underlying work. a16z “Can’t Be Evil” licences (CBE-CC0, CBE-ECR, CBE-PR, CBE-NECR, six standardised licences released 2022) attempt to address this; CC0 (Creative Commons Zero) has become dominant for major collections (Nouns, Moonbirds, CryptoPunks 2022 a16z transition)

  • Wash trading: 30-50% of pre-2023 NFT volume was wash trading (Chainalysis 2022). Royalty enforcement collapsed in 2023-2024 as Blur, OpenSea Pro and X2Y2 made royalties optional

  • Royalty Defence: OpenSea Operator Filter (2022, abandoned 2024), Limit Break Cre8tive Rewards, Manifold Royalty Registry, on-chain enforcement via ERC-2981 metadata

Components / Architecture

Across all three senses, a digital object exhibits the same five-component architecture:

  • Identifier component: Persistent, globally unique, location-independent identifier. Realised as Handle (DOA), tokenId+contractAddress (NFT), URI/URN/IRI with provenance signature (Spatial). Must survive registry-operator failure (federation, multi-signature, blockchain consensus).

  • Payload component: The typed digital content itself — file bytes, scene graph, image, video, 3D mesh, document. Storage layer varies: on-chain (Bitcoin Ordinals, fully on-chain ERC-721 SVG art like Chain Runners/Anonymice), content-addressed (IPFS CID, Arweave TX ID), centralised (S3, Cloudflare R2), federated (Handle URL records).

  • Metadata component: Descriptive metadata (Dublin Core, Schema.org, ERC-721 Metadata Schema, glTF KHR_xmp_json_ld), administrative metadata (registration agency, creation date, rights statement), technical metadata (MIME type, dimensions, schema version). Often mutable independently of payload.

  • Provenance component: Cryptographically verifiable chain of custody. Implemented via blockchain transaction history (NFT), digital signatures (C2PA), handle modification logs (DOA), Git-style commit chains (Activitypub/Nostr/Bluesky AT Protocol).

  • Rights component: Conditional rights expression — transferability, derivative permissions, royalty splits, expiry, jurisdictional constraints. Smart contracts (ERC-721 + ERC-2981 royalties), ODRL (Open Digital Rights Language), CC licences, “Can’t Be Evil” templates.

    Resolution stack:

  • L1 — Identifier registry: DONA Foundation MPAs (Handles), Ethereum mainnet (ERC-721), Bitcoin mainnet (Ordinals), MSF identifier reference architecture (proposed)

  • L2 — Resolution service: doi.org/handle.net (DOA, ~99.99% SLA, 5B+ resolutions/year), Etherscan/Alchemy/Infura RPC (Ethereum), Magic Eden API (Solana/Bitcoin), Sketchfab/Khronos repository (3D)

  • L3 — Content delivery: HTTPS endpoints (DOA), IPFS gateways (Pinata, Cloudflare IPFS, Fleek), Arweave gateways (ar.io), CDN-fronted glTF servers (Cloudflare, AWS CloudFront)

  • L4 — Application: Reference manager (Zotero, Mendeley resolving DOIs), NFT marketplace (OpenSea, Blur, Magic Eden, Tensor), XR runtime (Apple Vision Pro, Quest, Omniverse), digital library (Internet Archive, HathiTrust, Europeana resolving ARKs/Handles)

Use Cases / Major Families

Scholarly Communication (DOA dominant)

Every modern academic paper, dataset, software release, preprint, conference proceedings paper, and increasingly thesis carries a DOI. Crossref handles 160M+ scholarly DOIs at 1−1.50eachcost−recoveryfee,distributing50M+ annual revenue to literature infrastructure. DataCite covers research data (Zenodo, Figshare, Dryad, institutional repositories). ORCID provides researcher persistent identity (Wellcome Trust mandate 2017, NIH mandate 2024, UKRI mandate 2025 for grant applications). The combination DOI + ORCID + ROR + Crossref Funder ID powers the PID Graph — a knowledge graph of research outputs, authors, institutions, funders, and citations.

Economics: Cost-recovery non-profit model. Crossref membership starts at 275/yearforsmallpublishers,scalingbyrevenue.DataCitecharges4-15 per DOI to repositories. ARK is free. ORCID institutional membership 25K−75K/year. Total scholarly PID infrastructure spend: ~£250M globally per year.

Cultural Heritage and Memory Institutions

British Library, Bodleian, Cambridge University Library, BnF (France), Library of Congress, Smithsonian, MOMA, V&A, British Museum, Tate, Getty Research Institute, Europeana all deploy ARK or Handle identifiers for digitised collections. IIIF (International Image Interoperability Framework) combines Handle/ARK-resolved canvases with shared image API specifications, enabling cross-institutional zoom/annotation/comparison interfaces. 200M+ IIIF-served images globally 2025.

UK Persistent Identifiers Group (Jisc-coordinated, founded 2019): Coordinates UK higher education, GLAM (galleries-libraries-archives-museums), and research data PID strategy. Members include BL, NHS Digital, AHRC, Wellcome, ICR, all 24 Russell Group universities.

Internet of Things (DOA-IoT)

ITU-T Y.4459 (2020) adopts DOA for IoT device identification, particularly attractive in China’s industrial IoT (Sany Heavy Industry tractor fleet management, Haier ConnectedAppliances, Foxconn factory equipment) under Chinese Academy of Sciences MPA governance. Western IoT deployments favour competing approaches (DNS-SD, Matter device IDs, OPC UA NodeIds). Tension: DOA-IoT is geopolitically charged, viewed by NIST and EU agencies as a Chinese-influenced standard, while DONA Foundation insists on intergovernmental neutrality.

Future Directions (2026-2030)

Convergence of the Three Senses

The strongest 2026-2030 trend is active technical and governance convergence between DOA-style persistent identifiers, metaverse digital objects, and blockchain NFTs:

  • DOI-NFT bridges: Crossref/DataCite experimental DOI minting as ERC-721 tokens for scholarly outputs (Frontiers DOI-NFT pilot 2024, MDPI evaluation 2025). Provenance backed by Handle System resolution, ownership tracked on Ethereum/Polygon. Estimated 1-5% of scholarly DOIs to have NFT bindings by 2030.

  • IIIF + C2PA + NFT: Memory institutions (BL, BnF, Smithsonian) experimenting with IIIF Image API + Handle resolution + C2PA provenance manifest + optional ERC-721 ownership token. Pace Verso (Pace Gallery), Verisart, Artory deploying as digital art galleries’ core infrastructure.

  • MSF Identifier Reference Architecture: Active working group as of 2025 explicitly building Handle System / DOA-derived identifier model for cross-platform avatar/wearable/asset portability. Target 1.0 specification Q4 2026.

  • ISO/IEC JTC 1/SC 24 Metaverse Standardisation WG: Formal ISO process for metaverse identifier architecture, expected to ratify Handle-System-compatible identifier framework 2027-2028.

    Projected impact (2026-2030): A unified “digital object” infrastructure stack is plausible by 2030, combining DONA Foundation-governed identifier registries with blockchain-based ownership/provenance and MSF-coordinated metaverse format interoperability.

    Provenance as Infrastructure

    AI-generated content proliferation (2026: estimated 90%+ of new web content AI-assisted) is driving C2PA Content Credentials from optional metadata to mandatory provenance infrastructure:

  • Adobe Firefly, OpenAI DALL-E 3, Microsoft Designer, NVIDIA Picasso, Stability AI all emit C2PA-signed outputs

  • EU AI Act Article 50 (August 2026 enforcement) mandates synthetic media labelling

  • Sony Alpha α7 IV, Leica M11-P cameras embed C2PA at capture

  • Projected: 60-80% of new commercial digital content (image, video, 3D, audio) carries C2PA Content Credentials by 2030

    Quantum-Resistant Identifier Binding

    Post-quantum cryptography migration (NIST PQC standards finalised 2024, federal mandate 2030) drives quantum-resistant Handle and blockchain identifier binding:

  • DONA Foundation 2025 RFP for post-quantum Handle authentication

  • Ethereum post-quantum signature scheme (Account Abstraction ERC-4337 + post-quantum verifier contracts) target deployment 2027-2028

  • Bitcoin Schnorr-to-PQ migration plans (BIP-360 draft 2024)

    Decentralised Identifiers (DIDs) and Verifiable Credentials

    W3C DID Core 1.0 (Recommendation July 2022) and Verifiable Credentials Data Model 2.0 (Recommendation 2024) provide an alternative non-blockchain digital object model where the resolution mechanism is the DID Method (~150 registered methods, did:web, did:key, did:ion, did:ethr, did:cheqd most deployed):

  • EU Digital Identity Wallet (EUDI Wallet): 450M EU citizens to have DID-based digital wallets by 2027 (eIDAS 2 regulation, May 2024)

  • UK GOV.UK Wallet: HM Government wallet pilot 2024-2025, full rollout 2026-2027

  • DIF Decentralized Identity Foundation: 70+ member organisations, governance for did:web, did:ion, did:key, did:keri

    Projected: DID-based digital objects (verifiable credentials) become dominant for identity-bound assets (educational credentials, professional licences, government documents, medical records) by 2030 — a fourth sense of “digital object” partially overlapping with the three established senses.
